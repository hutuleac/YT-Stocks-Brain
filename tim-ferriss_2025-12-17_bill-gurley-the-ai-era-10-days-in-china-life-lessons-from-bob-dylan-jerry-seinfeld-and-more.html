Contested REAL WAVE + REAL SPECULATIONai-infrafinance

AI is both a real wave and a bubble, and circular deals are the tell

Gurley says bubble behavior comes with every real technology wave, and even the largest companies are now making deals he wouldn't call clean.

  • Carlota Perez's Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital (2002), flagged by his Benchmark partner Peter: fast wealth creation draws speculators and carpetbaggers, so the bubble and the wave come as a pair.
  • Tim cites Jeff Bezos's distinction between a financial bubble (2008) and an industrial bubble (1998-2000), where durable technology survives. Bezos puts AI in the industrial category.
  • Circular deals began with Microsoft investing in OpenAI while OpenAI agreed to buy Microsoft services; Nvidia funded CoreWeave and also agreed to buy any leftover capacity.
  • Dario Amodei at DealBook: "Amazon wanted us to spend money we didn't have, so they gave us even more money." Gurley: "that's precisely why this is a questionable behavior."
  • One explanation he's heard: like a gambler on a hot streak, winners feel less loss aversion and take more risk.
  • Retail warning: SPVs sold by "interlopers", some without the underlying stock. The 100x AI returns came from investments made before the boom, so odds now are "really, really low".
  • Tim: your real risk tolerance isn't known until you've lived through a big drawdown. Most VC-backed companies go to zero, and private-company financials are "super loosey goosey".

High conviction INSTITUTIONAL VC: "ZERO INTEREST" IN NON-AIai-infrasoftwarecareer

Where to angel invest now: domain experts with AI in unglamorous verticals

Back founders who are the smartest AI users in a vertical with its own workflows and data, which OpenAI won't bother to crush.

  • Institutional investors have zero interest in non-AI deals, so a non-AI angel bet risks dying of neglect at the next round.
  • Stay far from the frontier labs' next item: if Anthropic or OpenAI is working on it, you're not protected. And a frontier-model company needs a billion-dollar angel check.
  • Moats are workflows and proprietary data local to the customer (waste management as the example) that must be stitched together.
  • Zillow, where Gurley sits on the board, has spent 5 years building realtor workflow tools like ShowingTime for in-person tours, plus mortgage and sign-off steps.
  • Angel investing has a dark side: a successful Silicon Valley CEO told him "I got 50 of these things. People don't return my calls".
  • Career advice for everyone: the best protection from AI is to be the most AI-enabled version of yourself. CS went from the safest major to a somewhat risky one overnight.
  • A 28-year-old serial entrepreneur from near Corpus Christi, whom Gurley met fly fishing in Montana with his 93-year-old father, was running his businesses at triple speed by asking AI everything; Kevin Rose spends most of his free time vibe coding.

Contested 7TH TRIP — 6 CITIES IN 10 DAYSgeopoliticsroboticsenergy

China is misread: province-level hyper-competition, cheap nuclear and real innovation

Gurley came back convinced that "communism" hides an engine of brutal competition, and that the US makes bad policy when it misunderstands it.

  • Dan Wang's Breakneck: provinces compete under the five-year plan and their leaders get promoted for results, creating hyper-competition with hundreds of firms in solar, EVs and robotics (and some ghost cities).
  • Xiaomi founder Lei Jun, whom Gurley has known since 2005 (Joyo, sold to Amazon), is "the Steve Jobs of China": Xiaomi is #3 in handsets, then built a car with no factory experience after sanctions threats.
  • Lei Jun drove 200 employees' cars, asking each owner for three positives and three negatives. Ford's CEO toured the same SU7 plant and drove one for weeks in Michigan.
  • The Xiaomi factory used about a third of the employees per car, heading to a sixth in 10 years; reshoring car production would bring back hundreds of thousands of jobs, not millions.
  • China (and South Korea) build fission plants at 1/4 the US price. Supply chains are integrated down to raw materials, so a reshored plant would be an assembly shop; Dan Wang: America is run by lawyers, China by engineers.
  • Innovation is real: a $130 MEMS lidar goes on every car versus ~$5,000 for lidar on a Waymo.
  • Critiques: the tallest tree gets cut (Jack Ma and Ant Financial); ByteDance's CEO stays invisible and won't IPO; DeepSeek and others weren't meeting Westerners; surveillance (which would upset FIRE's Greg Lukianoff, though street crime is rare) and Huawei-style intelligence gathering abroad are real problems to negotiate, as with fentanyl precursors.
  • Gurley's provocation: China may not care about huge market caps. Does America benefit from $3 trillion Mag 7 market caps, or are excess profits a form of market failure?

Regulatory capture, rigged IPOs and the 2.5% card toll

Gurley sees regulation helping incumbents across finance and healthcare, and expects crypto rails to break the worst of it.

  • George Stigler's Nobel-winning argument: regulation becomes the friend of the incumbent. Gurley's All-In Summit talk on it has ~5 million views.
  • IPO pricing is "god-awful stupid": bankers pick who gets stock and at what price instead of matching supply and demand. A 1999 Goldman Sachs email talks about using a hot stock to reward top clients.
  • Bond offerings, direct listings and initial coin offerings already price algorithmically, which made him a late crypto enthusiast; tokenization can route around the process.
  • Visa and Mastercard's ~2.5% take is "ridiculous"; with stablecoin momentum, he expects both to be in real trouble within a 5-year window.
  • After 2008, Dodd-Frank brought bank consolidation and the end of free checking for poorer Americans.
  • Healthcare: barring physicians from running hospitals removed a big source of new competition in one move.
  • Congressmen on committees raise money nationally from the industries they regulate; Gurley wants donations on the blockchain (Rich Barton: "shining flashlights in dark places").

High conviction NEXT CHAPTER: P3 POLICY INSTITUTEpolicysoftwareai-infra

Open source over patents, and a new policy institute called P3

Gurley wants ideas shared freely, federally funded research open-sourced, and a policy shop that copies the nuclear-energy turnaround.

  • He's "a top 10,000% supporter of open source": Matt Ridley's ideas having sex; he doubts minds wouldn't innovate without a 17-year patent.
  • The NIH gives ~$40 billion a year, some to companies that go VC-backed and proprietary; grants should come with an open-source rider.
  • Elon Musk open-sourced Tesla's patents, and Ted Cruz told Gurley Elon thinks speed beats court defense. The paper OpenAI was built on came out of Google as open source; OpenAI just exploited it fastest.
  • China has 10 open-source AI models in hyper-competition, backed by a 20-year history and a five-year plan with a big open-source section; Alibaba and Tencent support Linux and MySQL.
  • Strategy call: Amazon, Apple and maybe Meta should jointly back an open-source model as a defensive counterpunch (like Android), or risk a rival's proprietary edge.
  • P3 (purpose, progress, prosperity) is modeled on how nuclear sentiment flipped in 5 years; his BG2 episode at Diablo Canyon took weeks of prep. Topics: US-China, healthcare, regulatory capture, IP, K-12, fair markets.
  • First P3 idea: a global database scoring countries on regulatory capture, surfacing best practices. He likes state-vs-state competition (Newsom vs Abbott) and dreams of a state paying teachers 50% more.

Running Down a Dream: learn in your free time, go where the action is, co-climb with peers

Gurley's book gives people permission to leave the pragmatic lane, with tests to tell real passion from wishful thinking.

  • Permission moments: Matthew McConaughey's father on film school, "Well, don't halfass it"; Phil Berger's The Last Laugh gave Jerry Seinfeld permission to try stand-up.
  • People spend ~80,000 hours at work, yet 59% aren't engaged (Gallup). Angela Duckworth now says she'd weight passion over perseverance in Grit.
  • The passion test: do you self-learn on your own time? Dylan studied folk music in Minnesota more deeply than anyone, then hitchhiked to New York to find Woody Guthrie; Picasso was a perfect realist at 14.
  • Go where the action is: luck is preparation meeting opportunity, and an epicenter multiplies both ~10x. Tim met Naval at a coffee shop and Kevin Rose at a barbecue; Gurley is glad Austin Ventures never gave him an interview. Look for what the nerds do on weekends (Bre Pettis and 3D printing).
  • Embrace peers: MrBeast's four-person Skype group ran "20 hours a day" for years ("40,000 hours" of shared expertise). Peers need trust and a shared love of learning.
  • Pivots: Danny Meyer left a ~$200,000 sales job for a tenth of the pay; Sal Khan left a hedge fund near 40; Tito Beveridge built Tito's at 40 on credit cards and owns 100%; Sam Hinkie went from consultant to youngest NBA GM in ~10 years after Moneyball.
  • Working for free works: Danny Meyer staged across Europe, Jen Atkin snuck into Paris fashion week to do models' hair, and Tim refilled water glasses as a volunteer.
  • Gurley's own exits: as a Compaq engineer, a third near-identical computer project; as a Wall Street sell-side analyst, looking into four corner offices at 11 p.m. and asking "do I want to be this person when I'm 60?"

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Circular deal — An investor funds a company that then spends the money buying the investor's services.
SPV — Special purpose vehicle: a one-off fund pooling money into a single private company.
Industrial vs financial bubble — Bezos's split between overbuilding that leaves durable technology behind and pure financial excess.
Regulatory capture — Incumbents shaping regulation so it protects them more than it restrains them (Stigler).
Direct listing — Going public by letting supply and demand set price and allocation, without banker allocation.
MEMS lidar — Solid-state semiconductor lidar replacing the bulky spinning unit.
Regret minimization framework — Bezos's test of whether your 80-year-old self would regret not taking the chance.
Staging (stage) — Unpaid kitchen apprenticeship in a top restaurant.
