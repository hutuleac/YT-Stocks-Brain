Bitcoin as the hedge against an abundant, deflating world

Visser says AI abundance makes fiat assets lose value to crypto, so a 5% Bitcoin allocation is insurance, and it is his largest holding.

  • 5% of a portfolio 'always deserves an insurance policy', and Bitcoin is the asset young people regard as real and are inheriting wealth into.
  • Valuation framing: about $750T of global assets versus ~$2.7T of crypto; 'fiat assets go down and crypto assets go higher'.
  • On his own position: the largest percentage of net worth, up 10x+ since 2020, never sold, 'maybe' sells one day but no interest today; a Buffett-style position that grew into the largest by compounding.
  • Explaining it to a 40-60-year-old: do you understand fractional reserve banking (the It's a Wonderful Life bank-run scene)? Only three stores of value lasted: religion, gold, and now Bitcoin, chosen by hundreds of millions.
  • Bitcoin is like the S&P 500: individual tokens come and go and excess wealth ends up in Bitcoin. Ford's stock is the same price as 35 years ago; Bitcoin is the only asset he can 'guarantee' will be higher in 10 years.
  • Caveat he states himself: if AI makes everything free then money, and Bitcoin's price, stops mattering; until then Bitcoin should outperform every asset over each five-year window.
  • On 'is $100K+ too expensive in 2026': he says skeptics are starting to change their minds because of tokenization and stablecoins (Circle got them going last year), merging the equity and crypto worlds (Bitcoin miners, AI compute).

Personal story HOW 2020 CONVERTED HIMcryptomacro-ratesfamily

COVID money printing, a 13-year-old's $70,000 and Michael Saylor

Visser says he only 'looked through a window' at Bitcoin until 2020, when pandemic money printing and his son's crypto trading changed his mind.

  • COVID: he watched $6T printed, was an exponential-innovation believer who thought recessions were over, and asked executives whether $100 trillion would change their minds; 'dead silence'.
  • The wealthy were worried money printing wouldn't protect them while the poor 'yoloed' their checks; Bitcoin started rallying before other inflation assets.
  • His son, 13, turned $700 into $70,000 then lost it in the crypto winter; he explained every token he held, learned via Reddit and Twitter; this showed him it was 'ideas that are hopefully going to be monetized'.
  • He listened to Michael Saylor 'about seven times' in a week; Saylor's story (a business destroyed by Microsoft and then by zero rates) matched his belief that AI will shorten every company's lifespan.
  • Volatility view: Bitcoin's volatility will fall over time while Micron and Nvidia now trade at Bitcoin's old volatility, yet nobody calls them a bubble.

Einsteins everywhere: abundance, free energy and why wealth games still persist

Visser predicts near-free basics within 15-20 years because AI turns every hard problem into a 24-hour Manhattan Project, but still expects greed and status games to survive.

  • Math: Einstein's IQ is estimated at ~160; ChatGPT 5.5 is above 140; 'by the end of this year we'll have Einsteins everywhere', billions of agents on fusion, fission and cancer.
  • Prediction: cures for almost all diseases and aging no longer a worry within ~20 years; energy free; advanced 3D-printed materials; humanoids working.
  • If everything is abundant why build wealth? His answer: a pre-capitalist community model (the plow let people leave farming); he picks Maine, where 'capitalism only peeked its head in'. Humans stay greedy, 'animals fight over women, men, everything'.
  • Education arbitrage: good schools just mean better memorization; AI lets you learn from the smartest person times 100. Zip code determines Wall Street success.
  • Most people barely use AI because schools and workplaces restrict it; he works for no one and has 'digital employees' instead.

Longevity through HRV, balance and nutrition

Visser treats heart rate variability as his main health KPI and says aging is a disease you control more than you think, mostly through food, balance and novelty.

  • Origin: his grandfather died 'too early' in his early 70s; goal is to ski at 100 (started skiing in his 40s). Acronym MINDS: meditation, immune system, nutrition, education, sleep.
  • HRV measures balance: how fast you recover from fight-or-flight; it declines with age, he moved his from average to above average for his age (Oura ring), and says the main benefit was not ruminating. Cosmonauts originated it to prevent panic in space.
  • Simple test: if you can stand on one leg for 10 seconds you're likely fine over the next 10 years; falling is the number two killer after heart disease because muscle atrophies faster after a fall.
  • Supplements: almost everything comes from food, about 10 pills a year; soil nutrients have declined; magnesium, potassium and calcium come from vegetables; ~100+ plants in a daily smoothie; muscle is 'the youth agent'.
  • Peptides and GLP-1s: no experience; the host reports a pro-hormone stack felt 'limitless'; Visser warns quick fixes break balance.
  • Allergies (non-peanut) are 'not real': an overreacting immune system rooted in gut health. Routine: 15-20 min meditation, garlic and a tonic first thing; vary workouts and 'do new' to keep the brain young.
  • Blood work once a year if insured; he notes cost and wealth inequality contribute to poor national health; cites the Limitless documentary with Chris Hemsworth.
Worth noting
Health claims are personal opinion from a finance executive, not medical guidance, and the allergy statement is a contrarian view he offers without citation.

Wall Street myths and why he doesn't want his kids to own a home

Visser says money signals neither intelligence nor happiness, and that young people should prioritize mobility over buying a house.

  • Background: first in his family to graduate college (father a construction worker who didn't finish high school); asked to open Morgan Stanley's Brazil office within five years; partner around 30.
  • Myths he says he saw: the biggest sociopathic risk-takers make the most money by statistical luck, and 'those are some of the most miserable people'; Wall Street success tracks zip code, not school.
  • Money lessons: he once said $100K a year would set him for life; 'no matter how much you make you think you need more'; wealth creates entitlement problems for children.
  • Housing: he would be 'extremely happy' if his kids never owned a home; keep mobility; plans to split time between Maine, Utah and Brooklyn; home ownership is more of a status game than an investment.

Glossary & Entities

HRV (heart rate variability) — Variation in time between heartbeats, a proxy for how well the body shifts between stress and calm.
MINDS — Visser's acronym: meditation, immune system, nutrition, education, sleep.
Fractional reserve banking — Banks lend out most deposits, relying on depositors not all withdrawing at once.
Tokenization — Representing assets such as stocks on a blockchain; Visser's reason ETH may beat Bitcoin.
