The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Dan Ives puts 15% odds on Jensen Huang no longer being Nvidia CEO in 12 months; an exit would hit the stock 25-30% on day one.
  2. Core worry is key-man risk: no succession plan, ~60 direct reports, and the AI financing web (neoclouds, Anthropic, OpenAI) runs through Jensen.
  3. Ives says US chips to China are "done" after the spy story landed a week after the midterm "blue wave".
  4. The blue wave pulls the capex peak into 2027 (from 2028-29): leverage worries, a better free-cash-flow rebound, more separation of winners and losers.
  5. Anthropic's IPO valuation talk fell from ~$2T to ~$1T; Ives calls the reset healthy and expects OpenAI to list by spring 2027.
  6. Still bullish: SpaceX (expects it to acquire Tesla), Palantir, Meta, hyperscalers; cautious on Oracle and CoreWeave.
  7. Calls for tech stocks up 20-25% next year despite a Nasdaq correction of more than 10%.

Jensen Huang is Nvidia's single point of failure

Ives prices a 15% chance Jensen is gone within a year, and says the whole US tech ecosystem leans on him.

  • Ives: a Jensen exit would send Nvidia down 25-30% on the first trade; the market is pricing in that he stays.
  • The pivotal question for Ives: did Jensen lie to the board when asked about this six months ago; if so, he is out.
  • A prediction market cited by Pompliano: 39% he is out by 1 Jan 2028, 48% by end-2028, almost 60% by 1 Jan 2031.
  • No succession plan: about 60 direct reports; Ives names the CFO and a few internal names as interim options, and Tim Cook only as a spitball.
  • He built the circular-financing web (neoclouds, Anthropic, OpenAI) "single-handedly"; ripple risk to Oracle, neoclouds and Anthropic.
  • Pompliano relays the counter-story: the woman at the centre may be a businesswoman using relationships; Ives notes her employer was already on the US government's suspicion list. Neither claims to know the truth.
  • Ives wants real congressional and board investigations; a quick board clearing would read as a check-the-box exercise.

Chips to China are 'done', and the US lead is at risk from only two things

Ives says the spy story plus the blue wave unites both parties against selling chips to China.

  • Ives: US chip sales to China are over; the story landed a week after the blue wave, which already rallied both parties around an anti-China mode.
  • Counter-risk he names: not selling chips can make China more powerful, and he calls it a complicated spider web.
  • First time in 30 years the US is ahead of China in tech; Ives sees two ways that reverses: politicians go anti-data-center/anti-AI, or a China leap built on Nvidia/Jensen information.
  • A frontier model or chip breakthrough in China in 6-12 months could look like it came from the leak, and nobody would know.
  • Pompliano relays Trump's unconfirmed remark about an AI-company leader told to stop or face jail; he treats the media narrative as unreliable.

The blue wave moves the capex peak earlier, not away

Ives thinks last week's selloff over-read the midterms: data centers are largely permitted and demand outruns supply 4:1.

  • About 75% of roughly 800 to 1,000 planned data centers are effectively permitted; Ives sets 10% as not happening, 20% worst case.
  • Demand to supply for compute still four to one; spending runs $3-4 trillion.
  • Capex now peaks in 2027 instead of 2028-29, which he reads as less overbuild worry and a faster free-cash-flow rebound.
  • Nasdaq is down more than 10%; Ives compares last week's panic (Nasdaq down 5%) to 2008, March 2020 and April 2025's Liberation Day.
  • Hyperscalers with existing capacity (Microsoft, Amazon) should be worth more post-midterms; Meta is in a great position (3.6B users, works with communities on data centers) that he says is not in the stock.
  • Pompliano's macro list: hotter inflation, 10-year near 5%, oil volatility, a Middle East war; stocks rallying through all of it would be the most resilient bull market ever.

Anthropic's valuation reset is healthy; OpenAI follows

Ives welcomes a roughly 50% fall in Anthropic's reported IPO valuation as the market setting a digestible price.

  • Pompliano: reported IPO talk went from about $2T three weeks ago, to $1.5T a week ago, to about $1T now (soft numbers).
  • Ives: justifying $1.5T would take $600-800B of yearly revenue in six to seven years; some hubris came off, which is healthy for tech.
  • He expects OpenAI to go public by spring 2027 (maybe late winter), having learned from Anthropic's pricing and with a stronger enterprise business than it gets credit for.
  • Pompliano says Anthropic's new personal-finance tool shows frontier labs can compete with their own customers.

Your AI vendor can become your competitor, so sovereign AI wins

Ives describes three phases: get in the game, wall off your data, then run on your own.

  • Pompliano's tweet on Anthropic's finance tool drew almost 2 million impressions; CEOs of large partners quietly engaged, so private fear differs from public partnership talk.
  • Ives: phase one partners (boards need an AI strategy), phase two CIOs digest and put up data walls, phase three companies go on their own; this is why frontier labs push hard into enterprise.
  • Spend caps on coding tools are another phase-two pressure.
  • Palantir is looking better and better: sovereign AI, a use-case strategy, and a founder who has never deviated from the vision.
  • Ives groups Palantir, Snowflake, MongoDB, Salesforce and ServiceNow as the 'rubber meets the road' use-case layer.

SpaceX needs Texas, Tesla pivots to robotics, and Oracle is the leverage poster child

Ives still expects SpaceX to acquire Tesla and sees a 20-25% political discount that clears if Musk makes peace with Texas.

  • Ives: the midterms hurt SpaceX because its data-center build-out is in Texas; Musk will have to offer an olive branch.
  • He calls the 20-25% political discount on SpaceX the opportunity if the Texas relationship is resolved.
  • Orbital data centers: Ives agrees longer term, but the near-term job is Texas.
  • Tesla ends Cybertruck production to focus on Cybercab; Ives calls it a good idea because the future is physical AI.
  • Humanoids: Ives says China is clearly ahead of the US outside Tesla; US humanoids need about two years to go from prototype to functional.
  • Oracle is the leverage poster child (cautious per Ives); CoreWeave is not in a good spot because of its debt but should survive; Nebius is better positioned; Marvell's $100B+ stock deal drew a hostile reaction.
