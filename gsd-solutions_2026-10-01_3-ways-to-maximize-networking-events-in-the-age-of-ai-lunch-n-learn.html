The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. The host's three rules for networking in the age of AI: know what you want out of the event, make it easy to connect (both directions), and follow up on a schedule.
  2. Plan the follow-up before attending — links, resources and messages stored in one place so follow-up isn't scattered.
  3. Her discovery stack: Luma (she calls it the 2026 Eventbrite), Partiful, Gary's Guide and Lunch Club for matching.
  4. Boardy is her main intro tool: it texts her, asks if she'd take an introduction, and sets up two to four conversations a week.
  5. The big find: Muse by Meta scans business cards from photos, logs them in a spreadsheet and drafts the follow-up email for her to approve.
  6. It replaced a four-stage manual process (spreadsheet, mail merge, a VA) after a Claude-based build felt too cumbersome.
  7. Throughline: relationship skills aren't going anywhere — use AI for the in-between admin so attention stays on the person in front of you.
Recommendation THREE RULES, SET BEFORE THE EVENTcareerrelationships

Start from your end goal, plan the follow-up in advance, make connecting easy

The host says networking still works in the AI era if you decide the outcome first and remove friction on both sides.

  • Know your end goal — meet people, sell, get feedback or expand your brand — and reverse-engineer from it; it pays off even if the other party turns out to be a bot or an assistant.
  • Plan the follow-up before you attend: what to send (a link, a resource) stored in one place, so you can copy, send and customize instead of hunting for it later.
  • Make it easy to connect: skip the QR code that leads to a form or 25 links; offer the one channel the other person actually uses — a LinkedIn message and an Instagram follow can leave both people not checking back.
  • Follow up means messaging, emailing and interacting after the first meeting; she blocks specific time on her calendar for it, the tip that changed her results.
  • She had stopped attending as many in-person events in recent years and relied on LinkedIn and social media, which gave less real interaction.

Recommendation HER CURRENT TOOL STACKcareerdev-workflow

Luma for events, Boardy for warm AI introductions, Lunch Club as a maybe

She uses Luma to discover and host events and Boardy to get introduced to people, at two to four conversations a week.

  • Luma is a discovery and hosting platform she calls the 2026 Eventbrite; she checks it once or twice a week, registers for a few events, and could host there too.
  • Alternatives she names: Partiful (captioned 'Partle') for tech, business and community events, and Gary's Guide NYC, another discovery and hosting platform she used before.
  • Lunch Club matches you with random people by interest; helpful in the past, she re-joined about two months ago but has had no conversations yet.
  • Boardy is a conversational AI network — like LinkedIn in one AI — that texts her about someone's market and background and asks if she'd welcome an introduction; the counter shows about 20,995 introductions (captions are garbled).
  • It can connect to a calendar and suggest times from the other person's calendar; she skips that because she keeps many calendars and blocks work sessions that look 'fake available'.
  • Boardy gives her two to four conversations a week, and one led to a podcast feature; she sees it as a faster, warm version of a mutual-friend introduction.
Worth noting
All results are the host's personal experience; she is a repeat user of these tools and gives no outcome numbers beyond conversations per week.
Recommendation TESTED ON FOUR CARDSdev-workflowcareer

Muse turns a photo of a business card into a spreadsheet row and a drafted follow-up

She sends business-card photos to Meta's Muse, which logs each contact and drafts the follow-up email for her to approve.

  • Her old process: type cards into a spreadsheet and email each person by hand; then mail merge with custom notes (an hour down to about five minutes); then a VA entering the scanned cards.
  • The goal: as soon as she takes the picture the card is in the sheet and the follow-up is drafted, so she only reviews and says send.
  • She tried a Claude-based build first and found it cumbersome, so she connected her calendar, email and a spreadsheet to Muse instead and it set everything up.
  • Test: she sent four separate images and it worked; next she'll try one photo with about 10 cards at once.
  • She calls Muse (Meta's new AI personal assistant) Jarvis-like and the best she's used at actually getting things done.
  • Next idea: have the AI scan Luma and pick the best events for her goals each week; she thinks someone already has that workflow.
Worth noting
Only four cards tested so far; the host connected her real email and calendar, and the Claude-versus-Muse comparison is told in a separate video she links.
Principle HOW SHE FRAMES THE POINT OF AUTOMATIONmindsetrelationships

Automate the admin so attention stays on the person in front of you

The aim is productivity for positive outcomes, not being stuck in an automation loop.

  • Being in the conversation without panicking about a follow-up email lets her focus on the actual interaction, with follow-up handled afterwards.
  • Entering emails into spreadsheets isn't the best use of her time; she wants to spend it on meaningful, strategic, builder-level work.
  • AI is moving faster than people, but she keeps human-to-human connection as the priority and says events aren't going away.
  • She flags the risk of being 'in a loop' with AI as a conversation for another time.
  • Losing relationship skills costs clients, business partners, friends, colleagues and life partners — and affects raising kids.
What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🎯
Networking: Write down your goal for each event before you go, then work backward to the follow-up you'll send.
📁
Workflow: Store your links and resources in one place before the event so follow-up is copy, tweak, send.
🔗
Networking: Share one link to one channel the other person already uses instead of a menu of options.
📆
Habits: Block calendar time right after events specifically for follow-up messages.
🗂️
AI tools: Test an assistant like Muse on a few business cards before trusting it with a full stack.
