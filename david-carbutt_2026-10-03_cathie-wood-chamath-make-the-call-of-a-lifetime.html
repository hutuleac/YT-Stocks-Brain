Contested AMERICAN AI FEAR VS CHINA OPTIMISMpolicyai-infra

Is America's AI fear organized, and what does it cost?

Chamath says the doom message is scripted and well trafficked; the upside message is not.

  • Poll read out by Peter Diamandis: 80% of Americans fear AI, 73% say no data center in their backyard (more than say no to a nuclear reactor); ~80% pro-AI in China.
  • Jason Calacanis met Delta flight attendants who said they were relieved to be retiring before AI took their jobs.
  • Chamath: well-known figures are "deployed with very tightly scripted talking points that all point to doomerism"; the positive side is "not as organized".
  • Chamath names the sources of the talking points: Dario Amodei (entry-level office jobs warning) and Elon Musk ("you're not going to need to have a job").
  • David Friedberg: the people behind the talking points want tighter rules and control over who builds AI, which tends to favor the biggest companies.
  • Nikhil Chandhok (Circle): AI must make housing cheaper, education better and healthcare more accessible; housing alone is about a third of household spending.

Cathie Wood: AI leaves America short of workers, not jobs

She says job-loss data is not showing up beyond entry level and expects labor shortages in a few years.

  • Cathie calls the job-loss coverage the "Crisis News Network"; the numbers don't support it, except maybe at entry level.
  • Her logic: AI raises each business's productivity and ROI, so firms want to do more and hire more.
  • "ROI on one person managing a bunch of AI" is far greater than one person working alone.
  • She concedes entry-level roles are where it could hurt first.
  • Rates: she says interest rates rise if growth picks up and should, "that's the market working"; narrator: faster growth means more borrowing demand.
Pull quote
“I actually think we're going to end up with labor shortages and that's what we should go out talking about.”— Cathie Wood

Chamath and Sacks: AI is a race the US has to win

Once Trump said "whoever wins superintelligence wins", data centers became a national-security issue.

  • Chamath was at the White House AI lunch that Sacks helped organize; Trump opened with that line, repeated it, then stopped talking.
  • Sacks framed AI as bigger than the industrial revolution (railroads, canals, electrical grid combined) per a Wall Street Journal piece.
  • All-In host analogy: blocking a data center is like blocking tanks being built in WWII.
  • Six leading AI companies signed a voluntary White House accord on superintelligence: safety checks and outside auditors; not a law.
  • Sacks: the US needs more compute than its adversaries, and the "anti-data center crusade" puts it at a severe disadvantage.
  • Sacks says data centers already serve email, banking and video; fix privacy by law, don't ban data centers.
Pull quote
“Whoever wins superintelligence wins.”— Donald Trump (as relayed by Chamath Palihapitiya)
Worth noting
Sacks served as the White House AI adviser and co-hosts All-In with Chamath, both of whom invest in AI and compute exposure.
Contested POWER, NUCLEAR AND DATA-CENTER FUNDINGenergyai-infrageopolitics

The binding constraint is electricity, not models

China doubles its grid every decade while US power output has been nearly flat for 25 years.

  • Sacks: China doubling its grid every decade; US flat about 25 years. US doubled every decade until the 1970s.
  • Narrator figures: China more than doubled capacity in a decade; US output up only ~16% in 25 years; one large AI data center uses as much power as a small city.
  • Beneficiaries beyond chips: power plants, grid equipment and cooling.
  • Cathie: without the nuclear shutdown and regulation, US electricity prices would be 50% of today's.
  • Microsoft signed a 20-year power deal with a Three Mile Island reactor due to restart in 2027; Meta and Oracle borrowed tens of billions in bonds for data centers.
  • Cathie: 90% of corporate bonds funding data centers are US; the rest of the world has the fear without the animal spirits.
  • Sacks: the race is not won only by cheapest compute, but it is a huge advantage.
