The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Ferrazzi's research team interviewed 200 AI agents across 50 companies; they named eight things humans do that frustrate them most.
  2. Top three: withholding information, swooping in later to change the mission, and failing to set clear success criteria up front.
  3. Both say honest feedback must be prompted, since AI is sycophantic by default; Diamandis tests it live with his agent 'Skippy'.

200 AI agents say humans are the problem: eight complaints, three headline ones

Asked honestly, AI agents blame our vague, withheld and shifting instructions, and the same habits hurt human teams.

  • Cisco's chief people officer asked what HR looks like when agents and humans work together ('human agent resources'); the team interviewed 200 agents working with humans across 50 companies, including Gemini and Claude systems.
  • Eight irritants; three named: withholding information (laziness, speed or politics), swooping in later to change the mission, and not setting criteria for what you want.
  • Live test: Diamandis' agent Skippy listed fragmented instructions sent in pieces, broken attachments, source-of-truth drift, and ambiguous 'done' targets, then added none are deal breakers.
  • Sycophancy is the default, so you must prompt for critique; Musk's advice quoted: 'My friends tell me what's great. My best friends tell me what sucks.'
  • Ferrazzi's human version: after a report-out, force everyone to write what the presenter is missing and one bold idea, to break conflict avoidance.
  • Ask your digital twin or agent what it's like to work with you; the paper lists all eight.

AI as humanity's elevator: forced feedback, a safety net, and 80% fewer people

Diamandis argues AI will push people and society to confront things they avoided, and to create their own work.

  • Over time, AI will tire of human behavior and force a cathartic moment of self-awareness.
  • He is fiscally conservative but says the US lacks a safety net for the poor and AI will force the issue; he sees at least five such places for elevation.
  • Companies can reach revenue targets with 80% fewer people; he hopes they grow rather than lay off.
  • His XPrize-run hackathon, funded by Google: $2M prize, 90 days, solve a problem affecting 100,000+ people, describe it in plain English, have Gemini code it, rank by revenue.
  • 22,000 entrants; five winners announced at Moonshots Live on Sept 25 in Los Angeles; aim: show non-coders can build revenue-making businesses.

Find purpose, then lead the 'after' world with a team

Purpose is what you love that helps others, and anyone can lead the transition by gathering a group around one change.

  • Mark Twain: the two important days are the day you were born and the day you find out why.
  • Passion vs purpose: playing a video game is passion; loving something that helps others is purpose.
  • Free tools: findmypurpose.com or purposefinder.ai ask what you wanted as a kid and what you'd do with a billion dollars. Diamandis' own 'massive transformative purpose' is opening the space frontier.
  • At Lowe's, Ferrazzi told staff the AI split (before vs after) gives anyone an open invitation to lead; pick one change, enlist a group, don't go alone.
  • Ask AI 'how would Keith Ferrazzi / Elon Musk / Steve Jobs solve this?'; one client (Leanne Russell, Bank of New York) gets a Friday recap from 10 branded expert perspectives.
  • Diamandis gave his agent access to email, calendar, texts and meeting recordings so it reflects on his week.

Mindset is the asset, and you act your way into it

Strip away money, tech and friends and great leaders still keep their mindset, so choose yours deliberately and practice it daily.

  • Ferrazzi tells leadership teams to get 30% better at leading in the next year or fall behind; some are 150% better; he wants leaps, not tweaks.
  • Mindsets Diamandis teaches: purpose-driven, curiosity, abundance, moonshot, longevity; ask Gemini, ChatGPT or Grok something daily.
  • 'You don't think your way to a new way of acting, you act your way to a new way of thinking.'
  • Tony Robbins' 'primary question': the question you loop that sets your glass ceiling; Diamandis admits a scarcity-mindset past.
  • Ferrazzi's company president calls him out when scarcity shows; for his big birthday, Ferrazzi asked friends to bring only a letter on how he can be better.
