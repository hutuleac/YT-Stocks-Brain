Strategy WEEKS-TO-MONTHS HOLDING PERIODfinancemindset

Trade the other side of the hype: leave at the seventh inning

Kassam gets in as a theme starts to percolate and gets out when everyone at the party is talking about it.

  • Rule: if someone at a party pitches a theme and every chart is 'screaming high', ask how much is already priced in.
  • It's musical chairs: 'you don't have to get all the way to the ninth inning', seven innings is a good return.
  • Since COVID, retail is more informed and speculation rampant; GameStop and AMC taught him that things go higher and longer than you think, so size carefully.
  • Never picks the theme in advance (autonomous driving, crypto, cannabis); spots it as it percolates, when companies move together and deals get done.
  • Holding period: weeks to months, longer for bigger positions; 'singles, doubles' with a high batting average; a big day is 2-4%.
  • Best day ever: ~$80M on a National Bank of Greece recapitalisation arbitrage.
  • Tells companies 'we're here for a good time, not a long time': sells into strength, comes back when they need capital.

From deal flow to believer: Bitcoin, treasury companies and Strive's preferred

Bitcoin came to Anson as Toronto deal flow; Kassam now says it has inherent value and is 'here to stay', but late treasury companies are bad deals.

  • Early crypto companies listed in Toronto, where few hedge funds operate, so Anson was exposed to what became Hive and was the original fund backing Mike Novogratz's Galaxy.
  • Treasury-company (DAT) wave: first movers like Saylor's MicroStrategy and Vivek Ramaswamy's Strive got far better adoption; ~74 companies followed.
  • Anson's checks grew from $20M to $50M to ~$1B total over 18 months; the later and louder the music, the worse the deals.
  • Most treasury companies now trade below NAV, but bad covenants mean removing management costs 3-5 years of their forward earnings, wiping out the discount.
  • Strive's new perpetual preferred came at 90 with a ~15% yield (these usually trade at 100 with ~13%), with 18 months of payouts set aside.
  • Anson underwrites a ~20% IRR on 90-to-100 within about a month, then sells to retail; same playbook as Morgan Stanley's original MicroStrategy deal and Barclays.

  • Anthropic has been more prudent with its structured holdings than OpenAI and others.

Trading the robots: LLM summaries, prediction markets and night-time dislocations

Kassam's edge is watching how everyone else reacts, and more of them now react to an AI summary.

  • Nobody reads short or buy reports anymore; they paste them into Claude or ChatGPT and trade the summary. He finds it misses something 75% of the time and gets it wholly wrong 10-15%.
  • Prediction markets: 'better than polling data'; bettors often have real-world information (Pompliano's example: a Tesla windshield supplier betting on deliveries). Anson uses them as one variable but doesn't bet.
  • Big funds model Apple from every chip supplier, even Tim Cook's movements; he doesn't compete there, he watches the output.
  • In-house software tracks positive vs. negative retail chatter per name and flags divergences; Anson doesn't post on social media (SEC and Canadian rules).
  • Pre-listing and overnight markets (e.g. SpaceX and SK Hynix before listing): margin stop-losses can knock names down 10-15% in an hour; traders cover 4am to 7pm.
  • Pod structure with sizing limits; above them traders call him, three or four times a night; a six-screen Bloomberg sits two feet from his bed.
  • Won't compete with Ken Griffin's Citadel on automated trading; Pompliano cites a hedge-fund line about a London poker player waking early to play drunk East Coast players.

Kassam deliberately stays small enough to play where big funds' screens can't reach.

  • Big funds screen for >$5-10B market caps or $100M a day; Anson looks at $100M+ companies with at least $4-5M per position, up to $100M checks.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

DAT — Digital asset treasury company: a listed company that raises capital mainly to hold crypto such as Bitcoin.
Perpetual preferred — Preferred stock with no maturity paying a fixed dividend; treasury companies use it for levered Bitcoin exposure.
Par — A security's face value (100); a preferred issued at 90 offers upside as it moves toward par.
SPV — Special-purpose vehicle pooling investors into one private stake, often layered with extra fees.
Trust value (SPAC) — The cash per share held in a SPAC's trust, which caps the downside before a deal.
Long activism — Buying a stake and pushing management (buybacks, asset sales, a sale of the company) to lift the price.
