High conviction 2003 STANFORD TALK, RESURFACED BY ELONspace

The 2003 plan executed — but launch turned out to be the means, not the end

Elon's 2003 sequencing (satellite cargo first, people later, then a super-heavy rocket) came true; what he didn't foresee is that the payloads would be the bigger business.

  • 2003 plan: build a sound business on a known market, putting small and medium satellites into orbit, then move into human transport.
  • The "holy grail": a Saturn V successor, a super-heavy lift vehicle for a moon base or a Mars mission. Elon reposted the clip this week as "this was me, 2003."
  • Goldberg: in 2003 a space-based internet was "just beyond our thinking"; launch is now how you lift "fantastic electronic factories," for comms or AI, into orbit, and later build industry on the moon and Mars.
  • Goldberg read Elon's early blogs, invested in Tesla (TSLA) as the first investable Musk company, then in SpaceX before the pandemic (~2019).
  • He suggests reading the old Musk blogs for how deeply Elon worked every engineering problem.

High conviction FIRST V3 SATELLITES LAUNCHED ON STARSHIPspaceai-infra

Starship x Starlink V3: 10x per satellite, 20-30x per launch, launches going hourly

More powerful satellites, as many per launch, and a far higher launch rate multiply together, so Goldberg says take the last six years of Starlink and "multiply by 10, then another 10."

  • SpaceX's Michael Nicolls: the first V3 satellites flew on Starship about a week ago, another 10x in fronthaul capacity per satellite.
  • Each Starship launch is expected to carry 20-30x the fronthaul capacity of a Falcon launch.
  • Turnaround: Falcon boosters refurbish and refly in 3-6 weeks; Goldberg expects Starship to refly within days.
  • Cadence math (Goldberg): daily vs weekly is 7x, three a day is 21x; Elon replied to Herbert's post that launches should be hourly in 2-3 years.
  • SpaceX's safety page lists plans for up to 100,000 Starlink and up to 1 million Star Mind AI satellites.
  • Herbert flags a mix-up on air: Nicolls was introduced as SpaceX president, then corrected to the SpaceX AI side; Gwynne Shotwell is SpaceX president.

Confirmed event STARGAZE ANNOUNCED · LUNAR CONSTELLATION PLANNEDspacepolicy

Stargaze turns Starlink into a space-traffic network, the first step to a moon-Mars mesh

Before scaling to a million satellites, SpaceX is showing it can see everything in LEO, and pitching the same technology as the backbone for lunar and Martian connectivity.

  • Stargaze: star trackers on Starlink satellites catch streaks of passing objects; 30,000+ trackers now catalog orbits.
  • It tracks 17,000+ objects in LEO and about 90% of maneuvering satellites crossing Starlink shells, spotting maneuvers and fresh deployments (e.g. a Transporter launch) quickly.
  • Goldberg: a public good for every LEO operator, given the volume of avoidance maneuvers SpaceX has run every year for 5 years; Herbert reads it as reassurance ahead of the satellite surge.
  • Nicolls: high-bandwidth laser links will connect the moon with a constellation, then Mars, forming an interplanetary mesh; Elon: connectivity "throughout the solar system and beyond."
  • Same day, Herbert notes the US government stated a permanent moon base as its goal; Goldberg, who came to the US to watch an Apollo launch, calls the return of that ambition "extraordinary."
Pull quote
“This is the beginning of the space age, not what happened in the last 50 years.”— Larry Goldberg
Skeptical FIRST OUTSIDE RAISE · $10B+, OVERSUBSCRIBEDspacefinance

Bezos has put $28B into Blue Origin; the guest won't buy the first outside round

Bezos says Blue Origin's financial future is now clear enough for outside money; Goldberg wants proof the new management can execute before investing.

  • Bezos: he personally invested $28B over 25 years; Blue is raising $10B from outside parties for the first time, oversubscribed, "may take a little more."
  • His reason for now: he sees Blue's financial future "with some clarity" and waited until it would be a good investment for outsiders. Elon replied "Bravo, I'm glad Jeff cares so much about space."
  • Goldberg backed Bezos early after reading his annual shareholder letters, but did not invest in this round and wouldn't.
  • He credits the cleanup after the launch-pad explosion and the booster return, but calls the old management "a disaster" and says Bezos wasted money until he found Dave Limp.
  • Herbert: "SpaceX is so far ahead," though both want Bezos to succeed.

Positive view ELON CLIP · DIRECT-TO-CELL PATENTconsumergeopolitics

Starlink as an anti-poverty and GDP lever

Elon argues connectivity may lift more people out of poverty than anything else, enough to move national GDP; Goldberg says he sees it happening across Africa.

  • Elon: without affordable bandwidth you can't reach MIT lessons or sell your goods and services; since GDP is productivity per person, Starlink should show up in GDP.
  • Goldberg, born in Africa: remote villages and towns now trade, buy, sell and finance globally over Starlink; he says it has lifted GDP in almost every African country that uses it.
  • His political claim: South Africa still blocks Starlink because of race-based ownership laws he calls apartheid in reverse.
  • New SpaceX direct-to-phone patent: buffers faster beams so signals line up with the slowest, giving phones consistent delay; Goldberg calls it one of many incremental fixes direct-to-cell voice will need.
  • Goldberg: Tesla and SpaceX patent defensively, so others can't block them, not to stop others using the idea.
Pull quote
“Internet connectivity I think might be certainly a candidate for one of the things that would do more to lift people out of poverty than anything else.”— Elon Musk (clip)
Worth noting
The South Africa explanation is Goldberg's own politically charged reading.
Contested GOLDBERG ARTICLE VS WASHINGTON POSTpolicyspace

$38B "from government" vs $20B+ saved for taxpayers

The usual knock is that SpaceX was government-funded; Goldberg argues the government saved more by buying from SpaceX than it paid.

  • Washington Post claim: Musk's companies received $38B from US governments.
  • Goldberg's article: SpaceX saved the US government and taxpayers an estimated $14B, measured as what the same work would have cost without SpaceX.
  • Elon replied the real amount is even higher; Goldberg agrees he underestimated by roughly $4-5B by not adding this year's figures from the S-1.
  • His bottom line: he sat at the bottom of the estimate range, and SpaceX has saved "in the range of 20 plus billion dollars. No question."
  • Benefits he lists: kept the US ahead in the space race, plus the AI, intelligence and energy races.
Pull quote
“SpaceX clearly has saved the United States government in the range of 20 plus billion dollars. No question in my mind.”— Larry Goldberg
Worth noting
Goldberg authored the rebuttal and holds SpaceX stock.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Stargaze — SpaceX system using Starlink star trackers to detect and catalog other objects' orbits in LEO.
Star Mind — SpaceX's planned AI-compute satellite constellation, up to 1 million satellites per its safety page.
Fronthaul capacity — Bandwidth a satellite delivers to user terminals; V3 is 10x V2 Mini per satellite.
Starlink shells — The orbital altitude bands where Starlink satellites operate.
Direct-to-cell — Starlink service connecting ordinary phones to satellites without a dish.
Saturn V — The Apollo-era super-heavy rocket Elon named in 2003 as the benchmark to succeed.
