AI fills in moats with bulldozers, but alpha still comes from people

Griffin says AI is game-changing for white-collar work, painful for incumbents and great for consumers and startups, while Citadel's edge stays human research.

  • Work that took Citadel's PhDs and masters 4-6 weeks now takes a few hours; the Stanford talk where he said so left him going home depressed one Friday.
  • Three emotions at once: the threat to white-collar jobs, competitive moats "filled in by bulldozers", and a golden era for new business formation.
  • English-to-German translators "probably have already lost" their jobs; the US needs reskilling "in a profound way" — there's talk but no action.
  • Startups hire people with "impaired pedigrees" and give them a new career lane; that's underrated.
  • Research is the core of the business, "almost like journalism"; AI sheds light on bits but the narrative is still assembled by a person.
  • Trading is the most automated, tech-forward part — monetizing research efficiently lets you run bigger positions.
  • Why it nets positive: "you and I would rather live right here in 2026 than in 1926."

The AI trade grows discerning: GPU overcapacity, chip useful life and token bills

After two years of "buy them all", investors are asking how long choke-point margins last and whether GPU supply will outrun demand.

  • Old playbook: buy the hyperscalers, the chip companies, and Korean/Taiwanese/Japanese suppliers "I've never even heard of 5 years ago"; gatekeepers like Microsoft and AWS earn the rents.
  • Supply-constrained DRAM and MLCC margins are extreme; investors now look 2-3 years out to ask how long that lasts.
  • GPUs are like airline seats — very low marginal cost — so overcapacity sends pricing into free fall, as the 2000 fiber build-out did to telecom prices.
  • Useful life is the second worry: 5-6-year-old GPUs earn extraordinary pricing now, which "probably doesn't persist for another 5 or 6 years".
  • Corporate America is learning which work needs top-priced Anthropic/OpenAI models and which runs on open-source at a fraction of the cost — a lot of waste in some areas.
  • Host cites a WSJ report of ~$750B of planned spending in Anthropic's S-1; the open question is whether Anthropic becomes a must-have (e.g. cancer cures in pharma) with pricing power to match.
  • Bubbles: "almost certainly bubbles within this moment"; transformations come wrapped in bubbles (railroads, internet) and the survivors lead for decades — "I just wish I'd kept my Amazon stock".

Situational Awareness rescue: three mid-size storms, and why deleveraging is excuse number 10

Griffin says Citadel bought the AI fund's portfolio on the theory that forced-sale prices snap back once the overhang clears, and "did okay".

  • "Everyone's favorite go-to answer when they're losing money is hedge funds are deleveraging" — he's seen the industry deleverage "to zero at least 10,000 times" in 10 years.
  • Makes it answer 10: first ask what Capital, Fidelity or Wellington figured out that Citadel didn't.
  • Situational Awareness needed to raise cash and protect a large year-to-date gain; it is still up significantly for the year.
  • Three storms: the Kimi announcement, chatter on foundation-model growth rates, and large pension plans reshaping portfolios — plus too much leverage at peak.
  • Fast money front-ran the expected selling, turning "a possibility into a crystallized reality" — a classic finance playbook.
  • Trade done in under 24 hours, risk managed over several days; precedents are Amaranth's energy book (~2006) and Sowood (2007).
  • Would be "a bit surprising" to see another like it soon; Citadel had its own moment in 2008 — had Morgan Stanley not landed its Japanese funding, "I don't think I'd be on stage today."

Griffin's Trump scorecard: Iran messaging, immigration and tariffs hurt; the border is a win

He expects Republicans to lose one or both houses and wants Trump to listen rather than double down.

  • Republicans "will almost certainly lose one or both" chambers; the House is in a really tough spot, the Senate will be close.
  • Backs Republicans but bundled for both parties: supported Rahm Emanuel for Chicago mayor and held an early Obama fundraiser; says a slight majority of Citadel staff are Democrats.
  • Iran war is very unpopular and badly explained, though a nuclear-free Iran is "of existential importance".
  • Treatment of illegal immigrants is "disgraceful"; employed long-term residents need a way to stay, even without citizenship.
  • Tariff battles with Canada and Europe "a disaster" — pro-inflation and supply-chain-severing; most people "don't want to be in factories making Nikes and t-shirts" (a comedian's line, he credits).
  • Reindustrialize with robotics instead — why he gave $3B to Carnegie Mellon, whose Miami campus he expects to transform the city.
  • Socialism: France spends 60% of GDP on government and its streets are in turmoil; Paris looked like Chicago in '21-'22. Names J.B. Pritzker, citing his failed Fair Tax amendment after rejecting a pensions-for-taxes deal Griffin offered.

Growth is the only way out of $40T of debt — and the bond market is already warning

Griffin's fiscal plan puts productivity first, then education, entitlement reform and skilled immigration; without it, inflation rises, the dollar weakens and living standards fall.

  • Pandemic spending as a share of GDP matched what the US spent to win World War II — "the cost of us sitting on our couches".
  • 2018 tax cuts were "spot-on"; renewing them a year and change ago lacked fiscal space — Republicans' hands are "dirtier with the tax cuts" than with spending.
  • With $40 trillion of federal debt (he said "deficit"), crowding out is back: high 30-year yields and expensive mortgages.
  • Priority one, "grow the damn economy": data centers in America plus fusion, fission, batteries and robotics; push Europe to grow too — France's working-hour limits while the US funds its defense "doesn't work".
  • Two, fix schools: 53 Illinois schools had not one child at grade level; Eva Moskowitz's Success Academies works.
  • Three, a Simpson-Bowles-style up-down vote on entitlements, including working 1-3 more years before Social Security. Four, give foreign graduates (15-20% of elite schools) a green card with their degree.
