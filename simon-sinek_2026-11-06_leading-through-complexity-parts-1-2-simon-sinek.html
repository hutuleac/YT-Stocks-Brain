Principle PART 1mindsetcareerrelationships

Optimism is not toxic positivity, and small leader habits create separation or agency

Say the hard thing plainly, believe in the team anyway, and hand problems back to the people who own them.

  • Toxic positivity: a well-meant 'everything's fine' while the floor crumbles. His first-time-leader story: his one direct report, Cynthia, heard him vent after a bad client call and said she was glad, because his constant positivity had made her think something was wrong with her.
  • Optimism is grounded in reality: 'this is the worst thing I've been part of in years, I don't know where to start, but if we stick together we will come out stronger'.
  • Subtle mistake: overusing 'you' ('you did this') is condescending and separates; say 'we did this'.
  • Practising optimism means believing in and trusting the team, like a parent catching a six-year-old helping a five-year-old; when something goes sideways ask 'what are you going to do?', don't swoop in.
  • A past boss, Peter Tomaggio, never answered his questions ('what do you think we should do?'); it built self-reliance and he treated 'Peter, I screwed up' as a cue to ask how to fix it, while having the team's back.
Pull quote
“Optimism is not naive.”— Simon Sinek
Recommendation PART 1softwarecareermindset

Experiment with AI, fall and get back up, and own uncertainty with confidence

Telling people to wait creates anxiety; inclusion reduces fear, and innovation needs psychological safety.

  • AI is happening like the light bulb, the internet or the printing press; the question is how we want it to happen, so ask people to raise their hand and experiment and report back.
  • 'Telling people to wait is the worst thing we can do.' Staff care about what their Monday looks like, not efficiencies five years out.
  • Change management has no home runs: the best change manager hits a triple, it is base hits, and there will always be obstructionists.
  • Authenticity means honest but owned: same words about not knowing how AI goes, said with confidence, so people follow your confidence in leading into the unknown.
  • Rejects 'fail fast' since failure ranges from stage-4 cancer to a minor melanoma and some jobs are no-fail; prefers 'fall': comfortable falling, and the team helps pick you back up.
  • From the Marine Corps: followers give best advice, the leader decides, then everyone works tirelessly to make it succeed, not 'told you so'. Good leadership is good followership.
Pull quote
“Innovation does not happen in places that lack psychological safety.”— Simon Sinek
Framework PART 1careerrelationshipsmindset

High-performing teams run on love and shared credit, not individual stars

It is 'team' performance, so incentives that reward only individuals work against it.

  • Some of the best companies detach individual goals from bonuses: if the company hits the numbers, bonuses are distributed.
  • Navy SEALs: not deep internal courage but such love for teammates that they fear letting them down more than death; love of making someone else the hero and giving away the credit.
  • Trust breakers: lying, public humiliation, saying one thing and doing another, shooting down ideas before trying them.
  • Idea session: 'no such thing as a bad idea'; collect all ideas, let anyone build on any idea, sort green / yellow / red by time and money (green = within a week for little money), and say yes to green first.
  • Pair idea people with implementers, and keep senior leaders quiet in the meeting.
  • Graduate marketing class: he put all top performers on one team; they never finished first because each was in it for themselves, while middle-performer teams relying on each other always beat them, like the first US Olympic basketball 'dream team' finishing second.
Pull quote
“They fear letting down their teammates more than death.”— Simon Sinek
Worth noting
The classroom result is his own recollection across years of teaching, not a study.
Self-critique, since resolved PART 2 · AN UPDATED BELIEFcareermindset

Strict rules on vacations stayed; the ban on nights and weekends did not

Occasional after-hours work signals engagement, so a blanket ban was the wrong rule.

  • Vacation rule: an employee who sends him work email on vacation is taken out of the bonus pool, because commuting from a beach is not a vacation; he wants people to go 'sharpen your axe'.
  • He used to yell at people for working nights and weekends, until he saw data on high-risk employees.
  • People who never work nights or weekends are at high risk of leaving (disengaged); people who work too many are at risk of burnout; the sweet spot is the occasional night or weekend from people who care.
  • He still watches engagement and burnout but is 'no longer absolutist about it'.
Pull quote
“I'm not absolutist about it anymore.”— Simon Sinek
Counterintuitive take PART 2 · AI AND LEADERSHIPsoftwarecareermindset

AI is an advisor with a perfect memory; the decision stays human

Treat AI like a lawyer or a metric: it informs, it does not decide, and predictions about the jobs it creates are 'hilarious'.

  • Plow analogy: powerful tools have been entering work for years; the plow cut a field from three people and a day to one person and an hour.
  • Goodhart-style trap (he forgets the name): the metric becomes the goal, as with grades over learning and revenue over customer value.
  • Lawyers advise on risk and don't make decisions; abdicating to the lawyer, the metric or the AI is abdicating responsibility. Data-driven companies, he guesses, lack a vision.
  • Two years ago 'prompt engineer' was sold as a career; within a year it was not a job, so he does not trust long-range predictions.
  • Disruption is coming for knowledge work as robots hit factories in the 1970s and 80s; he thinks plumbers, carpenters and mechanics do not fear AI and skilled trades may see a renaissance.
  • His advice to knowledge workers: get a hobby that uses the hands, for the different way of thinking.
Pull quote
“It's just a data point.”— Simon Sinek
Worth noting
He says he cannot predict what happens with AI in the workforce; the trades-renaissance idea is his own bet.
Principle PART 2 · YOUNGER GENERATIONScareerrelationshipsmindset

Younger employees mirror the rules companies set, and human skills are the double-down

Mass layoffs to balance the books did not exist in the US before the 1980s; a generation that grew up with them is not entitled, just rational.

  • Misunderstanding: they are not technology obsessed; they are the ones putting phones away and chasing real-life experiences.
  • Every one of them has a parent or friend's parent laid off 'through no fault of their own'; corporate jobs are no longer the safe path.
  • Entitlement example: older workers did the extra work then asked for a raise; younger workers ask for the raise first, because the rule is no loyalty either way.
  • They want belonging and to know about their Monday, and are loyal when they feel part of something.
  • Technologists say 80% of today's jobs did not exist 30 years ago; so predict that 80% of jobs in 30 years cannot be imagined. Double down on people: difficult conversations, giving and receiving feedback.
  • 'Soft skills' is a branding problem; call them human skills. Hard skills matter when junior, human skills as people lead.
Pull quote
“Work is human.”— Simon Sinek
Recommendation PART 2 · THE ONE MINDSETcareerrelationshipsmindset

Every new hire gets a call, and the closing mindset is service

Serve the people who serve others, and be the teacher someone remembers.

  • Every new employee gets a call from him with the same two points: you are part of something bigger with outsized impact, and the only expectation is that you take yourself on and grow as a human.
  • He wants people to leave better than they arrived, loved or hated it, and says leaders must model accountability when they screw up.
  • Service means looking out for people, helping them see blind spots, giving feedback in a way they can hear, and standing by them while they fail.
  • Test: you remember the one teacher who believed in you, and not the names of that year's other teachers.
Pull quote
“Don't you want to be that person to everyone you work with?”— Simon Sinek
What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

📊
Leadership: Use AI and metrics as inputs to your decisions and keep the call yourself.
🌙
Culture: Replace blanket after-hours bans with watching engagement and burnout together.
🤝
Careers: Teach human skills (feedback, hard conversations) as deliberately as technical ones.
🛠
Work: Knowledge workers: build a hands-on craft or hobby alongside the day job.
💡
Teams: Run idea sessions with no 'no', sorted green/yellow/red by time and money, and say yes to green first.
🗣
Language: Say 'we did this' instead of 'you did this', and answer problems with 'what are you going to do?'.
