Cyber Cab goes live in Austin — but the hosts say the upside is already priced in

Public robotaxi rides finally started in Austin, but every dollar of theoretical robo-taxi profit is already baked into Tesla's stock price.

  • Only 45 gold Cyber Cabs are on the road in the Austin geofence versus ~500 regular robotaxi Model Ys — about a 1-in-10 chance of getting the gold car, and it's a strict two-seat max.
  • A widely cited "1,000 cyber cabs on the road" claim is debunked as misleading — that's the transportation-safety audit's fleet population figure, not the number of registered Cyber Cabs (45 in Texas).
  • The factory's true maximum capacity is 125,000 Cyber Cabs/year, but that's a ceiling, not current output — hosts warn against multiplying max capacity by best-case economics.
  • No confirmed retail price or delivery schedule: the sub-$30,000 target is likely off by up to 50%, and the original ~$25,000 goal (first floated years ago) would inflation-adjust to roughly $37,000.
  • The $0.20/mile target ride cost is an unsupported "made-up number" — Tesla's own aspirational goal, not a current figure.
  • Tesla isn't selling Cyber Cabs yet, only taking fleet-interest signups; its model is to sell vehicles to third-party fleet operators and take a cut of ride revenue, avoiding fleet-operation headaches like cleaning.
  • Hosts calculate that 2 million deployed cybercabs could generate $105B in annual bookings at a 30% operating margin — $31B of annual profit that, at a mature-business multiple, is "a fraction of Tesla's valuation already today."

Optimus, not Cyber Cab, is Tesla's real thesis — and it's not winning right now

Robotaxi's real function is as a proof point for Optimus, and Chris's sourcing says the humanoid-robot program is falling behind.

  • Elon has said Optimus will be ~80% of Tesla's future value, and that robo taxi is 100-1,000x smaller in scale — robotaxi success mainly matters as evidence Tesla can execute on the harder Optimus problem.
  • If Optimus succeeds at scale, hosts frame a theoretical $10-20 trillion Tesla valuation as the real payoff — not robotaxi's near-term unit economics.
  • Chris says he predicted back in October 2025 that Optimus 3 would not launch in March/April 2026 — confirmed correct, with no reveal or announced date as of September 2026.
  • Per Chris's industry sourcing, the Optimus program has lost key talent and lacks "bleeding-edge" leadership in generalized humanoid robotics.
  • Chris dismisses Jason Calacanis's (All-In Pod) claim of having seen an impressive, undisclosed Optimus 3 demo video as hype from someone without deep robotics-industry sourcing.

Robotaxi economics threaten to shatter Uber's model — hosts favor shorting Uber over Tesla itself

Tesla's cost advantage doesn't need to make robotaxi a great business for Tesla — it just needs to wreck the economics of pricier competitors.

  • Dave's read: even a mediocre robotaxi business for Tesla can still "destroy the economic model" of higher-cost rivals like Waymo and, ultimately, Uber and Lyft.
  • Waymo has more cars on the road today but runs expensive, lidar-heavy vehicles and faces the same regulatory hurdles as Tesla without Tesla's mass-manufacturing cost edge.
  • Dave's trade: short Uber rather than short Tesla directly — Uber's economics "get shattered no matter how you look at this," even if Uber builds its own driverless fleet.
  • Chris pushes back that a straight Uber short is risky and timing is unclear, since Uber is a broader logistics company (Uber Eats) than just ride-hailing.
  • Personal Uber complaints — dirty, malodorous cars and a feedback loop that doesn't fix driver behavior — are used as evidence Uber's management "is incapable of existing in a world of driverless cars."

GTA 6's trailer lands well with gamers — Chris's Take-Two trade survives a chaotic week

A strong fan reaction to the GTA 6 gameplay trailer keeps Chris's Take-Two thesis intact despite a volatile, rumor-driven trading week.

  • Chris read "well over 10,000" fan comments over the 72 hours after the 27-minute gameplay trailer dropped and found roughly 90% positive.
  • His updated thesis: high engagement should push first-quarter unit sales toward the high end of the 30-50 million estimate range and support $2-3B/year in microtransaction revenue once the online mode launches.
  • Bank of America reiterated a $367 price target on Take-Two (~70% upside) despite one skeptical line from the report going viral out of context.
  • A trader shorted $700K of Take-Two puts Monday morning, and an unsubstantiated online-mode delay rumor spooked the stock — wiping out the gains from Chris's one-day options trade (which had made "a few hundred thousand") and leaving him net down a few hundred thousand after his equity position dropped.
  • Chris doubled his Take-Two position on the Monday sell-off, then trimmed it to fund adding Amazon on the same down day — Take-Two fell from his #2 to #5 position by size.
  • Sensor Tower's low GTA6 pre-sale numbers (under 5M vs. 7M for GTA5 in 2013) went viral, but Chris discounts them: it's a digital game with no reason to pre-purchase, over two months remain before release, and Sensor Tower has never modeled console pre-sales before (only Steam).
  • Chris argues a rumored two-year delay to GTA6's online mode is implausible: the last GTA (2013) launched its online mode within 2-3 weeks of the story mode, and so much of GTA's revenue model depends on microtransactions going live quickly.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Cyber Cab — Tesla's purpose-built two-seat robotaxi vehicle with no steering wheel or pedals, the hardware behind its robo taxi service.
Robo Taxi — The autonomous ride-hailing service Tesla operates using Cyber Cab and modified Model Y vehicles.
Geofenced — A service restricted to a defined geographic area — Cyber Cab's Austin launch is limited to a mapped zone.
ARPU — Average revenue per user/player — the key metric for whether GTA 6's online mode can sustain billions in microtransaction revenue.
Stock tokenization — Issuing a blockchain token backed 1:1 by an actual share held in custody, giving investors (often outside a market's home country) economic exposure to that stock.
