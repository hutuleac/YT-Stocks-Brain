High conviction S&P 500 AT RECORD HIGHSmacro-ratespolicy

The market is the referee, and official data can't count

Bearish macro calls on oil, rates and geopolitics were wrong because the stock market kept rising anyway.

  • Higher rates and higher oil were supposed to sink stocks; they didn't, so the theory is wrong, not the market.
  • Approach the market as a humble student: something has changed vs 20 years ago.
  • Oil price is a market price and a correct reading; the interest rate is wrong because the Fed can't say what inflation really is.
  • Jobs reports get revised by a year later ("we overcounted by a million"): bad data in, bad decisions out.
  • Inflation is personal: Manhattan, downtown Iowa and a farmer facing fertilizer and gas prices each face a different rate than the headline 3.5%.
  • Almost no academic navigated the last ~5 years correctly; many of the best investors opt out of timing and buy great companies or hard assets.
  • Host adds: S&P 500 at record highs, probably higher through the midterms; AI has redefined macro so Fed and academic data matter less.

Counterintuitive take AI ADOPTION STILL SHALLOWmacro-ratesconsumerai-infra

AI is a convenient story, not the main macro driver

AI is a small piece of what's going on; blaming it hides that the economic data was already wrong.

  • Most Americans don't use AI; the latest figure he cites is 2% who pay for it.
  • That compares with 5% of Americans who spend on sports gambling, about 2.5x more.
  • Credit card data shows more consumer dollars going to sports betting than AI, per his reading.
  • He is "as bullish on AI as anybody", but AI became a safe way to voice the taboo that the data is wrong.
  • Result of bad counting, in his view: $40 trillion of debt, a trillion dollar annual expense and continued money printing.

High conviction BITCOIN, GOLD, LAND — 10-YEAR HOLDSmacro-ratescryptofinance

Bitcoin, gold and land beat productive assets because of money printing

Stock gains are mostly money printing; hard assets are the resilient core of a decade-long portfolio.

  • S&P 500 up about 70% over five years vs 170% for Bitcoin, gold and land.
  • Priced in gold, the stock market over the last 25 years is flat to underperforming depending on the dates picked.
  • A headline 12% S&P year is not a real 12% once money printing is counted.
  • Gold is "a hedge on government printing"; he says he doesn't hold tons of it and isn't a gold bug.
  • Productive land is the third leg; the trio blends volatility, since Bitcoin can fall 40-50% (it used to be 85%) while gold and land are steadier.
  • Barbell: most of his portfolio is Bitcoin-like assets plus illiquid early-stage startups; pairing uncorrelated volatile assets should lift Sharpe ratio.

Contested 20-YEAR SURVIVAL IS UNKNOWABLEsoftwareconsumerai-infra

AI disruption makes even Microsoft and Netflix a 20-year question

With AI moving this fast, few public companies are certain to exist in 20 years, so own resilience plus disruption bets.

  • Microsoft: likely there in 10 years, good AI positioning, but would agents make Excel unnecessary? He has "no clue".
  • Netflix: some think it could be zero in 5 years (he doesn't say they're right); personalized, choose-your-own-adventure content and self-driving cars could change viewing.
  • A Mercor study had frontier models take accounting tasks: accountants scored about 37% and took about 5x as long; the model scored 100% in under 10 minutes per task.
  • Accountants won't disappear: a century ago buildings full of bookkeepers were automated into Excel or Stripe, and finance headcount probably grew.
  • ChatGPT is only three years old; the pace out of the labs is the uncertainty.

High conviction BITCOIN BACK FROM $65K TO $85Kcryptoai-infrafinance

AI first drained Bitcoin, then agents may become its demand

Capital rotated from crypto into AI; now AI profit rotation and agent payments are a tailwind for Bitcoin.

  • Short term, capital chased returns from crypto to AI; Korea was the clearest example ("crypto is old news, we gamble on AI stocks").
  • He tweeted in May or June that it would be "glorious" when AI profits rotate back into Bitcoin; the replies called him an idiot.
  • Bitcoin then jumped from $65K to $85K, from well down on the year to roughly flat, as AI trades cooled.
  • Investors now ask which memory, power, data center and chip names are sustainable, or whether to front-run Bitcoin's return.
  • AI agents widen the addressable market for payments, stablecoins and Bitcoin as digital money that works outside banking hours.
  • Bitcoin's supply doesn't rise with demand, so price must; he expects "slowly then suddenly".
  • At his public company there are already more agents than humans; he thinks that's true of most companies, though agents mostly don't transact yet.
  • Host: Bitcoin is "gold with wings"; Pompliano says gold bugs don't like it but it's true.
