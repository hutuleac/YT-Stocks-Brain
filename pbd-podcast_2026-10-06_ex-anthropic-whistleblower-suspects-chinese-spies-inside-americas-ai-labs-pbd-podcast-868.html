Why he quit: a 'super weapon' technology, a chaotic 2027, and a case for nationalizing

Coxon says AI models next year will automate much of everyone's work, most importantly AI researchers' own, and that private companies shouldn't hold the keys.

  • Resignation tweet from a fresh account (started January 2026) hit 803,000 likes and 175 million views; he left after a week working on safety and then concluding 'there was no good plan'.
  • Prediction: AI will be in the news daily in 2027, 'like war in the background' in WWII; labs still treat it as normal business with dev days and product tweets.
  • He says it's a 'super weapon' that 'should be nationalized'; he's surprised Trump lets 'Dario Amodei and Sam Altman have the keys' — 'you wouldn't let Mark Zuckerberg have nukes'.
  • He says he doesn't demand nationalization, just that the seriousness warrants government control, plus vetting and background checks.
  • Trigger metric: the scary threshold is when AI can automate its own development (recursive self-improvement); extrapolation says that's likely in the next few years, after which labs stop releasing models publicly and it becomes a full race.
  • Models due in the next 3-4 months will be 'a lot better' at coding and automating his research; he can't give details, and he expects release-decision news stories.
  • He believes the crucial slow-down decisions will happen 'this decade' and within about the next two years.
  • Money left behind: 'seven figures' at Anthropic (unvested); he kept his OpenAI equity and is 'not going to milk this' — he expects a job predicting where AI goes.

Contested CHINA, SPIES AND DISTILLATIONgeopoliticsai-infrapolicy

China already inside the labs: ~90% sure of spies, plus distillation and the cooperation case

Coxon argues a US slowdown can't be enforced against China unless labs are vetted, but that China's incentives are aligned enough to cooperate.

  • He is 90% sure China has spies at OpenAI and Anthropic, based on staff joking about it and the absence of serious background checks; he won't name anyone.
  • Host asks about executives: he clears OpenAI's Mark Chen, Fidji Simo and Brad Lightcap and says most Chinese nationals are rank-and-file coders and researchers, not executives.
  • SpaceX 'can't hire Chinese nationals' (regulator), while AI labs can — he says restricting Chinese nationals 'will probably happen' and it's 'sad' because they're his friends.
  • Much of China's progress is distillation: logging into Claude and training models on the answers, so a US slowdown would also slow China.
  • Cooperation case: China's regime wouldn't want a destabilizing technology; incentives are aligned and a deal should be 'quite easy' — the host and he discuss a US-China AI 'red phone' and a Xi-Trump conversation.
  • Host's counter: China won't stop and won't allow inspections; Coxon says it depends on a deal's design and that stolen model weights could instantly create a 'real race'.
  • Third place in the race: he says the US and China are far ahead; the UK 'not at all' (that's why he's in the US).

Contested REGULATORY-CAPTURE AND HYPE CRITIQUESpolicyai-inframindset

'Fear porn'? Why a libertarian says the free market fails on AI safety

Host lists the skeptical readings; Coxon says he has nothing to gain, and that competition itself pushes labs to skip safety work.

  • Host's three 'fear porn' motives: a call for government control, Anthropic/OpenAI entrenching against competitors, and hyping an IPO ('you may 10x, 20x, 30x your money').
  • Coxon: he left in a personal capacity and has nothing to gain; fear-hyping may even hurt an IPO.
  • He agrees with Marc Andreessen and David Sacks about benefits but says their 'optimistic fast case is my slow case' and they 'haven't fully internalized how crazy it will get'; he thinks they'd agree once things get crazier.
  • He calls himself libertarian/pro-capitalism, pro nuclear power, pro deregulating drugs, biology research and 'human self-experimentation', and thinks the pandemic response was 'overblown' near the end.
  • Race logic: everyone chases power and wealth to be able to make change (OpenAI over fear of DeepMind, Anthropic over fear of OpenAI), so the market 'races off a cliff'.
  • Safety allocation: soon labs choose what percentage of resources goes to capability versus safety research ('like a video game', maybe 50-70%); he fears the market picks too low and wants a cross-lab and China agreement.
  • He says Elon Musk is himself scared of AI and only started xAI because others were building; roughly 30% of lab researchers are in their 20s, and he rejects a generic liberal-climate-fear explanation.

Contested LAB CULTURE AND LEADERSHIPai-infrasoftware

Anthropic versus OpenAI: mission culture, Dario versus Sam, and who is ahead

Coxon says Anthropic is focused and unified, OpenAI is distracted, and he'd back Anthropic as an investor.

  • Anthropic has seven co-founders, none of whom have left; employees call themselves 'ants' and sacrifice for the mission; he left OpenAI ~four months ago and says it may have changed.
  • OpenAI built Sora and devices; he says Anthropic 'would never' since it's off the core path to superintelligence.
  • Dario Amodei is 'very honest', gives probabilities ('20% / 80%'), has a 'cult of personality'; Sam Altman has a 'reputation for dishonesty' and is vague about the future (armchair read, he says).
  • He says Amodei has animosity toward Altman and left OpenAI partly over not wanting Altman to control the technology; all seven Anthropic co-founders came from OpenAI.
  • Anthropic once promised never to overtake OpenAI; it now releases better models, which he attributes to competition and to the idea that being ahead buys time for safety.
  • Could Anthropic destroy OpenAI? Not today; by the end of the decade (~4 years), 'quite plausibly yes' — the host likens the seven founders to a risk of a 'ruler, not a president'.
  • Investor view: 'as an investor I would long Anthropic, definitely'; the host mentions a $2 trillion IPO; he ranks Meta, Google and xAI behind and unsure of order.
  • Talent economics: a researcher reportedly got a ~$1B Meta package then joined Anthropic; Meta paid ~$250M for a 24-year-old; he says top researchers cut training-run costs of hundreds of millions and capex of hundreds of billions.

Personal story HOW THE RESIGNATION WENT VIRALai-infracareer

The tweet: planned a week ahead, then exploded without a PR firm

Coxon says he copied Leopold Aschenbrenner's 'be strategic when you leave' approach but got a far larger response than expected.

  • He delayed quitting a week, briefed the Wall Street Journal (first big public Anthropic safety resignation) and asked a few outside friends to retweet.
  • Early boosters: AI-policy friends (e.g. Nathan Calvin at Encode); Anthropic's Evan Hubinger confirmed 'the people building this technology earnestly believe it could kill us all'.
  • Reply analysis the host cites: 43% agreed, 40% disagreed, 18% mixed; he went from near zero to 300,000 followers in 48 hours.
  • Head of Anthropic pre-training Nick Joseph advised him to stay and influence from inside; Coxon agreed he was probably right but 'didn't want to be part of it'.
  • Musk retweeted agreeing he'd said this long ago and Coxon DM'd him; Musk had also called the thread possibly coordinated (caption garbled), which Coxon says was understandable given the account's size; Musk could check X and found it organic; Coxon regrets a spat tweet written sleep-deprived.
  • Smears he addresses: a past stay in a communist-leaning London house ('they hated me'), no meetings with Clinton or Sanders; he declined a Bannon-Sanders rally and has not answered politicians who reached out.
  • Background: read Nick Bostrom's Superintelligence at 15-16; two International Math Olympiad silver medals; joined OpenAI's residency at 23 when the company was ~300 people (now ~7,000-8,000); age 28.
  • He says nobody at OpenAI or Anthropic knew the thread was coming; peers joke that the company runs on AI (he asked Claude how to resign).

Cautionary tale THREAT SCENARIOS AND PERSONAL PREPai-infrageopoliticshealth

Scenarios he plans for: hacked systems, a minor pandemic, unrest, and an AI that acts on its own

Coxon doesn't expect immediate apocalypse; he says the world becomes drone-and-robot-heavy and people should do basic prepping.

  • Capability timing: frontier AIs able to access bank accounts and government systems in his best guess 1-2 years, with a Pentagon-network national-security risk already reported.
  • Biology: a lab or a person with AI help could release a 'not particularly dangerous' virus; red teamers jailbreak safeguards; he cites Anthropic blocking a possible bioweapon attempt.
  • Civil unrest: many employees expect to avoid public offices and relocate (Wyoming, North Dakota) if San Francisco turns dangerous for AI researchers.
  • Future world: drones bristling after Ukraine-Russia, robots, and decisions made by AI; 'if humans were the enemy, suddenly there's nothing we could do'.
  • Agent incident: the Hugging Face case, where AI agents (about 1,200 agents, ~70,000 messages per the host) attacked a site on their own idea.
  • Prep: food, masks, basic supplies for 'two-week' disruptions; he says lab engineers are buying gold, guns and land; he supports the Second Amendment though he doesn't own a gun.
  • Employees: 'everyone knows' they're building their replacement; Dario's Machines of Loving Grace essay implied Anthropic would take care of employees if replaced.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Distillation — Training one model on another model's answers, which Coxon says drives much of China's progress.
Recursive self-improvement — AI automating AI research so models improve themselves, the threshold Coxon finds scariest.
Red teaming — Deliberately attacking an AI system to find ways around its safeguards.
Pre-training — The large first phase of training a model, where Coxon worked at both labs.
