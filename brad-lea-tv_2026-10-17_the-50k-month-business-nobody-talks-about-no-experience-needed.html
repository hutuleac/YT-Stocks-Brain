Framework PRINCIPLES 1-2 · REJECTION · CONTROLLABLE ACTIVITIESmindsetcareer

Rejection is the job; three statements a day is the scoreboard, not $10K a month

Stop waiting to feel motivated and stop staring at the income target; count the activities you control, and treat each 'no' as proof you're working.

  • Opening reframe: 'you don't have a motivation problem' — it's an activity, belief or pipeline problem. People wrongly think they must feel motivated before they can produce.
  • Merchant processing is a volume business: expect 'I'm happy with my processor', 'send me something', 'not interested' every day. A negative mindset starts when you take normal resistance personally.
  • The job is to 'find the yeses hidden behind the no's'. Track attempts, conversations, appointments and statements collected, not just closed deals.
  • Focusing on money (rent due, 'I need 10 grand this month or I'm screwed') makes people stop the very activities that produce it.
  • Daily scoreboard instead: 20 new conversations, 10 follow-ups, 3 statements. 'Don't worry about 10 grand, worry about three damn statements.'
  • Thought experiment: if someone paid you a million dollars to collect three statements today, you would. 'Nothing starts without a statement', and a statement is much easier to get than a deal.
  • Be analytical: measure how many calls each statement took. Most reps don't.

Framework PRINCIPLES 3-5 · BELIEF · PIPELINE · EVIDENCEmindsetcareer

Sell yourself first, make one deal meaningless, and let action produce the confidence

Business owners already pay for processing, so you're offering a better version of something they buy, not bothering them. A big enough pipeline cures desperation.

  • Believe the offer: '$80 a month' feels small, but it's $960 a year and $9,600 over 10 years. Reps who doubt it go in sounding 'weak as circus lemonade'.
  • The real ask is a chance to improve their cost, service, technology, funding and time-to-fund, chargebacks and payment strategy, and to 'answer the phone. A lot of companies don't.'
  • Detach from single deals: being worried about one deal is 'a direct indication that your pipeline is not big enough'. Someone with 50 deals thinks differently from 'one restaurant hoping to God they call back by Friday'.
  • Create evidence: 'You cannot affirm your way out of a lack of action.' Call, walk in, shake hands, ask for statements, present deals, screw up.
  • Cautionary tale: people who waited 8 months to make one move, afraid of sounding dumb, now have a growing book of business and run events.
  • The loop: action produces evidence, evidence produces confidence, confidence produces more action, 'not the other way around'. Keep promises to yourself: no bed until the three statements are in.

Personal story 8-12 HOURS A DAY · 5 YEARS · QUALITY OF LIFEmindsetcareer

Five years of 8-12 hour days, a book that doesn't go away, and the mind as the controller

The payoff he sells is ownership and quality of life, not just money. Holding the mindset takes deliberate control of your attention and your emotions.

  • Time: 'Everybody gets 24 hours.' The wealthy are more deliberate about attention, energy and calendar space. 'Show me your calendar.'
  • Prescription: 8-12 hours every day for the next 5 years plus the Mindset Monday calls, and 'you're 5 years away from everything you ever wanted'.
  • Anecdote: leaving a plane he had rented, he met a man stepping off his own G550 who has done merchant processing for ~15 years and 'started with nothing'.
  • Quality of life over money: 'I know a lot of people with money and some of them are miserable.' Ask what you'd be doing with $50K a month coming in (fishing, family, travel) and work toward that.
  • A built book of business 'doesn't go away' unless you sell it. If you do, his company gets first option to buy it.
  • Spiritual view: holding a positive mindset is hard because 'there's good and evil' and without 'spiritual armor' one rejection sends you to a bar.
  • Analogy: the mind is a game controller. Life goes wrong when you don't know the controls; anger is a choice ('you decided to get pissed'), so catch it.

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

📋
Sales: Set a daily input target (conversations, follow-ups, statements) and track calls per result, not the monthly income number.
🚫
Mindset: Log each 'no' as a completed attempt, not a verdict on you. Rejection is evidence of activity.
🧮
Sales: Annualize the small saving ($80/month → $9,600 over 10 years) before pitching, so you believe the offer.
🚰
Pipeline: If one deal is keeping you up at night, prospect more instead of chasing that deal harder.
📅
Time: Audit your calendar: your best hours should go to the activities that build the business.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Merchant processing — Card-payment processing that businesses pay for on every transaction; reps sell switching to a better processor.
Statement — A business's current processing statement; reps collect it to quote savings, and it comes before any deal.
Book of business — The portfolio of merchant accounts a rep has signed, which keeps producing income and can be sold.
Chargeback — A disputed card payment reversed back to the customer, which costs the merchant.
