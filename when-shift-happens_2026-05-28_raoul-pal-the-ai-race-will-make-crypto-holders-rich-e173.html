The AI race is too big to fail, so liquidity and capital keep flowing

Pal says game theory means neither the US nor China can stop, so every dip in AI-linked assets is a buy and the Fed will 'do nothing' and let productivity grow the economy past its debt.

  • Game theory: no superpower can allow a rival AGI, only the US and China can afford the race, so 'nobody will stop'. If OpenAI blew up, the US would auction its assets across Microsoft, Google and others rather than let one firm double its compute.
  • He frames Iran and Venezuela as the same game, 'converting units of energy into units of intelligence', his 'universal code'.
  • Demand: chip and compute firms are booked out ~3 years; xAI is handing older data centers to Anthropic, which cannot get enough compute; nobody can get enough chips or energy.
  • Financing: unlike the late-1990s boom there is little debt; spending is funded by the most cash-generative firms ever. Anthropic expected 10x growth in Q1 and got 80x; Google trades on 22x forward revenue while earnings, not multiples, are rising.
  • Policy path: Treasury Secretary Bessent's 'Greenspan-style' plan, with a new Fed chair (Warsh) who cuts and then does nothing, lets the economy outgrow debt so debt-to-GDP falls; debt has been rolled to the short end.
  • Valuation caution: semis (SMH), Nvidia and Google have gone through two standard deviations on their long-term log trend; the open question is whether 2022 was a trend shift or a bubble.
  • He holds Rocket Lab (about 13x since purchase) as an example of equity opportunities, and calls Nvidia the key 'units of energy to intelligence' bottleneck.

Reed's law, agents as economic actors, and the system breaking

Pal says AI is the first observed case of Reed's law (exponential of the exponential), which means institutions and economic measurement will stop functioning.

  • Definition: the system can no longer keep up with technology's speed; the growth formula (population + productivity + debt) breaks when population can be 18 billion, 100 billion or a trillion agents.
  • ARC research cited: AI's annual word output exceeded all humans' annual output in three years, and by 2028 will exceed all words ever written by humanity.
  • Speed: silicon runs about six orders of magnitude faster than a neuron, 'a million times the speed'; Elon's robots may be the largest product ever sold.
  • Economic agents were 'birthed' last October and are scaling; memecoins foreshadow instant capital formation and destruction, with agents building and closing digital businesses quickly.
  • He earlier said six years; now it's about four years to own as much as possible before the biggest uncertainty.
Worth noting
Reed's law as applied here is Pal's own framing and has no cited empirical source in the conversation beyond the ARC word-count claim.

A nasty correction in a bull market: the worst is over for crypto

Pal says liquidity is returning, agents give crypto an infinite addressable market, and Bitcoin and crypto are as cheap as ever versus the NASDAQ.

  • Cause of the slump: the US withdrew liquidity during the government shutdown, which lags crypto by ~3 months (the 10/10 crash); capital also flowed to AI, and SaaS and crypto traced identical charts.
  • Bitcoin's peak was last December; falling for ~6 months is the same length as 2021's January-July drop. Altcoins fall more in 50% corrections (Solana -80% in 2021).
  • Crypto's addressable market is now agents, not just humans; his earlier ~$100 trillion crypto target was human-based and 'just keeps going'. The Clarity Act signing is expected; stablecoins have a run rate near $100T a year.
  • Macro: ~$6-8T of interest payments coming means debasement continues; fear and greed index spent the longest period below 10 and hit its lowest reading ever.
  • Probabilities: bull case a permanent Middle East resolution; bear case unresolved war, inflation, tightening and starved tech financing; odds 70% positive / 30% negative.
  • Bitcoin/crypto sit at 2 standard deviations oversold vs the NASDAQ on the log trend; 'the market owes you nothing', product-market fit wins (Hyperliquid), and stablecoins plus banks arrive over 'the next 24 months'.
  • A K-shaped concern: many coins won't recover and stablecoin firms can't be bought via tokens; Pal says he doesn't believe that, and expects ISM strength and data-center buildout to feed the 'casino'.

Layer ones as 'universal basic equity', plus Zcash as Bitcoin with privacy

Pal argues three to five smart-contract chains will capture the machine economy and that owning them is how humans share in AI's gains.

  • Thesis: like operating systems and cloud, layer ones consolidate to 3-5 winners; DeFi suits machines (no front end) and AI agents will be the largest users, rebalancing multi-currency stablecoin treasuries.
  • Ranking by 'economic density': Ethereum has the most developers and Lindy security ('nobody gets fired for ETH'); Solana is faster and cheaper; Sui is earliest but has orders-of-magnitude speed and programmability; those three held density through an 80% market fall.
  • Metric he watches: stablecoins versus TVL; Sui's TVL roughly equals its stablecoin size and he wants twice that. DCF analysis misses the point.
  • Ethereum's economic value destroyed if unplugged is huge (every layer two, DeFi, RWAs and NFTs); he also predicted in 2014 that blockchain would be the financial industry's rail and that you can front-run the institutions.
  • Purchases: more Sui, plus Zcash on the correction ('Bitcoin with privacy', potentially 5-10% of Bitcoin's value, per Barry Silbert); right-curve angle is quantum-proofing against AI-empowered government. He bought too little as it ran up 50% and used new cash.
  • DeFi hacks (a $300M one) force better products; banks also lose a percentage to hacks, and layer ones are hard to destroy.
  • Bitcoin vs layer ones: Bitcoin targets global savings (power law, capped at 100% of savings); layer ones scale without limit.

A grail-NFT fund: digital art as the trophy asset of the machine economy

Pal plans to launch an NFT fund because he thinks digital art accrues vast value as crypto's wealth effect returns.

  • Structure: top tier 'grail' one-of-ones with proven social consensus (rare CryptoPunks, XCOPY pieces) trading ~$600K-$30M; a middle tier of artists with convexity; a lending arm on markets like Gondi at 15%+ yields against NFT collateral.

  • Return logic: an artist rerated 20 to 200 ETH is 10x, times ETH's own rerating; a 100x over ten years if ETH reaches ~20,000 is possible.
  • Real-estate analogy (from Jeff Park): the $20M penthouse holds value, the $1M flat does not; a cheap piece in a great 'neighborhood' (next to the $20M punks) is a decent bet; he admits some collections may never rerate.
  • Audience: family offices, curious non-crypto people and crypto OGs who never bought art. An unnamed partner announcement is coming.
  • He says art records the culture of the era in which humans stop being the apex intelligence, and that all tycoons buy art after making money. Time frame is much longer than the rest of crypto.

Never sell, buy monthly, and use the log-regression bands only at the extremes

Pal says almost nobody times tops and bottoms, so own the trend, add monthly, and act only when price is one to two standard deviations off its log trend.

  • Method: buy at 1-2 standard deviations oversold on the log-regression channel; sell only into 2-sigma overbought, mostly for lifestyle spending; do nothing in the middle. It implies trading about 2-3 times in five years.
  • Why not trade: selling 25-50% at the top and re-buying at the bottom is almost impossible; in 35 years he knows nobody who does it.
  • Evidence: most traders lose; the highest-profit brokerage customers are dead ones; Paul Tudor Jones, Louis Bacon and Stan Druckenmiller are 'five-sigma talents' who also earned asset-management fees. The crypto fortunes belong to people who did nothing.
  • Sizing logic: if the thesis hasn't changed and tomorrow is more digital than today, a 60-80% drawdown is a chance to add; he lives off salary and income so he doesn't need to sell.
  • Framing: 'humanity's pension plan'; trading burns mental and emotional energy, so hold Bitcoin and spend energy on AI.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Economic singularity — Pal's term for the point where AI moves too fast for institutions and economic measurement to function.
Reed's law — A network-value law (exponential in group-forming subsets), which Pal says AI is the first observed case of.
Universal code — Pal's idea that everything converts units of energy into units of intelligence.
Log-regression channel — A long-term trend band; deviations of 1-2 standard deviations trigger his buy or sell levels.
Economic density — Pal's measure of a chain's developers, applications per user and stablecoin-to-TVL strength.
