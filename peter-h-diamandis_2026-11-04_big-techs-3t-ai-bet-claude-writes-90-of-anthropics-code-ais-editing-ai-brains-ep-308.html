Big Tech's $3T AI buildout meets nervous lenders

The panel says the spend keeps doubling down because the prize is permanent business domination — the open question is how much money exists in the world.

  • Five giants: $3T+ in leases, buildings, power and equipment; Google alone over $1T (~8x last year), Meta over $800B, some leases 30 years; Apollo cost ~$280B in today's dollars.
  • Wall Street Journal: lenders now demand tougher terms; Financial Times: OpenAI cut its new round to ~$20B (from $30B) at a $1.25T valuation (from $1.4T).
  • Hedging everywhere: HSBC is talking to insurers about offloading data-center loan risk; CoreWeave put options hit a record in October; ex-tech employees hedge their stock.
  • Dave: Elon is "torpedoing" rivals' financing — lend to him at ~8% rather than to AI-only firms at ~15%.
  • Compute is scarce: H100 prices are rising on 3-year-old chips; Dave could only get an NVL72 via a $15M three-year lease, online in February.
  • Salim: biggest infrastructure buildout in history, likely with a bubble — direction right, timing hard; radically new compute is at least 3-5 years out.
  • Alex: next capital pool is insurance (~$6T life, ~$3T P&C); Dave says EverQuote's earnings call now centers on insurers swapping investment for compute capacity; a Berkshire-style "margin of safety" has no data-center equivalent yet (maybe Crusoe).

Expert work scores 12%, everyday errands 81%

GDPX shows frontier models barely start on real expert jobs, while OpenAI's own LifeEval says they already handle most errands.

  • GDPX (SemiAnalysis, Dylan Patel): agent left alone up to 8 hours on 206 tasks in 11 fields, e.g. a PE deal in a 2,000-file data room; built with 14 specialist partners.
  • Scores: Opus 5.5 12%, Gemini 4 Carbon 10.9%, GPT6 Astra 9.6%; almost every model scored zero on finance and legal.
  • Gemini 4 Carbon fills a gap on the cost/performance Pareto frontier — unreleased, so Google may be subsidizing tokens; results are spiky even within STEM.
  • OpenAI LifeEval (its own benchmark on its own models): 3,995 Americans surveyed, 169 tasks via browser; GPT 5.2 49.3% (Dec 2025) → GPT6 Astra 80.6% (Sep 2026).
  • Alex reads the tight GPT 5.4-to-Astra cluster as OpenAI de-prioritizing consumer tasks after its pivot to enterprise.
  • Bottleneck is connection, not capability: an EverQuote analyst lost 4 hours moving insurance by phone; Meta's Muse is "imminent" but Facebook's paid-click model has no clean AI equivalent yet.
  • Build your own benchmark for what you care about (Jonathan Hendler's MediationBench); Salim tracks economic value per unit of human involvement; Peter readies an Abundance/Singularity index.

Anthropic's S-1 makes recursive self-improvement a line item

Claude's internal usage now costs more than Anthropic's payroll and writes 90% of its code — the panel's financial proof that RSI has arrived.

  • Since April, internal Claude usage at consumer prices exceeds monthly cash payroll; usage grew 250x January-September.
  • Claude authored over 90% of September 2026 code contributions; daily code contributions per engineer are 45x two years ago.
  • Alex: by capital/opex, "we've arrived" — but real RSI is physical recursion (nanobots building copies); Salim notes Claude's share of its own next version went from ~10% to 40% and 60%.
  • Dave's math: top models cost ~$1/day per human-equivalent; a Lightwave engineer burned half a billion tokens (~$5,000) in a day ≈ 5,000 people; costs fall ~10x a year.
  • Salim's playbook: under 50 people, brute-force the shift; over 50, build a side stack and move workflows over one by one — one firm cut 22 steps to four.
  • Use cases: a Cisco junior employee reportedly asked to rewrite the whole codebase with AI; Kevin Weil's new fully automated biotech research company raised big.

Models editing other models' weights directly

MIT's "Sparks of Weight Space Programming" shows a frontier model surgically fixing a small model with no retraining — a possible shortcut to RSI.

  • Astra diagnosed why Qwen 3.5 got stuck in thinking loops, edited 0.016% of its weights and lifted its math score from 7.9% to 48.1% — no backprop.
  • Alex: future of mechanistic interpretability and of "abliteration" (stripping open models' guardrails); what OpenAI's shut-down superalignment effort meant to do.
  • Dave: a path to a Hugging Face for finished neural modules — bolt a biotech or Swahili module onto a ~40B base instead of a $100M+ retrain (e.g. a Moderna team).
  • Panel is "90 plus%" confident frontier labs already run self-editing models; risk side is de-guardrailing open-source Chinese models.
  • Likened to the first integrated transistor — profound, but only ~0.01% of a model so far.
  • Persistent rumor: Ilya Sutskever's SSI has been doing prop/quant trading; Alex calls that a likely "fail for civilization".

Hackers defrosted Europe's supermarket freezers

Internet-connected fridge controllers let attackers crank stores to 55°C — proof that legacy industrial systems are the soft target.

  • Attackers switched on defrost mode remotely, up to 55°C, in 8 countries incl. Belgium, Sweden, Ireland, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Estonia; ~€100,000 of spoiled food in Belgium.
  • Second wave: 3 weeks earlier the same attack hit 500+ stores in France; no one claimed it, fingers point at Russia.
  • Week before: one Binance engineer's laptop cost $6B in stolen tokens.
  • Offense is now humans-out-of-the-loop agent swarms while defense stays human-in-the-loop; Peter expects banks, power and the grid next over a tough 1-8 year transition.
  • Alex: AI is patching the Linux kernel at record scale but not legacy SCADA — "many billions of dollars of opportunity".
  • Too small to wake politicians — the warning hack Eric Schmidt has been looking for.

NASA back at Apollo speed, and a positron held for a month

Isaacman's plan puts humans on the Moon in 2028 against China; Northwestern's antimatter trap is a first step toward antimatter propulsion.

  • Artemis 3 (summer 2027): four astronauts practice Orion docking with both Blue Origin's lander and SpaceX's Starship in Earth orbit; three launches in quick sequence.
  • Artemis 4 (2028): first humans on the Moon since 1972; NASA picks the landing provider as late as possible; south-pole Moon base, first launch by year-end.
  • Isaacman calls it a race against China "much closer than it ever needed to be"; US imagery via Maxar shows an advanced Chinese space plane.
  • History: Apollo 7 to Apollo 11 took 9 months; an early-1970s report projected a 100-person Moon base by 1986; the panel blames cold-war budget diversion for the 50-year gap.
  • Northwestern (Gabrielse group): a positron held a month at 0.3 K in a "container without walls"; matters for CPT symmetry and Isaacman's interest in antimatter propulsion.
  • Efficiency of rest mass: antimatter ~100%, fusion ~1-4%, fission ~0.1-0.3%.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

RSI — Recursive self-improvement: AI building better AI.
GDPX — SemiAnalysis benchmark dropping agents into realistic 8-hour expert work across 11 frontier fields.
LifeEval — OpenAI benchmark of 169 everyday tasks done via browser.
Pareto frontier — Set of models where no other is both cheaper and better.
Abliteration — Removing an open model's safety behavior by editing a few weights.
SCADA — Supervisory control and data acquisition — industrial control systems like the hacked fridge controllers.
CPT symmetry — Charge-parity-time symmetry; antimatter measurements test it.
MTP — Massive transformative purpose — a cause to point intelligence at.
