High conviction MERCOR STUDY · 12 ACCOUNTANTS VS FRONTIER MODELSai-infracareer

Claude Opus 5 scored 100% where experienced accountants scored ~37%

A frontier model beat senior accountants on real tasks, and adding the human to the loop made the work slower, not better.

  • Mercor pitted 12 licensed accountants (avg 5.4 years' experience, 10 of 12 senior or managers, half Big Four alumni) against frontier models on real-world accounting tasks.
  • Humans scored ~37%, taking 30-180 minutes per task.
  • Claude Opus 5 scored 100% on all 20 solo attempts, finishing each in under 10 minutes.
  • Accountants allowed to use Claude were still slower and less accurate: those sessions ran about 15x slower than Claude alone.
  • Limit: Mercor, Ramp and other evaluators agreed AI still can't close a company's books by itself.

AI's first visible dent: finance and tech down 246k jobs while everything else adds 812k

Pompliano expects tasks, not roles, to vanish, and calls the fear headlines noise, while conceding the job data already shows AI pressure in finance and tech.

  • Warren Pies: since mid-2025, finance and tech industries are -246k jobs, all other industries +812k; he expects it to intensify next year and spread to other industries.
  • Pompliano's analogy: big companies once had floors of people doing the books; Excel replaced most of those hours, yet finance teams still employ plenty of people.
  • Expect accountants to shift to higher-value work even if AI eventually closes books on its own.
  • Steve Rattner's New York Times op-ed: since 1830, technology cut the US workweek from 69 to 38 hours and raised real income per person roughly 20-fold — "properly managed, A.I. can do the same."
  • His bottom line: no human should spend their time on menial tasks software can automate.

Speculative WILL MANIDIS POSTsemisai-infra

Every equity is mispriced, but the market reprices one sector at a time

Manidis' framing: if even a fraction of the AI story is real, mispricing is universal, and the market can only process it sequentially — memory first, then semis, then something next.

  • Manidis: "if you even believe 1/10th of this is real" then every single equity is drastically mispriced one way or another.
  • The market processes it one trade at a time: memories, then semis, then a blank — the next sector to reprice is the open question.
  • Pompliano's close: "the acceleration is just beginning" — hold on long enough to enjoy the ride.
