A weak NFP gave the Fed a pause, but the rally is narrow

Cooling without recession is the Goldilocks setup, yet only the index, not the market, confirmed it.

  • NFP: 29,000 jobs vs ~84-90k consensus and 133k in August; July/August revisions cut another ~60k. Unemployment 4.2%, wages +0.1% m/m and 3.0% y/y, participation 61.8%, claims 197k.
  • Closes: S&P 500 7,722.72 (+0.73%), Nasdaq Composite 27,190.86 (+1.19%), Dow 51,176.96 (+0.49%), Russell 2000 2,832.90 (+0.9%); VIX ~15.3 (-6%).
  • Chain he lays out: weaker NFP, less wage pressure, lower hike odds, lower short yields, lower discount rate, so growth and AI outperform. Hike probability ~17-20% from >60% a week before.
  • Limit: payrolls at zero or negative, unemployment 4.4-4.5% and a weakening consumer would end the 'bad news is good news' regime. He describes the labor market as 'low hire, low fire'.
  • Breadth: NYSE advancers/decliners ~1.6:1, Nasdaq ~1.4:1, NYSE volume ~7% above one-month average. But only ~6% of Nasdaq-100 hit highs and Nasdaq had 290 lows vs 136 highs.
  • For a healthy Q4 he wants three things together: Nasdaq at new highs, expanding new highs, and a rising share of stocks above key moving averages.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

NFP (Nonfarm Payrolls) — Monthly BLS count of US jobs created or lost, excluding mainly farm work.
Low hire, low fire — A labor market where firms neither lay off en masse nor hire at a healthy pace.
Discount rate — The rate used to value future earnings; lower rates lift growth-stock valuations.
Term premium — Extra yield investors demand for holding long-dated bonds.
Vendor financing — A supplier funds its customer so the customer can buy the supplier's products.
