Long speed, short friction: why agents threaten Visa, Mastercard and SaaS

Jordi argues agents pick on price and speed, so anything that acts as a middleman or a slow incumbent is exposed while compute and crypto rails benefit.

  • Visa and Mastercard are 'by definition middlemen': they won't go out of business, but 'in five years, will people still be paying a vig? Agents won't.' Agent behavior is about goals — find things fast and cheap.
  • Deflation, not inflation: AI's deflationary pressure is 'intense'; it's a question of how long it takes to filter through, and travel-booking agents strip out middlemen first.
  • Scoreboard: Salesforce down ~10% YTD vs the S&P +12% (22 points behind), his thematic semiconductor portfolio +45%, Mag 7 up ~10% (underperforming); Salesforce is down ~15% over 5 years, and Jordi says the software 'SaaS apocalypse' drops (30-50%) have not fully recovered.
  • Speed evidence: Anthropic went from $3B to about $80B of revenue in the time the Clarity Act went from idea to rejection; Jordi asks why the best models aren't at companies with the biggest balance sheets, and why senior people are leaving Google.
  • Consumer-agent ads: agents decide with 'no preferences' (price, reviews), so ad-driven models get squeezed; agents demand compute and use crypto rails — those are the trades; next 12 months' money is on compute and agent guardrails.
  • Portfolio call: a Mag 7 'buzz trade' won't outperform the S&P but should beat small caps as rates and oil scare people.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Vig — The fee a middleman takes on each transaction, such as card interchange.
RWA — Real-world assets, such as funds and property, represented as tokens on a blockchain.
Tokenization — Putting ownership of a traditional asset on a blockchain so it can move and settle like a token.
Breadth — How many stocks participate in a rally; weak breadth means gains are concentrated in a few names.
Debasement trade — Buying hard assets like gold or Bitcoin on expectation that money supply growth erodes currency value.
