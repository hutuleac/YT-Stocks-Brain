Framework THE FILTER FOR EVERY UPGRADE IN THE VIDEOmindsetfinance

Your buyback rate: outsource anything priced under half your hourly income

Before any lifestyle upgrade, ask whether it buys back your life or only makes you look richer — then price it against your buyback rate.

  • Formula: net income ÷ ~2,000 working hours a year = hourly income; halve it for a 50% margin. Any task you can hand off below that number is a yes.
  • Worked example: $100K income → ~$50/hour → $25 buyback rate, so a $10/hour helper is an easy call.
  • Her own extreme version: nail tech, hair and makeup artist, colorist, stylist and chef all come to her office, so she never goes to a salon.
  • Her rationale: running a nine-figure company while pregnant, married, with friends, family and travel only works if she buys time back from "all the nonsense".
  • Dismisses the FIRE movement for convincing people a good life means minimalism and saving every penny; "the world's hugely abundant."

Recommendation RULE OF ONE · RULE OF THREEmindsetconsumer

Buy one expensive signal item, then upgrade appearance in small swaps

Skip a fancy wardrobe; one premium item worn consistently signals wealth to buyers, investors and bosses.

  • Rule of one: a single nice item — she wears one Schiaparelli bag with every outfit; for men, one nice watch ("It could be fake. Nobody needs to know").
  • Rule of three: throw out three ill-fitting, worn items now and replace them; she runs a weekly one-in, one-out habit.
  • Cites research that people rated attractive earn 20% more on average — "don't hate the player, don't hate the game."
  • High-end treatments she pays for: PRP microneedling (~$5K with peptides), Halo laser ($3-5K, "look 5 years younger"), Sculptra (a couple thousand), facials (a couple hundred).
  • Cheap wins: red-light devices (one on her desk, one to lie under) and organic skincare/makeup such as OSEA.
  • Men can do the same treatments as an underrated edge — but no shine, no fillers, "don't look weird."

Principle NETWORK AS A MEASURABLE INCOME INPUTcareerrelationships

Proximity and repeatable skills, not pedigree, are what compound

She credits making $1M before 30 to one word — proximity — and picks skills by how often they will repeat.

  • At Goldman Sachs (7:30am start) she lived a 2-minute walk away; a smarter, better-pedigreed colleague lived 45 minutes out and missed every early and late moment.
  • Pay for rooms: a better gym, a co-working space over the kitchen table, and 3-4 masterminds joined before she made her money — "stealing their 10,000 hours."
  • Her friend Sahil Bloom became close to a former Apple chief through exactly this kind of shared space.
  • A Harvard economist mapping billions of Facebook friendships found the strongest predictor of climbing an economic bracket was knowing richer people — not school, neighborhood or income.
  • Repeat rule: value a skill by how often you'll reuse it — sales ~1,000 times, negotiation 100+. Tattoo rule: would you rather have it burned into your brain, or the tuition back?
  • Her top skills: negotiation (buying businesses), sales ("CEO is chief sales officer"), and operations/profitability.

Framework CADO · THREE RUNGScareermindset

Climb the assistant ladder with CADO: cut, automate, delegate, outsource

"It's faster if I just do it" is why most people stay broke — you're faster once, not the 51st time.

  • Unpopular rule: she won't invest in a founder who doesn't already have an assistant — advice she got from a billionaire.
  • Cites Harvard Business research: execs with a skilled assistant show 20-30% higher productivity and earn more.
  • CADO (adapted from Tim Ferriss): cut tasks (does the email list need organizing at all?), automate (she uses Superhuman for inbox management), delegate to a VA, then fully outsource to an EA who doesn't report back.
  • Rung 1, virtual assistant: part-time, overseas, $200-$3,000/month ($3K = full-time) for inbox, scheduling, repetitive work — clears almost everyone's buyback rate.
  • Rung 2, assistant: in-house or trusted remote, ~$50K up to $250K/year, filters daily-life tasks. Rung 3, chief of staff: ~$125K (up to $300K), runs projects end-to-end and is trained to be her duplicate.
  • Climb from the lowest-dollar tasks to the highest; she claims to be "100 times faster" than most employees and still delegates.

Counterintuitive take PAY FOR STRUCTURE AND EXITS, NOT ADVICEfinance

Hire a tax advisor instead of a financial advisor, and fund your exits up front

Past a couple hundred thousand a year you're probably overpaying tax; and the cost nobody budgets for is getting out.

  • Financial advisors take a percentage of every dollar they hold — she'd rather dollar-cost average into a cheap Vanguard or Fidelity structure and let it compound.
  • A tax advisor works on earned income — e.g. routing earnings through an LLC to legally cut what goes to the IRS; "tax is just the way most people stay rich."
  • Exit fund: a separate account used only to buy your way out — breaking a lease, eating a cancellation fee, paying a kill fee to drop a draining client or vendor.
  • Before signing a lease or 12-month vendor contract, price what walking away costs in month six and set that aside; add every recurring commitment's kill fee to the fund.
  • Amazon pays warehouse staff to quit; she reads it as a big company paying "five figures" to buy a clean exit, citing Jeff Bezos on employees who stay where they don't want to be.
  • Action: list 3-5 expenses to kill today; name the account something honest ("exit fund" or "you fund").

Recommendation BRAIN INPUTS PRICED THROUGH THE BUYBACK RATEhealth

Real food and real sleep are cheap through the buyback-rate lens

You can't negotiate a deal or show up for your kids on gas-station calories and short sleep.

  • Studies in young and older adults: every meaningful rise in ultra-processed food tracks with worse memory and concentration.
  • People spend ~7 hours a week cooking; meal services like Factor or Tempo run $200-300/week for 21 meals — run your buyback math. Her household chef costs ~$60/hour and cooks 3 days of food in ~3 hours.
  • Two fast-food meals a day for a household adds up faster than a grocery run. Action: clear the desk-drawer snacks and restock with food that spoils if not eaten today.
  • An economist links more sleep to higher earnings — "tired people make expensive decisions"; chronic short sleep costs the US economy ~$411 billion.
  • Her list: a hard mattress replaced every 5-10 years, an Eight Sleep if you can spare a couple thousand, otherwise earplugs and an eye mask — no Oura Ring, Whoop or sleep tracking needed.
  • Buyback math: ~$5K Eight Sleep plus ~$100 of extras over 2,500 hours a year ≈ a $2/hour buyback rate.
