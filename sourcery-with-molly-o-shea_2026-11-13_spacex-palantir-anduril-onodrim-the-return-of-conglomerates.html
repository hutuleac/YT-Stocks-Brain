SpaceX turns into a vertically integrated hyperscaler

Garrett calls SpaceX the most important company in the world, now monetizing AI from the app layer down to compute.

  • Q3: top line up about 160%, $4.4B net income, roughly $8.8-10B EBITDA; over 50% of revenue is attributed to AI.
  • AI revenue from Cursor and Grokbots; it builds compute capacity fastest and either uses it or sells it, with sub-12-month payback periods.
  • Vertical integration hedges the model mix: it wins whether open source or closed models win, and can shift capacity to its own products if demand slows.
  • Starship is working; a handful of Starlink V3s added about 1% of the world's capacity, and tens of thousands are coming.
  • Orbital compute satellites are slated for the back half of next year; Google tested a version in orbit, and StarCloud 3 will fly on Starship.
  • It compounded about 40% for 20 years; duration is what matters (9% over 30 years is 13x). It trades at 20-30x 2027 EBITDA as a $2T company.
  • Cheaper launch built companies like Varda (pharma, defense); Starship should cut costs by an order of magnitude and spawn as many new firms.

AI build-out financing and the cost-of-capital edge

Markets now doubt both who funds the spend and who pays for it, and Garrett says pricing anything means believing in out-year earnings nobody can see.

  • Volatility in public and debt markets: macro, an election cycle and October de-grossing all play a role, and both spenders and beneficiaries of the spend dropped.
  • Speed, payback period and cost of capital decide the build-out, since debt is the most efficient funding; CoreWeave-type names felt it first.
  • Elon's tweet on financing constraints is 100% right: low-cost-of-capital players will absorb supply at better rates.
  • Unknowns: model pricing, open versus closed mix, compute pricing, power access. It's hard to believe the next two years of earnings, let alone 20.
  • Bets at the harness and app layers: Cognition, plus a co-led round in Hex for data workflows; he expects open source to take a large share of models.
  • OpenAI and Anthropic are two of the most impressive businesses built in a generation; family members using chatbots signal early diffusion.

Paying up for winners: the growth-stage power law

Too much capital chases too few growth companies, but the power law still pays for it.

  • Molly: Cognition was at $48B and is rumored at $60-70B, Ramp at $60B, Anduril at $90B; Shardul (Index) will pay a 25% premium but not 2x.
  • Teddy Forstmann, Garrett's godfather: own a quality 5x asset at a 25% premium over a bad one at a 25% discount.
  • Ramp keeps consolidating the back office and is still underpriced and under-monetized on several products.
  • The COVID fintech flood left most companies dead, but Stripe, Revolut and Nubank became $100B-plus outcomes; he expects the same in AI and defense.
  • Growth portfolios behave like venture: a handful of companies drive all the returns.

The return of the dual-use conglomerate

New conglomerates share one tech platform, unlike the disparate 1980s versions, and defense needs commercial scale to stay affordable.

  • Old conglomerates offered diversification and cost-of-capital scale, but had no shared platform, made sum-of-the-parts hard, and suffered diseconomies.
  • New ones share a core operating platform and vertical integration (Apple, SpaceX); Anduril's Lattice means every product sold raises the value of the others.
  • Palantir and SpaceX get the majority of revenue from the commercial sector; China's defense primes get about 68% from commercial; General Mills once made cereal and weapon systems.
  • Industrial policy can't fund defense capacity alone; you must dominate commercially, as China's shipbuilding prime does. He expects Anduril to be by far the power outcome among neo-primes.
  • Anduril bringing computer manufacturing home is part of repatriating capacity, which Palmer Luckey pushed for years.

Onodrim: rolling up Europe's defense supplier layer

Garrett co-founded a platform to buy, modernize and scale the fragmented European suppliers below the primes.

  • US models like TransDigm, Teledyne, HEICO and Honeywell consolidated suppliers; Europe never had a HEICO, despite a mature Airbus supply chain.
  • Europe has 4,000 drone companies (overfunded) while engines, components and structures, the layer below, are the production bottleneck.
  • It acquires and holds long term, funds capex and new products, deploys technology into manufacturing, and targets dual-use firms. First acquisitions and prime partnerships are coming soon.
  • The team spans private equity, defense, government, Palantir and SpaceX, with nine nationalities; he is a minority co-founder.
  • Like Armada (incubated from a SpaceX idea), it started from a problem he was close to. Also bullish on Hadrian, AI integrating into PLCs, Applied Intuition and General Matter (uranium enrichment).
