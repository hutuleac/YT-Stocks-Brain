Robotaxis are safer than human drivers; the open question is how fast they scale

ARK says robotaxis already beat the national human accident rate, so adoption now depends on scale and price, not safety.

  • Waymo completes over 500,000 driverless rides per week and intends to scale to 1 million; it is in 14 cities after adding about nine this year.
  • Based on Waymo's disclosures, ARK says its police-reported crash rate is below the US average, and robotaxis are far safer on injury and worst-crash measures.
  • Tesla operates robotaxi in seven regions (about five added this year); Daniel tested it in Tampa last week and called it seamless.
  • Tesla is rolling the Cybercab, a purpose-built robotaxi with no steering wheel, pedals or wing mirrors, off the production line; officials have said cars without pedals or wheels will be allowed.
  • Tesla's customer cars act as mini R&D centers whose cameras collect accident and road data worldwide, which ARK calls an unparalleled data advantage.
  • Ride-hail costs about $2+/mile in Western markets versus ~$0.50/mile or less in China, so Chinese players (Baidu, WeRide, Pony AI) are moving to the Middle East and Europe where pricing is higher; ARK is focused on the US and Western markets.
  • Zoox also received recent regulatory approvals.

Confirmed event LIVE TODAY — ZIPLINE, WALMART, DOORDASHroboticsconsumer

Drone delivery is operating at scale and big platforms are verticalizing

Autonomous delivery is already running, and ARK expects drone delivery to push the delivery fee toward about a dollar or zero.

  • Zipline passed 2.5 million cumulative autonomous deliveries and is scaling its smaller Platform 2 drone for residential delivery (Platform 1 served medical deliveries in Africa).
  • Walmart passed 1 million autonomous drone deliveries, mostly Zipline plus Alphabet's Wing; it targets 270 locations covering over 40 million Americans by 2027.
  • DoorDash announced an in-house drone program last week and received FAA Part 135 certification, which allows airline-like drone operations.
  • Manna is scaling in the US after starting in Ireland; Meituan is scaling in China and the Middle East; Amazon sits at the bottom of the chart despite announcing drones decades ago.
  • The average US food delivery order is about $30, roughly half of it fees and markups; ARK thinks drones cut delivery cost to about $1 or less, likely folded into the item price like free shipping.

Early-stage $20T+ OPPORTUNITY, DEPLOYMENTS THE REAL TESTrobotics

Humanoids: huge market, but far harder and earlier than autonomous driving

ARK sees a $20 trillion-plus humanoid market, yet the gap between robots produced and robots deployed with customers is the metric that matters.

  • Cumulative production is roughly 40,000 quadrupeds and 20,000 humanoids, with many more unlisted makers; China has hundreds of humanoid manufacturers.
  • ARK sizes humanoids at $20T+, split between home robots and manufacturing.
  • Past ARK research estimates humanoids are 200,000x more complex than fully autonomous driving.
  • Data is harder to get: public roads allow driving data collection, but factory data requires being inside the factory long before the robot is useful.
  • Today's robots mostly dance, greet people in lobbies or chat like an LLM rather than do complex physical tasks; the supply chain still has to be built, and Tesla has said it is an entirely new product that will scale slowly.
  • Figure recently published a couple of demo videos; ARK wants to see customer deployments instead of production-count announcements.

SpaceX IPO, reusability dominance and the orbital AI data center thesis

ARK says SpaceX has a 10-year lead, and the Starship-enabled orbital AI buildout is what underpins its IPO story.

  • SpaceX IPO'd at just under $2 trillion in value, raising roughly $85B.
  • SpaceX landed an orbital-class booster in 2015; Blue Origin matched it in 2025 and China has since joined the group.
  • ARK estimates SpaceX cut launch costs about 95% since 2008; it holds roughly two-thirds of all active satellites.
  • Starlink is close to 11,000 active satellites and over 12 million subscribers, growing weekly.
  • The S-1 cites a $28.5T total addressable market, over 90% AI related, built on orbital data centers (AI satellites rebranded Star Mind).
  • Up-mass to orbit passed 3,000 metric tons in 2025; SpaceX projects launching about 1,000x that in 2032 (nearly 2.5 million tons) to support a million satellites.
  • SpaceX discontinued the Falcon 9 rideshare program to focus on Starship, opening the small/medium launch category to others.
  • Some satellite providers struggle to book launch for 2028-2029+ after the Blue Origin explosion in May; Rocket Lab announced it intends to acquire Iridium, showing the value of owning both launch and payload.
