Meditation as twice-daily vagus nerve stimulation

Tim thinks sitting still and breathing rhythmically twice a day may work by stimulating the vagus nerve, and that a simple concentration cue beats open monitoring for an obsessive mind.

  • Retreat at Mountain Cloud Zen Center, Santa Fe, with Zen masters Henry Shukman (The Way app) and Valerie; sits capped at ~25 minutes, questions allowed between sits.
  • Labeling thoughts "radio" or "video" turned stressful for Tim's OCD; Henry's swap to "just be still" for two sits calmed everything. He now favors 10-20 minutes twice a day with that cue.
  • The payoff came later: 3-5 days of calm, unrushed attention back in Austin, after feeling frustrated during the retreat itself.
  • Kevin Tracey's work: implants on the vagus nerve (two cables of ~100,000 fibers each) treat rheumatoid arthritis, FDA-approved, via the inflammatory reflex that damps cytokine storms.
  • A stimulation lasts roughly 12 hours, so twice a day is full coverage; a professor friend on the 10% Happier app lost debilitating aches within 1-2 weeks of twice-daily meditation.
  • Tim's bet: specific breathing patterns repeated morning and night for 10-20 minutes will show benefits for chronic pain.
  • Michael Levin (Tufts) recommended for bioelectric medicine, which Tim calls "the next frontier" for replacing pills with electricity.

Mixed results DEVICES TRIED — LITTLE PERSONAL BENEFIThealth

Vagus devices underwhelm; a $250 HRV breathing coach wins

Both hosts got little from stimulation hardware, while biofeedback breathing produced effects they could see in real time.

  • Tim used a neck stimulator (mostly for migraines and cluster headaches) for 4-6 weeks with no systemic benefit; a friend doubled his HRV on one.
  • Ear stimulation targets the cymba concha, toward the sideburn; contact point is critical. Tim stimulates 5 minutes before bed on a Scandinavian prototype.
  • Kevin's NeuroPod ($900): 2 weeks at 30 minutes a day, noticed nothing; Tim thinks the earpiece misses the right spot.
  • A DIY TENS rig costs $20-25 in Amazon parts, but Tim won't publish instructions: DIY tDCS users reversing polarity can fry their brains.
  • Kevin's pick is HeartMath ($250, 60-day money-back): box breathing drives HRV up live, and thinking of something stressful drops it.
  • Tim did ~3 months of HRV breathing training with Dr. Leah Lagos; also points to Andrew Weil's exercises and Kevin's free Oak app (box breathing, 4-7-8).
  • Kevin Tracey's book The Great Nerve has a chapter on Wim Hof: breathwork can also rein in excess immune response.

Recommendation KEVIN'S FOCUS FOR THE NEXT DECADEhealth

Balance boards, slacklines and a 10-minute tendon protocol

Small daily doses train the nervous system and tendons better than marathon sessions.

  • Falls and broken hips are the leading cause of permanent injury in people's 60s and 70s, with a sharp rise in all-cause mortality after a hip fracture.
  • Kevin does 5 minutes a day and 50 squats on a balance board; he cites research where it markedly improved ADHD symptoms.
  • Jerzy Gregorek at 67 did full Olympic snatches on a balance board with 150-200 lbs; Tony Hawk is vert snowboarding in Hokkaido in his mid-50s after hip screws.
  • Tim on slacklining: a few minutes daily beats an hour, because the nervous system needs sleep cycles to integrate. Climbing gyms often have lines; the brand Gibbon is named for the arm motion.
  • Emil Abrahamsson and scientist Keith Baar's tendon protocol: hang with 30-85% of body weight, 10 s on / 50 s off, 10 rounds, twice a day.
  • Tim travels with the Nug, a pocket wooden grip (25 mm / 20 mm edges) from Frictitious Climbing, recommended by ex-Navy SEAL Nick Norris; one-handed it's ~15-40% load.
  • Kevin's kids (7 and 8) crashed on hoverboards on day one and were flying by day two: adaptation takes a couple of days.
Pull quote
“Do not overdo finger training. You do not want to tear a pulley or something in your fingers. So, less is more”— Tim Ferriss
Personal story CAUTIOUSLY OPTIMISTIC — ~5 DAYS INhealth

Six years of back pain, possibly explained by Bertolotti's syndrome

A specialist who has seen hundreds of cases matched Tim's imaging to the exact spot he always pointed at, and a diagnostic nerve block made the pain disappear.

  • Bertolotti's syndrome: a transitional L5 segment forms a painful pseudo joint; most specialists see one or two cases in a career.
  • Test before treatment: a nerve block (lidocaine, prolocaine, Kenalog cortisone), then do everything that usually hurts. 3 days, zero pain.
  • Earlier specialists dismissed his pinpointing as referred pain; Tim calls it the first relief in 6 years.
  • Next step if it holds: radiofrequency ablation to silence those nerves for 1-1.5 years, enough time to reprogram the brain's pain patterning.
  • John Sarno's approach helps many people, but Tim finds "it's all in your head" infuriating as a blanket rule.
  • Separately: surgery on his forearm extensors (tennis elbow), 20-plus years overdue, has him back to rock climbing.

Treating Alzheimer's as a mitochondrial and vascular problem

With three relatives affected, Tim is looking past amyloid plaque toward mitochondria, blood flow and ketones.

  • Tim sees amyloid beta as a byproduct, not the cause; plaque removal drugs like donanemab can clear plaque with no cognitive change, and carry MRI-visible risks.
  • A 10-15 g ketone dose gave a relative of one- or two-word answers longer sentences within 20 minutes, lasting an hour to an hour and a half.
  • Dr. Francisco Gonzalez-Lima (UT Austin, via STEM-Talk): low-dose methylene blue plus photobiomodulation on the forehead, hitting two parts of the electron transport chain.
  • Infrared laser sessions of 8-10 minutes show weeks of effect after one session; clinic lasers can cost $30,000 and are not available as a treatment yet.
  • Methylene blue is now sold on Amazon, even as gummies; 120 years of use, but overshooting the therapeutic window can harm mitochondria.
  • MIT's Cognito headset (40 Hz light and sound) cleared plaque in rhesus monkeys, but needs an hour a day, which Tim's mother won't do.
  • Tim's mother is APOE 3/3 and declining while his 3/4 father is sharp; since mitochondria come from the mother, he's weighing levers for her and for himself.
  • Kevin backs Dale Bredesen's protocol (The End of Alzheimer's): supplements, light ketosis 5 days a week, no sugar, intense exercise. A retreat friend's father scores better 10 years on.
Pull quote
“just because it's a supplement doesn't make it safe folks”— Tim Ferriss
Worth noting

What stays in the stack: vitamin D, omega-3, creatine, urolithin A

Both keep a short core stack, and urolithin A is the newest addition, cheap to justify on mechanism but expensive to dose.

  • Ketone esters like Delta G that bind BHB to 1,3-butanediol gave mice something like fatty liver disease in early animal data; Tim says treat it like moonshine and caps himself at 15 g, not 30 g, which spikes his anxiety.
  • Urolithin A: Rhonda Patrick was bearish ~2 years ago, but new research and a Boston biotech review put it on Tim's list; he takes 500 mg, most studies use 1,000 mg.
  • Timeline's MitoPure runs $125 for 60 capsules; Pure Encapsulations ($80) buys MitoPure but needs eight capsules a day for 1,000 mg. It's a postbiotic from pomegranates and walnuts, with variable gut conversion.
  • Kevin's longest-running: vitamin D (chronically low), CocoaVia for vascular health, a quality omega-3.
  • Tim: omega-3 mostly from canned sardines and mackerel, B vitamins (poor methylator), vitamin D despite doubting a planet-wide deficiency, and prescription lipid drugs including Repatha.
  • Creatine dose by day: 5 g baseline, 10 g when lifting, 20-30 g on heavy travel to offset sleep deprivation.
  • Never stack double espresso, 10 g creatine and MCT oil before a flight, per Tim's airport-parking disaster story.
  • Kevin's injection tip: let the alcohol swab dry fully and bring the pen to room temperature, or it hurts and takes five times longer.

Framework TIM EXPERIMENTING WHILE TRAVELINGhealth

Lactate as a cognitive lever: 4x4 intervals, high-rep sets, BFR cuffs

If lactate drives the brain benefits of hard intervals, Tim can chase it in a hotel gym with high reps or blood flow restriction.

  • Dr. Tommy Wood's Norwegian 4x4: 4 minutes near max heart rate, 3 off, four rounds. Three times a week for 6 months reportedly shows benefits for 5 years.
  • Tommy told Tim lactate, not VO2 max, seems a big driver of the neuroanatomical and vascular changes; Kevin adds Klotho, which Tim says is one piece.
  • Hotel bikes wreck Tim's knees, so he does 20-rep slow squats or very high-rep leg presses: about 5 minutes of lactate.
  • It's heart-rate based, so the unfit can do it; Tim's legs give out before his heart rate gets there, which he ties to his mitochondria. He uses a Morpheus chest strap; NormaTec boots for recovery.
  • Joint-friendly lifting: slow tempo, 5-10 seconds up and down, forcing lighter weights and no momentum.
  • Kaatsu C4 cuffs ($1,259; cheaper brands exist): hammer curls drop from 40 lbs to ~20 lbs, and Tim's push-ups fell from 25 to 5 per set. He picked the C4 partly to avoid yet another app ("along the Bill Burr lines").
  • Dominic D'Agostino did a 500-lb deadlift for 10 reps after a 7-day fast.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Inflammatory reflex — Vagus nerve pathway that, when stimulated, suppresses excess cytokine release and systemic inflammation.
Cymba concha — Hollow of the upper outer ear, the main target for non-invasive vagus stimulation.
HRV coherence — Smooth heart-rate-variability rhythm produced by paced breathing, tracked by HeartMath devices.
Bertolotti's syndrome — Low-back pain from a transitional lumbosacral vertebra that forms a pseudo joint.
Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) — Heat from radio waves that temporarily disables pain-carrying nerves.
Photobiomodulation — Red or infrared light (laser or LED) used to boost mitochondrial function.
Urolithin A — Postbiotic gut metabolite of pomegranate and walnut compounds, studied for mitochondrial renewal.
APOE — Gene whose e4 variant raises Alzheimer's risk; people carry two copies, e.g. 3/3 or 3/4.
Norwegian 4x4 — Four rounds of 4 minutes near max heart rate with 3 minutes of recovery.
Blood flow restriction (BFR) — Cuffs partially occluding limb blood flow so light weights produce heavy-training effects.
