Cerebras: ten years early, now under-forecasting its own market

Feldman says the inference wave came bigger than any Cerebras forecast — the first time in five startups he guessed a market too small.

  • $25B backlog; manufacturing up 8x this year and 8x next; entered 2026 with several hundred MW under management, exits with contracts for gigawatts.
  • The wafer-scale chip: 46,000 mm², 4 trillion transistors, roughly $1 million each; pitched as 10-50x faster than a GPU.
  • Solved "a 75-year-old problem" in 2020 and nobody cared — fast AI was pointless while AI was dumb; premise was "probably seven years too early."
  • Chip share only moves on new workloads: Ethernet switching killed Nortel and 3Com in the late 90s; AMD and Intel got zero share of phones in the mid-2000s.
  • Early believers, per Feldman: Atiq Raza, Andy Bechtolsheim and maybe Lip-Bu Tan — "everybody said you're out of your mind."
  • May IPO was the largest semiconductor IPO ever (Dave: biggest IPO of any kind until SpaceX); 1,000+ staff; "the IPO solves nothing" technically.
  • Scale check: $50-60B Cerebras vs ~$5 trillion Nvidia; Feldman's lesson is Nvidia's grit trading near cash for a decade (2003-2013), not CUDA.

The bottleneck: data centers today, fabs next — and weights that shouldn't move

Asked to remove one constraint, Feldman picks data-center availability now and fab capacity in three years; Blundin says the real waste is inside the chip.

  • Feldman: software moves at software speed, data centers move like real estate — that is holding the whole industry back.
  • Raza splits it: energy and building data centers "we can't fix"; what goes inside them the people on stage can.
  • Blundin: burning energy on HBM, "the most expensive chips in the world," to move weights into a GPU for a picosecond and dump them is idiotic; weight-stationary designs are 1,000x-1,000,000x more efficient.
  • Why they don't exist yet: the supply chain is slow and nobody wants to hardwire an algorithm that software then obsoletes.
  • Raza: inference is going consumer → enterprise → multi-stage with fan-outs as context windows swell; each step must carry prior context, so it gets far more memory-intensive. In-memory compute is moving from edge to data center, but it also needs workload scheduling.
  • Whole supply chain "stretched past breaking": memory, CPUs, GPUs, accelerators, PCBs, substrates, power regulators.
  • Backup gas generators are short; Caterpillar and GE Vernova gear saw "no innovation in 30 years" — now diesel gensets, turbines, fuel cells and power switching are all innovating.

Architecture after the GPU: cores, memory, IO — and light

Feldman reduces all computer architecture to three levers, and the panel bets photonics and hardware no one has built yet pull them next.

  • Framework: every chip is faster cores, better memory/bandwidth, or better IO; Cerebras's bet was memory placed closer to compute to widen the aperture.
  • Blundin, from scratch: all matmuls photonic, transistor math in between; Raza: connectivity definitely goes photonic, compute and memory are an open question.
  • Feldman: transformer-based for at least several years — a dominant design with five or six companies pouring money in; attention is "a big matrix multiply"; proof is shipping volume, not conference slides.
  • Daniela Rus's Liquid AI changes attention granularity and has shown inference 3-4x faster; Kimi K3 mixes one attention layer with three KDA state-space layers, reconciling the two paths.
  • New Moore's law: transistor doubling slid from 18 months to 3-4 years, but Feldman says models are getting more than 2x smarter every 18 months — an exponential, not a line.
  • Blundin: fabs cap chip output for 3-4 years; within four years AI invents compute in crystals and liquids, largely in space on solar — "not an Nvidia rack floating around in space."
  • Raza's counter: Moore's law carried its death from day one (atomic limits) and token-efficiency gains will slow the same way.

The embargo made China better at efficiency — and the US is behind on robots

Starving China of Nvidia chips forced quantization and hybrid-model innovation the US is now scrambling to backport.

  • Transformers were born in PyTorch on CUDA in FP32; China shrank weights and activations to tiny data types and "it's worked shockingly well."
  • Blundin: nobody optimized data types since his MIT days in the 1990s; embargo started under Biden, accelerated under Trump — "no party component."
  • Raza's anecdote: a Stanford-PhD Chinese VC said the GPU cutoff was "the best thing that has happened to me" — now local innovation is investable.
  • Feldman: open models (Kimi) sit a steady 4-8 months behind OpenAI and Anthropic and he sees no reason it changes in the next several years; China leans on distillation from frontier models.
  • Frontier edge compounds: OpenAI gave its fast Cerebras inference entirely to its own engineers for the first six months; Dell's fast-follower, tiny-R&D model can't do that.
  • Raza: being even slightly behind multiplies in agentic workloads, so you spend more on compute even if the model is free.
  • Robots: "we're getting clobbered" — China is embarrassingly ahead, and Feldman knows of zero credible US actuator makers.

Once models are smart enough, speed is the product

"Dumb fast" helps no one, but with open, closed and small models all smart, the bottleneck becomes waiting for the answer.

  • Feldman's test: punish a naughty 12-year-old by setting their phone to dial-up — slowness is punishment.
  • Netflix went from mailing DVDs to being a studio only because the internet got fast; a four-hour download never gets you there.
  • Coding agents and personal agents (Instinct, Muse) die if slow — nobody waits eight minutes for an agent to order a burrito.
  • Law and accounting billing every six minutes would be less offensive with 30x the work done per block; Workday-to-ADP and Salesforce-to-accounting shuffling "all goes away."
  • Blundin: smaller Chinese models like GLM hit near-frontier benchmarks at 4x+ fewer parameters; fit one on a single Cerebras wafer or ten and throughput rises 10-100x.
  • Domain-shrunk models drop knowledge like Taylor Swift's Eras Tour you don't need to design a chip or rocket; Raza adds that edge devices and robots need exactly that.
  • Blundin: vision transformers (16x16 pixel patches treated as words) let a coding agent look at its own GUI and fix it — impossible six months ago; "that unlock is pure speed."

Bubble or foundation: overheating yes, bubble no

The panel expects misallocated capital and a panic, but calls AI the foundation of a new economy, like the internet was.

  • Feldman: take a 10-15-year view of Pets.com and online pet food is a great business — Chewy, and a big share of what Amazon sells; expect cul-de-sacs to back out of.
  • Blundin: Google was born in the middle of the dot-com collapse; the internet "was never a bubble," people just panicked — "America always overheats."
  • Feldman: someone born in 20 years may never know anyone who dies of cancer or is hit by a drunk driver (car accidents: top killer of healthy adults).
  • Moderator's cautionary tale: DEC — Georges Doriot put in $70,000, it went head-to-head with IBM, then Ken Olsen couldn't imagine people wanting small computers; Olsen came out of MIT's Project Whirlwind.
  • Raza: tools now attacking Navier-Stokes have Terence Tao worried about his own role — warrants a pause on socioeconomic impact.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

HBM — High-bandwidth memory stacked next to GPUs; the panel's example of costly weight-shuttling.
Wafer-scale chip — Cerebras's single chip built from a whole silicon wafer, keeping memory next to compute.
Quantization — Shrinking weights/activations from FP32 to smaller data types to cut memory and boost speed.
KDA / state-space layers — Non-attention sequence layers mixed with attention layers in hybrid models such as Kimi K3.
Vision transformer — Splits an image into 16x16-pixel patches treated as words so an LLM can read images.
In-memory compute — Doing math inside memory arrays to cut data movement, once edge-only, now pitched for data centers.
Distillation — Training a model on a stronger model's outputs — how followers cheaply learn from the frontier.
