Why this is the 'last normal year': AGI arrives field by field

Bhakdi says AI already exceeds humans in some skills and will exceed them on more than half of human work by the end of next year, so the near-term risks are political and cyber, not sci-fi.

  • Radar-chart framing: AGI fills the whole circle of human skill and ASI exceeds it, but today AI is far beyond humans in math (called 'cooked') and coding, and still weak in writing.
  • He wrote in early 2025 that there would be three years of a 'singularity continuum' from January 2025 to December 2027, and says we are well past halfway; his errors have been on the slow side.
  • A host notes that AGI-2040 talk, even from leaders like Demis Hassabis two years ago, is now off the table, and that ChatGPT launched four years ago with the next 12-24 months expected to be 'completely insane'.
  • He puts a loss-of-control ASI scenario deep in the 2030s and says it distracts from near-term issues: political disruption, the shift from labor to capital, and cybersecurity.
  • Cyber warning: hacks and exposures of key systems (power plants, groceries) arrive daily, so personal security should be locked down.

Cybercab unit economics, and a trillion-dollar Megapack business hiding in plain sight

The hosts argue Cybercab miles and Megapack profits are underpriced, and that the cars could double as distributed inference compute.

  • Per a host, Tesla's robotaxi does about 80,000 paid miles a day at roughly $2.50 a mile, growing 10% a week, which would reach a million miles by year end; the cars are in Austin first.
  • The speakers say Tesla has 3.3M miles with zero accidents and, from something read at 5 a.m., may now exceed Waymo's paid miles in Austin.
  • TAMs: Cybercab around $10T; optimistic around $30T. Gross profit per Cybercab: $15-30K a year, manufacturing cost $15,000, life of about a million miles.
  • Equivalence debate: one gigawatt of compute is worth roughly 300-500K Cybercabs (Bhakdi) versus a million (host); with about $11B vs $40B yearly profit per gigawatt, depending on depreciation.
  • Megapack: about 130-140 (GWh) of capacity, around $33B revenue at 33% margin is ~$11B profit; at 100x PE that is $1.1T for a company at $1.5T. A host says this was predicted in a paper years ago.
  • Distributed compute idea: AI5 chips in millions of Cybercabs, Megapacks and Superchargers (75,000 stalls, empty at night) linked by Starlink could add inference capacity; roughly 1.2M Cybercabs could equal 10 GW of inference.
  • Inference thesis: CPU demand could rise 10x with agents; the heavy cooling on Cybercab is speculated to be for GPUs.

The first gigawatt in space by 2029-2030

Bhakdi models SpaceX reaching its first orbital gigawatt in 2029 at best and 2030 conservatively, with launch cadence and pads as the constraints.

  • Starship should be ready to deploy by end of next year; Nvidia and SpaceX are building an orbital version of the Vera Rubin 72-rack scheduled for Q4 2027, so scaling starts in 2028.
  • A gigawatt needs roughly 15,000-20,000 tons; SpaceX has asked for about 450 flights a year; Bhakdi's bull case is 24 flights in 2028 and 75 in 2029.
  • Bottleneck debate: the host stresses reusability (catching booster and Starship on test flight 15) and ramping to six launches a month; Bhakdi expects launch pads to become the limit by 2031-2032.
  • SpaceX is said to be adding about a gigawatt-scale block every 30 days to reach 2.7 GW this year and 10 GW next year at around $50B a gigawatt of capex; a host models 12.5 GW live by end of next year.
  • Satellites are expected to be around 240 kW, so about 4,000 per gigawatt with laser links, fine for inference but not for tightly coupled training.
  • Why space: the 'walls are coming in' on terrestrial compute, with permits and local resistance getting harder each year, so orbit is perfectly timed for 2028-2029.
  • The permit conundrum: Elon says he has a 'magic hack' he won't share, and the speakers think siting in pro-build states like Louisiana, Tennessee and Texas is part of it.

Why Elon can build compute while neoclouds can't: a supply problem, not demand

Bhakdi says demand is effectively unlimited, so winners are those who can stand up compute fastest, and SpaceX has secured cooling, turbines, chips and 300,000-GPU clusters.

  • Elon is reportedly securing about 20 GW worth of cooling for next year; cooling and wiring are his bottlenecks, and chips, memory and land are already handled.
  • Turbines: when manufacturers said they couldn't get blades from the only three suppliers, SpaceX started casting blades with rocket-engine casting technology.
  • A rumor says he locked up 40% of Vera Rubin supply; coherent clusters of 300,000 GPUs versus others' roughly 30,000; data centers built in about 12 weeks and paid back in under 12 months.
  • Neoclouds: CoreWeave's build time went from 2.5 to 4 years, with each year of delay costing about $5B of interest at 15%; Bhakdi is not invested and sees them as supply-constrained.
  • Hyperscalers are 'toast' without compute (Google reportedly buying compute from Elon); H100 rental prices on a compute futures market are higher than six years ago.
  • Revenue scale: half a gigawatt makes a $26B run rate; a host expects a gigawatt to command about $72B by 2030.
  • Macro view: the AI sell-off ('fake bubble, nubble') is nearly over after maybe one more week; SpaceX and other AI names look undervalued on PEG ratios.

Midterms, gridlock and the tech-versus-Democrats split

The speakers say history favors stocks after midterms, that Wall Street likes a paralyzed DC, and that politics is now a fight over AI and tech power.

  • Over 19 midterm cycles since 1950, markets were up about 8% three months after election day, about 14% at six months and 20-32% at twelve; history shows 'a dip and a rip' around uncertainty.
  • Bhakdi expects the Republicans to hold the Senate and calls blue-red-red (a Democratic House with the Senate and presidency unchanged) the best Wall Street outcome.
  • He argues Wall Street prefers gridlock because politicians who can act raise debt and taxes, regardless of party.
  • Tech and politics: Silicon Valley has become a closed front against the Democrats, who attack AI and billionaires; Bhakdi says money managers like BlackRock, Blackstone and Apollo are investing in AI, so torpedoing AI hits them.
  • He frames the future as 'Snow Crash' (corporations over governments), with Elon's media ownership push seen as a bid for reality control; the hosts say they are analysts, not activists.
  • Taxes: both see tax regimes tightening globally (unrealized gains, billionaire taxes, crypto monitoring), pushing geographic arbitrage.
