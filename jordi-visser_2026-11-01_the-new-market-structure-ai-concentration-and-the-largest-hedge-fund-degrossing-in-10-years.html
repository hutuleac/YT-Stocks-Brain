October was a hedge fund deleveraging and rotation, not a crash

Morgan Stanley's largest-ever degrossing hit a concentrated AI trade while the broader market quietly rose.

  • The S&P fell 60 bp on the week and 93 bp for the month; 9 of 11 sectors were up and the share of S&P names above their 50-day average went from 20% to 61%.
  • Morgan Stanley Prime Brokerage reports its largest degrossing; Charlie McElligott's pair-trade monitor shows 124 pair trades unwound, 47 with moves beyond three sigma, in a year of widely high Sharpe winners that were almost all AI.
  • The 4% S&P drawdown most resembles the GameStop hedge-fund unwind inside a good economy; every other drawdown of 6%+ was 15%+ and tied to recession or tightening. The VIX barely budged (above 20 on Friday, then back) and IG CDX shows no credit panic.
  • Goldman's AI long-short basket (winners vs losers) gave back the whole year and the SMH fell to its 200-day, held it, and is the pattern of hedge funds getting over their skis; hedge funds are still up for the year in aggregate.
  • Technicals: head-and-shoulders and island-top shapes explain the bears, but the monthly charts look fine; average 14-day RSI of his 100-name agentic index hit the year's low; the VIX needs ~16 to signal calm. He expects no re-leveraging soon.
  • Screen idea: he runs weekly technical sheets through an LLM for names in advance or pullback with a rising MACD histogram above an upward-sloping 200-day; only five names fit.

High conviction MARCH VIDEO: LEAVING THE QE WORLDfinanceai-infra

Market structure has changed: strikeouts, then a home run, and borrowing hyperscalers

The hyperscalers left the debt-free QE world and now trade like leveraged bets; staying out risks missing the few months that matter.

  • Nvidia returned about 30% annualized over roughly 30 months, and a single four-month stretch made the entire move; fewer than 15% of months drove it. The S&P shows the same shape: one run from September produced 150% of the return over 13 months.
  • In March he argued the market was leaving the SaaS/Google/Amazon/Meta world of no debt and always-beat earnings for one of higher oil, rising rates, credit spreads and volatility, like the emerging markets he lived through.
  • The four hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta) are flat relative to the S&P since 2020 and flat since October 2025; this week Google fell 12% relative to the S&P, its biggest relative drop back to the financial crisis.
  • Single-day wipeouts this year: Google ~$500B, Microsoft $357B and Nvidia on DeepSeek $589B (about 20% of the whole Russell 2000). Microsoft is now the best performer because it is the one that hasn't raised capital.
  • Hyperscalers and the AI semis trade used to be uncorrelated (trade was underpriced); now they are locked together, so doubting hyperscaler capex means doubting memory and semis too.
  • Advice for his RAAS/FAS followers: trim hyperscaler weights, add agentic compute, keep longevity (Eli Lilly) and energy; rates stay higher and long-term yields probably peaked. Apple holds a ~40 P/E on a quality balance sheet with no spending.

Contested $1T AI DEBT · CCC ~16% · NOV 4 REFUNDINGmacro-ratesai-infrafinance

AI financing is the binding constraint, and rates decide the pace

Demand is not the issue; absorption, concentration and long-end rates are.

  • Goldman (Allison Nathan interview, August): financing needs peak in 2027; the constraint is investor absorption and concentration. Insurance demand for long-duration debt fell ~50% because of overexposure to 10 or so names; private markets and SPVs, with ~$4.5T of buying power, fill the gap.
  • About $1 trillion was raised this year across 16 companies (Meta, SpaceX via SPV and others), falling as rates rose; AI capex is 27% then 33% then 35% of the IG market through 2027. Microsoft announced a $25B deal and its CDS stayed calm because it hadn't borrowed.
  • Oracle had its best CDS week since September 2025 and the biggest tightening on record after pledging no more than $20B of extra financing through 2030, free cash flow positive in H2 2028 and $30B by 2029. Gavin Baker (TBPN): external financing for AI may be approaching a peak. CoreWeave debt also handled news well.
  • Compute has moved into contango and GPU rental prices are rising; Baker says future capacity is getting more expensive. Visser says capex guidance of $196B vs $185B is irrelevant next to the buildout.
  • Rates: the 10-year peaked around Oct 7 and broke its 50-day, with 4.50% a possible target if Bessent errs at the refunding; mortgage rates are up 100 bp since April (60 since Jackson Hole); CCC yields are ~16%, wider than 2022, so he expects bankruptcies.
  • Catalyst: the quarterly refunding on Nov 4. Citi expects Bessent to cut long-bond sales; Visser sees a possible twist or yield-curve-control-like shift if 20- and 30-year auctions shrink. Fed hike odds stayed near 75% for December, which he thinks isn't a given.
  • Goldman also shows energy, tariff and software inflation effects rolling off, and year-over-year DRAM prices collapsing; Intel was hit by memory costs.

Confirmed event ANTHROPIC S-1 · IPO IN ~2 WEEKSai-infrasoftware

Anthropic's S-1 removes most of the IPO risk

A $130B cash balance and fast growth mean Anthropic needs less financing than the bears assumed.

  • The S-1 shows $132B of cash, a surprise given the trillion-plus raised for AI this year not from free cash flow; revenue grows much faster than expenses and enterprise adoption is early, with half the Fortune 500 already customers.
  • Chamath said the IPO should be at a trillion dollars because of capital needs, then revised it: the company needs far less financing, so 1.3-1.7T. Hyperliquid's pre-IPO market traded around 1.7T Saturday. David Sacks, not an Anthropic fan, argued the capital backs its capex commitments to 2030.
  • Visser's own ChatGPT run on the S-1 gave a base case of ~$100B (95-110). Versus Google's S-1, Anthropic's prior full-year revenue was 1.4 vs 4.5 and its growth is far steeper; the October number was left blank, which Gavin Baker reads as likely strong.
  • He still doesn't want to own it: a hard business with commoditization, and he now uses ChatGPT, Grokbot and Muse more than Claude; Gemini impressed him this week. Opus 5.5 is a step up, and withheld better models limit distillation.
  • On Sam Altman's Cheeky Pint comments about building data centers and open source: not simple with memory shortages.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Degrossing — Hedge funds cutting both long and short positions, shrinking gross exposure.
CDS / CDX — Credit default swaps and their index; wider spreads mean the market prices more credit risk.
Contango (compute) — Future compute capacity costing more than spot, which Gavin Baker reads as tightening supply.
Quarterly refunding — The Treasury's announcement of debt auction sizes, due Nov 4.
RWA — Real-world assets put on blockchain rails through tokenization.
