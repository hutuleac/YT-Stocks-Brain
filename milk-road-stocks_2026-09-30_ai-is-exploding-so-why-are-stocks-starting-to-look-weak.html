Good growth data is bad news: yields at 19-year highs against AI revenue growth

The panel agrees the AI trade is strong but splits on whether yields and a narrow market force a correction.

  • Melvin: market closed red despite Nvidia's $150B buyback, Meta pushing Muse into enterprise and a SpaceX Starship milestone; the 10-year briefly hit ~5.25%, a 19-year high.
  • Oil held above $92, September flash PMI hit a 5-year high, Dallas manufacturing showed higher growth and inflation, and consumer spending and the labor market held up — all raise Fed-hike risk.
  • Fed officials said the AI buildout could cause near-term inflation (memory and laptops up ~20%); Melvin warns mortgage rates could approach 8% by next week.
  • Martin is more bearish: airlines, machinery, homebuilders and the Russell 2000 keep falling, so capex alone cannot lift everything; he holds 20% cash and may de-risk more.
  • Kyle: sentiment indicators are fairly bearish, not euphoric, despite prices near highs; he sees a market climbing a wall of worry.
  • Melvin argues Bessent has to bring yields down because the US must refinance almost $10T of debt this year and cannot do it at 7-8%; he also cites midterms as a reason policy favors a strong market.
  • Kyle cites Elon Musk and an unnamed Fed figure expecting GDP growth to double from 2% to 4% next year; he sees no 2022-style long bear market, only possibly a pullback.

OpenAI's $70B ARR jump and the always-on agent launch

A $30B ARR add in about six weeks convinced Kyle the frontier-lab growth is real; Martin says revenue is only half the picture.

  • OpenAI ARR reportedly went from $20B at end-2025 to ~$45B a month earlier to $70B; enterprise is its fastest-growing B2B line, up more than 700% since July.
  • ChatGPT has 1.2B weekly users and over 2.5M businesses; Anthropic is roughly $75B and still growing, and Grok's agent product has likely added billions.
  • Kyle credits the spike partly to the Astra model (and Codex); Martin counters that Claude Sonnet 5.5, announced the day before, is 5x cheaper and more performant.
  • On Dev Day OpenAI launched Dots, an always-on agent in ChatGPT with its own cloud computer, browser and thousands of app connectors — Kyle calls it Grok's bot inside ChatGPT.
  • Kyle: Anthropic showed a profit last quarter and OpenAI is rumored to show one in Q3; he says profit matters less than growth right now.
  • Martin disagrees: revenue is easy to boost, training and inference costs are unknown, and an OpenAI product manager said subscription token usage will be cut because subscriptions are heavily subsidized.
  • Kyle expects new compute commitments on Dev Day, recalling last year's 6 GW AMD deal that sent AMD up 24%.

Vincent and Martin's frontier-lab risk list, and Kyle's diversification rebuttal

The bear case is that compute demand rests on two heavily leveraged customers, while the bull case says new buyers diversify the demand.

  • Vincent: Anthropic's IPO numbers (via Reuters) show ~$518B of planned AI infrastructure spend, ~80% committed, which took a key risk off the table that day.
  • He flags that 25% of Anthropic's revenue came from two customers (end-2025 data); his guess is Microsoft and Cursor, and Cursor's integration into SpaceX could move that demand.
  • From a podcast he heard, ~25% of frontier AI revenue comes from venture-funded startups, which a rate-hike cycle could hit.
  • Vincent: with 40-60%+ of a portfolio in frontier-linked names, taking some chips off the table makes sense.
  • Martin cites SemiAnalysis: OpenAI and Anthropic could buy 40-50% of next year's new compute, so even the expectation of trouble could trigger a selloff.
  • Kyle's rebuttal: if SpaceX/xAI and Meta take a larger share, the buyers get bigger with other revenue (rocket launches, Starlink, a multi-hundred-billion-dollar ad business); Vincent agrees diversification is good but adoption of Meta's and xAI's agents is the unknown.
Worth noting
Vincent and Martin hold large AI allocations themselves; their warnings are partly portfolio-sizing advice.

Agents versus SaaS: round-two selloff or buy the dip?

Melvin expects another software selloff as labs go after enterprise; Kyle and Vincent say agents will use existing software and SaaS survives.

  • Meta launched its enterprise platform and Muse push earlier that week; software stocks dropped 3-4% the day before, echoing the earlier selloff when Anthropic went after legal and financial services.
  • Melvin: seat-based models break when agents use Salesforce or ServiceNow on a person's behalf; he expects some names to be decimated again.
  • Kyle: more agents means more businesses and more SaaS needed; Salesforce's Agentforce is its fastest-growing product, built on usage, and Grok's bot now runs inside Slack.
  • Kyle adds the labs must build on the application layer rather than just resell tokens; the risk is that OpenAI or Anthropic rebuild SaaS tools in-house.
  • Martin's near-term worry: the market will group all software together and sell it, as earlier this year, and many stocks are still down 50-60% even among the good ones.
  • Vincent: agents are rational and the world is compute constrained, so they will use legacy software rather than rebuild it; he would buy the dip, and billions of agents will use existing tools.
  • Martin and Vincent argue Salesforce and ServiceNow are embedded data companies the labs cannot easily displace; Kyle counters that Claude and OpenAI are also integrated everywhere.

Where the moat is: SpaceX's infrastructure, Meta's distribution and trust

As every lab converges on the full stack, Kyle backs SpaceX's physical moat, Melvin and Vincent back Meta's distribution, and Martin says data decides.

  • Kyle: labs can go up the stack to apps, or down to infrastructure as SpaceX and Meta are doing; SpaceX has the biggest moat via data centers, space-based compute and Terafab chip production.
  • An ARC analyst expects SpaceX to reach 66.6 GW of compute by 2031; Elon replied he would be disappointed if that were as low; SpaceX is at about 1.4 GW now.
  • Elon's end-of-year compute target rose from 2 GW to 2.6 GW, and concrete is already down for Terafab.
  • The Information reported Anthropic's SpaceX deal is worth up to $84B, up from an initial ~$45B, for terrestrial compute; Kyle says it was not priced in and SpaceX ran on it.
  • Martin: data is how companies differentiate (Figure, Eli Lilly, Bending Spoons, Tesla all chase data); Kyle adds you need both data and compute.
  • Meta: stock is up ~45% in two weeks; Vincent says the compute spend now has a clear agentic use; Melvin credits Zuckerberg for delaying Muse until it was trustworthy; both call distribution across Instagram, WhatsApp and Facebook the edge.
  • Kyle laughs off Blue Origin's raise (reportedly ~15x oversubscribed, $150B valuation) as having no shot against Starship; Martin says it is a decade behind.
  • Kyle's anecdote: Siri failed a simple gram-to-pound conversion, while Grok is built into his Tesla — Apple and Google could still act, and Vincent says Apple's CEO has a tech background.

Micron preview: long-term agreements and the 'not cyclical' narrative

Everyone expects a blowout quarter; the debate is whether guidance, long-term agreements and 2028 supply can move the stock.

  • Wall Street expects ~$51B revenue and ~$30-31 EPS; Melvin's estimate is $52.2B with an 87.5% gross margin.
  • Melvin wants Micron to address new supply and China's memory makers, its HBM4 shipping pipeline, and more long-term agreements than last quarter's 16 to kill the cyclical narrative.
  • Kyle: earnings won't move the stock; the risks are guidance and OpenAI/Anthropic. Vincent disagrees and says the outlook matters, including the base revenue and margin inside the long-term agreements and 2028 supply.
  • A report says Samsung's customers want deals of 10+ years, to 2035-2036; Martin heard the whole Korean memory supply is locked in through 2031.
  • Vincent: locked-in pricing is tricky because it trades off a lower fixed price against flexible capacity at higher margins; Melvin says memory sells to profitable buyers, unlike neoclouds.
  • Martin wants memory companies to announce buybacks or dividends since they trade at cheap prices; Micron cannot buy back until December.
  • Melvin notes Nvidia's buyback is $150B of up to ~$250B over 18 months with no stock move yet; Kyle says that's slow pressure on a $5T stock; a UBS analysis said Micron could buy back ~40% of its float.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

ARR — Annual recurring revenue — the annualized run rate of current revenue.
Long-term agreement (LTA) — A multi-year supply contract that fixes volume and pricing, used to argue memory is less cyclical.
HBM4 — High-bandwidth memory generation used with AI accelerators.
Seat-based pricing — SaaS pricing per human user, which agents could undermine.
Terafab — SpaceX's planned large chip-production facility.
Neocloud — A new AI-focused cloud provider that sells compute to labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic.
