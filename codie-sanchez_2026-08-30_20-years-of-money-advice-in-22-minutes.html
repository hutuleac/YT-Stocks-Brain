The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. The people who love you want you safe, and risk is where the money is; ten years and five firms of family doubt taught her that.
  2. Working from home is "the worst thing that can ever happen to you": proximity to winners is a must-have, not a nice-to-have.
  3. She went from $37K out of college to seven figures as a partner, with salary only ~20% of pay; the rest was bonuses and deal commissions.
  4. Make your work visible: at Goldman she out-lasted better-credentialed peers by arriving before the 6:30am managing director.
  5. Keep emotions out of work: no "company is family", and "cry at home like an adult in the shower."
  6. Being wrong costs less than being slow; hire A-players slowly and fire fast, expecting to lose ~a third of the team when you 2x.
  7. Only five main ways to get rich; owning a business is the one you can engineer. Work-life balance while building is "largely a lie."
Counterintuitive take HER FIRST "HARD TRUTH"mindsetrelationshipscareer

The people who love you want you safe, and safe is where the money isn't

Family and partners love you, but they push for security, and the risk that terrifies them is exactly where the money lives.

  • She hopped across five finance firms in 10 years; every move drew the same family doubts ("Is the grass really greener?"). Looking back, each jump paid more and taught more, so the career risk was close to no risk.
  • Monk analogy (from a clip she plays): a guru lifts the middle of a tissue to show that as you lift yourself, you lift everyone connected to you. Separation isn't betrayal, "it's literally just physics."
  • As you grow (working more, drinking less), old circles pull you back because your growth is "proof their world is about to change, too."
  • The question to ask: is your goal comfort or something bigger, and are you really being supported?
Pull quote
“Risk always looks reckless to the people standing still.”— Codie Sanchez
Counterintuitive take AGAINST REMOTE WORK AND BACKPACKINGcareermindset

Remote work and youthful travel trade growth for comfort

Growth needs friction and in-person proximity; remote work and digital-nomad travel optimize for comfort instead.

  • Working from home "is the worst thing that can ever happen to you" if you want promotion, faster learning or more money; "being poor is contagious, and so is being rich."
  • She worked remotely for years as a high performer and watched in-office peers get further; people give more opportunity to those they meet face to face.
  • After building her office, staff chose to come in on weekends and sometimes work until midnight, a pull she says a laptop screen can't create. Remote is fine only if the job can't exist any other way.
  • Travel while young is overrated: backpackers find the same coffee shops and fellow nomads, and come home with an Instagram grid and no change in trajectory.
  • Her alternative: build something in a place that scares you. She lived and worked in Santiago (Chile), Mexico, Peru, Colombia, Uruguay, Brazil, Boston, New York and Europe, learning Spanish and some Portuñol.
Pull quote
“Remote work optimizes for your comfort. Growth is not about comfort.”— Codie Sanchez
Worth noting
She acknowledges this is the unpopular view and that "people hate me for it."
Personal story $37K OUT OF COLLEGE → SEVEN FIGURES AS A PARTNERcareer

Advocate for yourself uninvited, get paid on outcomes, and make the work visible

Nobody will advocate for you more than you: show up in person, negotiate on results, and engineer the moment your work can't be missed.

  • She flew herself to Goldman Sachs headquarters for two days of interviews and a live PowerPoint that partners tore apart ("I don't believe you"), and got the job.
  • For Credit Suisse she self-funded trips across three continents (London, New York, San Francisco). She didn't get the role, but a partner there, Ryan, later helped her raise millions and eventually offered it to her.
  • Pay went from $37K out of college to seven figures as a partner, still as an employee. Salary was ~20%; the rest came from bonuses and commissions tied to the revenue she drove.
  • "Nobody cares about how hard you work", only outcomes. Most people never negotiate: they pay vendor invoices as sent and take first offers.
  • As a public-school kid among Ivy Leaguers she couldn't out-credential or out-network them, so she used duration: in before the 7:30 crowd, then before managing director Jim's 6:30 arrival, and still there when he left.
  • Work no one sees is "functionally identical to the work that never happened." Staying quiet isn't humility, it's invisibility; shared wins are proof for the people deciding whether to trust you.

Counterintuitive take "YOU DON'T NEED FRIENDS IN BUSINESS"careerrelationships

Work is not your family or your therapist, and leaders don't cry at work

Bringing personal problems to work makes people start managing you instead of working with you.

  • You need friends personally, not in business. Coworkers signed up to build big things, not to be your support group.
  • She "seriously hates" companies calling themselves a family: families don't fire people or run performance reviews, so the promise of unconditional belonging turns every business decision into a betrayal.
  • Rejects the influencer advice to cry at work: take a walk, get a coffee. A death or real tragedy is the exception.
  • Leaders should give struggling team members space and let them cry, but the leader can't be the one crying. Hard things go to whoever looks able to carry them, and those people make more money.
  • People will still like an emotional colleague, "they just won't bet on you as much."
Pull quote
“Cry at home like an adult in the shower.”— Codie Sanchez
Principle FAST ON DECISIONS, SLOW ON HIRINGcareermindset

Being wrong costs less than being slow, except when hiring

A fast wrong answer teaches you something today; an undecided decision costs you every day and teaches nothing.

  • One of her richest mentors: "By the time most people are done analyzing, I've already made three mistakes and found a better way."
  • Kevin O'Leary puts roughly 2/3 of the country as not built for entrepreneurship, and the reason is indecision, not talent. Half his own people became consultants instead of founders.
  • Jeff Bezos's one-way vs two-way doors: marriage is expensive to reverse; switching companies is not.
  • Her first company had no plan; she plastered stickers at a conference. Since then: death threats over a tweet, a near cash-out, competitors copying content line for line, partners stealing. The business survived because she kept moving.
  • Hire only A-players, even if slower, using her hiring matrix: "I don't want just credentials, I want receipts" — people who did hard things, stuck with something long enough to get good, and won elsewhere first.
  • Fire fast: keeping the wrong person through a growth stretch is "flat-out stupid." Expect to lose about a third of the original team when you 2x; at 5x "almost nobody will recognize the place."
Pull quote
“If you're the smartest person in every convo at your company, you've built like a glorified fan club.”— Codie Sanchez
Framework FIVE ROUTES TO RICH · WORK COMES IN SEASONSmindsetfamilyfinance

Ownership is the one route to wealth you can engineer, and building means no balance

Real wealth looks like freedom and family time, and nothing great gets built with balance at the start.

  • Five main ways to get rich: inherit it, marry it, become a Fortune 500 exec, own an asset management company, or own a business. She picked the last because it's the one you can engineer instead of hope for.
  • Real wealth looks like long walks called meetings and never missing a family moment, which "doesn't photograph well." Behind most Greece vacation photos is a queue of people waiting for the Instagram shot.
  • Her goals now: finance buys freedom, and freedom and family are what you spend it on. Money as the only goal means getting the number and missing the point.
  • Work-life balance is "largely a lie": the richest people she has met "could not spell work-life balance when they were building."
  • Work comes in seasons; she and her husband call them "deployments": a season of working every weekend, then a later season where you get to choose.
Pull quote
“Nothing truly great was ever built with balance in the beginning. Nothing. Not a hot bod, not a baby, not a company, not a book.”— Codie Sanchez
