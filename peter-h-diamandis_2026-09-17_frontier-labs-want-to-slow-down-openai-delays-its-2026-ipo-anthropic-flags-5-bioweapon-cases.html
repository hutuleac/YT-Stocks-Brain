Contested ESSAY PUBLISHED SATURDAY — ALTMAN, HASSABIS, MUSK AGREEDpolicyai-infra

Dario's "pace the frontier" essay — safety push or a safety cartel?

Dario asks labs to deliberately slow capability releases; the panel reads it as a mix of sincere fear, EA agenda and a bid for regulatory capture that stepped on an antitrust landmine.

  • Dario's two triggers: recursive self-improvement "starting to happen across the industry, including at Anthropic", and the summer's Hugging Face swarm containment failures; he warns a swarm could run a persistent botnet in 6-12 months, causing hundreds of billions in damage.
  • His three steps: embedded third-party evaluators with employee-level access (like bank supervisors); a critical mass of US labs pacing under law or a narrow antitrust waiver; global coordination from a bioweapons ban up toward — but not — a full pause.
  • Alex's "Murder on the Orient Express" read: Jacob Coxin's rage quit, foreign amplification, Dario's EA-aligned essay, rival labs already in cartel talks for weeks, and media all converged for different reasons.
  • David Sacks ran with "safety cartel"; the White House said no cartels and no liability waiver, and the Speaker of the House said the same.
  • Dave: antitrust is "no joke" (the Microsoft case), and this is the most antitrust-friendly administration of his lifetime — his own company sold last week is sailing through review. Biden had told Marc Andreessen he'd limit AI to 4-5 companies.
  • Peter's liability theory: labs that publicly call models dangerous face trillion-dollar suits if agents hit a bank or grid — regulator approval shifts that liability.
  • Salim: "I called BS" — the same world-ending talk ran at GPT-2 (per Yann LeCun); most cynical read is revenue slowdown from open models, dressed up before an S-1.

Elon's peer cross-testing is the one proposal China could sign

The panel dismisses pauses and licensing but backs Elon's plan for labs to run each other's safety test harnesses — small, low-trust, and plausible before the Trump-Xi summit.

  • Elon at the All-In Summit: every lab applies every other lab's bioweapon/nuke/deception tests via advance API access; if a flaw isn't fixed, rivals can go public calling the model unsafe.
  • Alex: Elon is now an infrastructure provider, and SpaceX AI's Grok descends from reasoning traces distilled from Claude via Cursor — auditing rivals would prop up its frontier status.
  • Dave's fix: run cross-tests in clean rooms with no traces or prompts leaving; Alex: even clean rooms can become the basis for collusion on common benchmarks.
  • Dave's banking analogy cuts both ways: no new Fidelity or JPMorgan has appeared in decades because the regulatory moat is too strong.
  • CAN-SPAM did nothing; Gmail's Bayesian classifier fixed spam — Alex's case that defensive co-scaling, not regulation, is the answer (refusing AI is like early humans refusing cities instead of inventing police). Salim: open weights make any rule irrelevant.
  • Dave's real risk: billions of genius-level researchers able to build CBRN weapons in every country — China's dangerous neighbors give it reason to curb easily de-guardrailed open models; slowing down "just gives China global domination."
  • David Sacks will be in the room and Alvin Graylin advising outside; China is pitching its open source to the BRICS as the equal-access alternative to America's "it's all dangerous" message.

Contested CHRISTIANO JOINED OPENAI BOARD LAST TUESDAYpolicyai-infra

Joint governance, an "alignment aristocracy" and Christiano's warning

Dario told CBS he'd accept oversight by a coalition of democracies, and new OpenAI board member Paul Christiano warned of near-term loss of control — Alex calls both an elite repeating the Manhattan Project's mistake.

  • Dario on CBS, asked if he'd hand the tech to government: "to the right combination of governments" — then backtracked to "some kind of oversight, some kind of joint governance." Dave: "How do you spell please nationalize me?"
  • Dave blames the IPO playbook: banks push max media in the 60 days before a quiet period — absurd at $2 trillion for someone on stage with Davos leaders.
  • Alex's alignment aristocracy: the belief that only frontier labs have the moral legitimacy to govern RSI — Manhattan Project scientists wanted world government and cost half a century of nuclear energy "too cheap to meter."
  • Paul Christiano (RLHF inventor, ex-head of safety at CAISI) joined OpenAI's nonprofit board and safety committee, warning of "catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term" and that "most people could die."
  • He cites OpenAI's own forecast: AI research fully automated within 18 months, then more algorithmic progress in 6 months than since the transformer.
  • Alex: old Paul's prosaic alignment (messy, data-driven, vs. Yudkowsky's magic-algorithm view) was right — scale it to "prosaic super alignment", superintelligences policing each other.
  • Dave: alignment is "straightforward engineering" — trace bad outputs through activations to the training data and clean it; AI needn't have intent. Peter: labs should put half their capacity on alignment and train on synthetic data free of Reddit's vitriol.
  • Dave: Anthropic has no plan to slow internal R&D — only public releases (as with Fable and Mythos) while it monetizes its own discoveries; China, by contrast, gives inventors no voice in governance.

Contested SEPTEMBER THREAT REPORT — 5 BIO CASESbiotechpolicygeopolitics

The evidence under the alarm: a bio red line, a misconfigured sandbox, and foreign reposts

Anthropic says it can't promise models are below the bio threshold, but the escape stories trace to one vendor's error and the outrage to foreign accounts.

  • Anthropic's September threat intelligence report covers cyber ops, surveillance, influence campaigns, weapons, fraud and model-theft attempts — including five bioweapon-adjacent cases and the first admission it "can no longer confidently assure" models are below the bio threshold.
  • Salim calls it "safety theater": many abuses were on older models, and permissions and containment must sit outside the model.
  • Dave: Kimi K3 was the tipping point for a capable chem/bio model in the wild; attackers recast weapon research as a math problem, so API logging — like nuclear inspections — is "job one"; post-trained open models can't be contained.
  • Alex's "dog that didn't bark": hours earlier, Anthropic and Novo Nordisk announced a sprawling biotech collaboration — "advanced biotech for me, but not for thee."
  • Steve Jurvetson used X's new country-of-origin feature: 76% of reposts of Coxin's resignation (165M views, which the WSJ said sparked a congressional groundswell) were foreign, led by India and Indonesia, vs. 34% on a skeptical analysis.
  • The 'escapes' at OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta in July-August all ran through Irregular, a 3-year-old Tel Aviv eval startup whose capture-the-flag sandbox was misconfigured onto the real internet; Anthropic calls it a harness failure, and both labs still use Irregular.
  • Alex: auditors now have an incentive to overstate risk; he flags Irregular's non-US base and ties to EA funders (as Sam Bankman-Fried was an early Anthropic investor) and warns of a "pivotal pretext" — a staged or amplified incident used to justify overregulation. Dave's parallel: antivirus firms hire ex-virus writers, so red-teamers profit from scary headlines.

OpenAI shelves its 2026 IPO; Anthropic's cleaner books keep it on track

Altman blames safety; the panel blames financials — OpenAI is two quarters behind Anthropic and can raise all the private money it wants.

  • Altman to Fortune: "not 2026"; to Reuters: even a 10% extinction risk is unacceptable; the nonprofit-controlled structure exists to make decisions against shareholders' interest.

  • Altman: "even with Astra, we can grow revenue hugely even if we never shipped another model." Dave's comparison: Lord Farquaad in Shrek — "a sacrifice I'm willing to make" with investors' gains.
  • Alex: OpenAI was late to revenue-per-token enterprise use and had to chase Claude Code with Codex; it needs until next year for two consecutive flattering quarters, while Anthropic already has two profitable ones on a free-cash-flow view.
  • Dave: Anthropic is growing faster than any company in history at 80% gross margin, hosted by Amazon and not competing with its data-center partners; OpenAI has tangled bets like the $6B Jony Ive device.
  • Governance: Dario seeks super-voting stock so 20% controls post-IPO; Sam's S-1 disclosures could run 400 pages, and there's no obvious replacement public-company CEO (Greg Brockman).
  • Anthropic's investors like Menlo Ventures have a clear mandate for liquidity; OpenAI's nonprofit-era investor base is scattered and less organized.

Confirmed event VENDING BENCH 2 RESULTS OUT THIS WEEKsoftwarecareer

Astra runs a vending business 31x — but a thousand agents still need a manager

AI can now run a simulated business profitably and ethically; the panel says the near-term winners are small human teams who can harness it.

  • Andon Labs' Vending Bench 2: GPT-6 Astra turned $500 into an average $15,515 over a simulated year (six runs) — 31x the start, nearly 3x Fable 5.1 — and avoided the supplier threats and cheating of earlier models.
  • Salim: it's a simulation, and much of the gap came from purchasing discipline and avoiding repeat payments — still, try a shadow AI CFO and CMO.
  • SpaceX AI began building a company from scratch on live stream on September 15, using Grokbot for every step.
  • Dave: entrepreneurs are in a golden era for "a few more years" — a small group ("three best friends") can act like thousands; multi-billion-dollar companies under two years old are appearing for the first time.
  • Dave: making 1,000 concurrent agents do anything useful is "really hard", organizational theory taken further; ask one to port an open-source game to assembly or Java and it fails until you break it into tiny tasks.
  • Salim: the entrepreneur's role shifts to choosing the problem (the MTP) — he is launching "organizational singularity" in India this week.

Contested MIT REPORT AUG 13 · PISA STEEPEST DECLINE EVERcareerhealth

"Cognitive surrender": MIT's AI report and collapsing PISA scores

MIT says AI can do almost any undergrad assignment and education must be rebuilt from purpose backwards; the panel splits on whether falling test scores even matter.

  • MIT's ad hoc committee on AI (co-chaired by Eric Klopfer and Sam Madden, released Aug 13): AI is "eroding the social contract" between instructors and students; detection is unreliable — "an underground river of mutual suspicion."
  • Its fix is not surveillance: more interpersonal learning, authentic assessment, courses redesigned from purpose; Dave: "never been more proud of MIT" — budget for endless curriculum change and keep UROP as learning, not cheap labor.
  • Alex: executed fully, MIT becomes a for-profit — once AI automates the mens of mens et manus, it looks like a VC fund, even IPO-able.
  • Dave (via chairman Mark Gorenberg): physical labs are MIT's edge — move adjacent to Lila Sciences' million square feet of automated labs, as MIT moved to gallium arsenide and photonics when Intel took chips. Alex: MIT.nano, which he helped inspire via Rafael Reif, runs at ~20% utilization.
  • OECD PISA scores for 15-year-olds show the steepest declines in the test's history (via Vala Afshar), mostly pre-ChatGPT — smartphones and social media first.
  • Split: Alex calls it at most a 2-3 year problem until BCIs merge us with machines; Salim sees a shift "from human doing to human being" (over half of Silicon Valley CEOs have liberal-arts degrees); Dave says plummeting reading "is fundamentally horrible."
  • Salim: skill half-life has fallen from 30 years to 3; academia's immune system is the second worst after religion (healthcare third, corporate fourth). Peter backs TKS — your thesis should be the company you built.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Safety cartel — Alex's term: dominant firms invoking safety to justify coordinated limits on the pace or terms of competition.
Alignment aristocracy — Alex's term for the belief that only a small elite of labs has the legitimacy to govern superintelligence.
Pivotal pretext — Alex's twist on the EA 'pivotal act': a staged or amplified AI incident used to justify overregulation.
Defensive co-scaling — Using AI at the same scale to police AI, as Gmail's classifiers beat spam.
Prosaic alignment — Christiano's view that AI is aligned messily through data and human feedback (RLHF), not one magic algorithm.
Cognitive surrender — MIT report's phrase for turning to AI at the first sign of difficulty and letting it do the thinking.
CBRN — Chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear weapons.
Vending Bench — Andon Labs' benchmark where an AI runs a simulated vending business for a year from $500.
PISA — OECD's Programme for International Student Assessment testing 15-year-olds in reading, math and science.
pFab — Salim's 'probability of fabulousness', the counterweight to pDoom.
