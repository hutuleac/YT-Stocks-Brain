To profit from tokenization, own the rails — not the tokenized Tesla share

A tokenized ETF or Tesla share is still an ETF or Tesla share; the upside sits in the infrastructure everything moves on.

  • Marginal perks of a tokenized asset: trades 24/7, easier to borrow against, "a little more functional" — but the bulk of your return is still the underlying.
  • Rail #1: high-performance layer-1s — Ethereum and Solana "serve as the basis for tokenization."
  • Rail #2: oracle providers like Chainlink that feed real-world data about tokenized assets back on chain.
  • Rail #3: the companies "building aggressively in the space" (none named).
  • Framing: a "once-in-a-generation or once-in-every-three-generation" upgrade of the financial system — "every time that happens, there's massive opportunities."

All assets go on chain because 1920s plumbing can't run a 24/7 world

Tokenization is "the email to the traditional snail mail" for moving securities, and Hougan treats the end state as settled.

  • Appeal to authority: the SEC chairman and BlackRock's CEO both say all assets are going on chain — "I'm in good company."
  • Today: stocks settle T+1 through a "Rube Goldberg-style contraption" with half a dozen intermediaries; markets trade 6.5 hours a day, 5 days a week.
  • Speeding up the existing system would take "years and years"; tokenization gives 24/7/365 global movement with instant settlement.
  • BlackRock wants all its ETFs live on chain within ~12 months (per a BlackRock contact Hougan cited in an earlier clip).
  • Inclusion: anyone with a cell phone can own fractional real estate, bonds or US private credit — and self-custody the tokens.
  • DeFi loans for everyone: borrowing against stocks today is for the $100k-$1M Schwab client; tokenized, wealthy-investor services reach every investor.

No off button: 24/7 markets bring weekend crashes and leverage blowouts

A tokenized world is better for most people, but crypto's thin-liquidity and leverage problems will show up in traditional markets too.

  • No more Friday 4:00 close to Monday 9:30 open — markets trade 24/7/365 and investors "have to have systems to deal with it."
  • Upside: after a big geopolitical event you can reposition immediately instead of waiting for the open.
  • Downside: nights and weekends are less liquid "cuz people are asleep," so shock events trigger outsized sell-offs.
  • More leverage builds up — useful but risky; crypto's 10/10 event is the template for leverage blowouts reaching TradFi.

Wall Street validates tokenization — and lobbies to capture it

Institutions arriving is both validation and a regulatory-capture threat; Hougan expects concentration but a better system overall.

  • Validation: BlackRock, JPMorgan and major exchanges now accept that 9:30-4:00 trading and T+1 (T+2 a few years ago) are absurd in an age of instant Amazon delivery.
  • Capture playbook he sees: tokenization only on quasi-private chains, small closed ecosystems, no lawsuit protection for DeFi builders — lobbying to "distort what the Clarity Act is."
  • What to fight for: open, permissionless rails with reasonable AML/KYC limits that lower costs and improve uptime.
  • Concentration likely: Ethereum, Solana and a few others as primary L1s; large asset managers keeping a "chokehold" on creating assets.
  • Better, not perfect: ETFs created giants and passive distortions but cut fees from 1% to 10 basis points — "hundreds of billions of dollars in everyone's pockets every year."
  • Same with brokerage: $200 fixed commissions fell to zero at Robinhood, with payment for order flow as the catch — still a net improvement.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Tokenization — Representing a real-world asset (stock, bond, ETF, real estate) as a blockchain token so it can trade and settle on chain.
T+1 — Settlement one business day after the trade; US stocks moved from T+2 to T+1 a few years ago.
Layer-1 (L1) — A base blockchain such as Ethereum or Solana that tokenized assets are issued and settled on.
Oracle — A service that feeds off-chain data (prices, events, dates) into blockchains; Chainlink is the dominant one.
DeFi — Decentralized finance: lending, trading and other services run by on-chain protocols like Aave and Uniswap.
Agentic finance — AI agents transacting on their own, which Hougan expects to multiply on-chain transaction volume.
10/10 — The October 10, 2025 crypto crash, a record leverage liquidation he cites as the blowout template.
Clarity Act — US crypto market-structure bill whose final shape, he says, Wall Street lobbying is trying to distort.
Regulatory capture — Incumbents shaping rules to protect their own position, here by pushing private chains and limiting DeFi.
Payment for order flow — Brokers routing orders to market makers for payment; the hidden cost of zero-commission trading.
