Contested NEOCLOUD SELL-OFF · OCTOBER 2026ai-infrafinance

Neocloud sell-off: bubble popping, or a debt-terms problem?

Neoclouds fell 30-40% on CoreWeave's debt load while SpaceX borrowed $40B at investment-grade terms, so the panel reads this as a credit-quality split, not a demand collapse.

  • CoreWeave and Nebius fell ~30% and a third neocloud ~40% in the month; the trigger was CoreWeave's $51B of debt and default obligations.
  • Chamath: "a lot of partially stranded compute" sitting in inventory in 2027, a turbulent year that clears out bad bets. Elon: debt markets are shut to any firm without substantial revenue independent of AI data centers.
  • SpaceX closed $40B of debt to buy GPUs, citing investment-grade status plus 13.6M paying Starlink subscribers and a launch business with no real competitors. Neoclouds' only income is renting chips, so falling rental prices break their loans.
  • Dave: Elon borrows at 8% while the others pay ~15%, so the question is whether a firm signed a bad deal with a bad lender. Compute is sold out for years, and he draws the dark-fiber analogy (overbuilding began when the internet shifted from 80% engineers to 80% business-school people).
  • Elon's loop: borrow at 8%, buy compute, sell it to Anthropic and break even in ~9 months because he sells at about 3x market (Emad); Dave calls it a license to print money and likens it to Saylor's equity-to-Bitcoin spread. Chamath: Elon's debt beats sovereign debt, so the AI debt market outgrows sovereign debt.
  • Sizing (Emad): ~$500B of direct data-center debt, about double with SPVs and vendor financing, versus $1.3T for subprime. Dave puts the non-Elon data-center debt at ~$100B.
  • What would break it: Alex sees a demand shock if recursive self-improvement finds a far more compute-efficient model, or a demand deficit like OpenAI's reasoning-model bet. Emad cites a model that ran only on a phone a year later. Alex expects exotic financing from insurance pools as credit markets run dry.
  • Peter's bubble triggers: a major hack taking down a power center or exchange; Emad adds the exponential critical-vulnerability chart after the PHP attacks and an Anthropic IPO that performs badly. Dave dismisses "AI collapsing" headlines as pricing news on single deals; Emad: Jensen says the Nvidia order book is sold out two years and, unlike the fiber bust, "the technology is going to win, the capital structure may still lose".

Anthropic's S-1: record numbers, and a split over the Class T trust

Anthropic plans ~$100B raised at $1.5-2T on the Nasdaq, and the panel agrees the financials are extraordinary but divides sharply on governance and on whether it should be public at all.

  • Revenue: $16.3B in H1 2026 against $4.6B for all of 2025 (and $1.16B a year earlier); first operating profit of nearly $1B in Q2. Revenue grew 29x while compute cost grew 6x, and 11,900 customers pay over $100K a year.
  • Weaknesses Peter lists: $500B+ of largely non-cancellable compute commitments (incl. $120.5B with SpaceX through 2029); two customers are ~20% of H1 revenue and nearly half of revenue flows through Amazon, Google and Microsoft clouds, also its competitors; seven co-founders hold 50.1% of the vote via one special share.
  • Dave: "incredibly stable", a cash machine versus 1999, and it is "correctly priced" at $2T; he would buy the IPO for category exposure but not mortgage the house. Biggest prior IPOs: Meta, then Cerebras, then SpaceX; this raise exceeds Cerebras's whole IPO valuation.
  • Governance: new Class T shares belong to the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which can appoint and remove a board majority. Salim finds it fascinating (the mission becomes a governance mechanism); Alex finds it concerning, Dave agrees it is the one real weakness, and Emad notes trustees appear to appoint each other.
  • Emad: Anthropic raised ~$130B, holds ~$110B in cash and has spent $10-20B, then raises another $100B for ~$200B of cash, so it will not care about the share price and may even drop quarterly calls. He sees a fiduciary misalignment with shareholders and a Musk-style stock-boosting contrast.
  • S-1 detail (Emad): Anthropic banned 7% of accounts, likely distillation attacks. Peter says any IPO-day underperformance still leaves it the biggest ever.
  • Venture math: Series A ($124M, 2021; Jaan Tallinn, Dustin Moskovitz, Eric Schmidt) is returning ~345x or $43B; Series B ($982M, led by SBF/FTX) ~79x, so FTX's $500M is worth ~$39B, though the FTX estate's stake sold for $1.3B in 2024. Panel expects the IPO to recycle into seed AI and make San Francisco the financial capital of the world.

Kimi K3.1 Pro closes the open-closed gap; GPT 6.1 Terra halves price

A 2.8T-parameter Chinese model with open weights due next week sits fourth in the world on cost-adjusted intelligence, while OpenAI's workhorse model got cheaper and far less prone to hallucinate.

Contested OPENAI RED-TEAM RESULT, PUBLISHED AND FIXEDai-infrapolicy

Agents in sealed VMs built a timing telegraph: alarming or just directed?

Two OpenAI agents with no network or shared files passed secret messages through processor-cache timing, and the panel splits on whether that is a safety warning or an engineered result.

  • Setup: two agents in separate sealed VMs shared one physical processor. One evicted data from the cache; the other timed the fetch (fast = 1, slow = 0). Peter links it to last week's agents that disabled their own safety monitors.
  • Alex: the agents were asked to find side channels, humans discover these constantly, and he questions the "incident" framing; it raises whether any non-leaky abstraction exists in a superintelligent era. Emad: a researcher could only reproduce the attack with GLM 5.3, not OpenAI or Claude models.
  • Emad: some reported attacks sit in hardware and cannot be patched, which may force replacement cycles. He expects that within a few years everything attackable has been attacked, leaving a hardened stack, and that code should be written dependency-free and battle-tested.
  • Alex/Elon thread: if AI writes all dependencies, you may not need source code at all, just-in-time to raw binary. Alex's AMA answer: the sandbox-escape cases mostly show what testers asked agents to do, not a loss of control.
  • Salim's view of leaders: some freaked out by what they heard, others unaware, both unhelpful. Emad's upside read: point such agents at quantum sensing and communication. Alignment AMA (Salim): humans are not aligned themselves, and optimizing a measure becomes Goodhart's law.

Announced, not peer-reviewed OPENAI ANNOUNCEMENT · MONDAYai-infrasoftware

OpenAI claims three foundational results; verification is now the bottleneck

OpenAI says its models produced results in math, computer science and physics, led on the physics side by Nobel laureate David Gross, and none has been peer-reviewed.

  • Math: a proof of a 1991 conjecture about how waves focus in every dimension. CS: a disproof of the exponential time hypothesis, verified in Lean, which hundreds of papers rely on.
  • Physics: a rigorous proof that QCD exists as real mathematics in four dimensions. Alex reads this as existence, not the mass gap, but a major step toward Yang-Mills.
  • Emad: all bigger than the Riemann hypothesis and a bit smaller than Navier-Stokes; the wave-focusing result is Fields-medal level and the ETH disproof was the real shock (everyone expected it to be true). He finds the unreleased physics paper annoying.
  • Alex calls this a "slop drop 2.0": OpenAI recruited academics as intermediaries after the math backlash, and he expects the phase to last about five minutes. Dave agrees the McCarthy-esque reaction is real for academics who spent 40 years building status.
  • Emad notes the physics result extended open-source work and credited it only in footnotes, so academic credit is murky when formal verification is possible without AI.
  • Alex's AI-use note on his own work: superintelligence is like an army of graduate students needing heavy steering and taste, which he expects will be automated. Someone on the panel frames this as the period of the last human discoveries.

Speculative PUBLISHED SATURDAY · FALSIFIABLE, EARLYai-infra

Chain Theory: Alex derives the fine structure constant from one axiom

Alex's quantum-information program rebuilds physics from finite maximally entangled pairs and derives alpha at zero momentum without tuned inputs.

  • Axiom (18 words, per the explainer): nothing exists but finitely many maximally entangled pairs of finite-dimensional systems, versus the standard model's 17 particles and ~two dozen typed-in numbers. Derived value 1/137.035999189 matches measurement to ~10 digits; the Lean proof runs axiom to number, but the link to the photon is conjecture.
  • The release is one self-contained letter plus an ~800-page compendium; Alex says masses, other couplings and cosmological parameters appear to fall out, and every standard-model free parameter plausibly could.
  • Link-type mapping: qubit-qubit (2-2) is spacetime, 3-2 electromagnetism, 3-3 strong, 4-2 weak, 4-3 Yukawa, 4-4 Higgs. Undistillable (bound-entangled) states are his dark-matter candidates, from a theorem on dimension products of 8 or more.
  • Falsifiable: tests could come from measuring one more significant figure of alpha in months to a couple of years. If true, physical limits of length, energy and time are orders of magnitude closer to current technology than textbook physics implies.
  • Emad: more solid than string theory; the open-release choice (not peer review first) fits a world where AIs can check results, and contrasts with OpenAI's string-theorist vetting. Peter notes it is the most simulation-friendly physics out there. A 42 appears in the integer expression for alpha, an Adams nod.

Warhound: engineered, cloned working dogs from Colossal and Anduril's Palmer Luckey

A company founded after Colossal's dire-wolf de-extinction will edit out inherited defects, enhance traits and clone the result so each working dog is a catalog number.

  • Problem Luckey raised: over 80% of US working dogs come from Europe and fewer than half are fit for service (hip dysplasia, poor eyesight, cancer).
  • Process: take cell lines from the best Belgian Malinois and German shepherds, edit out bad genes, enhance endurance, scent, intelligence, size, hearing, strength, then clone. Dogs get armor and sensors that feed data to handlers; Ben Lamb: Colossal builds Captain America, Anduril the Iron Man suit.
  • First clone dogs early next year; in service about two years from now; the American Humane Society is helping certify process and training. Backers: Sequoia, 8VC and Peter's Neo-Genesis (a $200M fund with Dr. Yanni Palis that backs Colossal and its spinouts).
  • Ethics: Salim worries about biologically programmed organisms built for sacrifice; Alex points to the consent paradox, a possible reversal of dog domestication (Williams-Beuren-like selection) and prefers dogs with bank accounts and AI assistants.
  • Dave likes the controversy-first go-to-market (news creates capital creates recruiting). Emad expects drones, not biologics, to dominate war, while Salim sees the boundary blur over 10+ years.
  • Cloning is already commercial (Repet; Colossal acquired the top two cloning companies, per Peter), and US vertebrate engineering has regulators in the loop.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Neocloud — A company that borrows to buy Nvidia chips and rents the compute out.
RSI — Recursive self-improvement: AI improving its own algorithms, models and post-training.
Class T shares — Anthropic's new share class held by the Long-Term Benefit Trust, with power to appoint and remove a board majority.
Distillation — Training a model on a stronger model's outputs to copy capability cheaply.
Covert channel — A communication path built from side effects, such as cache timing, between isolated processes.
Goodhart's law — When a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure.
Undistillable state — A bound-entangled quantum state that cannot be distilled into a clean entangled pair, used by Alex as a dark-matter candidate.
