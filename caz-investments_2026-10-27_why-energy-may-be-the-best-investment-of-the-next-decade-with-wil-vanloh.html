High conviction OVER 80% OF SECTOR PE CAPITAL GONE SINCE PRE-COVIDenergyfinance

A capital-starved sector that looks like the mid-1990s — with the best risk-adjusted returns anywhere

Capital fled oil and gas on poor returns and ESG just as demand fundamentals turned the best they've ever been.

  • VanLoh: oil, gas and midstream have the best risk-adjusted returns of any major asset class globally — he sees nothing better from his family office.
  • Over 80% of private equity available to the space five to six years ago is gone — poor returns plus ESG.
  • Quantum lost every European institutional investor in its last fundraise (closed last fall); its European ultra-high-net-worth families mostly doubled or tripled.
  • In the US a third to 40% of LPs leaned in; the 2021-23 wait-and-see LPs are now actively looking, a swing that took off after Trump's election.
  • Private companies drove over half the shale boom but now have 10-20% of the capital they need; public equity goes to buybacks and distributions, not growth.
  • Demand drivers: billions of people climbing the economic ladder plus AI as a "massive massive consumer of energy."
  • Echo of the mid-'80s-to-mid-'90s bust, when 90% of companies went out of business and the survivors had real competitive advantage.

High conviction RESERVE REPLACEMENT 9% THE LAST 5 YEARSenergy

Peak shale and a 9% reserve-replacement alarm: a $3.5-4T hole

A decade of underinvestment shows up as the lowest reserve replacement on record, and shale can no longer bail the world out.

  • Global cycle time from first exploration dollar to first production is 7-8 years; shale is the exception.
  • US shale revolution "is going to peak very soon" and no other shale revolution is expected globally — back to slow conventional assets.
  • Underinvestment of $350-400B a year since 2014 has built a $3.5-4T global deficit.
  • Reserve replacement: 200%+ from WWII to the '90s; below 100% in the '90s (lowest decade ~60%); above 100% in the 2000s; below in the 2010s; 9% in the last five years.
  • Zook: the ~60% decade was followed by $140 crude.
  • Too much capital rushing in? "We are nowhere close" — VanLoh doesn't see it as an issue for a very long time.
  • Shale tech (horizontal drilling plus hydraulic fracturing) took the US from most energy-dependent to most energy-independent country in ~20 years.

High conviction OPEC BREAKEVEN $90-95 VS OIL ~$67-68energygeopoliticsmacro-rates

Pricing power shifts back to OPEC — oil "should be" triple digits, gas $10

With US shale no longer supplying all global growth, OPEC+ regains pricing power, and its budgets need far higher prices.

  • Over 15 years the US delivered almost 100% of global oil supply growth, tripled oil output, more than doubled gas, and now produces ~22% of global supply.
  • OPEC pumps ~30M of a ~100M barrel/day market and Russia ~10M; Saudi, UAE and Russia run their governments off oil revenue.
  • OPEC breakeven without tapping sovereign funds is $90-95/barrel versus ~$67-68 today — and they want more to refill the coffers.
  • Housing, food, healthcare and education are up 60-80% over 15 years while oil fell by half and gas ~75%.
  • Inflation-adjusted, oil could be triple digits and US gas ~$10/MMBtu (the global price) vs a ~$3 five-year average.
  • Cheap energy "basically subsidized every other sector"; that ending is inflationary, matters for S&P valuations and has geopolitical consequences.
  • Zook: energy build-out is inflationary any way you slice it — one reason CAZ is in the higher-for-longer rates camp.

High conviction BOUGHT 11 GAS PLANTS BELOW 50 CENTS ON THE DOLLARenergyai-infrapolicy

Power demand wakes up after 15 flat years — build-baby-build makes existing plants worth more

Data-center demand meets an old grid, slow courts and a hollowed-out turbine supply chain, so existing assets trade far below what new ones cost.

  • US power demand grew just 0.2-0.3%/year for 15 years (1-2% before); projected ~20% growth over the next five years, ~2.5-3%/year.
  • Antiquated transmission, little new build, and more wind/solar make the grid less stable; battery storage adds cost.
  • Williams CEO Alan Armstrong at CERAWeek: $2 on regulatory for every $1 spent putting pipe in the ground.
  • A GE or Siemens gas turbine takes five years minimum; US turbine manufacturing capacity is down ~75% since the early-2000s build-out, with global back orders.
  • New gas plants cost ~$1.7-1.8M per MW (up to $2.5M) vs ~$1M before — "build baby build" strains supply chains and is inflationary.
  • Quantum bought Cogentrix (11 gas-fired plants) earlier this year for about $3B, under 50 cents on the dollar of replacement cost.
  • Nuclear is "the absolute best answer"; tech and society are getting behind it, and Quantum is building nuclear capability.

Confirmed practice DATA SCIENCE IS QUANTUM'S BIGGEST DISCIPLINEenergysoftware

Machine learning on frac knobs: proprietary drilling data as the investing edge

Shale has dozens of tunable variables, and a firm with clean proprietary data can price risk — and dodge bad deals — better than one using public data.

  • ML is "statistics on steroids"; shale has many knobs — lateral landing, sand, water, pump rate, stages, clusters per stage — so the payoff is large.
  • Industry is in the first or beginning of the second inning of applying AI at scale; VanLoh says he has still "meaningfully underestimated" its impact.
  • Quantum (founded at VanLoh's age 28) staffed engineers, geologists and geophysicists beside investors from day one, borrowing the Silicon Valley VC model; it started collecting digital data a decade-plus before peers.
  • Its portfolio is the largest driller in North America, ~65 rigs or 12-13% of US rigs running.
  • Public data is dirty (production allocated by algorithm); proprietary data cleans it. Hired Chevron's global digital architect; ~15 of a 75-person investment team are data staff, plus 30-40 full-time contractors in India.
  • ROI is tracked directly (cost saved, revenue added), but the bigger value is separating bad rock from bad operators, spotting juiced production, and walking away from deals that could lose hundreds of millions.
  • Generalists who chased the first shale decade mostly lost; "five or six" specialists survived by identifying, pricing and managing risk.

Integrity, downside focus, lifelong learning and grit — "80% of the battle"

VanLoh backs people for integrity, proven capital allocation with a downside focus, creative lifelong learning, and grit.

  • Origin: small-town central Texas, a football injury at TCU, a value-investing course from a Graham & Dodd disciple, then Kidder Peabody's energy group.
  • Started Windrock (energy capital-raising and M&A) just before 24 and Quantum at 28 — once had investors rent his car because he was under 25.
  • Traits: integrity and win-win; proven capital allocators who obsess over the downside; lifelong learners who hold views loosely; grit to get up after plans get "ripped up."

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Reserve replacement ratio — New reserves found per unit produced; below 100% means the resource base is shrinking.
Midstream — Pipelines, processing and storage between production and end use.
Breakeven price (fiscal) — Oil price a producer state needs to fund its budget without drawing reserves or borrowing.
Baseload power — Generation that runs steadily around the clock, unlike intermittent wind and solar.
Replacement cost — What it would cost to build an equivalent asset today.
Lateral — The horizontal section of a shale well drilled through the reservoir.
