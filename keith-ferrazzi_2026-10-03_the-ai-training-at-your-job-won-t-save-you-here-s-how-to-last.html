The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Ferrazzi says nearly every company bought the same AI tools and almost none see the expected results; adoption moves far slower than the technology.
  2. His model: three layers of AI use: redesign the company, re-engineer daily workflows, and boost individual productivity. Most firms sit in the third and gain least.
  3. Best example: Red Hat's CEO built a 'superbrain' of executive and report dossiers, ServiceNow process data and ERP feeds that lets leaders query the whole company.
  4. Culture changes through process, so he trains AI black belts (Six Sigma style): yellow to brown requires measurable outcomes, not burned tokens.
  5. He expects at least a couple million jobs to vanish without retraining, with call centers first, and companies to shrink by up to 80% in headcount.
  6. His answer is Workbridge, a nonprofit with ex-Virginia governor Glenn Youngkin: move displaced workers to internal roles, external training, or a points-based income marketplace.
  7. Personal root: his father, a Pittsburgh steelworker, lost his job in the 450,000-job steel collapse and was never retrained.

Everyone bought the same AI, few redesign work: three layers of use

Productivity tools help individuals, but the biggest gains come from re-engineering workflows, where traction is lowest.

  • Even leading AI labs don't use the tech at the speed it advances; his research institute has to scrape to find real corporate results.
  • Layer one, redesign the company (Amazon reinventing commerce). Layer two, re-engineer your daily workflow (e.g. in procurement). Layer three, individual productivity.
  • Most firms start at layer three: engineers see 50-80% productivity gains, but not everyone uses the tools well.
  • Layer-two traction is lowest but the greatest upside: customer service (call centers shrinking to basics), sales support, supply chain.
  • Y Combinator example: ~200 companies a quarter at $500K each; roughly 12% of unicorns come from there. Its CEO Garry Tan built a 'superbrain' where AI sets agendas, prompts accountability and refines workflows.
  • Tokens aren't the goal: Ferrazzi rejects 'burn tokens' mandates and Jensen's 'token maxing'; he wants measurable outcomes.

The 'superbrain': a company-wide AI built from dossiers, process data and ERP

Red Hat's CEO turned a 20,000-person company into a queryable system, and Ferrazzi says commercializing it would make old change management irrelevant.

  • Matt Hicks had executives write 10-page dossiers of their daily workflows, then their direct reports did the same: two layers of documentation fed to the AI.
  • Added ServiceNow process inputs and an ERP plug-in so data flows in; he can ask why New England sales trail Florida, and offices can ask how to match the best team.
  • The AI also recommends workflow re-engineering, which Ferrazzi's Cisco research could not otherwise produce on its own.
  • Nobody sells this yet; he is working with partners to commercialize it. Cisco procurement/supply chain research found end-to-end the key issue.
  • Similar idea: Musk's Macrohard digitizes employees, and tools that observe you all week, record calls and Zooms (ambient listening).
  • Fear: workers wearing recording glasses (Amazon, Meta) are training the AI that could replace them; implication is companies reaching the same revenue with up to 80% fewer people.

AI black belts: change the process and culture follows

A Six Sigma-style belt system builds AI skill through peer coaching and measurable results.

  • Culture isn't hard to change: change the process (Japanese quality circles beat top-down US steel; agile beat waterfall; safety process raised care).
  • Anyone who raises a hand at an AI day becomes a yellow belt, placed in small peer groups coached by a black belt proficient in that role.
  • Brown belt needs measurable outcomes from tool use; black belt means redesigning workflows around you and teaching others.
  • Uses positive psychology, ownership, branding, celebration and 'co-elevation'.
  • A government leader rejected the 'outrun the bear' (outrun you, not the bear) message for a promise to make every employee the most competitive in industry.

Workbridge: a transition path for displaced call-center workers

Stop debating the macro; fix the first displaced role with a concrete package and a model others can copy.

  • Roots: his father lost his steel job as ~450,000 steel jobs vanished; 5M+ jobs came later but the displaced were never retrained. Ferrazzi links that group's disenfranchisement to later politics.
  • Forecast: at least a couple million jobs, starting with call centers, will go without retraining; AI interactions may be more satisfying.
  • Goal: help 2 million people, starting with call-center staff at large firms.
  • IKEA turned a feared loss of 85,000 call-center staff into interior-design advisers.
  • Three options: internal moves (e.g. to care roles at healthcare firms like Elevance), external training, and a points-based marketplace income tied to local service, with government as partner.
  • Partners: former Virginia governor Glenn Youngkin (ex-CEO of Carlyle). Idea of billionaires funding it (Musk teased giving away money; Michael Dell's Trump accounts).
  • Diamandis: AI is also the best teacher to retool from the bottom up; fear of 'job apocalypse' has swung to 'new jobs' backpedals from lab leaders.

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

📝
AI workflow: Document each role's daily workflows in writing before feeding an AI; start with leaders and their direct reports.
🎯
Metrics: Reward measurable outcomes from AI use, not token or usage counts.
🥋
Culture: Run a belt-style peer coaching program to change behavior through process.
🚨
Careers: Pick your workflow and redesign it before the role is automated, especially in service roles.
🤝
Workforce: Plan the transition package before layoffs: internal moves, training, income support.
🔁
Strategy: Aim above individual productivity toward workflow and business-model redesign.
