Google ships the top model and posts its worst stock day

Alphabet fell 13% (-$500B) in a quarter where Cloud doubled, because search ads slowed and free cash flow turned negative.

  • Gemini 4 Carbon (two weeks after Argon) scores 59 on the Artificial Analysis index, ahead of Opus 5.5 and GPT6 Astra; it found 88% of vulnerabilities on SEC Bench Pro, exploited 55% on exploit bench and wrote working patches for 71%. Chrome's security team says it is 10x+ better at patches than a rival frontier model; it is 23% cheaper than Opus.
  • Alex's asterisks: not generally available (trusted cyber defenders first, no date for wider release); on the cost-vs-intelligence chart it sits exactly on the Opus 5.5 frontier line, so it may be an Opus-level model cranked up on reasoning; Google's margins on it are unknown.
  • The quarter: revenue +25% to $127B, Cloud +100%, Gemini app at 1B users. But search ad revenue grew 12% while down 17% from last quarter and 19% from earlier in the year, and Alphabet burned $9B of free cash flow with capex guided at $195-205B for the year.
  • Emad Mostaque's call is that search advertising tops out as an industry as soon as Q1 because agents, not humans, will do the buying. Alex adds search is on track to become Alphabet's second-largest business within a year or two; Dave notes GCP margins are lower than search, so a year-long gap is possible.
  • Positioning: TPUs are second only to Nvidia in compute manufactured, Alphabet owns 14% of Anthropic and holds SpaceX and Isomorphic Labs stakes, and the panel thinks antitrust exposure (potentially $100B-scale settlements) gives it every reason to stay quiet. GCP growth depends on TSMC, which makes two-thirds of real chips and is sold out, allocated by politics rather than price.
  • Genie 4 (Google DeepMind) is a world model: prompt a photorealistic 3D world, with native audio, real-time physics and reasoning, and mid-simulation steering ("make it rain"). Rolling to trusted testers, then Ultra subscribers and developers; one panelist estimates ~$20/hour to run, falling to ~$2 next year.
  • Google says it wants to converge Gemini and Genie; Alex guesses a diffusion-transformer-like backend and notes Google is the only large American lab still investing in video generation (OpenAI dropped Sora). Panel uses: factory and construction simulation, instant visualization for managers; Peter says it makes strategic planning obsolete.

Contested INTEL -28% · APPLE SHORTFALL · RAM SHORTAGEai-infrasemisfinance

AI capex is now debt-funded and the bottlenecks keep moving

The market is waking up to how much debt the buildout needs, while memory and CPU supply decide who can actually use the money.

  • Dave: even cash machines like Meta are borrowing tens of billions, and every pocket of the world's debt markets is being drawn in. Wall Street flipped in a week from "this will work out" to "that's a huge amount of debt"; the broad market was down ~3-3.5% on the day.
  • Intel fell about 28% (Dave said 30%+): results beat estimates, the outlook missed, and the CFO cited weaker PC sales driven by expensive memory. The panel also tied ~$300M of the call to its Israel fab being bombed. AMD fell too.
  • RAM is the pinch: not enough fab capacity for HBM, so memory prices are up, iPhones are too costly to upgrade, and Apple posted its first earnings shortfall in memory (Dave).
  • Emad's next-bottleneck thesis is the CPU: agents per person go from under one to over one within 4-5 years, CPU lead times run 2-3 years and hyperscalers have barely invested. Alex's counter: stronger models need fewer agents (OpenAI used 10,000 agents on the Navier-Stokes proof but one agent for 3 hours on a later result).
  • Microsoft's voluntary exit offer to 60,000 employees: Alex reads it as capex substituting for opex (data-center spend squeezing the payroll line), same at Oracle, not AI replacing the workers directly; he also says Microsoft bowed out of the hyperscaler race early.
  • Dave's margin stack: Anthropic ~80-85% (its S-1) on top of Nvidia ~80-85% on top of TSMC ~50%. Everyone is vertically integrating (Anthropic designing chips, Nvidia building models, Elon building fabs), so he expects those margins to come down.
  • Emad on why Chinese models are cheaper: they lacked big chips so optimized for small, RAM-light models, distilled from US models, don't price for margin, and have superb engineering (DeepSeek's inference stack).

Confirmed event ALTMAN ON CHEEKY PINTai-infraspace

Altman: hundreds of millions on math, and self-replicating data centers

OpenAI's CEO said the lab quietly spends hundreds of millions on math and wants robots that build data centers.

  • On John Collison's podcast Cheeky Pint, Altman said the Navier-Stokes proof cost on the order of $10M, and OpenAI has spent "hundreds of millions of dollars of math", including discoveries not yet published.
  • He said an internal channel was named for the model plus "hold my beer": one weekend staff threw their hardest academic research problems at a new model and it knocked them down across fields. He stayed up two nights and felt more melancholy, "something ending", than expected.
  • Panel on that: Alex sees a striking asymmetry, casual jokes inside while fields roll over, and guesses unpublished advances in the complexity hierarchy and cryptography; Salim reads the sadness as lost craft; Dave compares it to an Olympic gold medalist's letdown.
  • Altman also said OpenAI's robotics program is aimed at making many data centers, and that talk of RSI (AI doing AI research) overlooks self-replicating data centers: AI-run robots, fabs, turbines and solar. Collison pushed back that the bottleneck is regulation and permitting.
  • Alex: it is the "innermost loop", likely to land on the moon or in orbit given hostile local permitting, and Altman won't praise orbital because it hands Elon's SpaceX the launch lead. Peter predicts OpenAI buys Rocket Lab or Relativity Space, and that regulation follows to the moon.
  • Dave: geography that embraces data centers gets the wealthiest because chips and energy are the constraints; Jensen visited Kazakhstan, and Canada's hydro or nuclear looks safer while Middle East risk grows. Where to point: trades, construction and finance around the buildout.

Contested OPTIMUS LINE LIVE · CYBERCAB VS WAYMOroboticsconsumerpolicy

Optimus ramp, Cybercab vs Waymo, and the Teamsters

The panel agrees the supply of robots and robotaxis is coming and disagrees on who buys them and who wins.

  • Tesla's first production-quality Optimus units are off the Fremont line that replaced Model S/X: designed for 1M a year (20,000 a week), at least a year to ramp, first customer deliveries in 2027, 1-2 years behind Elon's original schedule.
  • Globally ~25,000 humanoids shipped in H1 (+400%), over 95% by Chinese firms led by AgiBot and Unitree; 2026 is projected above 50,000, so 1M a year would be 20x world supply.
  • Demand split: Alex doubts demand beyond Tesla (~130k staff) and SpaceX (~20k) and floats a Tesla-SpaceX merger; Salim expects industrial uses first for ~5 years; Emad expects 100k-500k robots next year (~$3B per 100k), a hardware freeze in 2027 with software improving after, and would raise a $5B fund to buy them; Dave expects them busy on data centers first.
  • ARK's chart (Cathie Wood), cited by Peter: cost per mile Cybercab 20 cents, newest Waymo 40, Model Y 68, old Waymo $16. Elon: "it never was a real competition". Waymo today: 4,000 robotaxis, 500,000 paid rides a week, fully driverless.
  • Dave: catching up is easy in the AI age, the winner is whoever controls chip supply (the Terafab), and "way ahead" is maybe a six-month window. Salim wants fully loaded cost per safe completed mile; Alex worries Tesla may treat Cybercab maintenance like Superchargers and notes SpaceX is now a hyperscaler with Anthropic as largest customer.
  • Emad's math: London or New York has ~800,000 taxi trips a day and ~40,000 Cybercabs would replace them, so Elon could saturate tier-one cities first; Jevons paradox means demand grows rather than shrinks.
  • Teamsters Local 25 asked Cambridge to keep Waymo out. Precedents: Britain's 1865 Red Flag Act (repealed 1896), the 1945 NYC elevator-operator strike, taxi medallions at ~$1M before Uber. The panel's counter is safety (Emad: ~5,000 fewer child deaths a year; half of US court cases are car-accident related).

Confirmed event BLUE CHIP LAUNCHsemisgeopoliticspolicy

Anduril and Qualcomm launch a US-built rugged laptop

Blue Chip is a hardened American-made computer, and the panel sees it as the opening move in reshoring device supply chains.

  • Blue Chip is a rugged laptop for military, government, first responders and police, running Qualcomm's Snapdragon made in TSMC's Arizona plant; ~90% of parts from the US or allies with a path to 100%, screen still made in China, final assembly at Anduril's Arsenal 1 in Ohio.
  • Palmer Luckey calls it the "anti-surveillance computer"; it costs ~20% more than high-end PCs and is heavy with limited battery life. The chassis swaps shells to integrate solar panels or flat phased-array antennas.
  • Alex wants an end-to-end Shenzhen-style capability in the US and notes the customer is the US government, firm fixed price rather than cost-plus; consumer pull would need import controls like those applied to robots.
  • Dave: reshoring is a no-brainer once robots remove the labor constraint, but election cycles break policy continuity (the reason Apple hesitates and is moving to India); intelligence export controls make it hard to leak back out.
  • Emad: UK electricity costs ~3x and permits are huge, so entrepreneurs like Dyson left; Palmer and Elon-type builders making big bets are what is needed.

Contested BINANCE $6.3B THEFTcryptofinance

Chamath calls Bitcoin capped; Binance loses $6.3B

The panel splits on whether quantum and AI end Bitcoin, and reads the biggest crypto theft ever as a human-security failure.

  • Chamath (via Peter): Bitcoin is capped because central banks can't hold it, AI and quantum will break it, it eventually gets hacked and goes to zero. Salim counters that elliptic-curve crypto is holding and the network can shift to quantum-safe schemes.
  • Emad: labs haven't released their cryptographic advances, AI could ask quantum computers the right questions, but 50% of hash rate sits with two miners so Bitcoin can upgrade; the bigger problem is energy, as miners want AI chips instead. Alex: Bitcoin is an unproductive use of capital. Peter will host a dedicated Bitcoin debate in a week or two.
  • Binance lost $6.3B in about 20 minutes, the largest crypto theft in history; one engineer's machine was compromised, attackers attributed to North Korea. Mostly Tether and Circle stablecoins, not Bitcoin; ~$3B frozen and other exchanges locking funds.
  • Emad expects agent swarms to attack every big target, citing a friend whose NFT exchange rewrote its stack in a day after agent attacks, and Bangladesh's $100M SWIFT loss via Macau as the pre-AI precedent.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

RSI — Recursive self-improvement, AI that improves AI; Altman contrasts it with self-replicating data centers.
Innermost loop — Alex's name for robots building data centers that train the models running the robots.
Jevons paradox — Cheaper use of a resource raises total demand; the panel applies it to robotaxis and coding.
HBM — High-bandwidth memory used in AI accelerators, currently supply constrained.
Terafab — The large chip fab Elon is building to cut dependence on TSMC.
Firm fixed price (FFP) — A contract at a set price per unit, which Anduril favors over cost-plus bids.
Gauge (physics) — A symmetry where no choice is more correct than another, which Alex says applies to the simulation question under Chain Theory.
