Video Snapshot

  2. The panel's answer to 'what is left for startups?': formalize messy domains, own proprietary vertical data, and run physical-world loops (wet labs, robots, sensors) that labs won't prioritize.

AI math breakthroughs push investors toward formalization, wet labs and physical AI

The panel treats math as the first verifiable proof that AI can invent, and invests where results must still be formalized, tested or manufactured.

  • Deedy Das's framing: of seven Millennium Prize problems, one was solved 20+ years ago; OpenAI's new result plus its earlier Navier-Stokes work means progress on four of the six remaining.
  • Math is first because it is easy to verify; the extrapolation is to chemistry, physics and biology, making invention itself cheaper.
  • Kanu Gulati's bets formalize messy domains so AI can build proofs on proofs: one startup targeting taxes, another (Logos Research, from Imperial College) targeting financial code.
  • Biology angle: capture failed experiments too, not just clinical-trial successes, so models learn from them; breakthroughs on the brain, cancers and 'planetary health' are expected.
  • Limits: an AI-designed cancer therapy still needs safe-in-humans proof, and a better battery chemistry still needs manufacturing and testing, which is why physical AI is attracting money.
  • One panelist: a molecule costing $50M today may cost thousands in a few years, shifting the constraint to lab capacity, trials, manufacturing and patents.

Can startups beat labs with 5,000 GPUs? Context, vertical data and new architectures

Deedy asked how startups compete when labs commit billions to compute; the panel's answer is that intelligence is an input and context, permissions and proprietary data are the product.

  • Deedy's challenge: labs run hundreds of thousands of GPUs against a startup's 5,000-10,000, and get the best revenue per incremental compute dollar.
  • Panelist: frontier-deployed engineers exist because enterprises need change management, guard rails and trust; one fund flipped from ~80% horizontal SaaS to ~80% vertical AI for data density.
  • Examples: a personal-injury-law AI player seen as dominant because 99% of its negotiation data is private, and a clinical-grade wearable with personal bio context.
  • Kanu: models are very inefficient in training data, training compute and serving; 1M-token context may become 100M, and lack of HBM is already driving open-lab innovation. Windborne's in-situ atmospheric data is her physical-world flywheel example.
  • She does not believe scaling or transformers alone reach AGI; compute constraints likely bind for the next 2-3 years, not 5-7.
  • Enterprise shift cited from Vercel data: proprietary to open-weight models went from roughly 80/20 to 20/80 in a matter of months.

RL-environment and data companies: explosive revenue, unclear terminal value

A tweet says the fastest company to $1B run-rate revenue is a data company, and the panel splits on whether those businesses last.

  • Deedy cited Gokul's tweet and noted most such revenue is non-recurring, margins vary, and investors disagree on whether to touch the category.
  • For: a panelist's fund backs Micro1 and sees the work moving from labs to enterprises as open-weight adoption rises; expects some demand to 'come back down' as fast as it rose.
  • Against: Kanu says labs eventually don't need the data, open-weight models can distill from top labs, so her fund has avoided the category.
  • Shiel Mohnot: look at gross profit, not headline revenue; customer concentration, structural vs temporary demand and little proprietary IP are open questions, but little fixed capital is needed.
  • Everyone agrees founder quality decides these deals; Shiel hasn't invested in any.

Personal agents: big category, but a $10B entry price faces incumbents

The panel thinks personal agents could be a major interface, but many doubt a startup beats Meta, Google, OpenAI or Apple on distribution, trust and monetization.

  • A $10B-valued consumer-agent startup (captions render its name 'Instinct'; not identified here) and Meta's 'Muse' agent drew questions from hedge-fund friends of Deedy: why fund it for $10B when it doesn't make money?
  • Bear case: only so many entry points into private data (Gmail holds SSNs, taxes); Facebook has ~3.5B users and Google ~4B, plus ads, wallets and commercial networks; startups would also need ads, hardware and payments.
  • Bull case: hundreds of millions of daily users plausible; one user got a €600 flight-delay refund handled end-to-end, paid a medical bill, and split bachelor-party costs instead of Splitwise; the winner needs only to be a top consumer interface.
  • Fatigue and security: Hark launched the day before, Sesame has a product, and another panelist reports agent fatigue; an agent reportedly posted bank details in a company Slack.
  • Kanu has avoided sharing card or SSN data; she wants an agent for phone calls (booking a child's party) and notes Rabbit, her early bet, 'promised too much' but described this world.
  • Fintech view: frictionless agents let users move debt or bank deposits quickly, pressuring rates and fee-based business models; a viral post imagined an agent-driven bank run.
  • Agents search differently: they visit hundreds of links where humans read one or two pages, so sites and businesses must be rebuilt for agent traffic.

New founder class and the squeeze on top-down SaaS

Small teams running agents are changing what a good founder looks like, while seat-based SaaS loses growth physics and switching costs.

  • Deedy: teams of five run thousands of agents; panel agrees vision, selling and recruiting are evergreen, but a young cohort scales in accounting and legal without domain backgrounds.
  • Kanu: a chip-design startup plans a chip with ~20 people; 'AI-pilled' bets on frontier-model capability are an important axis, at any age.
  • One investor sees the median getting slightly worse and the extremes much stronger, e.g. a 19-year-old running ~50 agents to find website bugs and collect bounties.
  • Fund approaches: back deep domain experts and also 'heat-seeking missile' hackers; cloud-era cheapness is repeating with AI tools.
  • SaaS: traditional growth is 3x, 3x, 2x, 2x, 2x (per GC's Hemant, via Deedy); consumption-priced AI grows faster and sales talent leaves.
  • AI moves vendors from tech budgets to the labor layer, an order of magnitude bigger, so vertical beats horizontal; a panelist calls some SaaS 'cigar butt' businesses as seat pricing shrinks and agents handle migration.
  • Bending Spoons buys SaaS names (Airtable cited), cutting costs and raising prices; Figma is cited as still delivering strong numbers; compression also reflects ending zero-rate multiples.

“Switching costs have gone away. It's just so easy to switch from one product to another because AI can do the switching for you.”— Panelist

Funds backing rivals: Factory vs Cognition and the AI co-opetition web

A public founder spat reopened whether VCs should fund competitors; the panel says pivots make overlap inevitable and Chinese walls manage it.

  • Spat: a Factory board-related incident led to posts from Factory's CEO Matan and Cognition's founder Scott, and Vinod Khosla calling Factory second-tier despite Khosla being a Factory investor.
  • Menlo invested in Factory that same week; Deedy says that decision predated the news and that both companies are great.
  • Kanu: multi-stage funds now often hold rivals; her firm uses a Chinese wall between partners; Shiel says his fund doesn't at the outset but has had portfolio pivots into rivalry.
  • Examples: Microsoft and OpenAI, Nvidia selling to labs while competing on open-weight models, clouds competing on chips.
  • A model-agnostic assistant product (name garbled) will use whichever frontier model is best, and xAI's data-center capacity was leased in a tens-of-billions deal to Anthropic.

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🧪
AI science: Look for startups that formalize messy domains or close the loop with wet-lab and robot experiments.
🗂
AI infra: Test any vertical AI pitch for proprietary data that labs can't see; that is the defensibility the panel cites.
💸
Venture: Judge RL-data companies on gross profit and customer concentration, not headline revenue.
🤖
Consumer AI: Treat personal-agent startups skeptically against incumbent distribution, ads and payments unless they own a narrow domain.
📉
Software: Reprice seat-based SaaS for shrinking seats and zero switching cost; favor labor-layer AI.
🛡
Venture: If funding rivals, set Chinese walls and tell founders upfront.
