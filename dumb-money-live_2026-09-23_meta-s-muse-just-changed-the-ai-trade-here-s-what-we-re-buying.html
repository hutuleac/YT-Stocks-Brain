Muse is the first AI agent ordinary people actually use

Hosts see Muse as the ChatGPT-launch moment for agents, driven by Meta's distribution rather than a technical breakthrough.

  • Use cases from the hosts: preparing a show, tracking a flight, fixing calendar and Apple TV issues, monitoring a condo group chat in Mexico, and flagging six duplicate credit-card charges from one restaurant.
  • One host has Muse triage all DMs every 15 minutes, scoring media and podcast offers by real audience and replying on their behalf.
  • A non-technical friend saved about $50/month on an AT&T bill within minutes, cancelled subscriptions and found card issues within an hour.
  • Meta currently gives users about $60/day of free tokens; estimates cited put compute cost at $30-60 per user per day, using segmented VMs that spin up on demand rather than always-on.
  • Positioned against Claude, which hosts hear is starting to limit tokens: Claude serves a handful of AI geeks, Muse reaches everyone with no thought required.
  • Muse launched two weeks earlier but only started to matter last Thursday-Friday when podcasts and press picked it up; the Muse website looked like a wall of text, while the app, pushed via Facebook/Messenger, overtook ChatGPT in the App Store and stayed #1.
  • Word-of-mouth in group chats (tables booked, bills cut) is the marketing engine; Threads stayed top 5-10 in iOS downloads for 1.5-2 years purely on Meta promotion.

Amazon, not Meta, is the second-order Muse trade

Chris argues Amazon's Graviton CPUs and Bedrock/AWS capacity power Muse, so every Muse conversation is a check written to Amazon.

  • In April, Amazon signed a deal with Meta for the VMs and processors underpinning Muse — valued in tens of millions of Graviton cores, not dollars (a mid-show correction).
  • Meta is already one of AWS's top five customers; Amazon's chip divisions are Trainium (AI accelerator) and Graviton (CPU line).
  • Meta could build its own chip line eventually, but that's a multi-year project — it can't flip a switch on data centers.
  • Chris sees a repeat of early this year, when the market missed how big Amazon's chip business was; the proof point is the next earnings call discussing Muse.
  • Amazon fell this week on fears of lost ad revenue and a possible rate hike raising AI-infra debt costs — Chris calls both non-issues.
  • Chris likes that the trade is one of the world's most liquid megacaps rather than a niche Korean infra name everyone has already found.
  • Secondary CPU plays: AMD and Intel; Qualcomm cited for a possibly more efficient approach Chris read about.

Amazon blocks Muse from shopping; ads for bots become the fight

Agents that don't watch ads threaten Amazon's ~$80B ad business, but hosts expect a paid Amazon-Meta deal rather than a lasting blockade.

  • Amazon blocked Muse from its commerce site; Shopify, Expedia and PayPal all announced support — hosts read the rapid partnership wave as pre-arranged.
  • Risk case: an $80B ad revenue line plus product-pushing power erodes if bots shop on price and ignore sponsored placements.
  • Chris calls the block temporary: Meta must monetize expensive compute, consumer subscriptions won't work (see how few pay $10/month for YouTube without ads), so Meta needs Amazon's revenue share.
  • Expect multi-year, massive deals between retailers and every major agent owner — Meta, Apple, Google.
  • Amazon's moat per hosts: 20 years of logistics, speed, delivery photos, returns at Whole Foods, Kohl's or UPS; Shopify is a supply-chain black box by comparison, even with the improved Shop app.
  • Worst case for Amazon is advertising, not shopping — they'll need a bot-targeted ad model; Chris thinks Amazon will pay Meta happily and fold it into its costs.

Opus 5.5 and the recurring 'efficiency is bearish' mistake

Chris says cheaper, better models expand use cases and compute demand, so selling infra on efficiency news is backwards.

  • Claude Opus 5.5 does animation, video and games and went viral in tech circles in under a day.
  • An article claimed Amazon fell because Opus 5.5 is 40% more efficient — Chris suspects algorithms auto-sell infra names on any AI efficiency headline.
  • AI video is the next compute sink: every model will do video, and video eats huge amounts of compute.
  • Jordan's long-standing concern is AI's real cost, but falling prices are a net gain; the host rejects the 'cost converges to electricity' view because chip depreciation, R&D and profit must be covered.
  • The market can't track more than one story at a time — Muse then Opus 5.5 within a week, with more coming in weeks.

Privacy tradeoff, and why Apple and Google are missing

Muse asks for Gmail and API keys aggressively; hosts split on trust but agree most people will trade privacy for usefulness.

  • One host's first Muse conversation was entirely about privacy: it cited a private VM and summarized the policy, then offered to skip Gmail — they connected a junk Gmail anyway.
  • Another host gave Zuckerberg more data in 5 days than in 5 years on Facebook; others refuse to connect Gmail, and Muse accepts API keys it says it uses without seeing.
  • Full Gmail access with no topic scoping feels aggressive; the 'mention something, get a Facebook ad 12 hours later' phenomenon fuels doubt.
  • Counterpoint: even if 20% refuse on privacy grounds, 80% will sign up — people always end up doing it.
  • A host wishes Apple ran this on-device via Siri (new Siri is good but can't audit card transactions or book a noon-3pm trip to New York); another says Apple leaks show no such work.
  • Google has 'literally zero answers' to GPT/Claude agent tooling so far, despite having the reach.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Muse — Meta's consumer AI agent app with a plush mascot that runs tasks in a per-user virtual machine.
Graviton — Amazon's in-house Arm-based server CPU line.
Trainium — Amazon's in-house AI training chip.
Amazon Bedrock — AWS's managed service for hosting and running AI models.
Agentic AI — AI that takes multi-step actions on a user's behalf, not just answers questions.
Bot-to-bot advertising — Hosts' term for ad models aimed at AI agents rather than human browsers.
