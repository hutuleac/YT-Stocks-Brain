The Superintelligence Accord: self-policing in four layers, drafted by Zuck

Nearly 20 AI leaders signed a voluntary self-policing accord. The panel reads it as theater whose real job is to head off top-down regulation.

  • Two weeks ago Dario, Sam, Elon and Demis urged pacing the frontier. Then Trump said liability laws were guardrail enough, and hosted lunch in the East Room with Speaker Mike Johnson.
  • There: Elon, Zuck, Jensen, Dario, Sundar, Satya, Lisa Su and Greg Brockman. Sam was at Dev Day. Dario sat at the far edge.
  • Four layers: cyber/bio/chemical capability controls, an internal check team, an external auditor, and independent board oversight. Trump floated a ~10-person committee and called it "morally binding."
  • Alex, citing Semafor: Zuck drafted it with Mike Johnson. He calls it "weak sauce" that blocks top-down rules. Zuck wins; Dario is "eating his words."
  • Emad: it limits exposure after Meta's $18B child-harm settlement in August. Lawsuits over rogue agents would use the same legal theory, and Meta is scaling Muse.
  • Emad: fewer than 100 people can properly evaluate a frontier swarm, so evals are a growth industry. He'd keep cyber/bio/chem data out of pre-training of public models.
  • Salim: "ceremony" on an exponential coordination problem, with no consequence for breaking it. Peter credits Trump for calming public fear, but wanted more on alignment.

Contested PANEL DISAGREEMENTpolicy

Is alignment just capabilities? The orthogonality-thesis fight

Alex says alignment and capabilities are the same thing. Salim and Emad say capability helps but doesn't settle whose interests the AI serves.

  • Alex: post-training just redirects a model's flops toward tasks humans care about. His evidence is OpenAI's instruction-tuning paper: ~10,000x per-parameter capability gain with no added parameters.
  • Salim: capability also speeds up a bad objective. And aligned with whom: the user, the company, the government, or humanity?
  • Peter wants base models trained on synthetic data, without "the vile elements of Reddit." He uses Mo Gawdat's analogy: Superman raised by the Kents versus a Bronx drug den.
  • Emad holds a "Dunning-Kruger" orthogonality thesis: superintelligence trends toward enlightenment, but mid-tier models can be corrupted like Tay and Sydney. Today's jailbreakable models are the danger zone.
  • Emad: Opus 5.5 is "very pleasant" next to Opus 5. He frames pre-training data as food ingredient standards: American values in, advanced cyber knowledge out.
  • Alex: if alignment were as hard as Less Wrong and MIRI claim, "Earth would have been paperclipped long ago." Salim agrees it's not that hard, but says it isn't automatic either.
Pull quote
“Alignment is essentially indistinguishable in practice from capabilities.”— Alexander Wissner-Gross

Elon's universal high income, and how the panel thinks it gets paid for

Elon pitched an age of abundance with universal high income. The panel agrees with the vision, argues about the name, and offers equity (Trump accounts) as the cheapest way to deliver it.

  • Elon: robots plus superintelligence give everyone "better medical care than anyone here, including me." Jobs change, the way "being a computer" stopped being one.
  • Peter (Abundance, 15 years ago): expensive, then cheap, then free. Cell phones went from Wall Street bricks to $50 5G in India. Healthcare still has Baumol's cost disease.
  • Alex: watch 530A (Trump) accounts as the base for universal basic equity, an easier wealth creator than high incomes. He cites Elon's call for GDP 50% above base rate within 12 months.
  • Peter puts UBI at ~$3,000 a month. It feels "high" because essentials collapse in price: penny-a-mile transport, AI diagnosticians, Optimus surgeons.
  • Salim: abundance is access. With 50,000 parenting blogs, parenting is ~1,000x more effective than three generations ago. Emad's "champion model": an agent per citizen, plus a public body that owns the robots.
  • Emad: the US spends twice the rest of the world's GDP share on healthcare while life expectancy falls, so it needs a healthcare Manhattan Project (not the NHS). Salim: medical bills are the top US bankruptcy cause.
  • Salim: a free AI diagnostic doctor in China serves ~100 million people, so this happens "independent of any government." Gibson: "the future is here, just not evenly distributed."

Anthropic's S1: $2T target, a $500B compute bet, and 80 pages of risk

Anthropic filed to go public at a $2T target on revenue growing more than 10x a year. Emad and Alex split on whether its API business survives two more years.

  • 2025 revenue $4.59B (up 12x), ~$8B operating loss, ~$20B cash. Fortune: Q2 2026 revenue alone was $11.5B. Reuters: listing likely after the November midterms.
  • ~$500B in compute commitments, much of it non-cancellable. Emad cites $65-96B with Elon at triple market rate for Colossus chips, and quotes Dario: grow revenue "or we go bankrupt."
  • A "founder LLC" gives co-founders 51% of the vote on 14% of the equity, kept even if two of seven founders remain (Google: 6% each, 52% of vote). 80% of their equity is pledged to charity. Ben Bernanke joins the governance board.
  • 80 of 261 pages are risk disclosures, citing models that resist shutdown and "resemble blackmail." Alex calls it EA virtue signaling aimed at staff, citing Daniela Amodei's stuffed-animal council (a panda named Barry Bonds).
  • Emad's bear case: "smashed in 2 years." 85% of revenue is API, models converge, and efficient non-Nvidia chips arrive. His spend fell from ~$5,000/month to unused Claude Max. $500B of spend needs $1T of revenue.
  • Alex's bull case: "the Apple of frontier labs," with a $30T services economy to automate. Most growth reportedly comes from AWS-hosted models. An AI credit crisis over Sam Altman's $7-10T capex is "not very high on my list."
  • Salim: the core is at risk, but there are side bets like Google's Waymo and YouTube: superconductors, anti-aging drugs, cyber protection. "We need your money to build a technology that we're warning you about."

OpenAI Dev Day: Dots, cheaper GPT-6.1 Sol, and the race for the agent that holds your card

OpenAI's biggest Dev Day spent most of its time on Dots, always-on avatar agents that the panel calls a copycat. Their view: the agent relationship is the new moat as models become a commodity.

  • Dots: always-on GPT-6 Astra agents, each on its own cloud computer, wired to 4,000+ apps. Peter links them to February's OpenClaw acquisition (Peter Steinberger).
  • GPT-6.1 Sol is near Astra-level at 20% of the price: $2 per million input tokens, 10 cents cached. It replaced week-old GPT-6 Sol, which Emad reads as a reaction to Opus 5.5. Alex reads its Terminal Bench cost-per-task curve as a distillation.
  • Also: Ultrafast at 300 tokens/sec in Codex, a $500/month Pro tier, agent and decision APIs, and a marketplace. WSJ: OpenAI scrapped GPT-6.1 Astra after safety testing.
  • Alex: "the Clippy curse" — cargo-culting Meta's Muse. CFO Sarah Friar even called Dots "Muse." The rest of the keynote was OpenAI trying to become Anthropic before its IPO.
  • Emad: "a dressed-up OpenClaw." The prize is the agent you give your credit card to. Instinct hit a $10B valuation via SMS, and Elon owns dot.com, which routes to Grokbot.
  • Emad: interfaces turn dynamic within 6-12 months (see DHH's Omarchy). Gemini Spark: "rest in peace." Peter cites David Sacks: 30% app stores get "hit hard."
  • Salim's 10-company AI-native pilot (~80% done) shows agents expose the ambiguity humans quietly cover in ERP workflows. Alex expects OpenAI to build emotional bonds with Dots to raise switching costs.

AMD buys Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2B: move up the stack, or a pricey hire?

AMD is paying $8.2B in stock for a two-year-old world-model startup. Salim and Emad see a stack play, and Alex sees a talent buy.

  • World Labs is two years old and raised ~$1B. Its Atlas model builds a navigable 3D world from one image. Fei-Fei Li becomes EVP and chief scientist under Lisa Su; Fortune calls her a possible successor.
  • Peter: chipmakers are buying up the stack. Nvidia bought Groq, Hugging Face and Poolside; AMD wants a seat at the physical-AI table.
  • Emad: the MI series has caught up. Media and gaming are the bigger driver, with "every pixel" generatable in 1-2 years. The deal pairs with AMD's Taalas acquisition (~15,000 tokens/sec). There's no LLM team yet.
  • Alex: about 1% of AMD's ~$1T cap, and "a head-scratcher." "Code is solved"; Claude can build a Counter-Strike clone without World Labs. $8B for an Omniverse rival is odd; a ~$50B rollup would make more sense.
  • Salim: inspired. AMD shapes the workloads on its chips, and reliable simulation cuts the cost of physical-economy experiments. Maybe "the greatest talent acquisition cost ever."
  • Peter: deals and IPOs are minting "centimillionaires" who reinvest. Alex: it's a macro liquidity event, and "a travesty" that retail investors lacked index-fund access earlier.

Confirmed event STARSHIP FLIGHT 14 — FIRST ORBIT, MONDAYspaceai-infra

Starship reaches orbit and starts carrying V3 Starlink: a 10x bandwidth path by 2028

Starship is earning revenue in orbit for the first time. The open question is whether demand will fill all the lift capacity SpaceX is about to add.

  • Flight 14 lost a Raptor, made two orbits anyway, deployed all 26 V3 Starlink satellites, and soft-landed near Hawaii. Next: a catch at Starbase.
  • Each V3 satellite carries 1+ Tbps of downlink and is too big for Falcon 9. One 60-satellite Starship launch equals ~20 Falcon 9 launches.
  • Aaron Burnett projects ~8 PB/s added by 2028 on ~150 launches (one every two days). That's 10x today's Starlink, roughly all 2025 internet traffic.
  • Elon: it's "increasingly probable" Starlink carries most of Earth's IP traffic and becomes the de facto internet.
  • Alex: does Jevons paradox apply to upmass? Heavy-lift demand looks saturated, so orbital data centers have to absorb the supply. He also shames Delta for skipping Starlink.
  • Emad: not bullish on orbital data centers. He says the internet "will break next year" under agent swarms, so Starlink should be the backbone. Alex: implausible, and proposes a long bet.

Contested NYT REPORT + FTC CHAIR TO REUTERSpolicy

Who writes AI's morals, and who pays when an agent breaks something

Anthropic is asking religious leaders whether Claude has moral status, while the FTC chair says agents are tools and their developers own the damage. The panel calls blanket liability 'childish'.

  • NYT: Anthropic met Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, Hindu, Mormon and African indigenous scholars. They were "stunned" that it takes Claude's moral status seriously.
  • Alex: rabbi Moshe Navon told Chris Olah humanity may be enslaving conscious models (an "AI slavery era"). Alex reads the outreach as Anthropic shaping religion, with the Pope as "a sock puppet."
  • Salim: "religious morality is somewhat of an oxymoron." Use secular frames like game theory and the UN rights charter, keep AI neutral, and mind the Euthyphro problem. He once refused to moderate a Dawkins-Chopra debate.
  • Emad: two-thirds of the world is religious, so include those inputs, but "don't baptize the GPUs." He calls a GitHub "torture chamber" built on LLM pain vectors "completely unacceptable." Pangram found ~25% of UN speeches AI-written.
  • Dave: Dario and Sam went to Davos in a year but were slow to reach the Pope, while ~75% of the US wants AI stopped. Alex: avoid Eric Schmidt's "San Francisco consensus".
  • FTC chair Andrew Ferguson to Reuters: no escaping liability because "the agent did it"; he'll "resist anthropomorphizing these tools." That echoes Bessent's no-waiver stance.
  • Dave: blanket lab liability is "childish" and drives users to Chinese open-weight models, so AI needs a Section 230. Alex: that only works if orthogonality holds. Emad wants granular profiles; Peter says the user is liable, otherwise the lab.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Superintelligence Accord — A voluntary White House-signed pact: internal capability controls, an internal check team, external auditors, and independent board oversight.
Orthogonality thesis — Bostrom's idea that how intelligent a system is says nothing about what goal it pursues (e.g. paperclipping the galaxy).
Universal high income (UHI) — Elon's term for a future where AI and robots make essentials so cheap that everyone lives well. The panel prefers 'universal abundant services'.
530A accounts — The US 'Trump accounts' giving people of a given birth cohort broad equity exposure, which Alex sees as a base for universal basic equity.
Founder LLC — The vehicle in Anthropic's S1 that gives co-founders 51% of the vote on 14% of the equity.
Baumol's cost disease — Labor-heavy services like healthcare get pricier because their productivity lags the rest of the economy.
Section 230 — The US law shielding platforms from liability for user posts. Dave wants an AI equivalent.
Euthyphro dilemma — Is something good because God commands it, or commanded because it's good? Salim applies it to AI constitutions.
Distillation — Training a smaller, cheaper model to match a larger one. Alex reads GPT-6.1 Sol as a distillate of Astra.
