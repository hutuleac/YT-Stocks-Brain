Video Snapshot

  1. Weekly speed-round answering Ray Dalio's "AI bubble may burst": the bubble is in rate-dependent zombie companies, not the digital economy; position long AI, short the rest.

High conviction REBUTTING RAY DALIO'S BUBBLE CALLmacro-ratesfinance

The bubble is in zombies that need zero rates, not in AI

Higher rates are exposing old-economy companies that survived on cheap debt; that is the bubble, and it is unwinding without dragging the AI economy down.

  • Ray Dalio said the AI bubble "may burst" on rising rates; Visser counts many Dalio bubble calls since 2012 and calls it an endgame bias shared by perma-bears who are "selling something".
  • Three bubble types: speculative (dot-com Cisco at 100 P/E vs Nvidia at ~15), capacity (supply ahead of demand) and credit (housing); none shows up, and much AI debt is asset-backed against compute.
  • His AI risk scorecard (an LLM fed his weekly data, scoring on its own): demand strong, compute still scarce, capital on watch list as funding needs push yields up, valuation fine on PEG.
  • "400 stocks go down and 100 go higher" because zombies kept alive by low rates are losing; a Jason Furman chart is "long AI short the rest of the world"; borrowers have termed out debt and liquidity is ample.
  • Credit: CCCs weakening vs high yield but BBBs are not ("getting rid of the weak"); the Morgan Stanley leverage factor is falling with the S&P at highs; he worries only when hyperscalers fall with it. Johnson Redbook consumer spending still fine.
  • Drawdowns since the iPhone recover fast: ~40% (COVID), ~30% (2022), 20% (tariffs). Longest gaps without a new high since Dec 1969: 1973 (7 years), 2000 (back ~2007), 2007 (back 2013), 2022 (only 500 days, fourth longest). A pandemic, the fastest hikes since the 1980s, the largest bank run, tariffs and $200-oil/Strait of Hormuz fears all failed to break it.
  • Origin story: stopped traveling to China in 2012-13, went to Silicon Valley to understand why Amazon could trade at "infinity P/E"; says barely 2% of people over 50 use AI, so the money-holders fear bubbles.

Signal EQUAL-WEIGHT MACD BUY FIRED THIS WEEKmacro-ratesfinance

Bad breadth is a buy signal in the digital economy

Weak breadth just means rate-sensitive zombies are oversold; when it washes out, equal-weight bounces, and this week's signal has a perfect record since 2007.

  • Share above the 200-day: utilities 18%, staples 33%, household durables 30%, autos 12% vs technology 73%, financials 74%; all the weak ones are rate-sensitive.
  • Equal-weight S&P (SPW) got an oversold MACD buy signal this week; since 2007 it was higher 6 months later 22 of 22 times (100%), 91% at 3 months, 90% at 12 months.
  • Breadth is "like a high VIX": on the day OpenAI headlines caused panic, equal weight rose as hedge funds covered shorts on the old economy.
  • S&P is up 14% this year and only energy and tech beat it; the RSI on breadth hit rare oversold levels last week.
  • Europe vs US since the iPhone (July 1, 2007) is the AI trade in reverse; the US is 56% technology; IWM vs QQQ is the same trade, so "broadening out" works only in brief bursts.
  • 10-year yield shows a triple RSI divergence against new price highs, looking "tired"; the MOVE index (bond vol) had an outside reversal week; even flat yields a year out are no reason to be bearish stocks.
  • The Economist's "Will bonds blow up?" cover lands the same week as Dalio; he reads both as contrarian reversal signs for bonds and rate-sensitive assets.

High conviction DEMAND > SUPPLY, BUYER IS AGENTSai-infra

Agents, not humans, are the demand side, so compute stays short

Every past bubble had humans on both sides; agents multiply far faster than nominal GDP and need tokens, so supply cannot catch up.

  • Agents: "thousands" now, billions next year, then trillions, each working ~5x human hours; efficiency gains as the answer are "hocus pocus".
  • Compute is the new oil: oil grows with nominal GDP because humans set demand; agents are not nominal GDP, they grow "faster than rabbits and cockroaches".
  • His run-rate model: $70B annualized OpenAI revenue by year-end and ~$100B for Anthropic; OpenAI has since logged "hundreds more math results" that got no press.
  • Aaron Levie: compute for personal agents, agent swarms and 24/7 background agents "is going to be insane", said in response to the hardware Meta's Muse needs for 100M users (of its 3B).
  • Instinct AI's founder: compute needs "orders of magnitude more" than thought; Cloudflare forecast (March 2026) AI bots overtake web traffic by 2027, then said it already happened.
  • Lumentum's CEO says it is sold out through 2029. A widely shared revenue-math paper: "The math is right. The future is completely wrong"; it was written before personal agents existed.
  • Exponential frame: Kurzweil's curve put one human brain ~2021 and all human brains (ASI) by 2050; Karpathy said on Dwarkesh agents were a decade away, a year before they arrived. Next year's innovation "equivalent to like a decade"; the S&P is an innovation index.
  • Don't trade the personal-agent race (Meta's Muse, Amazon's Alexa, Apple's Siri, Google): same TAM, extreme competition, unclear profits (discussed with Pomp); trade the compute instead.

Contrarian MULTIPLE COMPRESSION, NOT A SHORT CALLfinancecrypto

Bank apocalypse: Stripe is building the bank agents will use

Banks face a SaaS-style derating within ~9 months as consumer agents arrive, and Stripe's growth shows who takes the fast-growing customers.

  • Bloomberg wrote up his "bank apocalypse" video without asking him; clarification: banks won't collapse and he isn't shorting them, but a revenue "flinch" in the next nine months triggers SaaS-style repricing as consumer agents (Siri etc.) land next year.
  • Stripe built agent rails: 288 launches, agent wallets powered by Link, acquired Privy (wallets), Metronome (metered billing), Bridge (stablecoins) and now OpenRouter (token routing).
  • Stripe letter: "January 1st marked the beginning of the singularity"; internet economic infrastructure is mostly the same thing as AI's. His Substack "Stripe Sessions and the coming agentic commerce economy" (June 30, 2026).
  • H1: net revenue +41%, billing +71%, free cash flow +43%; Capital over 100,000 active loans; Connect platforms activating at 2x last year's rate; Treasury among fastest-growing products ever.
  • Citigroup 226,000 employees vs Stripe 10,000: $214B vs $160B valuations, ~15x per employee; Google 199,000 vs Anthropic 6,100, also ~15x.
  • SWIFT is too old and too hackable: bank to Robinhood took 5 days, Robinhood to Coinbase 5 seconds; agents need same-second settlement and metered micro-billing.
  • Bears' inconsistency: tokens get cheaper, yet agents will only pay through Mastercard and Visa. Thousands of businesses launch on Stripe every few hours; a ladder of AI apps ($2K, $10K, ten at $12K) lets people quit jobs and erodes slow incumbents ("Think Ford verse Tesla and humanoids").

Watching PROFITS UP, HIRING DOWNmacro-ratescareer

Strong investment cycle, weakening labor market

Earnings can outrun higher rates, but jobs are already rolling over, and he expects that to pressure bonds and wages next year.

  • Henry McVey (KKR, ex-Morgan Stanley): the global investment cycle is "broader, more durable, and probably more inflationary"; Europe stronger than expected, Asia strong, defense and infrastructure booming.
  • McVey: earnings strong enough to overcome higher rates; judge nominal GDP vs EPS vs rates, not rates alone. The conundrum: corporate profits rising rapidly while labor isn't.
  • Warren Pies: job losses are already here. Healthcare openings and jobs slowed over the last six months; without healthcare, negative payroll prints become regular.
  • Jobs plentiful vs hard to get is weakening; claims won't rise but "nobody's hiring", bad for wages; he expects jobs to pressure the bond market soon.
  • Agency advice: build an AI stack and side businesses, give kids the tools, and avoid big organizations that will "cap pay and use agents".

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Bank apocalypse — Visser's label, borrowed from the SaaS apocalypse, for a coming multiple compression in banks as agentic payments rails take growth.
MACD buy signal — Momentum crossover where the MACD line rises above its signal line, here from oversold levels on the equal-weight S&P.
Triple RSI divergence — Price makes three successive highs while RSI makes lower highs, a sign of fading momentum.
MOVE index — Measure of implied volatility in US Treasuries, the bond market's VIX.
PEG ratio — P/E divided by expected earnings growth, used to judge valuation against growth.
Tokenization — Representing stocks and other assets as on-chain tokens that can trade 24/7.
Zombie companies — Firms whose survival depends on cheap refinancing, exposed when rates rise.
