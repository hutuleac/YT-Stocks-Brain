Credo bought on a post-earnings dip while Vincent rotates out of Corning

Melvin sees Credo's deceleration as a buying window; Vincent keeps AI-infra exposure but rotates toward names with a nearer earnings spike.

  • Melvin bought Credo (CRDO) after earnings ~2-3 weeks earlier; the stock sold off on a deceleration even though it had been growing at triple digits.
  • Credo builds active electrical cables (advanced copper) that connect GPUs, memory and switches inside AI data centers faster and more reliably.
  • Growth guided from 115% this quarter to roughly 98% next quarter; investors wanted a bigger guidance raise.
  • Stock is ~$150 against an all-time high of roughly $300; the host notes Melvin bought at $107 in March and took no profits on the run to $300.
  • Vincent is adding no new capital to AI infrastructure; he is moving money out of Corning (GLW) into Bloom Energy, Micron and Infineon for a clearer path to the next earnings spike.
  • On Infineon, he says the market is fully selling the name without understanding power semiconductors in 800-volt DC data-center power.

Oracle and AppLovin framed as oversold names that should rerate

Both are pitched as punished too hard, with Oracle's debt as the one real rate sensitivity.

  • Oracle (ORCL) is the bet on OpenAI and is down ~50%; OCI, its cloud business, is what everyone cares about.
  • Melvin's risk flag: Oracle's very large debt makes it sensitive to rate hikes, but its latest quarter showed higher revenue and cloud growth.
  • Martin says AppLovin (APP), his worst performer, was sold off for missing Q2 by 1%, a huge overreaction in his view.
  • Management said the miss came from not updating the model quickly enough, and guided much higher for Q3.
  • Martin expects the FUD to clear once Q3 results land and the stock to rerate higher.

IREN and TeraWulf: access to power as the next AI bottleneck

Martin thinks energy access is going to matter again and that power-rich names are priced down too heavily.

  • Martin added IREN (IREN) after ERCOT approved 2 GW classified as conditional base load (subject to meeting conditions), on top of ~3 GW already confirmed, for 5 GW of confirmed access.
  • His logic: companies with energy access are priced down heavily, including Galaxy, his biggest position.
  • He is finishing the IREN thesis first to share on the platform, then will look at TeraWulf (WULF), a similar story.
  • The host's concern: IREN is B2B infrastructure yet sponsors the Golden State Warriors at what he believes is the highest amount ever for a basketball team, and also the All-In Summit; he finds it odd.
  • The host adds that IREN's price still moves with Bitcoin because mining is its largest revenue stream; TeraWulf was identified as no longer mining.

Contested CLARITY ACT LOOKS STALLEDcryptopolicy

If the Clarity Act fails: a dip in crypto equities, then regulators fill the gap

The host expects Bitcoin and its related equities to dump on a Clarity Act failure and wants to buy lower; Martin pushes back that it matters a lot.

  • The host says the Clarity Act (crypto market-structure regulation) looks unlikely to pass; hopes Bitcoin falls hard, with Galaxy, Robinhood, Coinbase and miners-turned-AI names following it down.
  • Reason it's not fatal: the CFTC and SEC are ready to release their own framework regardless, which he expects by the end of that week.
  • He cites Brian Armstrong as saying Coinbase will be fine without it, while Mike Novogratz really wants it; Martin counters that the Coinbase and Galaxy CEOs lobbying in Washington shows its importance.
  • He wanted to buy Galaxy (GLXY) at $26-27 the week before, held off, and it is now ~$22.80; he hopes for a lower entry.
  • Vincent would buy dips even on a no: Robinhood (HOOD) (one of his biggest convictions), Ethereum (ETH), no strong view on altcoins.
  • Vincent would not be surprised if John, deepest in crypto, ends the year or starts next year ranked number one as alts begin to outperform.

The application layer: Bending Spoons, Eli Lilly, Robinhood and the Palantir valuation fight

Vincent and the host argue the next leg is AI adopters and the application layer, not infrastructure; Palantir's valuation splits the room.

  • Vincent will deep-dive Bending Spoons (BSP) on YouTube that week; it IPO'd in May or June and the CEO calls it a 75% tech, 25% private-equity company.
  • Its playbook: buy companies and apply ~50 AI-enabled tools to cut costs and enhance products; barely anyone is discussing it on X.
  • Vincent: AI is at a level some call AGI, so it is now about adoption; his X chart shows AI-adopting companies building a performance wedge over non-adopters.
  • Examples he likes: Eli Lilly (LLY), Robinhood and Bending Spoons; the host notes a bank raised its Eli Lilly price target to $1,400 while it trades ~$1,100.
  • The host sold all Salesforce (CRM) after a ~60% gain, keeps a small ServiceNow (NOW) stake (bought near $100, now ~$144) and says software rises when infrastructure falls.
  • Palantir: Vincent says he ignores valuation (~$180-190, P/E 147); Martin loves the business but struggles at this price and is considering buying; the host says compare Tesla's P/E of ~300.
  • One panelist says Salesforce and ServiceNow can sell agents and become the orchestration layer for firms that cannot build their own.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Conditional base load — An ERCOT classification granting a large load access to power provided it meets stated conditions.
Clarity Act — A proposed US law to create a regulatory framework for crypto market structure.
Active electrical cable — A copper cable with built-in electronics that boosts signals between chips and switches in a data center.
800V DC — High-voltage direct-current power distribution used in AI data centers, where power semiconductors matter.
Orchestration layer — Software that coordinates multiple AI agents inside a company's workflows.
MRMI — Milk Road macro index: shows analyst cash allocations and market indicators.
