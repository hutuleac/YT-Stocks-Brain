A 38 GW power gap and an energy basket: Vertiv, GE Vernova, Constellation, Vistra

The panel's view is that electricity, not chips, is AI's binding constraint, and the cleanest way to play it is a basket of power names rather than one winner.

  • Earnings day split: Vertiv revenue +61%, orders more than doubled, stock +16%; GE Vernova $29B orders, $197B backlog and a guidance raise — and the stock went down.
  • Morgan Stanley: AI global power shortfall of 38 GW through 2028 — about 38 large nuclear reactors' worth.
  • Jordan likes Constellation and Vistra: long-term power purchase agreements with Meta, Amazon and Microsoft, plus government loans pushing nuclear restarts alongside legacy gas.
  • Jordan: Vistra guiding roughly 20-30% earnings growth for 2027 after ~40% this year — 'a good boring energy trade'.
  • GE Vernova is sold out to 2032 on turbines; Jordan trades in and out of it. Nobody has a view on Solaris.
  • Chris: hyperscalers locking up electricity for 20 years means 'someone is spectacularly wrong' — he sides with the contracts being real and plans to buy some of the 'boring' names for a basket.
  • Jordan on natural gas prices after weather events: don't try to get alpha predicting them.

Bloom Energy: Chris's 'biggest winner' of the AI power trade

Chris frames Bloom as a technology company at the start of monetizing a decade of R&D, so its high P/E and negative cash flow are the point, not the problem.

  • Bloom fell 20% in two days last week, then rebounded 18% in three — Chris has been 'loading up on even more'.
  • On-site fuel cells need only a gas pipeline — no turbine, lower noise, no grid draw — an easier sell at angry data-center town halls.
  • Knock: power costs a premium, but it gets cheaper every year; Chris sees it reaching parity with grid-equivalent cost plus faster ramp.
  • Dave (also long) worries about valuation and negative cash flow; Jordan owns it but flags it still runs on natural gas — Chris: gas 'is not going anywhere the next five years'.
  • Recurring short reports (the Hunterbrook scandium claim, roughly every nine months for three years) were refuted by the CEO and independent reports, per Chris.
  • Chris's exit trigger: large deployments failing to scale; hyperscalers take 'baby steps' because a Bloom failure would sink a whole data center, and one proven deal unlocks the rest.

The 'AI is fake' narrative and the misinformation trade

Chris argues the bubble crowd confuses circular financing and slow enterprise adoption with fake demand, and that uncorrected viral stories make short-term trading irrational.

  • Trigger: a viral Jon Stewart clip with Ed Zitron arguing AI demand is circular, fabricated revenue; Chris says the bear 'bar moves every 60 to 90 days'.
  • Chris: 'finance by its nature is circular' — the test is whether end-user value exceeds spend, and that isn't supposed to happen on day one.
  • CRM analogy: firms spent up to two years onboarding Salesforce and another year before staff used it right — AI adoption is 'a multi-decade journey'.
  • Jordan: companies use AI in silos that mirror today's org chart; restructuring around it will be slow but inevitable.
  • Jordan cites a TikTok stock creator whose bearish videos got ~10x the engagement of bullish ones.
  • Misinformation examples: Anthropic's '$80B loss' was a mark-up of notes issued to Amazon; Google took a non-cash write-down on its SpaceX stake this quarter — Chris: '95%' never see the correction.
  • Chris still takes AI doom seriously: a 'meaningful tail risk' it could kill us all.

Google's AI mode is succeeding so well it's eroding search economics

Chris's call from a year ago landed on last night's call: AI mode usage is dragging search revenue growth because it monetizes worse than classic search.

  • Google's earnings call showed AI mode usage negatively impacting search revenue growth — Chris: the story is AI succeeding spectacularly, not failing.
  • Chris shorted Google a year ago on 'faking' search growth via unmonetized AI queries, got roasted, and covered — Jordan: 'way too early'.
  • Search was historically ~85% of Google's profits; the bull case is TPUs plus cloud offsetting it — Chris would rather get that AI-infra exposure elsewhere.
  • Advertisers on X and Reddit say Google is squeezing every penny — Jordan points to a Max Anderson thread on exact-match keywords being broadened.
  • Chris: Google Trends data is now so polluted by AI queries it's 'essentially trash' for social-arbitrage research.
  • He'd change his view if Google monetizes AI mode as well as traditional search.

Amazon into earnings: agentic-shopping fears overblown, capex reaction is the risk

Chris holds a large levered Amazon long and would buy any capex-driven sell-off, but cut options live because he doesn't trust the market to read the print rationally.

  • Bear worry: shopping agents erode Amazon's high-growth ad business; Chris: 'many, many years' to play out and Amazon will run its own agents.
  • Amazon blocks bots to keep control of the customer — unauthorized purchases mean chargebacks, returns and fraud that must be solved first.
  • Viewer question on Elon praising Shopify and knocking Amazon for blocking GrokBot — Chris puts eventual Amazon bot partnerships at 'close to 100' percent.
  • Last capex guide: $220B for 2026; Chris thinks $300-350B for next year is possible and the market would 'smoke' it. Microsoft is down after its report too.
  • Other overhangs: diesel costs for the largest logistics operation, RAM costs hitting Alexa devices; the revamped Alexa lineup includes a ~$700 16-inch counter screen, but ad-heavy devices draw complaints.
  • Chris trimmed options mid-show — 'deleveraging live' — warning viewers not to be over-levered short term.

Reddit: AI is killing its traffic, but its data deals were sold for pennies

Chris thinks the market is fixated on falling search-driven traffic while Reddit's AI licensing contracts are set to reprice from tens of millions to potentially billions.

  • Reddit has been punished all year because Google AI mode and chatbots answer questions without sending visitors — 'does anyone click those little links? No.'
  • Licensing deals: Google's ~$60M (signed Feb 2024) and OpenAI's ~$70M (May 2024) — Chris calls them 'misnegotiated' and 'embarrassing'.
  • Chris expects renewals between Q2 and Q4 2027 to be far bigger and usage-variable, so Reddit gets paid as AI queries grow.
  • He calls Reddit possibly the most valuable acquisition target in the world for an AI company; Dave notes a rival's attempt to build a competitor would cost billions.
  • Dave: upvotes provide a validation layer, and AI can cross-check Reddit answers against other sources.
  • Chris is long (unlevered, no options) and 'would like to be levered' but can't time the leak; one co-host has a small underwater position, another has none.

Take-Two: the GTA 6 debate gave way to 'is there value in a game publisher in 2035?'

Chris cut most of his Take-Two stake once investors began pricing AI-democratized game development as an inevitability, not a near-term event.

  • Chris and Dave argue over 'it'll be a hit': GTA 6 is projected around 30M copies — Chris says only a runaway beat (e.g. 130M) moves the stock.
  • New narrative: if anyone can generate games on the fly, legacy publishers' long-dated revenue is in doubt even with 3-4 strong years left.
  • Salesforce analogy: not going away, but startups will use one of 100 cheap AI-coded rivals while American Airlines keeps the system of record — a multiple headwind.
  • Future of gaming may look like content creation: tens of millions of creators, tiny communities, near-free games.
  • Chris heard claims GTA 6 could be reverse-engineered with AI in days; Jordan is skeptical — 'the engine does a lot of work'.
  • Chris sold ~75% (keeps 20-25%): 'you have to be open to new information'.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

PPA (power purchase agreement) — A long-term contract to buy electricity from a producer at agreed terms — here, hyperscalers locking up power for ~20 years.
Circular financing — Money flowing between AI companies as investments and purchases, which bears say inflates demand.
AI mode — Google's AI-answer search experience, which monetizes worse than traditional ad-driven search.
Social arbitrage — Chris's approach of trading on consumer trends spotted in social and search data before Wall Street notices.
Agentic shopping — AI agents making purchases on a consumer's behalf, raising chargeback, return and fraud issues for retailers.
