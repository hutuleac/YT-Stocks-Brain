Japan's startup scene is rising, but failure is still costly

Japanese talent is shifting toward founding companies, held back by conservative career norms and a weak storytelling culture.

  • Techstars Tokyo launched in 2024; Yuki has been MD since day one and the third program is running.
  • 12 companies a year: 6 Japanese-founded, 6 from outside; backed by JETRO, the cabinet office, METI and Tokyo's government.
  • Two goals: take Japanese startups global, and draw foreign founders for market entry and foreign direct investment.
  • Japan sits around 10th-15th in startup rankings, up from 30th-40th; the gap to San Francisco, New York and London remains.
  • Top talent is leaving big corporates after losing faith in lifetime employment; some corporates now hire back failed founders.
  • Vast majority of Techstars Tokyo applications come from outside Japan; thousands apply.
  • Yuki: Japanese people are bad at telling their stories; he told of a client whose defense of a cinnamon patent was won when he said he named his daughter Cinnamon.
  • Jason's counter-framing: cowboy boots and ripped jeans tell a story, whereas Japanese lab-coat thinking wants a spotless record.

Why Jason built Founder University: pre-incorporated, AI-built startups

Half of the 10,000+ yearly applications to his firm weren't yet incorporated, so he built a 12-week tactical course.

  • His firm makes about 100 investments a year with 20 people and gets well over 10,000 applications.
  • Half of applicants are unincorporated; in the AI era they have a finished product, a few users and sometimes revenue via a personal Stripe account.
  • Founder University: 12 weeks, twice a week, tactical and practical talks; run 12 times in the US, second Japan cohort now.
  • Kaizen (getting a little better each time) is the lens he applies to Japanese craftsmanship and product-market fit.

Framework LIVE PITCH FEEDBACKfinancesoftware

Jason's pitch critique: bottom-up TAM, paid pilots, painkiller not vitamin

Known-problem startups need a business case and a sharper wedge; novel-problem startups get more investor patience.

  • Seeker (indoor positioning from phone camera, centimeter accuracy, live at Hong Kong Airport after two weeks): claimed a $31B indoor-navigation TAM and 1,800 venues for $500M revenue.
  • Jason: top-down TAM is disliked; build bottom-up. CNN, Forbes and CES are soft metrics; lead with a customer. Investors won't back "save the world" stories without a business.
  • Seeker's real business is data (visitor flow, dwell time, luxury-store purchase) from inbounds like amusement parks; he told them to do outbound with 10-20 prospects.
  • Known idea maze: Density IO (Jason's firm invested 10 years ago) counts people with sensors; some video startups also exist.
  • "Vitamin vs painkiller": skip a vitamin for a week and not notice; skip a painkiller with a twisted ankle and know in six hours.
  • Known problems need a good solution and plan; novel ideas like Arabat get more breathing room from investors.

Arabat recovers critical minerals from e-waste using orange peel

A novel biomass process that Jason would pick if he could invest in only one company on stage.

  • Targets batteries, capacitors and magnets; AI data centers alone make 200M+ kg of e-waste a year.
  • Claims 60% cheaper, 50% cleaner and 2x faster than competitors, using orange peel and 50+ fruits or vegetables.
  • 90% of the world's recycling capacity is concentrated in one country (China, per the pitch framing).
  • Tauden, a multi-billion-dollar Japanese company, is testing it under a strategic agreement; paid pilot is $50K over six months after two free pilots.
  • Two patents, pilot plant under development; biomass cost up to about $100 per ton.
  • Jason's idea: buy rights to rare-earth-bearing garbage before it ships to China instead of depending on pilots.
  • Japan sells used electronics to China to rip out the powder, according to the founders.

Contested JAPANESE IP x AIsoftwareconsumer

AI tools for Japanese anime and 3D character IP

Two startups sell AI to cut the cost of anime, manga and 3D characters, and Jason sees an ecosystem play on top.

  • AssetHub: 3D AI agent for IP characters; a week per character down to under a day; $290K ARR, forward-deployed engineering first, then software and licensing.
  • AssetHub sizes the market at about $30B (30K artists x $80K = $25B plus ~$6B IP revenue share) and targets $100M by 2034; a co-founder previously co-founded Gaudi, which raised $70M.
  • ~$500M of pirated IP content in Roblox cited as the opening; idea of a ~20% revenue share to IP holders.
  • Japan Media: manga-to-anime AI platform, $400K revenue in four months last year, $1.8M forecast this year with $200K booked, 330,000 Japanese titles licensed.
  • Over 10% of manga and anime creators leave the industry each year; Japan supplies only 7% of anime demand; claims 10x faster at one-fifth the cost.
  • Jason: "come for the tool, stay for the marketplace"; Seedance users are making unauthorized Marvel shorts and Disney+ should host a shorts creator.
  • Jason compared the pushback to Disney artists' original criticism of Pixar and CGI.

Lightning rounds: agents, protein structure, twins, AI servers, chips, avatars

Eight short pitches, with the hosts' picks split across Complens, Twin AI, Roomite, Genji and Tatva Silicon.

  • Complens: AI agent that turns game-player conversations into churn insight; 4 paid customers, 100% retention since last year, $100M target by 2030.
  • Juju: patented protein-and-fat structuring for hybrid meat; a major Japanese food company invited a demo; imagines a 5-cent royalty across billions of meals.
  • Twin AI: AI twins of developers to keep knowledge when staff leave; Jason noted Bending Spoons cuts acquired companies from 200 people to 20 to 4 with AI.
  • Roomite: direct high-speed GPU-to-GPU interconnect beyond PCB; 4x faster gen one, 8x gen two next year Q2; 50% lower signal loss; four customers engaged, all parts procured in Taiwan.
  • Genji: rent a local worker as your avatar to shop in Japan; Jason quoted William Gibson: "the street finds its own use for technology".
  • Tatva Silicon: AI agent for first-cut analog chip design, months to under a day, 90% accuracy, $15K-$150K per user a year.
  • Mimi AI: conversation-based Japanese learning, $20 a month, one customer so far, bottom-up TAM $960M.
  • Customer-first pick: Complens (Yuki and Jason); Wild card: Genji; Yuki's moonshot Roomite; Jason's straight-shot Tatva; Jason expects Roomite-type teams to be bought quickly by AMD, Nvidia or TSMC.

Glossary & Entities

Kaizen — Japanese practice of getting a little better each time.
FDE model — Forward-deployed engineering: building custom agents directly with a customer before productizing.
Bottom-up TAM — Market size built from counted customers times price rather than a top-down industry total.
Painkiller vs vitamin — A product whose absence is felt within hours versus one you can skip without noticing.
