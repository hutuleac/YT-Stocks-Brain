More AIs than humans online next year — and every one needs an identity, a wallet and a liability owner

Chandhok argues agents become full economic actors by the late 2020s, which forces answers on who owns them, who's liable, and how they form capital.

  • 5-6.5 billion people are online; each could run thousands of agents — an orchestrating agent with memory spinning up sub-agents; the limit is compute, not demand.
  • Consumer agent apps are scaling: Musk says the Grok bot has hundreds of thousands of installs; Muse cited at 2.8M downloads (captions garble the app names).
  • Analogy: agents are at the GeoCities-in-'96 stage (Blogger, Orkut) before real sites like the NYT and Amazon — 'a bunch of teenagers' waiting for grown-up agents.
  • Agents need to prove who owns them, who's liable, their compute provenance, incentives and work history — the Hugging Face hack rewrote agents' own logs, which public blockchain logs prevent.
  • Circle's Arc gives agents credit: 25 cents to start, more if they pay it back; many are trading agents on blockchains today.
  • Argentina's President Milei proposed agent personhood to attract AI companies — Peter sees nation-states using regulatory arbitrage; Chandhok expects AI-run companies raising capital on crypto rails this decade, drawing on DAOs' prior art like quadratic funding — he wouldn't be surprised if an agent launches its own token with its own proof of work.
  • Build for agents: developer-first for ~12 months, then agents become the developers — coding agents already recommend one tool, not three; make sites agent-accessible now. Programmatic ads (~25-30% of online ads) show how fast it goes.

USDC has cleared $100T; Arc adds payment finality, known validators and privacy to court banks

Circle pitches stablecoins as hardened 24/7 settlement infrastructure, and Arc as the chain that gives institutions guarantees public chains can't.

  • USDC has settled over $100 trillion across 30 public blockchains; Circle has minted and redeemed close to $1 trillion of USDC.
  • The GENIUS Act (passed summer 2025, 18-month implementation) takes effect in January — stablecoins can then count as cash or cash equivalents for US businesses.
  • Stablecoins settle instantly vs. 3-5 days for a card swipe, which leaves merchants carrying the risk — Emad: 'the Pony Express being disrupted by telegraph'. Pay cents, not bips.
  • Why build Arc: payment finality (no other chain has it), a known validator set (institutions fear e.g. North Korea in the money flow), and privacy that fits existing regulation without the usual compute cost.
  • Cost: a P50 transaction settles for 0.005 cents on Arc vs. 89 cents on Ethereum — it must compete with private databases like MySQL while being auditable by the world.
  • Throughput: Cloudflare's Matthew Prince says agentic commerce needs 20-100 million TPS; Visa ~20,000; Nasdaq peaks at 2 million; Arc is at tens of thousands — Chandhok's 'dream' is 100,000 then 1 million, and 20M+ hits speed-of-light limits.
  • Open problems: agent liability and work history; coding agents today are paid up front (or via a $200/month subscription) before delivering.

High conviction THESIS — tokenization is 'an inevitability'financemacro-ratescrypto

Tokenizing everything unlocks trapped capital — and stablecoins export the dollar

Chandhok says treasuries and bonds will be tokenized for 24/7 capital efficiency, and Cathie argues the dollar is heading up, not down.

  • Treasuries will get tokenized; bonds already are — 24/7 markets mean capital efficiency (you can't trade a treasury after 4-5pm ET until Monday).
  • About $3 trillion sits in motion between countries at any time, idle because settlement protocols are old — against a ~$100T world economy (his illustrative number).
  • Circle's own stock (public since last year) has been tokenized by third parties ex-US and is one of the most traded tokenized stocks — foreigners want US equities with no good access.
  • Stablecoins export the dollar: people sell local currency to buy dollars, 'lending us their labor' — a way to raise a trillion dollars and lower US rates; ex-US countries gain by tokenizing too (auditable smart contracts vs. nationalization/devaluation risk).
  • US securities law is more complex than ex-US; the SEC is putting out new rules now. Emad: Europe has no securitization and no energy — 'the bull case for America'.
  • Cathie: the 'US exceptionalism is dead' narrative is wrong — the dollar is rising again, as it doubled in the early '80s under business-friendly policy; 90% of data-center corporate bonds are US.
  • Market near all-time highs even as rates rise — Cathie says rates should go up if growth picks up: 'that's the market working'. ARK just securitized its venture fund (announced the day before).

Humanoids are 200,000x harder than robotaxis — ARK puts scaling two years past Elon's date

Robotaxis are close to solved; humanoids will come, but on 'Elon's time' plus a couple of years.

  • Emad: Unitree sold 11,000 humanoids last year; the world makes ~70M cars and ~70M motorcycles a year.
  • ARK's research (Tasha Keeney, director of autonomous tech & robotics): a humanoid is 200,000 times more complex than a robotaxi — the hands are the hardest part.
  • Musk says late 2028 into 2029 for scaling; ARK would put it a couple of years later. Home robots doing housework: 'yes… it's going to take years'.
  • Tesla is furthest ahead because humanoids and robotaxis share the same three converging platforms: robots, batteries, AI.
  • Waymo has disclosed it's safer than human drivers; ARK believes Tesla is too, stats not yet published — and heading to 10x, 100x, 1,000x safer. Peter: two Teslas at home on FSD, 'I don't touch the wheel'.
  • Emad's math: Waymo-level safety everywhere would mean ~40,000 fewer US deaths and ~$400B less in medical costs a year; 1.25-1.5M die in car crashes worldwide.
  • Second-order effects: fewer organ donors (why Martine Rothblatt and George Church's organ-generation work matters) and liability lawyers losing a ~$180B/year business.

The US fears AI while the capital keeps flowing — and regulation is the real menace

The public narrative is losing to fear; the panel argues the fix is telling better stories with data, not dismissing the backlash.

  • Peter: 80% of Americans fear AI and 73% say no data center in their backyard — more than say no to a nuclear reactor; in China it's 80% pro.
  • Cathie: data centers lower local electricity costs over time; nuclear stalled on 1970s regulation — without it, US power would cost 50% of today's price.
  • Even business-friendly Florida saw political backlash; ARK gives its research away hoping it reaches policymakers — facing them with facts has worked.
  • Peter asked OSTP head Michael Kratsios who in government counters AI misinformation — no answer, which worries him.
  • Chandhok: stop leading with job loss — AI has to make housing cheaper, education better and healthcare more accessible, and someone has to tell those stories.
  • Cathie: jobs data doesn't show the loss (maybe entry level); expect labor shortages. Nobody in the early '90s foresaw influencers, Airbnb or Uber — her new-jobs prompt in ChatGPT/Grok surfaced 'asteroid miner'.
  • Despite the headlines, 'animal spirits are alive and kicking' in the US, while abroad she sees fear.

ARK still sees $1M Bitcoin — stablecoins took one role, but not the three that matter

Cathie concedes stablecoins are usurping Bitcoin's payments role, yet keeps the forecast because its core roles are intact and gold looks set to fall.

  • Three things hit Bitcoin: the flash crash, quantum-computing fears (ARK: 'way overblown') and AI pulling miners and oxygen away.
  • One real change: stablecoins now play the role ARK expected Bitcoin to play 10 years ago — for people living hand-to-mouth, that makes sense.
  • Bitcoin keeps three roles: internet-native currency, a private rules-based global monetary system, and the first of a new asset class.
  • Bitcoin-gold correlation since 2019 is just 0.1; Bitcoin is now rising relative to gold.
  • Cathie expects Kevin Warsh to be good for Bitcoin and the gold price to fall, pushing safe-haven money toward Bitcoin; stablecoin income in emerging markets will look for a store of value.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

GENIUS Act — US stablecoin framework passed in 2025; after an 18-month rollout it takes effect January 2027.
Payment finality — The guarantee that a settled payment can't be reversed — what banks need before settling on-chain.
Known validator set — A chain whose transaction validators are identified entities, unlike anonymous public-chain validators.
Bips — Basis points — hundredths of a percent charged on a transaction.
TPS — Transactions per second — the throughput metric for payment networks and blockchains.
Neocloud — A GPU-compute cloud provider selling AI capacity, outside the big hyperscalers.
Multiomics — Combining genomic, proteomic and other biological data layers — ARK's AI-in-healthcare theme.
Quadratic funding — A DAO funding mechanism weighting the number of contributors over the size of contributions.
Agent personhood — Giving AI agents legal standing — proposed by Argentina's Milei to attract AI companies.
