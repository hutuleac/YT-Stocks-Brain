High conviction THESIS — HUMAN DATA IS THE SCARCE INPUTai-infrasoftware

Model-on-model training makes fresh human argument the scarce asset

Chris's bet: labs will pay whatever it takes for permissioned human signal, and Reddit holds the broadest pile of it.

  • Opening framing: Claude does 26% of Anthropic's research, up from under 1% in February, with 30,000 agents running experiments; a federal judge ruled 10 days ago that a lab can't scrape for free.
  • Hosts concede nobody can say with conviction how model collapse plays out — papers and opinions conflict — but locking up fresh, permissioned human data is a no-brainer for the labs.
  • Data owners: Google (GOOGL) has the advantage via YouTube video and email (hosts unsure email rights allow training); xAI has X data, which Jordan sees as narrow (tech, politics, finance) next to Reddit's breadth.
  • Reddit's CEO (heard on a podcast) pitches the data as highly human — arguments, differing opinions, use cases — and Reddit gives its data free to researchers but charges commercial users.
  • Alternatives fall short: buying old books teaches language but not current information; timely signal still comes from X, Reddit or TikTok. Chris expects dozens of big tech buyers and rising renewal prices.
  • Reddit stats: user growth slowed to about 7%, yet it says 5% of all content ever written there came in the last quarter — Chris can't tell how much of that is AI-written.
  • Meta's announced Reddit rival hit the stock intraday (a 2.5% flash drop, half recovered); hosts say Threads already failed and Meta's personal-relationship graph doesn't suit interest-based forums. Yahoo Answers and Google's forum also failed; only Reddit and Stack Overflow stuck.

Contested HOLDING — LONG AMAZON INTO EARNINGSai-infraconsumer

Amazon: agents are a huge AWS tailwind and a possible hit to the ad layer

Chris sees agent compute as the biggest infrastructure positive since AI began, with agentic shopping the one risk to Amazon's advertising.

  • Agents consume huge compute through simultaneous token runs; compute prices still rise week over week, feeding AWS, Bedrock, Graviton and Trainium.
  • Agentic AI could demolish the advertising layer as we know it, but Chris expects infrastructure spend to make up for it two or three times over, and nothing to happen quickly.
  • Amazon is building its own agent: project Moonraker, the internal code name for a full agentic Alexa+ that executes multi-step tasks from a single request — backed by more shopper data than anyone.
  • Open questions: an agent-to-agent advertising model, and paid or preferential product placement. Chris expects management to tackle agentic shopping on the next earnings call.
  • Amazon designs its own chips, so it is not beholden to Nvidia purchases. Diesel prices and shipping probably cost Amazon this quarter.
  • An Anthropic IPO near $2T would help Amazon; Chris says most X commentary on the filing is wrong — a half-trillion spend is over 10 years and the losses are an accounting shift.

Speculative OPEN QUESTION — WHO CAPTURES AGENT VALUEsoftwareconsumer

Agent sprawl: four assistants, no sync, and unclear winners in agentic shopping

Hosts use several agents at once, find the experience fragmented, and can't say which software layer survives agentic shopping.

  • Dave runs four agents reading his email, each flagging different things, and wants someone to make them share what they know about him — or one to win.
  • Dave would prefer Apple win so the assistant runs on-device with private cloud compute; Siri on iOS 27 can act on what's on screen, such as adding to the calendar.
  • Chris let Muse read Instagram DMs; Instagram locked him out for a week after Muse scraped them every 15 minutes. Muse also can't read WhatsApp: its virtual computer lacks a persistent browser session.
  • Hosts want a sentinel layer that approves every action an agent takes on your behalf.
  • Shopify (SHOP) looks like an obvious beneficiary of agentic shopping, but each agent might clone its own storefront — Chris says value capture is mind-boggling to call.

Contested CAUTION — KEEP LEVERAGE UNDER CONTROLmacro-ratesai-infrapolicy

Chris has never felt a market this uncertain, and says to keep leverage low

The AI trade is whipsawing on unknowns — model pace, safety delays, debt — and Chris advises no overconfidence for days to weeks.

  • Chris, trading for decades, has felt this uncertain only briefly during COVID; the next few weeks bring Gemini, new models, a lab delaying a model as dangerous, and labs asking government to step in.
  • Bloom Energy (BE) swings about 12% a day and 9-13% in the latest days; hosts like it because it sits upstream — the world needs electricity whoever wins.
  • The real bubble, a host argues, is debt, not AI: US fiscal debt plus heavy borrowing to fund AI raises rate worries.
  • Chris fears model speed more than danger — markets and companies need time to adjust — and sees no competent regulator, while labs want rules so they can say they followed them.
  • Chris trims leverage and tells viewers: if you are overconfident or levered right now, that is too much.
  • Dave points to an energy episode with Jordan next week, since energy is the one theme that doesn't feel confusing.

Contested UNRESOLVED ON AIRparentingmindset

Heated debate: AI tools in school versus pencil and paper

Chris wants teenagers taught to wield AI; Jordan wants screens limited, and neither can produce hard evidence.

  • Chris sat through seven classes at his kids' high school open house and heard AI mentioned zero times — curriculum he calls 1970s-style.
  • Jordan argues any screen time hurts learning, handwriting beats keyboards for language, and AI belongs in one separate class; some districts are dropping laptops-for-all.
  • Chris cites Alpha School in Austin, where kids reportedly learn 5x faster with AI tutoring and operate like 30-year-olds; Jordan wants the peer-reviewed study.
  • Chris says test scores and IQ are the wrong measure — impact on the world is — and calls peer-reviewed studies mostly biased; Jordan retorts that this is anti-science.
  • Both admit the evidence is weak (Chris: intuition; Jordan: hasn't studied it as much); Chris's fallback is that content creators and sports may matter most in 30 years.
Pull quote
“I want them in school actually learning how to learn.”— Jordan Giesige
Worth noting
