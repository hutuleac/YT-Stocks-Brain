High conviction JORDI'S CASE — THIS ISN'T THE PRE-2007 ECONOMYmacro-ratessemisai-infra

Doom-on-rates ignores that AI names now dominate the index and don't care about rates

Jordi argues the economy changed after the iPhone and the financial crisis, so 50-year analogies about oil and rates no longer apply.

  • Rates normalized rather than spiked: nominal GDP is about 6.5%, and 10-year yields have traditionally sat about 200 basis points above it until 1997 — so today's yields are cheap against growth.
  • In 2015, Ford, GM and the homebuilders had almost 20x the annual revenue of Micron and Nvidia; today Micron and Nvidia alone exceed them combined, and Nvidia's expected next-year revenue growth is almost the entire size of those groups' revenue.
  • Tech plus Netflix, Google, Meta, Amazon and Tesla is 56% of the S&P 500, versus about 13% when the iPhone launched at the end of a 7-year tech bear market — so poor market breadth is a mechanical result, not a warning.
  • Micron's margins hit 87% in earnings this week; Anthropic and OpenAI sell compute at prices buyers accept because they need it, so he sees no rate sensitivity in the AI trade.
  • Staples, utilities and real estate are making 52-week lows because they trade like synthetic bonds as yields rise; Jordi and Pomp agree rate-sensitive businesses stay under pressure.
  • Homebuilders and carmakers are 'zombie companies' that will struggle long-term as demographics and mobility change; Buffett-style quality cash-flow value investing may have a hard time.
  • Pomp's counterpoint: Fed rate-hike signals marked the top in November 2021 and 2000, which is what doomers lean on — though rates and markets are both rising now.

Contested HOSTS PARTLY DISAGREE, THEN AGREE ON NUANCEai-infrasoftware

Are OpenAI and Anthropic peaking? Falling tokens-per-customer versus a still-huge revenue base

Pompliano sees pressure on the model labs from falling token use and prices; Jordi says growth has peaked in rate but the labs are still the fastest-growing companies ever.

  • Pomp's three signals: token use per customer is turning over (Ramp AI Index spending down, OpenRouter-type metrics lower), prices are falling (Grok's newest model chart), and tokens times price equals lab revenue, which shows plateauing growth; he raised these with Dan Ives this week.
  • Jordi: early-2026 token maxing was inefficient, token costs are deflationary, and the agentic wave will explode volume — he does not call himself a lab bull and says growth has already passed its fastest point.
  • He cites Anthropic at an annualized run rate of $100 billion-plus, and says OpenAI plus Anthropic revenue by year-end will exceed the entire software industry's; Anthropic went from $50 billion to a multi-trillion valuation in a year and a half.
  • Jordi: they will not reach $1 trillion revenue, but frontier intelligence demand from biotech, nanotechnology and physical science is underestimated; Jane Street and Millennium already use Anthropic.
  • Pomp: Sylvia, with fewer than 10 engineers, built models more accurate than trillion-dollar labs for personal finance; customers adopt labs as an on-ramp, then optimize in-house, so labs must keep recruiting new customers.
  • Both agree on Jevons paradox (cheaper intelligence drives more use), that the easy money in AI is over, and that the labs won't capture all the value; Jordi finds Gemini error-prone and Muse good enough for basics.
  • Jordi stopped using his Open Claw and Hermes setups after finding Claude and OpenAI did the work better.

High conviction JORDI'S THESIS — AGENTS ARE THE ECONOMIC ACTORScryptofinance

Agent swarms need instant settlement, so crypto rails beat Visa and Mastercard

Jordi argues slow card and wire settlement is a security hole for agents, and instant cryptographic settlement closes it.

  • A Friday 3pm wire settles Monday, and a card payment passes many hops; every extra second is a window for an agent swarm to intercept funds — instant settlement is the defense.
  • He rejects the view that Visa and Mastercard win from agents: they have no chance given the number of steps between buying and settling.
  • We didn't need altcoins or most tokens — we needed the layer ones to pull in capital and build infrastructure; with Bitcoin and Ethereum as digital collateral, Michael Saylor's capital-structure change becomes possible.
  • Analogy: Uber couldn't exist until the app store did; agents plus crypto will spawn innovation after innovation the same way.
  • Illia Polosukhin of Near (a co-author of the transformer paper) said in a 2023 No Priors interview that agents would be the economic actors of the future — Jordi's vision source; he also cites William Mougayar (book due in 3-6 months) and a former BlackRock executive.
  • Jordi has bought more crypto every day since putting Muse on his phone, and says three hedge fund and asset-manager calls this week asked about crypto.

The 46-name crypto index and the push-button agent portfolio, with regulation as the brake

Jordi wants subscribers to buy an equal-weight crypto ecosystem basket through an agent dashboard, while Pompliano warns the rules aren't ready.

  • The Visser Labs 46-name index (L1s plus many L2s, equal weight, 42 tokens and 4 public stocks) is up about 50% year to date against Bitcoin down 3.2% — the reverse of Bitcoin's peak, when altcoins lagged.

Glossary & Entities

Token maxing — Early-2026 habit of throwing huge numbers of tokens at everything inefficiently.
Jevons paradox — Cheaper intelligence leads to more total use, so falling token prices can raise demand.
Agent swarm — Many coordinated AI agents performing tasks together, the same capability that enables personal agents.
DAT — Digital asset treasury company holding crypto on its balance sheet, one of the few ways institutions can buy exposure.
Micron moment — Jordi's label for a stretch when the market underestimates how fast a trend accelerates.
