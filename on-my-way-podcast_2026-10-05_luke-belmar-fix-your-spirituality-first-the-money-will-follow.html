Luke Belmar : Fix Your Spirituality First, The Money Will Follow

The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Luke Belmar (moved from Argentina at 16 with $200) says he hit 8 figures at 29 and felt unfulfilled on a beach in Puerto Rico; his message shifted from making money to self-actualization and faith.
  2. Core line: money is a lagging indicator of success, a byproduct of service; there are only two ways to make it, stealing and serving.
  3. He bases himself in Dubai, which he calls better than America and 100 years into the future, citing trust, respect, 24/7 access and low friction.
  4. Faith framework: he follows Jesus, not a label; pride (choosing yourself before God) is the root of sin; the devil is largely our own corruption.
  5. Relationship views: become a husband to find a wife; 'double-minded' men are hypocrites; irreversible decisions (e.g. sex outside marriage) carry weight forever.
  6. He sees the internet as the great equalizer (no latency, free AI tools) but warns that money without self-development is a razor in a baby's hand.
  7. The last stretch is his worldview of a coming control grid (a 'great reset' in 2030, vaccines, biometrics), with self-sufficiency advice; he cites no sources for these claims.
Principle PURPOSE AFTER WEALTHmindsetcareer

Money is a byproduct of service, and winning the game felt empty

Luke argues money is a lagging indicator, so he moved from teaching people to earn to teaching self-actualization.

  • At 29, sitting at the Ritz-Carlton in Puerto Rico with 8 figures in the bank, he asked 'did I just beat the game?'; he likens it to Grand Theft Auto with all the cheat codes, which makes the game boring.
  • He calls himself a 'millionaire monk': he enjoys the Maybach but doesn't mind the back of the bus, having come from $200 in a suitcase and parents who did mission and nonprofit work.
  • Experience is the only thing nobody can take away, which is why entrepreneurs who lose everything can start again.
  • There are only two ways to make money, stealing and serving; the wealthiest person is the one who wakes with the least pressure on their chest.
  • Money without self-development is 'a razor to a baby'; the spiritual, mental, family and relational work is the anchor and matters more as AI makes money easier.
  • Gratitude test: if you're not grateful with a cup of coffee, you won't be with a $1,000 bottle of champagne; chasing money from lack or ego (bullied as a kid) fills nothing.
  • Friends paying $50,000 a month on the Palm work more than 9-to-5 workers who are happier: 'a bigger rat, but you're still a rat'; he urges family, parents and community over individualism.

Worth noting

Recommendation WHERE HE'S SPENDING TIMEmindset

Dubai as a low-friction hub of trust and respect

He ranks Dubai above Bali, Singapore, Hong Kong, New York and Miami for ambition, safety and ease.

  • Society needs trust (top-down) and respect; he reads Dubai's order as enjoyment of mutual respect, not fear, and credits Sheikh Mohammed with designing the environment.
  • Greenhouse image: opportunity can flourish inside regardless of the weather outside; he keeps extending his stay and says he's friends with the royal family.
  • Versus Bali: lifestyle on $2,000 a month, but crowded, typhoon season, an island; Dubai has a 'different level of ambition'.
  • Versus Singapore: more central, more fun, and in his view more lenient on rules; most places offer a 90-180 day visa.
  • He contrasts New York ('feels like an accident of energy', rats, stabbings) with Dubai ('energy well managed'); he was jumped by four men in Barcelona about 8 months ago and had a Richard Mille watch stolen.
  • His 'base' is his body, wherever he can serve with no latency; the host's wealthy contacts talk of bases with house, driver and staff in London or Singapore.
Pull quote
“the reason it's better than America is because we're 100 years into the future”— Luke Belmar
Worth noting
Luke says he is friends with the royal family and is recorded in Dubai; both speakers praise it throughout.
Framework HIS FAITH, IN HIS WORDSmindset

Following Jesus, not a label: pride, God's will and the devil within

He frames life as obedience to God's will, with pride as the root of every sin.

  • Buddha seeks peace; he seeks to do God's will: 'life is suffering' but all of life is a range, and suffering also comes from detachment.
  • He calls himself a follower of Jesus, not a Christian, and a 'glitch in the matrix' meant to disrupt people's patterns of thinking; God changed his heart in 2002 (prodigal son parable).
  • Destiny: the script exists and prophets foretold it, but the 'doors' you open are your choice, with the deeper question being which door God wants.
  • Sin means separation; pride is choosing yourself before God, whatever the action.
  • The devil: 'enough corruption within our own minds'; he denies a favorite sin and says his own sins he repents of.
  • Solomon's vanity, the commandment against coveting, and 'you cannot serve both God and money' anchor his view that wealth only helps if held as a steward.
  • Hell is a choice of separation, like a timeout; Capital Club is nicknamed CC33 for Jesus' age at death; he's reading a study Quran given by a friend.
Pull quote
“If you're not serving God, you are serving the devil by default.”— Luke Belmar
Worth noting
This is his personal belief system, stated as fact; the host (who says he went to a Christian school) pushes back lightly on Jesus and on hell.
Counterintuitive take MARRIAGE, TRUST AND CONSEQUENCESrelationshipsfamily

Become a husband, and treat irreversible decisions as permanent

His relationship advice rests on intention of the heart, integrity and carrying consequences.

  • 'If you want to find a wife, become a husband'; he quotes 'he who finds a wife finds a good thing' and says you get what you look for.
  • He criticizes 'double-minded' men who go to church then use Hinge or Tinder; he reframes women as God's daughters and so as sisters.
  • He says he has only been with one woman, his wife.
  • He says society has taught women to pick a provider who buys a bag over a trustworthy man, and calls OnlyFans a 'very well-paid prostitute' life; these are his stated opinions.
  • Two kinds of decisions: reversible (a U-turn, a failed business, a stock that falls) and irreversible (infidelity, sleeping around), which you carry with your consciousness forever.
  • He has lost friends to the message, to giving them too much money, to greed and pride; he says everything can be restored unless you betray trust and respect.
  • Meek means power under control, and being calm makes friendships easy.
Worth noting
These are his religiously grounded opinions; the host adds the Quran's version (wicked men attract wicked women) and agrees.
Recommendation OPPORTUNITY ONLINEcareerdev-workflow

The internet removes latency; be a producer, not a consumer

He tells people to treat what they see online as inspiration, since there are no gatekeepers and AI tools keep getting cheaper.

  • Online removes the 'latency' of commuting and friction, like a highway without traffic.
  • Examples he cites: people in India making $20,000 a month clipping short-form content; people in China making $20,000-$100,000 a month dropshipping; people from Detroit who now own banks.
  • A 19-year-old e-commerce reader makes about $100,000 a day and asked how to reach peers distracted by phones; Luke's answer: pray for them, no convincing works.
  • He had made millions in ecom and crypto before becoming popular, and has 10 million followers, 10 billion views and speaks four languages.
  • Wealth is stewardship: let it flow through you rather than hoard it; he sees money as a tool for time and freedom, not the end.
  • Capital Club events around the world are often free, to 'humanize the digital experience' with counsel and an amalgamation of minds in a room.
Worth noting
Income figures are his anecdotes about people he knows, not documented numbers.
Speculative HIS PREDICTIONS AND BELIEFSgeopoliticshealthpolicy

A coming 'beast system': self-sufficiency, biometrics and sedation

In the closing stretch he lists what he sees as the next 'scoops' and advises independence from fragile systems.

  • Next scoops he lists: future vaccines and pandemics, smart cities, AI, digital lockups and control grids; he mentions 'the great reset in 2030'.
  • Advice: get your money up, restore family unity, find allies, and own power and food so you don't depend on a grocery store.
  • Biometric cameras are everywhere in airports now; he uses a boiling-frog image for controllers always asking for 'one more inch'.
  • He says Bill Gates and Peter Thiel 'literally hate God', and calls the Epstein list proof that nobody goes to jail.
  • Peptides: unsure; points to the Ozempic craze and reports of people going blind, prefers waiting years for the science; he likes plasma from your own blood.
  • He says people are sedated by food, 5G and 6G frequencies, water, lust and money, and points to a UFO sighting and Pentagon footage on CNN that barely registered.
  • The host says Luke called BlackRock's rise early; Luke predicts Argentina will win the World Cup.
Worth noting
All of this is Luke's belief stated without sources; the host mostly agrees (including that peptides are a 'genius play').
