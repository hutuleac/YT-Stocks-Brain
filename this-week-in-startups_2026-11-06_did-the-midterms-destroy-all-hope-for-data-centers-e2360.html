Voters restrict data centers; Calacanis says the sell-off is a buying chance

Local bans and a Texas governor-elect's pause hit the data-center trade, but Calacanis expects a deal where builders pay their way.

  • Lon Harris: Democrats won both chambers, many on slowing AI and data centers; over a dozen Ohio towns voted to halt or restrict development, with similar bans in Kansas, Florida and California.
  • Texas' governor-elect campaigned on pausing construction until new regulation (per the Wall Street Journal); Texas, Georgia and Ohio hold 40%+ of planned capacity.
  • By Wednesday senior OpenAI leaders were discussing whether to reset next year's capacity expectations; Bloomberg says Microsoft is souring on neoclouds such as nscale and Iren.
  • Calacanis: tech fell 5-6% in a day after a rally the morning after the vote, and he says a slowed AI trade could mean a two-to-four-year recession that is blamed on Democrats.
  • He cites Gavin Baker: Texas laws for data centers delivering in 2028-29 would require taxes, no power-price increase, no water impact, union labor and a seat for Texans; Calacanis calls it a reasonable middle ground.
  • He says it is a good moment to nibble on Nvidia, Amazon, Uber, Tesla and SpaceX, and notes he predicted this post-election peace on the Aug 31 episode (E2332).

Reading the midterms: Iran war, cost of living and corruption beat data centers

Harris and Calacanis review the host's earlier Trump calls and say the vote was driven by affordability, the war and perceived corruption, with AI close behind.

  • Harris reads exit polls: the Iran war first, data centers high, then a perception of corruption; Calacanis adds cost of living and affordability.
  • Calacanis gives Trump an A to A-plus on supporting AI and data centers, and says gridlock is the preferred case; he thinks Trump made data centers a wedge issue.
  • Clips replayed: the Feb 18 2025 take that Trump shouldn't have launched meme coins; the May 14 2025 view that tariffs were chaotic though right in principle; the May 3 2026 view that AI and jobs split the GOP (Bannon, Fuentes, Tucker, Megyn Kelly).
  • He blames sycophants for tariff chaos, says tariff opponents were brigaded, and cites a Trump-supporting founder's firsthand account as his source.
  • Calacanis thinks the new Democrats are mostly moderates and will move to the center; Harris says their message was affordability, no corruption and no wars.
  • Both say Democrats laid low for two years (0 for 4 branches) and let Trump hurt himself; Calacanis would back whoever is president and would invite tech CEOs to DC.
  • Harris says a winning Democrat (Tlaib Frico, a Texas winner) praised Elon Musk and Ted Cruz the day after, despite Musk spending $200 million against him; memes now joke that Zuckerberg and other CEOs, as Reed Hastings predicted, flip back to blue.
  • Calacanis dismisses Governor Abbott's data-center pause as appeasing voters who hate AI after Dario Amodei's job-loss warnings, and expects the Aug 31 prediction to hold.

OpenAI publishes community pledges; Calacanis says repeat them and depoliticize

OpenAI commits to pay full power costs and publish plans, and the host argues the answer to backlash is relentless repetition.

  • OpenAI will pay the full cost of powering its data centers, seek community input, publish construction plans and avoid NDAs with public officials, and urges lawmakers to turn this into law.
  • Meta, Microsoft, Google and Anthropic have outlined similar commitments; OpenAI told investors in February it targets $600 billion in compute spend by 2030.
  • Calacanis: nothing revolutionary, but leadership is repeating the same line; he gives the example of comparing a data center's water use to a golf course, with seven golf courses locally.
  • Both argue data centers are not inherently partisan (a Walmart is a more progressive target), and Harris notes cities have long subsidized big-box retail.

Anthropic's first licensing deal exposes the unsettled AI copyright map

Anthropic signed with the Associated Press for election coverage; Calacanis lays out four copyright exposures and says two are still open.

  • Adweek: Anthropic's first publisher deal, with the AP for election coverage, signed just before the midterms; terms undisclosed; OpenAI has more than a dozen (Axel Springer, News Corp, The Atlantic).
  • Calacanis' four issues: using stolen content (Anthropic's billion-dollar pirated-books settlement), breaking terms of service (OpenAI, Greg Brockman's paywall comment), training on content, and outputting others' IP.
  • He says the first two are cleaned up, training is murky but will go to content companies, and that real-time use of news is not fair use.
  • His fix: give 3% of revenue (about $3 billion a year at a $100 billion run rate) to content owners; Substack would pass 90% through to writers.

Apple cuts engineer AI spend caps; Calacanis sees corporate seats rising to $100-300 a month

Apple lowered engineers' daily AI budget from $300 to $200, and the host reads that as proof the all-you-can-eat plan era is ending.

  • Bloomberg: Apple cut its daily AI spend cap for engineering teams from $300 to $200 in October, with some going to $100 and a $25 limit weighed outside engineering.
  • Calacanis' team pays about $40 a month per head, burning perhaps $2,000 a year in tokens; he expects the minimum corporate price to rise to $100-300 a month ($3-5k a year).
  • He says Apple will come in last but best, with the best local hardware (and a possible $20,000 Mac Studio for everyone), and that Nvidia and Windows with Microsoft are working on the same.
  • Harris agrees a model-agnostic Apple platform makes sense, and they price a top-spec Apple desktop at $14,549.

Calacanis fears 60% state tax rates and proposes taxing large margin loans

He expects the next fight to shift from income to assets and says the borrow-against-stock loophole is the easy target.

  • Calacanis: if taxes become 60% he would retire; he expects New York and California to reach 60%, and says the failed billionaire tax (about 60/40) will return.
  • His rate math: $1M salary in Texas is about 39.4% (37% plus Medicare) vs 23.8% on capital gains; in California about 54% vs 37.1%.
  • Harris cites Tim Dillon on the All-In election stream: the focus shifts from income to assets and equity.
  • Proposed fix: tax margin loans above a threshold (for example $10 million), since borrowing against stock defers tax until death.
  • Harris says he's an AOC fan but that Ossoff makes more sense for 2028; Calacanis says he would support AOC as president but not necessarily vote for her.
Worth noting
Calacanis says he has used margin loans himself, including to buy a ranch.
