Gulf pipelines defuse Hormuz and set up a disinflation convergence

Diversified export routes shrink Iran's main weapon, and cheaper oil plus AI deflation could bring rates down.

  • CSIS: the post-war threat is to Gulf energy infrastructure; Tehran's strongest weapon is weaponizing the Strait of Hormuz.
  • New pipelines could add ~4M barrels/day of export capacity by end-2027 and ~7M by end-2028, routing to the Red Sea and Mediterranean.
  • Pipelines can be attacked (as in Saudi Arabia) but are buried, with only pumping stations exposed, and no sailors die.
  • Gulf states are tired of Iranian attacks and don't want Iran to have nukes.
  • Convergence: lower oil, AI deflation after the infrastructure buildout, and rate relief — good for real estate, stocks and the economy.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

The suckening — Paffrath's term for large AI capital raises pulling money out of stocks into bonds.
Par value — A bond's face value (100); trading below par raises the yield for new buyers.
2s10s spread — Gap between 10-year and 2-year Treasury yields; a falling 10-year with a stable 2-year is a bull flattening.
Vendor financing — A supplier funds the customer's purchase, booking revenue now and holding the receivable.
PEG ratio — Price/earnings divided by expected earnings growth; below 1 suggests cheap for its growth.
