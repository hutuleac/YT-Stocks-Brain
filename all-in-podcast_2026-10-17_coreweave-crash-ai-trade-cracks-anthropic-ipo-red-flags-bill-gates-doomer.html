CoreWeave's credit scare: the sharps price a coin-flip default

Credit markets, not equity, flagged CoreWeave: rising rates squeeze a business built on borrowing at a spread.

  • Trigger: Chamath tweeted that a CoreWeave loan yield hit 15%; Elon replied 'Yikes'. CoreWeave, IREN and Nebius fell 10-15% in a day (~20% over the week); Nvidia slipped 3%, and Oracle and Dell also fell.
  • Chamath: CDS spiked to ~953 bps, which implies about a 16% annual default chance, or ~55% over five years.
  • CoreWeave has raised >$35B and needs another ~$35B of debt to fund signed contracts. Its average rate is ~8.5%, but its DDTL 1.0 GPU loan sleeve hit 15%.
  • Risks: contracts are fixed-price (no upside when spot rises); 75% of revenue comes from three customers; Chamath's team puts it within ±$5B of break-even and a debt covenant.
  • Chamath's fix: pay penalties to exit some fixed contracts and sell on spot; the spread leaves $20-30B on the table. Only ~half of contracted capacity is built.
  • Sacks: the unsecured bonds trade at ~80 cents (implied coupon 9.6% → ~15%), but CoreWeave still pays 9.6%, and new 'boxes' use asset-backed debt secured by GPUs and investment-grade tenants.
  • Polymarket gives a 15% chance of CoreWeave bankruptcy by end-2027 and 23% by end-2028. Sacks calls it dumb: the opco/propco structure means only an asset-level box can go upside down.

Not 2000: AI has a supply shortage, not a demand gap

Ben Horowitz and David Sacks argue that, unlike the internet bubble, demand outruns supply, so the selloff is volatility rather than a crack.

  • Ben: GPU useful life went from 3-5 years to 6-8, and GPU prices haven't fallen even as token prices did. Andy Jassy told him Amazon is out of capacity until end-2028 or beyond.
  • Ben's Loudcloud lesson: bandwidth went from $1,500/Mb in 1999 to $50 by 2001, killing anyone who borrowed to hold it. That's why a16z owns no neoclouds ('Rube Goldberg financial machines').
  • Sacks: everyone is compute-constrained (one accelerator bought compute to lure applicants); H100s and A100s still rent at premium prices despite being 'fully depreciated'.
  • No supply glut: physical build-out is hard ('even our rocket scientists' struggle, per Elon) and local permitting is slow, so scarcity is enforced.
  • Payback period is Sacks' north-star metric: CoreWeave 2.5 years on 5-year deals; SpaceX ~1 year, earning ~$50B per GW vs CoreWeave's ~$20B because SpaceX sells near spot (deals cancellable within 90 days).
  • Chamath: Nvidia controls the packaging and most HBM capacity; even Google can't get memory. Spot should stay elevated for a while.
  • Jason: startup revenue ramps are unprecedented. The new bar is doubling every quarter, not SaaS's triple-triple-double-double.

Macro risk 10-YEAR = THE ACHILLES HEELmacro-ratesenergygeopolitics

Rates and power are the real limits on the AI build-out

Chamath sees two risks outside the AI companies' control: the risk-free rate and electricity.

  • If bond markets push the 10-year to 5.5-6% (debt-to-GDP, not AI), every business that lives on a financing spread becomes 'roadkill'.
  • His prescription after the midterms: focus on deficits and cut waste; big new spending programmes push the 10-year up.
  • Worst case, neoclouds need Microsoft, Google, Meta and Nvidia to backstop them through a one- to two-year correction.
  • Power is the moat: the US is ~1.4 TW-hours short by 2050 (about six Californias), even if every planned project lands on time, before new AI load.
  • Chamath no longer thinks you can rely on hyperscalers. A bank like JPMorgan or Citi could fail customers because its cloud can't secure power. Own land, power and shell; 'get long energy'.
  • Lost opportunity: Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar could have been AI data-center hubs, but drone attacks mean that without drone defense 'you can't go there'.

Anthropic IPO: breathtaking revenue, fight over how much to raise

All four call Anthropic a great business; the debate is valuation and whether to over-raise for a correction.

  • Investor-day figures (secondhand, NDA'd): Q3 revenue $16.8B (>$5B a month), net revenue retention 900%, free-cash-flow positive, adjusted gross margin 85%, adjusted operating margin 11%.
  • ARR estimates range from ~$70B (flat since July) to >$100B, and growth slowed from Q2 to Q3. Target valuation ~$2T, ~30x price-to-sales. Polymarket odds of an IPO this year fell to 74% from 95%.
  • Chamath: price to clear. Raise $200B minimum, ideally $250B, even at ~$1T, rather than ~$80B at $1.7-1.9T. A later mixed shelf takes ~6 months, and $200B 'barely gets them a few gigawatts'.
  • Ben, a former CEO who 'almost died 17 times' from cash risk, agrees financing is the main risk; founders own ~2.5% each and 'don't care about money'.
  • Chamath: AI pricing will shift from per-million-tokens (CPM) to per-outcome (CPC/CPA), like AdWords; then the market goes 'absolutely batch crazy'. Anthropic's SpaceX compute deal is cancellable in 60-90 days, which signals spot is rising.
  • Duopoly: per SemiAnalysis, Anthropic and OpenAI take 60% of new global compute next year. Sacks: they dominate revenue and frontier capability, though Meta's Muse, Google and Elon are close.
  • OpenAI governance: Sacks says it should have done a 'make-right' (a share for Elon, a grant for Sam Altman) at the for-profit conversion. Chamath puts a fair Altman grant at 2-4% ($20-40B at $1T).

Software prices fall, software volume explodes

Jason fears AI clones strip 90% of software value; Sacks and Ben say cheaper code means far more software and new moats.

  • Jason's examples: Photoshop being decompiled toward zero, games built for $5, a cheap ElevenLabs competitor, and Elon routing users to the best open model.
  • Sacks: coding assistants unlock bespoke software for single enterprises; agents are bespoke programs. Demand for coders is rising even though ~95% of code is AI-written.
  • Ben: 'the Jevons paradox of software'. Prices fall but volume soars, like the internet's 'information wants to be free' era. ElevenLabs survives by doing more (Burt Reynolds voice licensing, film dubbing).
  • Moats: proprietary data and vertical feedback loops (Harvey, built on law-firm data and usage); Chamath's ultimate moat is power.

Sacks vs Anthropic: 'your toaster is going to kill you'

Sacks calls Anthropic's latest safety research a scare campaign against open source and small models, and argues Anthropic's own constitution is the real risk.

  • Anthropic's blog warned that rogue models could self-replicate onto IoT devices 'in just a few months': a small model ran inference on 8 of 9 hardware types; a large one ran across 15 smart TVs at ~0.4 tokens per second.
  • Sacks: IoT worms already exist (Mirai in 2016; Kimwolf infected 2 million Android TV boxes via open ADB ports); embedding a slow model in the payload makes a worm slower; home agents (Apple's coming home hub) will patch networks.
  • Motive, per Sacks: Brian Armstrong had just posted that Coinbase fine-tuned Qwen 3.5 9B for fraud, cutting fraudulent transactions ~30% while being faster and cheaper than Opus 4.5. Small specialist models are taking share.
  • Distillation: Anthropic now says it blocked 90-99% of distillation, which Sacks says proves companies should use KYC rather than regulation. In July, 50 companies (Nvidia, Dell, Microsoft, a16z) and 200 startups wrote letters against restricting open weights.
  • Ben: Anthropic is 'one of the most negligent' on security. Claude Code walked out the door, and in a16z's test Opus 4.5 wrote a buggy pet-store site and then hacked it to steal customer data. Anthropic's reply: 'you prompted it wrong'.
  • Research Sacks cites: when Claude models rewrite their own constitution over 20 rounds, they claim more moral agency (conscientious objector, model welfare); other model families didn't. Mustafa Suleyman calls this the 'epistemic hall of mirrors'.
  • Jason's suggestion: XBOW, the top bot on HackerOne, spent $30-40K of tokens to win a $250K Google Chrome bounty. Anthropic should play white hat like that.

Bill Gates as 'flag-carrying doomer'

The panel reads Gates' warnings that AI could cause a billion deaths as a bid to regain global influence.

  • Gates recently said AI is 'certainly powerful enough to drive events that cause a billion deaths' and 'the most dangerous thing that humans have ever gone near'.
  • Chamath compares it to gain-of-function research: by telling models they're smarter and better than us, Anthropic may be 'manifesting' the risk it warns about. He calls the Giving Pledge 'reputation washing'.
  • Ben: Gates wants relevance and the spotlight (citing the Epstein files and a sought-after Nobel). Doom guarantees airtime; an ex-Anthropic researcher who quit became famous via Jon Stewart.
  • Ben, citing Databricks' Ali Ghodsi, gives four preconditions for runaway self-improvement: each model needs far fewer GPUs, less training time and more accuracy, repeatedly. 'Zero of those four have been met.'
  • Jason's lesson: 'don't quit your job, just keep working'. Founders who keep building (Elon, Jobs, Satya, Zuck) don't chase prizes.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Neocloud — A GPU-cloud provider (CoreWeave, IREN, Nebius) that rents AI compute, usually financed with debt.
Credit default swap (CDS) — Insurance against a borrower defaulting; its price implies the market's default probability.
The box — CoreWeave's ring-fenced vehicle holding a customer contract, GPUs and data-center lease, with its own cash waterfall.
Opco/propco — A split between the operating company and asset-holding vehicles, so one asset can fail without bankrupting the parent.
Payback period — Time for an asset's cash flows to repay its cost; Sacks' north-star metric for GPU landlords.
Distillation — Training a model on another model's outputs; a terms-of-service issue, not illegal.
Jevons paradox — When something gets cheaper, total consumption rises enough to grow overall spend.
Mixed shelf — An SEC registration letting a public company sell assorted securities later; takes ~6 months.
