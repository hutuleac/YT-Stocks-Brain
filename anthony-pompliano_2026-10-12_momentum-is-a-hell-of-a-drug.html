Every shock since 2020 failed to break asset prices, so crash calls keep missing

Pompliano argues the bear case keeps losing because the US economy and asset prices have absorbed every shock since 2020.

  • Shocks absorbed since 2020: a pandemic, nearly 10% inflation, the fastest rate-hike cycle in history, multiple double-digit corrections.
  • Also: 3 of the 5 largest US bank failures, sweeping tariffs, and the closing of the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Result: the US stock market sits near all-time highs; companies post record revenue and profits.
  • Cited stat: the S&P 500 will add more EPS this year alone than the combined EPS gained in 2016-2021 — "ridiculously bullish."
  • Jordi Visser made the same resilience case in their Saturday conversation.

Medium conviction MACRO READmacro-ratespolicy

The floor under the market may be government spending, not fundamentals

He reads resilience as a sign the economy may lean on money printing more than anything else, and as long as that continues, a long crisis is unlikely.

  • Condition: "as long as the government is spending trillions," the economy and stocks are unlikely to see a prolonged crisis.
  • Behavioral layer: consumers and investors have convinced themselves nothing can break the market, so they keep spending and buying assets.
  • That belief feeds back into the momentum he describes next — spending and buying keep prices moving.
Worth noting
His own framing hedges ("may be more dependent"); the thesis rests on fiscal spending continuing.

Buy last year's best stock: $176K in 15 years, a 97% hole in the dot-com bust

A one-stock momentum rule crushed the index, but only with a stop-loss does it survive a bust; Pompliano treats it as a mirror of the whole market.

  • Rule: each January, buy the single best-performing stock of the prior year.
  • Last 15 years: $1,000 → $176,114, vs $8,000 in the S&P 500.
  • Last 30 years: still ahead, but required stomaching a 97% drop after the dot-com bust and 23 years to regain the 1999 high.
  • With a 35% stop-loss (sell and sit in cash until Jan 1 if the pick falls 35%), 30 years: $1,514,581 vs $89,418 without the stop and $1,752 in the S&P 500.
  • Pompliano: "I am not a big trader," but the strategy shows that chasing momentum over the last decade has worked.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Momentum strategy — Buying recent winners on the bet that strong performance persists.
Stop-loss — A rule to sell once a position falls a set percentage, here 35%, then hold cash until the next annual rebalance.
EPS — Earnings per share; aggregate S&P 500 EPS growth measures index-level profit growth.
