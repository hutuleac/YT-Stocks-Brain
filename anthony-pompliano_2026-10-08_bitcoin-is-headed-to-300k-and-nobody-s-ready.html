Bitcoin tracks liquidity: $250K-$300K, with volatility compressing

Barhydt says Bitcoin has risen against every macro headwind because liquidity and relief from ambiguity matter more, and global-M2 charts point to $250K-$300K.

  • Backdrop: oil is higher, the 10-year is over 5%, there is a rate hike, and the Clarity bill failed, yet Bitcoin is higher; 'knowing we won't get clarity' removed ambiguity.
  • Next ambiguity is the midterms, which he thinks go badly for the administration; he says the bond market always knew rates were going higher and yield-curve control never came.
  • Global M2 vs Bitcoin charts say $250K-$300K; a Treasury-conference attendee said that if Bitcoin isn't at $300K in 24 months his thesis is wrong.
  • Volatility compression: the 2018-21 cycle ran from ~$3,200 to $69K (~30x); the last cycle from $15-16K to ~$125K (~8x). Pompliano's cut-in-half math gives only 3-4x next.
  • Barhydt's counter: AI agents expand the addressable market, but the power law makes $5T to $20T a huge absolute move; he sees run-ups continuing 'at least for one more cycle', with a couple of million possible 'at a slower time'.
  • Biggest path up is 60/40 reallocation: RIA clients selling the 40% bond sleeve, though even a debt spiral 'can't get Bitcoin to 100 trillion'.

The debt 'death spiral' and the AI-productivity bet

Barhydt argues weak Treasury demand forces ever-higher yields and eventually money printing, leaving AI-driven growth as the only plausible way out.

  • Mechanism: Treasury yield works like a digital-credit coupon; if demand lags, the government pays more, then becomes buyer of last resort, and inflation becomes the lever.
  • He compares it to WWII war bonds, where more demand meant raising money at lower rates; today demand is missing, so the cost of borrowing keeps climbing.
  • Fix he'd want: stop financing wars, policing the world and running deficits; cites the Nick Shirley video of a $20B train project that laid ~100 feet of track.
  • He calls the policy bet 'immoral' toward grandkids and says the alternative bet is 10% GDP growth from AI and robots.
  • Debate with Jordi Visser: Visser says $40T of debt is meaningless given AI productivity; Barhydt, an exponential-tech career man, says it may be true but is 'a huge bet to make with people's lives'.
  • He calls the data-center backlash 'the mother of all psyops': a state that spends a gallon of water per almond complains about AI water use, which is pushing talk of data centers in space.
  • Inflation: whoever is in the White House, 'physically nothing' keeps it below 3%, because nobody has the political will to absorb the pain; AI-driven GDP growth is the only answer.

Agents at work: non-coders become engineers and old software becomes a database

Both men say agents changed their own work in six months; Barhydt expects pure-play software engineers to keep only about two years of edge.

  • Pompliano says he can now build software without a CS degree and jokes about changing his Twitter bio to software engineer.
  • Barhydt: pure-play engineers have 'another two years of half-life'; a non-technical PM couldn't do his architecture talks with AI, but PM, architect and engineer roles are merging into one, so Abra needs far fewer people.

  • Pompliano sometimes can't tell whether a Slack reply comes from a human or an agent.
  • Pompliano told his engineers (mostly in their 20s) at his AI CFO product that dashboards look backward and he wants it fully agentic: text, Slack or bots, interface built on the fly.
  • Adoption signal: Pompliano's wife and her 'mom group chat' use a personal agent for email search, home schedule and outfit planning; both predict an agentic OS layer will replace the app layer.

RIAs are unprepared: regulation, fee incentives and a killer AI-era model

Barhydt says wealth advisors are slowed by compliance and misaligned fees, and that perps, prediction markets and tokenized yield reach them by end of next year.

  • Drag: marketing rules, SEC audits on materials and pricing, and no A/B testing like a normal product; many RIAs outsource their systems.
  • Incentive problem: RIAs earn ~1.5-2% regardless of outcome, so 60/40 portfolios persist despite years of drag on returns.
  • His 'killer RIA' design: a fixed fee plus a market-beta formula (about 5% for a bond/S&P 60/40) with no pay under the hurdle, custom AI portfolio construction, and a tokenized portfolio. He admits 'there would be riots in the street'.
  • Today's intake fits each client into one of ~10 boxes that output an allocation roadmap. Product gap: RIAs are shut out of perpetual futures, prediction markets, tokenization and crypto, not by law but because they don't know what to do.
  • Timeline: by end of next year RIAs will be able to offer perps, prediction-market access and tokenized yield.
  • Next-gen clients: ~$20T in US wealth managers and ~$20T in Europe; heirs will ask to move assets to Robinhood and want a tokenized portfolio that earns yield and works as collateral (instant home loan, 5% as a 20% levered AI bet).
  • A new service (name garbled in captions) that turns a 'take' into a trade shows the legal gap: SEC may call it an unregistered fiduciary, and a shutdown-able company may need robo-advisor registration.

Abra: tokenized yield turns Bitcoin collateral into a near-free loan

Abra now acts as an RIA itself and has issued yield-bearing tokens that can be used as collateral across DeFi apps.

  • Services: custody of Bitcoin and other assets, yield on stablecoins, Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana staking; borrowing against crypto is its fastest-growing business.
  • Products: BTCAF and USDAF tokens (Bitcoin in, more Bitcoin out; dollars in, more circle or tether out), tradable and usable as collateral.
  • Math: DeFi borrowing against Bitcoin costs ~4.5% vs 3-4% Abra yield, so the loan is 'close to free' (no yield promised for next year).
  • Distribution: tokens launch on Solana first, solving the Bitcoin interoperability problem (Hyperliquid and Aave 'know nothing about Bitcoin'); dApps for lending and futures coming this year.
  • Roadmap: tokenized stocks with yield (partners include exchanges; QQQ borrowing cited), then collateral for futures, payments and prediction markets in one commingled portfolio.
  • He frames Abra as a step-by-step 'neo-banking stack' and says RIAs need not understand smart contracts to use it.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Global M2 vs Bitcoin — Chart overlay comparing the world money supply to Bitcoin's price, used to argue Bitcoin lags liquidity.
60/40 problem — Barhydt's term for advisors keeping clients in 60% stocks and 40% bonds; selling the bond sleeve into Bitcoin would push up yields.
RIA — Registered investment advisor, a fiduciary wealth manager regulated by the SEC.
Tokenized yield — A token (for example BTCAF) that earns yield itself and can be used as collateral on other apps.
Perps — Perpetual futures, derivatives with no expiry that crypto venues offer.
