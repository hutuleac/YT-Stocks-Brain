Conversation with Ayar Labs CEO Mark Wade on Silicon Photonics, CPO, and Next-Gen AI Infrastructure

Channel: The Circuit · Speaker: Ben Bajarin, Jay Goldberg, Mark Wade (Ayar Labs CEO), Jesse Leiter (Ayar Labs) · 2026-09-21
5 threads · interconnect as the AI bottleneck · why optics is hard · TSMC COUPE bet · customers, timing 2027-2031 · funding and IPO path · Watch on YouTube ↗
The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Mark Wade (Ayar Labs CEO) argues AI data-center revenue is gated by interconnect performance, and only optical I/O fixes the electrical bandwidth-distance trade-off.
  2. Optics is hard because it stacks materials, devices, lasers, CMOS and advanced packaging; his optical engine uses a 3nm EIC in the TSMC COUPE process.
  3. Ayar is fabless and bets that photonics becomes a foundry-plus-OSAT feature, like HBM integration, by 2030+.
  4. Wade expects leading GPU vendors to ship CPO in 2028-2029 and the rest of accelerators in 2029-2031; 2027-28 is a chaotic stopgap period.
  5. Ayar's cap table includes NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, TSMC and now Wiwynn; total capital on the balance sheet topped $650M.
  6. Market size: optics in scale-up is tens of billions to ~$100B per Wade; Ben cites $30-50B by 2030.
  7. IPO intercept is timed to the 2028-2029 ramp; no firm IPO date is given.
High conviction AYAR LABS' CORE THESISai-infrasemis

The interconnect is the wall limiting AI data-center revenue

Wade says copper's bandwidth-distance trade-off caps GPU scale-up, and optics inside the package is the only first-principles fix.

  • Ben: GPUs and even memory are scaling faster than the interconnect; "the interconnect is a wall".
  • Wade's claim: data-center revenue scaling is limited by interconnect performance in the scale-up fabric (tokens per second per dollar per watt).
  • Electrical links: the faster the data, the shorter the distance; optics solves it "from the ground up".
  • Optical TAM is 10-100x the datacom volumes the optics industry used to serve; ChatGPT's release was the wake-up call that made connectivity-gated revenue a big enough market.
  • Wade's spreadsheet math: tens of billions to approaching $100B market; Ben quotes $30-50B by 2030.
  • Founded out of MIT in 2015; first processor-to-DRAM optical interface published in Nature in 2015.

Optics is hard because every layer is hard and they must all work together

Difficult on difficult: advanced CMOS plus advanced packaging plus a new photonics process plus fiber and lasers.

  • Telecom had specialized firms per domain (laser, DSP, optical device); AI scale-up needs all of it integrated into one product.
  • Ayar's optical engine = a leading-edge 3nm EIC with hundreds of millions of transistors, built via TSMC's COUPE process.
  • Adds to already-scarce CoWoS-class, multi-reticle advanced packages; then an optical connector must attach to the package.
  • Foundry photonics wafer "just isn't that hard": silicon waveguides, silicon nitride, germanium detectors; fiber attach is the hardest piece.
  • Current crunch points: laser manufacturing, production test architecture and wafer/assembly-level testing.
  • Wade is building a digital-twin manufacturing model with coding agents (a DAG with lazy evaluation) to optimize test policy across five test insertions.
Pull quote
“If you want to build things in this area, you have to be a bit masochistic.”— Mark Wade
Contested TSMC COUPE VS OTHER FOUNDRIESsemis

Betting on TSMC COUPE and a foundry-plus-OSAT supply chain

Ayar chose the TSMC route because optical I/O only matters at volume scale inside leading-edge packages.

  • Ayar has worked on TSMC COUPE for approaching four years and has "lots of wafers flowing" from the fab.
  • Ben relays whispers that TSMC is immature in photonics and that Tower and GlobalFoundries are further along; Wade says a photonics chip from another foundry works for pluggables and NPO modules but not for scale-up attach.
  • Ayar is part of the NVIDIA ecosystem, attaching to NVLink and NVSwitch.
  • Wade says finance people track progress weekly or monthly while tech progresses quarterly or yearly.
  • Ayar is fabless; Wade hopes for a rich foundry set in 10-20 years.
  • Success metric: how you build a product, supply chain and ecosystem, not the photonics chip alone.
Pull quote
“A photonics chip is nowhere near the final product form factor.”— Mark Wade

A chaotic 2027-28, then CPO rolls out 2028-2031

Leading GPU vendors adopt first; others follow once optical I/O is treated like HBM integration.

  • Short term: not in high-volume production, so customers scramble for 2027 or 2028 stopgaps; everyone optimizes for time to the first revenue-generating token.
  • Wade expects exciting leading-edge GPU announcements in 2028-2029, proliferation to the rest of the accelerator community in 2029-2031.
  • Beyond 2030, optics simplifies into a foundry-delivered advanced feature, ending the pluggable vs board-integrated vs co-packaged chaos.
  • ASIC-service route: close ties to MediaTek, Alchip and GUC let hyperscaler and startup accelerators integrate Ayar's engine at near-zero risk.
  • Jensen laid out a CPO switch roadmap at GTC, which prompted sell-side coverage on CPO for the first time.
  • Ben: we are far from giant clusters functioning as one computer; everyone is making trade-offs now.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

CPO — Co-packaged optics: optical engines mounted inside the processor or switch package.
Scale-up fabric — The high-bandwidth network linking GPUs and accelerators inside a rack-scale system.
COUPE — TSMC's compact universal photonic engine process that 3D-integrates an electrical IC with a photonics chip.
OSAT — Outsourced semiconductor assembly and test provider that packages post-foundry wafers.
EIC — Electronic integrated circuit: the CMOS chip driving the optical devices.
Bandwidth-distance trade-off — Electrical links lose reach as data rate rises, which optics avoids.
NPO — Near-packaged optics: optical modules placed beside, not inside, the package.
