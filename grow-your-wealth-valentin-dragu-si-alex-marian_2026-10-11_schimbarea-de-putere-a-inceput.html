Who gains from blocked Gulf oil, and who blew up Nord Stream

Valentin reads Middle East escalation as the US securing control of oil, the resource that still runs world industry.

  • Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia caused deaths. He says it's not market-moving yet, but the US reaction may depend on the midterm outcome.
  • Thesis: the strongest country must control oil to protect its currency. Iran retaliating by blocking exports to Europe and Asia does 'exactly what you need'.
  • Before the Iran conflict, a US delegation with Trump and business leaders went to Riyadh with plans that included data centers.
  • Nord Stream: per BRICS News, Trump wants to negotiate reopening it with Putin. Valentin says the US blew it up and is now selling the fix to its own problem.
  • Europe needs cheap Russian energy and casts Russia as the enemy to justify rearming, for example turning Volkswagen car lines into tank and gun lines. Alex thinks both sides use the narrative.
  • Valentin also argues 2nd- and 3rd-generation immigrants drive unrest in France and Germany, and warns Romania could face the same in 20-25 years.

Brazil tilting toward Washington would be a big loss for China

A Washington-friendly Brazil completes US control of the Americas and opens a huge market for US companies.

  • Alex: the political change in Brazil was awaited by the US. Stanley Druckenmiller had bought call options on the Brazilian index.
  • Argentina was bankrupt; Milei, backed by the Trump administration, saved it with US help. Brazil is far stronger: the most powerful state in South America.

  • Venezuela taken; Colombia, Panama, Honduras and Guatemala under the US umbrella; Ecuador to be brought back into control.
  • Brazil's former president blocked Starlink, costing SpaceX 'maybe 20-50 million' users. A friendly government lets it push in aggressively.
  • Analogy: after communism fell, Eastern Europe flipped from producer to consumer market for Western companies. Brazil could do the same for US firms.

  • Valentin still sees yields able to drop below 4.8% within about two weeks.
  • Alex: since Kevin Warsh's rise, the narrative is that Scott Bessent and Warsh have every tool needed. Valentin jokes the statistics bureau will produce whatever data they ask for.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

0DTE — Options expiring the same day they're traded, the most volatile day-trading instrument.
Lockup period — The months after an IPO when insiders can't sell; its expiry often brings heavy selling.
Unrealized loss — A drop in the market value of bonds a bank still holds, booked only if they're sold.
Low-band spectrum — Lower radio frequencies that carry signal further and through walls, used for broad internet coverage.
52-week low — A stock's lowest price of the past year; many stocks sit there while indices hit records.
