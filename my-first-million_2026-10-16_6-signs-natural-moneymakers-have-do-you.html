6 signs natural moneymakers have. Do you?

The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Sam Parr's term: a natural money maker brokers deals, builds quietly, and wins $100M-to-billions across unrelated fields — distinct from a great founder or CEO.
  2. Shaan Puri's markers: a high hit rate (succeed, succeed, fail, succeed) and instinct over explanation — 'a great nose'.
  3. Sam's attributes: unemotional about business, relentless maximizers ('diminishing returns are still returns'), and comfortable with complexity.
  4. Shaan's correction: they don't see complexity — the reason to act fits on a fortune cookie, and they find the simple path to a hard execution.
  5. Money rules: Syed Balkhi's mentor — spend only the 'second derivative' (investment returns, never income); Sam keeps his annual budget under 3% of liquid net worth.
  6. Shaan's caution: past a point they trade valuable hours for useless dollars; he took up piano to be a beginner at something.
  7. Other play styles: missionary, extreme balancer, tank, trickster, fierce nerd, deep student, and the sniffer — conviction arbitrage on ideas before the world catches up.
Framework SAM PARR'S COINED TERMmindsetfinance

The natural money maker: deal brokers who win across fields by smell, not skill

A separate species from the great founder — less vision and passion, more nose for where money will be made or lost.

  • Sam's definition: involved in dealmaking and brokering, inserts themselves into situations, doesn't build publicly, and has built businesses from $100 million to billions in multiple fields.
  • Sam calls himself a decent entrepreneur, not a natural money maker; neither was his hero Ted Turner (CNN) — vision and recruiting, but not the cold rationality.
  • Shaan: a high hit rate that defies normal batting averages, plus instinct they can't put into words — his buddy Sulie as the example.
  • Nick Kokonas (on an upcoming Shaan episode): philosophy grad turned trader who'd win or lose ~$300K a day; his firm made $100 million in its best year off one early-ETF arbitrage, running fiber from New York to Chicago to beat 30-second paper-slip quotes.
  • Kokonas then partnered with chef Grant to build Alinea, declaring it would be America's best restaurant: ~$26M a year revenue at almost 20% profit at peak, profitable through COVID on $35 takeaway dinners.
  • Still at it: right after recording he described buying a million-dollar wine collection for $150K.
  • Charlie Munger fits too, per Shaan: still doing deals weeks before he died; money from Berkshire, real estate, a law practice, newspapers.
Pull quote
“They just sort of sniff out [ __ ] where [ __ ] is. is they sniff out opportunity where opportunity is and they win across multiple categories.”— Shaan Puri

Framework THREE ATTRIBUTES · ONE DISPUTEDmindsetfinance

Cold, maximizing, and unbothered by complexity — or just seeing it as simple

Sam's attributes hold up, but Shaan reframes the complexity one: the reason is simple, the execution hard, and they find the simple version.

  • Unemotional: Carl Icahn bought (Sam believes) a shipping company, fired 12 floors of people within two weeks, and months later nobody had noticed they were gone.
  • Maximizers: a wealthy mutual friend, on his phone at dinner, buying defaulted credit-card debt for ~14% returns; Sam just buys index funds and calls direct indexing's tiny spread 'too much trouble'.
  • Jay Gould — whom Rockefeller called the greatest business mind — dying of tuberculosis, told his family he had to 'return to my first love, which is making money', bought a failing railroad and died ~6 months later.
  • Complexity: Sam sees very high IQs and deals full of contingencies; Syed Balkhi went from a WordPress blog to plugins, real estate, stocks, private companies and $200,000 gas stations despite being worth hundreds of millions.
  • Shaan's counter: a DTC founder facing rising COGS on 6-14% EBITDA margins found a European factory, asked the machine price and two-month payback, paid them to install it in the US — COGS halved within a year.
  • Balkhi on gas stations: 'a hard corner in a good area' on a triple net lease — nothing to manage. Shaan: 'I'm confusing uncertainty with risk... unfamiliarity with complexity.'
  • What separates smart non-money-makers, per Sam: a competitive 'gene of wanting to conquer' from a young age (Napoleon: 'descendants of conquerors').
Pull quote
“They don't need to write a six-page memo to convince themselves. The logic fits on a fortune cookie.”— Shaan Puri
Worth noting
The two hosts disagree on whether natural money makers embrace complexity (Sam) or simply don't perceive it (Shaan).
Recommendation PERSONAL FINANCE RULESfinancefamily

Spend the second derivative: live off investment returns, never income

Invest all income and let lifestyle grow only with the gains — a 'never go broke' rule that compounds into living off returns in 5-7 years.

  • Syed Balkhi buys a gas station every time his wife gets pregnant — the station pays for the kid.
  • His mentor's rule: on $100K income, invest the $100K and spend only what it earns (~$10K), adding another $100K each year until the gains cover your life.
  • Sam's simpler version: annual budget under 3% of liquid net worth, so it can never run out. Shaan: same idea — don't touch the principal.
  • The test, per Shaan: as income rose, did you upgrade your lifestyle, or invest and upgrade only with the gains?
Counterintuitive take SHAAN PURImindsetfamilyrelationships

Natural money makers aren't misers — but many trade valuable hours for useless dollars

Happiness spreads across this group like any other; the real trap is staying in the feedback loop you're good at instead of the life areas you're bad at.

  • Sam sees no link between the type and unhappiness: friend Val is happy and generous; others are 'pains in the asses' and misers. Brian Johnson says he's happy skipping birthday cake.
  • Dismissing disciplined people as 'no fun' is a defensive reaction to guilt, per Shaan — get curious and steal what fits instead.
  • Past the point money improves life, they keep trading time for money they'll never spend, justified as 'for my kids' — who mostly want them home.
  • Shaan's own avoidance: reading the next article over going for a run; asking how to add 20% more fun and spark to his marriage instead of more portfolio dollars.
  • He became a beginner piano player last year — no money, no status, sucking for months — and it added a new kind of joy.
Pull quote
“You're trading valuable hours for useless dollars.”— Shaan Puri
Framework OFF-THE-CUFF ARCHETYPESmindsetcareer

Sniffer, tank, trickster, missionary: other ways to win the game

Natural money maker is one play style among many; Ray Dalio's point is to work with your nature rather than fight it.

  • Missionary: Brian Chesky, dedicated to Airbnb and making travel good. Extreme balancer: Jesse Itzler, regimented about adventures and family (Sam doesn't class him as a money maker).
  • Tank (Shaan, Overwatch's Reinhardt): Frank Slootman — relentless, operational, huge pain tolerance and span of control.
  • Trickster/magician: Alex Tew (Million Dollar Homepage, then Calm, whose first app said 'do nothing for two minutes'), Pet Rock, a $30 star-naming registry making millions; Eric Ryan (Method soap, Olly vitamins, Welly bandages).
  • Fierce nerd (Paul Graham's essay): Zuckerberg and Travis Kalanick — nerdy, but ferocious on their own battlefield. Deep student: Dan Carlin, David Senra.
  • Sniffer: Morning Brew's Austin Rief told Sam ~seven non-obvious things early that proved right. Val put Sam into Bitcoin in 2014 and called stablecoins, privacy coins and prediction markets before the terms were mainstream.
  • Shaan: the Napster-era investor who made almost as much off Facebook as Zuckerberg and emailed Spotify's founders believing in it before they did — 'conviction arbitrage'.
  • Ray Dalio's case for personality tests: 'Do you think you'll have more success fighting your nature or working with your nature?'
Pull quote
“It's a conviction arbitrage. You you you believe before everybody else believes.”— Shaan Puri
Worth noting
Sniffers differ from money makers: enthusiastic, high-conviction, and they share; natural money makers 'shut up'.
What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

💸
Finance: Invest income first and grow lifestyle only from the returns — or cap spending near 3% of liquid net worth.
🧭
Decisions: Before calling something complex, check whether it's only unfamiliar — find who has already done it and ask the payback.
🎮
Mindset: Name your play style and borrow single tools from the others instead of envying them.
⏳
Life: Audit where more money no longer improves your life, and move those hours to an area you're bad at.
👃
Investing: Keep a list of the sniffers around you and act on their early calls instead of dismissing them.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Natural money maker — Sam Parr's term for a cold, rational dealmaker who wins repeatedly across unrelated fields.
Second derivative (spending) — Spending only the returns earned on invested income, never the income itself.
Triple net lease — A lease where the tenant pays taxes, insurance and maintenance, leaving the owner little to manage.
Direct indexing — Owning an index's stocks individually to capture small tax or cost advantages.
Conviction arbitrage — Profiting by believing in an opportunity before the market does.
COGS — Cost of goods sold — the direct cost of producing what a company sells.
