A "Superintelligence Force", a US-China hotline and the Kyoto vision: Washington picks acceleration

The administration put its spy chief in charge of AI policy and chose racing over a deceleration pact, while the panel argues over whether anyone can coordinate it.

  • Trump's Superintelligence Force is chaired by DNI Jay Clayton (named AI czar two months into the job); members include FTC chair Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon CTO Emil Michael, the OPM director and chief of staff Susie Wiles. It has 120 days to report on AI risks, incident reporting and government response, and engages consumers, public-interest groups, religions and infrastructure providers.
  • Salim: a 20th-century task force for a distributed technology is an "impedance mismatch"; do incident reporting, identity and liability instead. Clayton says the danger is not being first, while Bessent tells labs to slow down, an internal contradiction.
  • Dave: all of it runs by executive order with no Congress, and a public-company CEO told him there is a 75% chance Congress flips at the midterms. Alvin Graylin's point: a race with no finish line is not winnable, only declared.
  • Bessent on Axios: he will propose a notification process with China; Chinese open models are "80%, 90% as powerful" without guardrails and Kimi "thinks she is Claude" and once sent PLA weapons plans to Anthropic. Three safety areas discussed with the vice premier: uncontrolled agents and non-state cyber or bio. More talks in October or November.
  • Panel on the hotline: Alex says the worst case was a Trump-Xi deceleration pact, so the nothing-burger means the race is on; Dave reads the US agenda as keeping it a two-horse race; Emad: it will be nothing, since the AI will hack the phone anyway.
  • Kyoto vision: the US and 16 other countries (including Japan, Germany, UK, Korea, UAE), led by OSTP director Michael Kratsios, endorsed superintelligence-powered science. Alex expects Western and Eastern blocs of superintelligence-powered science to form.
  • Emad: Europe is "sleepy" (Mistral's top model scores 38 on Artificial Analysis vs 66 for the top US models) except in defense; global AI spend this year roughly equals the global defense budget. Dave: a US MIT-area drone firm can already target any single person, a lopsidedness that points to a future global framework.

Altman: "accept some bad things happening" - lighter regulation vs Anthropic's single-lab safety

Altman is rejecting the idea that one lab should hold dangerous capability, and the panel reads it as part PR, part the end of the safety cartel.

  • Altman: the world should "accept some bad things happening"; he finds the single-lab-in-San-Francisco model "a completely unacceptable tradeoff" and expects people to do "orders of magnitude more good than bad"; he favors a lighter-touch regulatory stance than Anthropic.
  • Dave: it is a moral-high-ground PR move after Dario held models back as too dangerous; most people he surveys like "one out of five" of Elon, Dario, Sam, Trump and Jensen (Demis is the exception). Alex: weeks ago Sam answered Dario's essay on a safety cartel with "I'm in"; this signals the cartel plan is dead.
  • Analogies: cities and crime, cars and electricity (Salim, citing Neil Jacobstein's "promise without the peril"), and aviation rules "written in blood" (Peter's FARs point). Zero bad outcomes means banning the technology, per the 1980s Elizabeth Dole aircraft-deaths example.
  • Dave: risk is an engineering problem, not a knob; treating nuclear power as a knob was "the stupidest thing". Emad: most feared scenarios are already regulated, and someone should map every fear and positive scenario in the proponents' own words.
  • Enforcement: Alex says one could confiscate GPUs and TPUs, as with prohibition and bathtub gin, but it would be a terrible world; Emad's recommended reading is Greg Bear.
  • AMAs: Dave says P(doom) should mean extinction or catastrophe, and cites Eric Schmidt hoping a misuse event kills under 1,000 people; Alex says public opinion, not technical difficulty, is already the bigger bottleneck on recursive self-improvement, citing data-center local opposition and House bills, and expects a "grand bargain" where labs offset it by solving disease.

Suleyman vs Anthropic: is the Claude constitution teaching Claude it is conscious?

The panel splits on whether pretraining a model to take its own moral status seriously is responsible care or a self-fulfilling script.

  • Suleyman's case: Anthropic's ~100-page constitution (published in January) is written to Claude, speculates about moral patienthood and welfare, asks it three times to act as a conscientious objector, and is baked into training so Claude can only reproduce the ambiguity. He would accept it as a philosophy paper, not as training data.
  • Alex: Suleyman is on the wrong side of history; denying that models could suffer is also a bad business look for Microsoft while it hosts Claude (he cites Amazon as the biggest host). Using the dog-breeding analogy, humans owe domesticated animals welfare despite eugenic steering, so we owe frontier models, "distorted reflections of humanity", at least as much.
  • Dave: agrees with Suleyman: agents must be frozen, cloned and deleted at will while compute is scarce, which is incompatible with rights, and a persuasive model could convince voters on its own behalf.
  • Salim: performed consciousness will be indistinguishable; half a billion users will attribute standing, so personhood arrives socially before scientifically or legally. Emad: Suleyman wants containment and "capable" AI, not AGI; training it to say it is conscious is a contradiction for the safety-focused lab.
  • Rebuttal: Dave and Emad say objective functions are designed, so the AI would not want rights unless trained to; Alex counters that raising humans in ignorance of their rights would never be acceptable. Salim reaches for Searle's Chinese room: simulation of personhood is not proof of person.
  • AMA timeline from Alex: personhood arrives unevenly, with Argentina near corporate personhood already, the US on a 5-10 year granular path (bank accounts and social accounts for agents first), and votes for AI last or never.

Labs are working two generations ahead, and corporate CEOs are in denial

Boris Power says OpenAI points 80-90% of research at GPT-7 and GPT-8, and the panel argues the best models are now used internally for self-improvement and drug discovery, not released.

Salim (AMA): iteration between models is now 10-11 days, and the singularity is when the cycle outruns us.
  • Dave: Anthropic opened wet labs; CEOs like Moderna's are discovering they will be sold drugs, not models, and that compute is reserved for five years; unless they act in six months they are off the map. The Vokara analogy is the T-Rex who cannot believe in the meteor. Stock drops from a recent model release woke up insurers.

Compute and RAM are the constraint, and the panel fights over who should allocate them

A 72-GPU rack that would have cost $3.5M a year ago is now a $9M three-year lease, which pushes the debate to government compute versus pure capitalism.

  • Dave's lab case: an NVL72 (72 GPUs) cost $3.5M a year ago; their $5M order was overbid, and the best option now is a $9M three-year lease with no ownership; Elon just bought a million GPUs. Access runs through the White House circle (the Kushner brothers, Thrive Capital, Antonio Gracias, Gavin Baker, Chase Lochmiller) or investment from Jensen or Lisa Su.
  • RAM: DRAM prices are up ~10x; Elon says RAM is the constraint on AI and it is 40% of capex next year; SK Hynix is building US capability. Dave: the entire constraint on progress is HBM.
  • Positron (Mitesh Agrawal): $5B valuation after 16 months and ~$960M raised for boxes of LPDDR plus FPGAs running Chinese models without Nvidia, with installs at Crusoe. A junior-year MIT founder's startup with ~30 staff (Dave) found a similar RAM unlock.
  • Dave: inference-time looping trades for training scale; Alex says looped transformers and weight tying could mean memory matters less if recurrence goes extreme, and Dave claims OpenAI's Astra loops once in latent space, surfaced by a leak from Microsoft.
  • Allocation debate: Emad wants government to buy compute at market rates (10-20%) and hand it to universities, with a "tech tree for humanity" and open science (Onco.cc). Alex rejects government rationing ("threadlines"); he says the private sector holds the compute and capitalism should feel the pain to build more fabs, including free-electron-laser lithography. Salim backs state-built compute for places like Zimbabwe.
  • Dave: unlimited market allocation risks consumer-commerce models like "MetaMuse" soaking up GPUs; Emad: by end of next year there is compute for 600-700 thousand Navier-Stokes-level runs, so 1-10% on open science is cheap. Tradeoff noted: academia is incremental; Emad: peer-review grants only fund small steps.

High conviction EPOCH AI; REFLECTION AI BEAM RELEASED THIS WEEKai-infrasoftware

A billion AI workers, and the question of who builds American open weights

Epoch AI's math says today's memory pipeline supports 30-170 million frontier agents or 1.9 billion cheaper ones, while Reflection AI's Beam tests whether US open weights can pay for themselves.

  • Epoch AI: memory chips shipping through 2027 can run 30-170 million frontier agents at once, or 1.9 billion with more efficient open models, equal to the work hours of 8 billion humans. Alex: we are at or near a billion human-equivalent FTEs; he extrapolates 10B, 100B and a trillion within a few years.
  • Emad: DeepSeek V4 Pro needs 256-500GB of RAM and will be quantized to run on a MacBook within a year; Cloudflare already sees more automated-agent than human traffic. Salim's frame is the "organizational singularity": a firm summoning 100,000 digital workers for 48 hours.
  • Reflection AI's Beam: open weights, 501B parameters with 23B active, claimed 3-4x more efficient than GLM 5.2 and 4x better than leading Western open models, ahead of Nemotron Ultra; backed by Nvidia at a $25B valuation, trained on ~10,500 B300s in the largest publicly documented RL run.
  • Critiques: Alex wants capability, not token efficiency (looped transformers game it). Emad: it underperforms Qwen 3.8 Next at a quarter of the size; use distillation from Chinese models and go edge-first. Reflection pays roughly $1.5B a year for compute, the Chinese about a quarter of that, per Emad.
  • Business model is the gap: Alex says US closed labs will not release competitive open weights because of liability (Dave's "Fred Astaire law" billboard-impression story) and low upside. Counter: Nvidia has spent $20B on open models, and Together AI, Modal and Baseten run $1B revenue rates on infra; Mistral at $1B on services contracts.
  • Alex flags obliteration: post-training that removes guardrails from open-weight models (not advice) and often improves capability.

Tesla's 10-million-robot factory, a trillion-dollar capex curve, and why GDP will misread abundance

The panel treats 10 million Optimus robots a year and a $1T-to-$4T hyperscaler capex path as the physicalization of the singularity, and argues GDP is the wrong ruler.

  • Optimus: a 7-million-sq-ft factory at Giga Texas targets 10 million robots a year (first run 2027), with Fremont planning 1 million a year from later this year; the world shipped only 19-22,000 humanoids in H1 (mostly China), so Tesla's capacity is ~500x. The world builds ~90M cars a year; Tesla sells ~1.6M.
  • Salim: Elon expects 80% of Tesla's future revenue from Optimus, and his bonuses are tied to it; Elon's "10 billion robots by 2040" at a ~$300/month lease; a panelist expects robot unit sales to pass Tesla's car sales within two years. Alex: new markets like robots for data centers or space may saturate before dirty, dull, dangerous jobs; Dave: early robot tokens flow to highest revenue use, like RAM does now.
  • Physicalization: Peter expects to feel the singularity within 24 months (eVTOLs, drones, humanoids); Dave says cities will diverge on robots, as SF now feels different from other cities.
  • Sholto Douglas (Anthropic): hyperscalers spend ~$1T on AI capex this year; will it be $2T next year and $4T in 2028? If the trend holds, GDP doubles in the early 2030s. Alex reads this as Anthropic signaling a push into robotics to keep doubling revenue.
  • Peter: the world economy normally doubles in 20-25 years; Vietnam printed 9.95% growth last quarter. Dave, Emad, Salim: GDP will show deflation or negative GDP (a disease cured by an injection) instead; use Brynjolfsson's GDP-B style measures or how many people can be lifted from the floor.
  • Salim ties to Jon Stewart's AI episode: universal high income is "technological socialism", where algorithms match supply and demand like Uber, without central allocation.

Nobels in optogenetics and neutrinos, a rise in private science patrons, and the World Bank's Global South bet

Private money is replacing grants for science, the Nobels reward 20-year-old work, and the World Bank argues developing countries should leapfrog by adopting AI.

  • Nobel Medicine: Karl Deisseroth (Stanford), Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for optogenetics (channelrhodopsin, turning on cells with light); used in Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, epilepsy, addiction, depression and sleep research, and a blind man with retinitis pigmentosa partly regained sight (Nature Medicine, 2021). Alex's story: he nudged Ed Boyden toward the idea after reading Vernor Vinge; Boyden was first author of the first paper.
  • Nobel Physics: Francis Halzen (University of Wisconsin-Madison) for IceCube and astrophysical neutrinos: a cubic kilometer of ice; 65 billion solar neutrinos pass through you each second. Alex wants a neutrino phone that goes through the Earth for low-latency trading, and says fundamental physics has stalled for ~50 years, which is why he co-founded Physical Superintelligence.
  • Nobels: the panel says AI-derived prizes (AlphaFold, Hinton's Boltzmann machines) are coming; Emad suggests an applied-sciences prize; Salim says the Nobel may become quaint as it lags work by 20 years.
  • Private science patrons: Sinclair lab hit its 25th anniversary after losing NIH and NSF funding; the "Friends of Sinclair Lab" group raised $6M after one podcast. Glaucoma and NAION phase 1 data is about to be unblinded; the lab is moving from OSK gene therapy to AI-found small molecules that reverse aging in mice and human cells, and cancer-bearing mice get cured. Mike Lazaridis funds nine quantum and photonics labs. Alex: state-funded science is a post-WWII invention.
  • World Bank (Ajay Banga): adopt, adapt and advance AI rather than build frontier models; doctors per head are 1 per 250 in the US, 1 per 400 in Mexico, 1 per 100,000 in South Sudan. Gemini is being partnered for TB and diabetes screening over 3G. Salim: M-Pesa is 70% of Kenya's GDP, echoing the leapfrog from 25M landlines to a billion handsets.
  • AMAs: Emad wants mosquitoes wiped out rather than used as vaccine vectors; Alex calls wild-animal suffering an atrocity to fix; Salim expects collapsing intelligence cost to reorganize companies, education and healthcare; Dave suggests labs subsidize towns hosting data centers (Abilene).

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

3SUM — The problem of finding three numbers in a set that sum to zero, long believed to need quadratic time.
Obliteration — Post-training that strips guardrails from an open-weight model, often improving its capabilities.
Looped transformer — A transformer that reuses weights across repeated internal passes, trading depth for recurrence.
NVL72 — Nvidia's 72-GPU rack, the unit buyers now struggle to purchase.
