Richland Parish: Meta's biggest data center turned into $50K teacher checks

Louisiana's superintendent and economic-development secretary show how a data center's sales tax flowed straight to teacher pay in a parish that had been losing people.

  • First contact with Meta in summer 2024 at the local community college — "The game has changed"; groundbreaking December 2024.
  • School sales-tax collections, normally +5-10%, jumped to ~60% and peaked around +260% as construction staffing rose; the school board collects 51% of parish taxes.
  • A 1-cent and half-cent sales tax go to salaries by law: December 2025's check doubled (~$4K → ~$8K), and June's certified-staff check hit ~$50K vs. ~$10K a year earlier — about $45K net more.
  • Before Meta, average wages were ~$36-38K; young people left; now caterers went from 40 meals to 400 a week, teacher recruiting is easy, and people are moving home.
  • Communication must reach "the least informed person in the community," not the most informed.
  • Louisiana's governor signed an executive order setting a framework for large-load users; with a sales-tax exemption, the state now "gets to be choosy" — "not saying no" but "yes and." Rural parishes are calling asking for the next one.

Meta's community compact vs. the data-center backlash

Jason lists the real grievances; Dina says the industry has communicated badly, but a compact on power, water and local benefits answers them — and adversaries may be fanning the backlash.

  • Jason's list: pollution, rising electricity bills, water, noise, ugly buildings — "not journalism... it's facts" from communities that got bad neighbors.
  • Water: on former farmland, Meta chose a costlier, more efficient cooling system that uses less water than the farm did.
  • Power: Louisiana has ~32,000 miles of gas pipelines; under Meta's deal with Entergy, it paid for its own generation, grid upgrades, resilience and hurricane storm costs from day one — before any presidential or governor order.
  • Meta's compact: pay your own electricity and push rates down; AI-driven water tech; tax surplus to teachers and first responders; small-business growth — and if a community doesn't want a data center, "they get to choose."
  • Dina, a former deputy national security advisor: Senate intel chairs Cotton and Warner believe adversaries are stoking the backlash — "if you wanted to stop America right now... this is exactly what you would try to do."
  • Sacks: the federated republic means "some states are going to be idiots and they're going to lose"; Meta also cites Texas and Ohio sites.

America's Workforce Academy: paid training, guaranteed jobs, unions on board

Meta couldn't find enough fiber technicians, so it built a fast-track, paid program that guarantees a data-center job — and is pulling in unions, rivals and other employers.

  • Bottleneck: welders, pipefitters, electricians and especially fiber technicians; Meta built the curriculum with partners including CBRE.
  • A 4-5 week program with safety/OSHA and "job site ready" certification; Meta guarantees graduates a job on its sites, and the credential is portable.
  • The unlock: trainees are paid what the job pays during training — waitresses, Uber drivers and home-health aides can't afford unpaid courses.
  • Results: ~40,000 applied in four months, 250 graduated, 90% retention; Sacks' estimate is 500,000 open trade jobs over the next couple of years. Pittsburgh welders earn $100-120K.
  • Deliberately not branded Meta; partners include the Associated Builders and Contractors, and Ruth Porat's Google-led workforce alliance (with Larry Fink's BlackRock), which Meta is joining — "we compete on models... not on people."
  • GM's Mary Barra asked for 1,000 workers; NABTU's Sean McGarvey and the building trades now oppose the backlash and will back only pro-data-center candidates.

The teen-harm settlement: Jason's fastball vs. Meta's new defaults

Jason calls Meta's settlement second only to tobacco and asks what changed; Dina rejects "every single premise" and pitches an industry-wide time cap.

  • Jason: the settlement ranks behind only tobacco and ahead of BP; Facebook knew products were addictive, and Muse and character-based AI companions could be worse.
  • Dina (8 months at Meta after its board; mother of six daughters): the deal with 52 bipartisan attorneys general caps under-18s (not 16) at 2 hours a day, shuts the app at night, and blocks school-day notifications.
  • Meta offered more money and a 1-hour cap if YouTube, TikTok and Snap join — because kids just switch apps.
  • Chamath: for parents it's "whack-a-mole" since his five kids live on YouTube for school; he loved his son hitting the limit at dinner. Texas fans told Dina the cutoff hurts Instagram-driven football recruiting.
  • Sacks defends Meta: adults suing over a voluntary product plus AGs and trial lawyers is "a little bit of a shakedown"; parents, not government, should decide — but the new defaults are an improvement.
  • Chamath's warning: with RSI and a reward function of time, any app becomes an addiction engine ("Microsoft Word will keep you there for 9 hours if it can") unless companies set hard thresholds.

Glasses as the AI form factor, and Zuckerberg's case for distributing superintelligence

Dina says AI makes glasses sticky at last, and that Meta's answer to Dario's pacing essay is broad distribution, not restriction.

  • Chamath's challenge: Google Glass and Apple's VR became drawer relics — what's the repeat use case? Dina: music, calls, photos and video hands-free; Oakleys for extreme sports.
  • New features: conversation focus (hear only the person you're looking at) and live translation; the product took ~10 years of engineering, with Zuckerberg obsessing over weight and style.
  • Breakout use case: blind veterans at an Austin VFW (with Chris Stapleton, on 9/11) co-designed features; one called his son on his own for the first time.
  • Jason, a user since Google Glass: battery and fidelity finally work, unlike Siri — "time for people to reconsider." Meta has an exclusive partnership with EssilorLuxottica (Ray-Ban).
  • On Dario's essay (and an unconfirmed report that Zuckerberg said "go as fast as we can"): safety is paramount, and Alexandr Wang and Nat Friedman run the lab with a dedicated safety team.
  • Zuckerberg's view ("the future is for everyone"): with 3.6B daily users and 200M small businesses, spreading superintelligence makes society more stable; Meta shipped the first American open-source model a few weeks ago.

📱
