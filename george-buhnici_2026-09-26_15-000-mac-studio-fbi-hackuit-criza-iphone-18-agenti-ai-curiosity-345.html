Confirmed event RELEASED THIS WEEK — OPUS 5, GROK 4.7, GPT-6 SOL/LUNA, GEMINI 3.8 FLASHsoftwareai-infra

Models get cheaper and sharper at once: Opus 5, GPT-6 Sol/Luna and Grok 4.7 start a price war

For the first time in months a heavy user didn't burn through the usage window, because efficient models now carry frontier-grade work.

  • Opus 5 (Anthropic): at least 40% more efficient than the previous generation, 'works like Fable at half the price', $4 input / $20 output per million tokens; Buhnici moved most of his coding there and kept 10% usage unused.
  • Fable 5.1 is very good but consumes heavily; Sonnet 5.5 costs only 30% more than Sonnet 5, which he runs for the store and site agents that talk to customers.
  • GPT-6 Sol (OpenAI): $2 input / $10 output, claimed to deliver 90-95% of Astra at one fifth of the cost per task; GPT-6 Luna, the Sonnet-5-class tier, is $0.10 / $0.50 — about 30x cheaper than the same capability two months ago.
  • Grok 4.7 (xAI, released September 21): 2.1 trillion parameters, 40% better than its predecessor, context cut from 1M to 500k tokens, price unchanged at $2 / $6; Buhnici doesn't count it as frontier but it powers Grok Bot.
  • Google shipped Gemini 3.8 Flash, text-to-speech and a voice-to-voice model, with no Pro newer than 3.1 yet; voice cloning is available everywhere except Europe, and Buhnici chose not to clone voices in his own content.
  • Cost tip: caching cuts costs ~60%, so start a new task per job in Claude Code instead of piling onto the last window, where every command re-reads the whole prior context.
  • Race is now price per token between the US labs, the Chinese labs and whoever owns hardware; Buhnici expects costs to fall another 10-20x by year-end while memory gets dearer.

Mac Studio becomes the local-AI box while RAM stays locked up for two years

With 1.2 TB/s memory bandwidth Apple has the only machine for 500 GB local models, and the RAM supply behind it won't loosen.

  • Hardware rule of thumb: 32 GB RAM, take care of it; 128 GB, you're strong; 192 GB and up, you're emperor.

Romanian IT firms lay off after switching to Claude Code: market forces or bad bosses?

The host argues competition pushes every firm toward efficiency, while the co-host insists revenue and sales come first.

  • A Romanian IT manager cut 10 people last month, 5-10 more this week and ~10 yesterday after giving everyone a monthly Claude Code subscription; several laid-off staff offered to work for 'anything above zero'.

  • Radu: the first fix should be sales and customer loss, not staff; replacing people with an AI trained by them is shortsighted, and a new hire costs more and takes long to integrate.

  • Labor is heavily taxed — nearly double the pay for an employer; one of his suppliers raises tariffs 36% from October 1.
  • Best case: employer and employee ask each other what they want; unsaid things don't exist. He invites comments, 'treat them like Glassdoor, no IPs'.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

TCO (total cost of ownership) — Purchase price minus resale plus running costs, divided by kilometres driven, giving the true cost per km.
Prosumer — A household that both consumes and sells electricity, typically via rooftop solar.
Foveated rendering — A display technique that renders full detail only where the eye looks.
Pixels per degree (PPD) — Angular resolution of a headset; about 60 PPD is the limit at which pixels vanish for 20/20 vision.
Headless agent — An AI agent with no app or interface of its own, reached through messaging such as WhatsApp or iMessage.
Prompt caching — Reusing an already-processed context so repeat calls cost far less.
