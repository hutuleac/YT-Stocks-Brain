Anthropic's S-1: $16.3B half-year revenue, an operating profit, and a $1.5-2T ask

The filing was framed to calm the profitability debate, and the panel mostly accepts it while arguing over the price.

  • Public S-1 for a NASDAQ listing as ANT, price range and share count left blank; H1 2026 revenue $16.3B vs ~$1.2B in H1 2025 (14x); Q2 operating profit $974M.
  • Net loss $80.7B, of which $72.4B is a non-cash change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, mostly tied to the Amazon and Google note purchase agreements; it is re-measured every period and added back on the cash-flow statement.
  • WSJ: listing targeted the week of November 16, raising $100B or more at ~$1.5-2T.
  • Wallach: the timing was meant to put investors at ease on inference profitability — "sitting on a ton of cash"; still only "fragmentary views of the elephant" from every Gavin Baker interview and All-In episode.
  • tickertrends.com shows ARR flat at $76.8B after steep January-July growth (Harris: ARR here is annualized run rate, not recurring revenue); Replit recently switched its default model to OpenAI.
  • McClure: two to three trillion would have been easy last quarter; his partner's teardown shows at least $5B of new revenue in the quarter; he expects $150-200B run rate next year, not the $200-300B once floated.
  • Black: AI tear-downs of the S-1 appeared within hours; SaaS tools like ARR don't fit a business selling compute across models with falling costs, and "who wins the framing in the next two weeks" decides whether $1.5T holds.

Is intelligence the new cigarettes? The 0.1% spend gap

Customers spend about a tenth of a percent of opex on AI; Anthropic reads that as headroom, while Wallach sees a usefulness question.

  • Calacanis: formed in 2021, revenue from 2023, now profitable — "the greatest business created by humans"; ~$100B a year would be a quarter of Alphabet's or Apple's ~$400B and a third of Microsoft's ~$350B, from one product sold two ways (subscription and tokens).
  • Wallach: the best stock of the last century was Altria/Philip Morris — huge margins, addictive, no churn, a billion cigarettes a day; AI engineers taking up smoking to signal AI will cure cancer reads to him as "a very toppy signal".
  • S-1: customers spend ~0.1% of opex on AI. Wallach: if cost were the barrier they'd already spend 0.5-1%, which complicates the Jevons-paradox story; he thinks it is mostly utility.
  • McClure: top companies spend ~2% and tens to hundreds of millions on tokens; the question is whether AI just eats the SaaS budget or adds to it.
  • Calacanis: a "meat sack constraint" — Uber burned its token budget in Q1 and put ~30,000 staff on OpenAI; Microsoft's buyout offer to 60,000 (one in four employees) is the roadmap. Andy Berman of RunLayer (This Week in AI): companies haven't wired AI to their data and systems; cyber fears hold them back.
  • Black: 900% net revenue retention (same cohort, same three months a year apart); hard part is pricing durability as models age and buyers route to cheaper open models through OpenRouter. Aaron Batalion (ex-CTO LivingSocial): new revenue lines from science breakthroughs aren't measured yet.
  • McClure's analogy: early innings, like teenagers talking about sex — everyone says they're doing it; most still use AI like Google prompts.

Consumer AI subscriptions, Apple's wait, and why Canva lost its momentum

Distribution owners can wait, while companies that lose momentum get re-marked fast in the secondary market.

  • OpenAI reportedly has 50M paid subscribers out of ~900M weekly users (~6%); Microsoft 365 has ~110M subscribers and $10B+ in estimated consumer revenue; Anthropic's count is undisclosed.
  • Subscription run-rate chart: Anthropic ~$65B (if all subscription), OpenAI $50B, Netflix $49B, Salesforce $40B, Adobe $27B (seen as at risk), Spotify strong.
  • Wallach: Apple and Google haven't reached steady state; mobile is theirs to lose, and Apple can wait, preload a good-enough model, or buy one "for $10 billion"; no iPhone successor yet (Sam Altman's device, Meta glasses).
  • Wallach hasn't given any AI access to his email — "that's the test"; he wants AI mostly local, with ~99% of consumer use doable on the iPhone within a few years.
  • McClure: by usage the top consumer AI platforms are OpenAI, Google Gemini, then Canva; Canva's growth slowed from 35% to 20% amid Claude Design fears, and its secondary price fell ~25% (Black) to ~30% (Wallach).
  • Black would put a college fund entirely into OpenAI and Anthropic over Figma or Canva — "perception of loss of momentum is everything"; those two "are not going to change the world anymore".
  • Wallach (a Canva holder) disagrees on Figma: network effects and designers who trained on it for a decade; shorting Anthropic's IPO ("Jim Chanos or whoever") would leave "egg on their face".

True believers vs operators: who wins the lab race long-term

Wallach argues the labs are run by sincere believers who will eventually lose to people who are better at business.

  • Wallach: every person has an incentive to offer intelligence cheaper, so being Anthropic means a target on your back; Dario and the co-founders "are not in this for the money" — cynics like Chamath see regulatory capture.
  • Wallach: they are "religious extremists" who may be right; if wrong they get beaten by better operators — Scientology grew for about a decade and couldn't sustain it.
  • Jensen built GPUs 15 years ago without knowing what was coming, and Dario studied physics at Caltech; by contrast Zuckerberg, Nadella and Altman "are not here by accident".
  • McClure: you can believe in a benevolent AI and want money; Dario believes it ("Machines of Loving Grace"), Altman is more of a venture capitalist in Elon's mould.
  • Black: watch actions — Dario publicly scolded Altman over reckless compute deals, then signed the same kind of deals once locking compute proved necessary.
  • Calacanis: a 20% less chaotic Altman might have kept Dario and Elon, and there might be no Anthropic.
  • Wallach's endgame: unless this is "software Jesus", it ends as normal business with Microsoft and Google much bigger, maybe Oracle; Larry Ellison's ~$400B capex bet at 80 is "the riskiest hand" in American capitalism, alongside buying media assets with his son.

Compute payback: SpaceX and the hyperscalers are fine, the neoclouds are the risk

The panel trusts the hyperscalers' math but not leveraged neoclouds whose cost of capital and timing are out of their control.

  • McClure: SpaceX Q3 shows compute payback under 12 months, with Elon aiming for six; Chamath's analysis puts other neoclouds at two to three years or worse.
  • McClure: AWS-linked notes subsidized Anthropic's compute, so true compute cost is still unclear; chip depreciation over 4-6 years may not hold for neoclouds.
  • Calacanis: servers live 7-10 years, moving from frontline to inference, storage and background jobs; only the human cost of pulling them limits it.
  • Wallach: hyperscalers borrow at 6-7% and earn 10-14%, so it is rational; the lab-cloud-chip loop works as one organism, but the question is whether customers feed it — watch who supplies the revenue, likely via investment banks. Failure mode: a dark-fiber overhang with no buyers.
  • Black's PowerLaw Corp (NASDAQ: PWRL, closed-end fund): top holdings SpaceX, OpenAI and Kalshi; selling down SpaceX bought in early 2025 for about a 3x.
  • Black: avoided all neoclouds — cost of capital and build timelines out of their control, plus Jensen's favor; missed CoreWeave; "these guys were all Bitcoin miners two years ago." CoreWeave bonds are under pressure.
  • Calacanis: a loan to a neocloud is still backed by data centers, chips and memory — better collateral than speculative crypto.

Bitcoin is dead (again), SPVs crowd out small funds, and alicorns skip rounds

Calacanis says money is leaving Bitcoin for AI, and the money in venture is moving to SPVs and to founders who barely need capital.

  • Calacanis's monthly "Bitcoin is dead" post (~2M views): ETF outflows continue, a retest of the low is coming, and Bitcoin "will never break its all-time highs again"; three people offered to bet him $1M. He still holds millions in Bitcoin as a long-term holder, plus a ~$750K bet on "Tau".
  • Wallach: the Ponzi energy is gone, leaving a debasement-trade thesis — has Bitcoin earned a place next to gold? Gold fell over the past six months; rates may stay high and inflation elevated for decades.
  • Calacanis's real use cases: stablecoins, tokenized real-world assets (Robinhood), and DeFi lending in the long run.
  • Calacanis: 10-20 VC firms take ~75% of capital; emerging managers struggle (Black runs the Raise Conference) and move to SPVs — "SPVs are killing the VC radio star." SPV fees (2% and 20% carry) are getting commoditized.
  • Wallach: AI makes building cheap, so the best founders will raise less than ever and small funds writing small checks will win; Lightspeed shows "no interest" in 16-year-old token-maxers, who he calls "a different species".
  • Calacanis: seed rounds at $5-20M valuations become alicorns that skip Series B on revenue; he pushes some to take $10-20M at $250M-$1B (about 5% dilution) as a rainy-day fund.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

S-1 — SEC registration statement a company files before an IPO, with audited financials.
Fair-value convertible notes — Notes re-measured every period at current value, so a rising valuation books a non-cash loss.
Net revenue retention — Spend this period from a fixed customer cohort divided by its spend a year earlier.
Annualized run rate — Latest month or quarter multiplied out to a year; not the same as recurring subscription revenue.
Neocloud — A GPU cloud provider outside the hyperscalers, often debt-financed (e.g. CoreWeave).
SPV — Special purpose vehicle pooling investors into a single deal, usually charging fees and carry.
Closed-end fund — A listed fund with a fixed share count that trades on an exchange, here holding private companies.
Alicorn — Calacanis's term for a startup that reaches unicorn scale while skipping funding rounds on revenue.
Debasement trade — Owning scarce assets like gold or Bitcoin as a hedge against currency dilution.
