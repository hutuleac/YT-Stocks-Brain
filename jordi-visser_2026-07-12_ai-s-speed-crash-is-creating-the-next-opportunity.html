This Is a 'Speed Crash' Inside a Structural Bull Market, Not a Bubble Popping

Visser's framing for the last month of AI-name weakness: tech momentum volatility spiked hard (60-day realized vol ~70 to 87) and flushed out leveraged retail, systematic quant funds, and pods globally — a sentiment/positioning cleanse, not a demand-side breakdown.

  • Samsung's preliminary quarterly profit beat estimates by a wide margin and the stock still sold off the same day, with Micron falling too — Visser argues this looks like a 'news failure' only because the correction had already happened beforehand, not because good news is actually bearish.
  • Samsung's 10-day rate of change was the largest since mid-COVID panic and the biggest outside of the period right after Lehman Brothers — a genuine speed crash by his metrics, even though it doesn't 'feel' that dramatic within an overall uptrend.
  • On valuation: Samsung's PE for next year is cheap enough that analysts estimate 2026 operating profit could exceed the company's entire cumulative operating profit over the past 40 years — Visser calls dismissing this as 'just another AI overbuild' story 'silly.'
  • Systematic long-short quant managers gave back about a third of their year-to-date gains in a rough stretch (still positive for the year); retail using 2x leverage gave back 62% even though the underlying names were down only ~23-38% — evidence of a leverage flush, not a fundamental repricing.
  • Technical read across his own 100-name thematic portfolio: percent of names above the 20-day moving average bottomed at 21% (from 73%) then bounced to 31%; percent above the 200-day (his 'structural bull market' gauge) stayed elevated at 86%, which he reads as still-intact trend strength underneath the correction.

The Memory Shortage Guidance Just Got Worse, Not Better — and It's Not Fracking

Visser directly rebuts the 'this is just like fracking, supply will crush the price' comparison he says he keeps hearing, arguing token/AI demand is structurally different from any prior physical-commodity demand cycle.

  • Industry guidance shifted from 'memory shortage until 2030' (March) to 'shortage beyond 2030' (a recent update) — long-term supply contracts are now being signed for the first time, and memory capacity is growing only 20-30% a year against demand that's doubling.
  • Core distinction from fracking/oil: fracking was a supply-side parabola against flat human-driven demand; here, demand is driven by AI agents/tokens, which scale on a fundamentally different (exponential, non-human) curve that physical, human-built supply structurally can't catch up to quickly.
  • Cites Elon Musk building a dedicated 'Terafab' specifically because he doesn't believe there will be enough memory/chips for humanoid demand by 2030 as further evidence the constraint is real and industry-wide, not confined to hyperscalers.
  • Trimmed his own Micron position significantly starting in mid-May (largest reduction in his concentrated basket), explicitly not trying to time the exact top — expects to start rebuying given the SK Hynix deal and reduced bottleneck concerns after the latest earnings.
  • States a 6-month forward view that prices will be 'significantly higher' than current levels once this pullback phase resolves, given the supply side (TSMC discipline, gas-turbine/power constraints) structurally prevents the kind of overbuild that would create a real bubble.

The Token Expenditure Index Is Being Misread as a Bubble Gauge

Visser says he's been asked about this index more than anything else in three weeks — his position is it should be read as an adoption-cycle artifact, not a signal that AI demand is fading.

  • Traces the index's rise and fall: a coding-agent capability shock, then OpenClaw driving a surge of AI-native/open-source token usage, then enterprise FOMO ('token maxing') causing a step-up, then a 'bill shock' pullback as budgets got scrutinized and usage patterns normalized (including seasonal effects like school ending and summer vacations).
  • Argues the index doesn't capture the underlying adoption still happening — illustrated by SemiAnalysis founder Dylan Patel's own firm going from a ~$100K AI budget in November to an ~$11M annualized run-rate today (peak week equivalent to ~$14M) for a 90-person firm.
  • Cites Patel's point that the 'smartest model' can actually be cheaper overall for assistant-style work because it needs fewer tokens, fewer iterations, and less human correction time — cheap models fit fixed workflows, expensive models fit variable/judgment-heavy work.
  • Backlog data from the most recent earnings season as direct evidence demand still exceeds supply: $627B commitment backlog for Microsoft, $462B for Google, $225B for Amazon, $638B for Oracle, with Microsoft, Google, and Meta all separately confirming demand still exceeds their build capacity.
  • Notes Anthropic is reportedly reaching cash-flow-positive or operating-profit territory, which he reads as margins coming through as expected — undercutting the 'nobody's making money on this' bear case.

Consumer AI Agents Are the Next, Much Bigger Token-Demand Wave

Visser argues consumer agents (not coding agents) will be the defining token-demand story of the next 12 months — the bottleneck isn't model capability, it's product/interface maturity.

  • Cites a Mark Zuckerberg town hall reportedly acknowledging Meta's agentic AI progress has been slower than expected, plus a separate interview with Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth arguing the industry is moving beyond one monolithic model toward a mix of open-source and frontier models.
  • His framing for why consumer AI has lagged: the product layer, not the model, is the constraint — consumer AI needs to 'disappear into daily life' the way search, maps, or the camera did, which requires solving context, trust, timing, and permissions, not just raw intelligence.
  • Personal anecdote: used Grok conversationally through an entire Tesla drive this week in a way he says isn't yet replicable on a phone — sees this kind of ambient, voice-first interaction as the missing habit-formation piece, comparable to how mobile data usage was originally friction-constrained before the iPhone.
  • Cites a Goldman Sachs estimate that AI agents could drive token consumption up roughly 24x, to around 120 quadrillion tokens/month — with FSD/robotaxis and humanoids (vision-language models, not just LLMs) representing an even larger step-function in compute demand beyond that.
  • Reads Apple's stock action as consistent with this thesis: the stock made an all-time high on its AI/Siri announcement, sold off ('sell the news'), dipped again on a 20%+ price hike tied to memory costs, then went right back to all-time highs — a pattern he reads as the market pricing in more consumer-agent upside than the current PEG ratio reflects.

Bessent's Speech Reframes Crypto as US 'Financial Statecraft' — Visser Launches a Crypto-Focused Format

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's speech at the New York Economic Club is treated by Visser as a watershed moment — digital assets, stablecoins, and tokenization framed explicitly as national economic/security strategy, not speculation.

  • Cites Mohamed El-Erian's characterization of the speech as 'remarkably important,' arguing the Bessent administration sees economic security as inseparable from national security — trade, tech standards, supply chains, and now the payment system and the dollar's role are treated as tools of strategic power.
  • Draws a direct parallel to Marc Andreessen's January 2014 essay 'Why Bitcoin Matters,' arguing Andreessen told investors why Bitcoin mattered before the state understood it, and Bessent is now telling policymakers the same thing 12 years later — with agentic AI commerce (agents transacting, settling invoices, managing subscriptions) as the accelerant this time.
  • Personal parallel drawn to his own AI positioning: says crypto sentiment right now looks 'very reminiscent' of where AI sentiment was roughly 14 months ago when he was buying Micron under $100 and being told nothing was happening — explicitly dismisses Jeremy Grantham's public 'Bitcoin is worthless' commentary the same way he dismissed AI skeptics last year.

  • Announces a new, crypto-focused YouTube format spending roughly two-thirds of each video specifically on crypto/tokenization/stablecoins/the new payment system, aimed at traditional finance viewers who've mostly ignored the space.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Speed crash — Visser's term for a fast, sharp price decline driven by leverage unwinds and volatility-forced selling within an intact longer-term uptrend, as opposed to a bubble genuinely deflating on broken fundamentals.
Token expenditure index — A market-watched gauge of AI token spending trends; Visser argues it reflects adoption-cycle phases (FOMO spend, then budget scrutiny) rather than whether real AI demand is rising or falling.
Second derivative (slowdown) — When growth is still positive but decelerating in rate — used here to describe AI infrastructure spending still rising, just no longer accelerating at its prior pace.
PEG ratio — Price/earnings ratio divided by expected earnings growth rate — used to argue certain AI-exposed names (Samsung, Apple) are cheap relative to their growth even after large share-price gains.
