High conviction ELECTION-WEEK SELL-OFF — NOV 2026 MIDTERMSpolicymacro-ratesgeopolitics

Midterm angst is normal; the Iran war was ARK's miss

Wood reads the election-week drop as pre-priced angst, not a break in the bull case — the market usually rises in the year after a midterm.

  • Midterms 1958-2022, Republican or Democrat: the market was generally up a year later, roughly 13-14% on average; incumbent seat losses are typical.
  • She likens today to the 80s and 90s — 20-25 years of strong, up-and-to-the-right markets if ARK is right; even 1987, when portfolio insurance failed and one day fell 22%, ended marginally up the year after its midterm.
  • Surface read is a Democrat sweep of Congress, a turnover near Obama-midterm extremes; underneath, pocketbook measures leaned conservative — no wealth tax in California, new state and city tax limits.
  • ARK got the Iran war wrong — expected it to end quickly; oil and gasoline inflation angered all voters, and the market priced a Republican defeat from May-June, when many AI stocks peaked.
  • Defense stocks hit hardest: higher health-care spending must be funded somewhere given the deficit; extra taxes on banks and energy companies are a risk to watch.
  • Deficit still on track for 2.8% of GDP by end-2028, she thinks, via gridlock and vetoes — or a Trump art of the deal return to Reagan-style bipartisanship.
  • Growth call unchanged: real GDP averaged 3% since the industrial revolution, ARK sees 7% through 2030 and ~9% through 2035; Q3 GDP printed 3.4%.

High conviction DATA CENTERS DOMINATED ELECTION ADSai-infrapolicy

The data-center backlash is a messaging failure, not a reason to stop

Builders raced in without reading local politics, but where data centers land well, towns get lower taxes, jobs and new schools.

  • Florida has no large data center, yet its election advertising was all about them.
  • Companies built willy-nilly, assuming good jobs and tax revenue would sell themselves; headlines hit electricity prices (true), water, generator pollution, blight and displacement (Texas).
  • Water is an old issue: Meta and Amazon run on the equivalent of recycled water, with no real extra usage.
  • Elon Musk's offer in Sulphur Springs, Texas: cut property taxes 20%, electricity bills 40%, employ 5,000 people in a town of 20,000.
  • Virginia, the world's biggest data-center cluster (per Brainstorm's Matt): property taxes down, about a high school a year, parks and aquatic centers; Larry Goldberg cited a similar North Carolina town.
  • Wood blames Anthropic's CEO Dario for the mood — the 10% extinction-within-a-decade odds and mass job-loss talk; ARK believes neither and expects a labor shortage.
  • Data centers are the expression of an American Renaissance, a revolution bigger than the industrial one; power, chip and data-center shortages force efficiency early, so it should last longer with less fallout than the internet bust.

Watching NO SYSTEMIC SIGNAL — NEOCLOUD STRESSfinanceai-infra

Credit check: hyperscaler and CoreWeave CDS are the stress, banks are calm

The CDS market shows AI-buildout stress concentrated in CoreWeave, not a systemic break.

  • High-yield CDS a little elevated, not even at a new high and nowhere near the COVID heart attack.
  • Hyperscaler CDS exist only recently, as investors fear the giants turned from huge free-cash-flow generators to FCF-negative; they now sit where investment-grade CDS were at COVID's worst.
  • The election slightly improved hyperscaler CDS — markets expect states and cities to slow the buildout; she thinks the worst is in.
  • CoreWeave CDS above 1,000 vs ~300 for high yield, even though its contracts are mainly with hyperscalers; as a neocloud, a data-center slowdown is its entire business.
  • Bank CDS show a small pickup, nothing like 2008, the European sovereign debt crisis or COVID; the high-yield chart says the same.

High conviction PRODUCTIVITY +3.6% ANNUALIZED THIS QUARTERsoftwaremacro-rates

AI adoption is barely started, but its productivity shows up in the data

A thin layer of heavy AI spenders is already pulling ahead on output and hiring, and the rest will be forced to follow.

  • Ramp data on frontier-model spend per employee per month: top 1% almost $10,000, top 10% a little over $800, everyone else $15.
  • Inference costs fall 99% a year at fixed performance (Wright's Law), so firms like Apple steer lower-intelligence tasks to cheaper models — the efficiency ARK wants to see.
  • Risk: many top spenders are tech and venture-backed firms; a full venture-funding drought would hurt short term, ARK expects only a brief interruption.
  • Real output per payroll job in AI-intensive sectors is 11% above trend in a few years; the rest show no improvement, and competitors will eat their lunch.
  • Productivity growth 3.6% annualized vs unit labor costs -0.7%, so compensation per hour runs a little below 3% and should turn up — gains split across wages, margins, R&D and lower prices.
  • Labor: average hourly earnings +0.4% for the month (~5% annualized) vs ~3% a year; unemployment about to enter the 3s as boomers retire and immigrants leave; 16-24 unemployment fell from 9.9% to 9.3%, and AI-intensive companies hire faster (Ramp).

High conviction SAASPOCALYPSE — FRONTIER LABS WINNINGsoftwareroboticsspace

Hardware beats software: SaaS losers, frontier-lab winners, robots next

Technology spend is shifting from SaaS incumbents to frontier labs and to hardware, from data centers to embodied AI.

  • Software crossed hardware as a share of GDP after the tech bust; data centers now lift hardware, back to tech-bubble-peak levels while the journey has just started; equipment's GDP share has gone nowhere since the early 80s and should go a lot higher, now out of a 20-year ceiling dating from the tech bust.
  • SaaSpocalypse: Atlassian fell this week, ServiceNow dropped 27% in one day weeks ago despite CEO Bill McDermott calling it the execution layer of AI.
  • OpenAI and Anthropic have stolen the show; Google's Gemini is in the pack but has lagged, with some leapfrogging.
  • SpaceX AI gets an underrated edge from orbital computing as the data-center backlash grows — compute and power are the keys to this revolution.
  • Free AI personal assistants — Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, Anthropic's Duet (too new to judge), and Instincts from a private company — threaten any transactions-first app; like Uber beating the NYC Taxi Commission, AI that delights users won't be stopped by politics.
  • Embodied AI: ARK's Tasha Keeney and Daniel size humanoid robots at $26T within 10-15 years (~20% of global GDP); robotaxis a $10T opportunity sooner.

High conviction MESSAGE FOR FED CHAIR WARSHmacro-ratesenergy

Inflation will undershoot: AI deflation, cheap gas, falling oil and gold

Wood sees disinflation hiding in mismeasured software prices, falling energy costs and productivity, and wants Fed Chair Warsh to see it.

  • Equipment inflation has diverged from software inflation — a supply-demand imbalance and a clarion call for more equipment supply, maybe data centers in space; DRAM prices have skyrocketed.
  • If AI training and inference costs fall 75-99% a year, CPI and PPI understate software deflation.
  • US natural gas, the grid's biggest feedstock, is down a little over 35% in a year while gasoline rose ~30%; US winter storage is full, Europe's well below, so US gas demand rises if export infrastructure allows.
  • Oil: high prices accelerated the EV shift; transportation is 57% of oil use and moving to gas, hydro, nuclear, solar and wind, so oil should fall fairly significantly.
  • Gold fell below $4,000 from roughly $5,600; ARK expects further declines if Warsh delivers, and Bitcoin to keep rising against gold — correlation 0.1 since 2019, recently negative.
  • 5y5y inflation says post-election spending won't be inflationary; M2 grows 5.7%, but inflation has run below money growth since velocity started falling in 1997; the diffusion index still has to break down.
  • Rates: recent rises came from real yields, even after the election; the yield curve keeps flattening (in the industrial revolution it inverted, long rates held back 100-500 bps); faster nominal GDP lifts short rates more than long, painful for bond-heavy boomers but no crisis.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Credit default swap (CDS) — Insurance-like contract that pays if a borrower defaults; its spread is the market price of default risk.
Neocloud — GPU cloud provider such as CoreWeave whose whole business is AI data centers, unlike diversified hyperscalers.
SaaSpocalypse — Shorthand for the sell-off in subscription-software stocks on fears that AI displaces them.
Wright's Law — Costs fall a constant percentage for every cumulative doubling of production.
Unit labor costs — Labor compensation per unit of output; it falls when productivity outgrows pay.
5y5y inflation — Market-implied average inflation for the five years starting five years from now.
Mortgage buydown — A builder pays to lower the buyer's mortgage rate, a hidden price cut absent from reported home prices.
Rolling recession — Sectors contract one after another rather than all at once.
Velocity of money — How fast money changes hands; its fall since 1997 kept inflation below M2 growth.
Embodied AI — AI running physical machines such as humanoid robots and robotaxis.
