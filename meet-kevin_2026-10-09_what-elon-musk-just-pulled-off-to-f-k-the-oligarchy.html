SpaceX buys low-band spectrum to turn Starlink into a US mobile carrier

Low-band spectrum is the missing piece that lets satellite-fed service work indoors, and it moves SpaceX from phone-feature to carrier.

  • Low-band frequencies penetrate walls and fiberglass; Kevin says SpaceX's buy is of 800 MHz-class spectrum — the fix for 'it'll never work indoors'.
  • Architecture as Kevin describes it: satellites link to cell towers and the towers radiate over low band — a hybrid, not satellite-only.
  • Goal: let Starlink operate as a major US mobile carrier; Kevin says he would happily switch his own phone service to Musk.
  • Apple already partners with SpaceX for direct-from-space phone service, but that needs open sky — useless in a tree well or under snow.
  • Kevin's Apple thesis: the 20-to-45 yr-old buyers who upgrade every year are the audience for a Starlink connectivity pitch; older buyers keep phones ~4 years.
  • Kevin's SpaceX stance: bought exposure in several ways, floor ~$132; stock fell ~32% from IPO price against his ~30% pre-IPO warning, and fundamentals have 'substantially improved' since.

Contested CARRIER PRICING AS THE TARGETconsumergeopolitics

The 'oligopolistic monopoly': same network, three price tiers

Kevin argues the big carriers protect margin by selling one network under multiple brands, and that is the spread SpaceX can attack.

  • T-Mobile premium plans run about $140/month (Kevin pays that; plans from ~$80 depending on data), versus Mint Mobile's dirt-cheap tier of roughly $15 for 5 GB (he is unsure of the exact pricing).
  • Kevin's test: he uploaded video through Mint and got speeds as good as T-Mobile's — 'same tower, same network'; he rejects the claim that cheap tiers get meaningfully lower priority.
  • Why it persists: a flat $15 tier would pull $60-$80 customers down, so T-Mobile hides it behind a separate brand rather than cutting prices.
  • Analogy: Kirkland salted nuts at $12.99/lb versus Planters at $18/lb — same product, different label.
  • T-Mobile was among the first partners of SpaceX; Kevin calls this the incumbent 'partnering with the little guy to maintain control'.
  • Kevin's network verdict: T-Mobile has the fewest dead spots and fastest service he has used, across five carriers/lines (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon, Mint, Google line).

Tesla solar + Powerwall: great product and service, one install gripe

Kevin has no complaints on product or service, only on how the conduit was routed.

  • Installed Tesla panels plus a Powerwall; he says the app is polished, installation and service were 'fantastic'.
  • Gripe: conduit runs down the outside wall and is painted over rather than going through the roof, because the installer won't flash the roof — he'd call a roofer.
  • Context: he already runs a separate solar system with German-made cells on his roof for about 8 years.

Glossary & Entities

Wedge deal — Kevin's term for a deal that exploits a price or structural gap to capture a spread.
