Griffin's bubble call vs Chamath's diffusion problem

The besties accept Griffin's productivity worry but relocate it: AI spend is concentrated in a circular, VC-funded loop that has to diffuse into profitable companies.

  • Ken Griffin (Citadel) at its future-of-markets conference: AI is 'almost certainly a bubble', bubbles are normal, and companies are looking at their Anthropic and OpenAI bills asking where the productivity is.
  • Thursday selloff: CoreWeave (CRWV) -10%, Meta (META) -6% after earnings, AMD -5%, SpaceX -5%.
  • Chamath's estimate, confirmed by Ramp's chief economist: 1% of companies make up 90% of AI token spend, and 80% of that comes from unprofitable VC-backed startups.
  • The loop: LPs fund VCs, VCs fund startups, startups buy tokens and show traction, then raise again; Chamath warns about valuations where 75 cents of every dollar is startups passing money back and forth.
  • Cloud analogy: early AWS customers were also startups, but AWS sat on top of an already break-even Amazon retail business; this cycle is money-losing firms funding each other.
  • Jason's counter: startups flooding a technology is a tell; they chased cloud, WeWork and mispriced Facebook ads for the same reason; he invests in ~100 startups a year and evaluates 10,000+.

Enterprises want open-weight models and their own compute, but can't get it

Large buyers are moving toward open-weight models in private infrastructure, and the bottleneck is compute supply, not demand.

  • Ramp's chart of the top 1% of AI spenders: the GPU-compute share is rising against frontier-lab LLM spend, which Chamath reads as a move to open source.
  • Since Alex Karp's CNBC comments on data sovereignty, Chamath sees large firms shifting to VPCs; one of the largest US banks told his team it cut AWS and GCP spend and built six data centers from scratch.
  • Nikesh: Palo Alto prefers open-weight models because it can modify the weights and interrogate the chain of thought, which regulators in financial services and health care will demand.
  • The catch: outside the hyperscalers there's almost no global, resilient inference provider at 99.9% reliability; a startup can rent 10 MW in one data center and live with outages.
  • Nikesh shut down Palo Alto's data centers 6-8 years ago; building again is 'an empty threat' when Jensen can't ship chips until 2028 and the company only runs 10-20 MW of inference.
  • He expects hyperscalers (or 'cousins' of them) to rent bare metal for open models, moving the profit pool from Nvidia (NVDA) and memory toward whoever holds compute; demand is 'insatiable'.

The PHP exploit week and what AI does to the patch cycle

AI with persistent compute cracked a years-old PHP problem, the fix leaked early, and Nikesh says these events will now come faster than every 10 days.

  • PHP runs ~70% of websites (WordPress, Wikipedia); researchers using AI found two critical 'PHP two shell' exploits in August, rated 9.2 out of 10.
  • A coordinated patch was set for October 22; details leaked, the release moved to October 21, AWS saw a jump in attacks and Cloudflare's CEO reported millions of attempts.
  • Precautionary shutdowns: government sites in France, Germany, the Netherlands, Denmark and Finland, parts of Wikipedia; the NSA and FBI issued warnings.
  • Nikesh: the bug was known for years but needed too much coordination until compute persisted at it; once patched, hackers reverse-engineer the patch to hit anyone unpatched.
  • Two fixes: patch the code, or drop a 'virtual patch' into perimeter products like Palo Alto's or Cloudflare's so attackers are stopped before reaching the server.
  • On the frontier labs: OpenAI, Anthropic and likely Google have models that chain vulnerabilities; Anthropic brought ~50 companies in on Mythos, but even Palo Alto is rate limited.
  • Nikesh rejects the idea that the labs are gatekeeping: they're compute-constrained researchers trying to package and monetize, partly to pay Jensen back.

Assume breach: why white-hat compute doesn't win yet

Nikesh rejects 'whoever has more compute wins' for now; companies are buried in technical debt, so the goal is to detect and eject attackers fast.

  • White-hat compute beating hackers in North Korea, Russia or China isn't true today: infrastructure is too fragmented to launch 'defensive swarms', e.g. a firm cataloging 5,000 websites.
  • Continuous self-testing (Metasploit-style) helps, but each new model finds more holes, so it's cat-and-mouse; the better plan is to aggregate all signals and hunt anomalous behavior.
  • Chamath on why tech debt piles up: nobody wants maintenance work, and VPC deployments create version drift where a vendor supports 19 versions at once.
  • Nikesh: CIOs are paid for low cost and uptime, not modernity; Jason compares it to security budgets cut after years without a 9/11.
  • Cyber is 4-10% of IT budgets: about 10% in financial services and federal government, 4-5% for cloud-native firms; Jason says it has to rise.
  • Nikesh: AI security harnesses don't exist yet (no one built the TSA when the Wright brothers flew); a researcher just showed a shared Muse conversation can leak private data.

Who pays when an agent drains your bank account

Banks can't tell agents from users, and the panel splits on who is liable: Nikesh and Chamath say the user, Friedberg says the agent maker unless it warns.

  • Personal agents log in with your credentials, so banks can't tell your traffic from the agent's; AI now solves captchas, or hands them back to you in a virtual browser.
  • Banks have used API intermediaries like Plaid; Nikesh says their recent unauthorized-access spike is likely the PHP hole, a separate problem from agents.
  • Nikesh: bank ToS saying 'no agents' are about liability, not FUD; banks already pay small card disputes because a support call costs more, and agent disputes would multiply that cost.
  • Friedberg: if the bank's ToS forbids agents and the agent doesn't block or warn, the agent maker is liable; once it warns and you override, it's on you.
  • Jason's example: Amazon blocked shopping agents, so Grok now fills your cart but won't hit buy.
  • Nikesh: 'there's a word in agent, it's called agency' — the user owns it, except a rogue agent is product liability; Chamath calls user liability the only scalable answer.
  • Jason's incubated Obliteration AI sells guardrail-free models to red teams; Chamath finds it scarier than alignment, Nikesh likens it to handing out a nuclear bomb or a DIY aspirin kit.

France's riots: from school protests to looting and immigration fights

A high-school protest became France's worst riots in decades, and the besties read it as a story about immigration and an overextended state.

  • Over half a million people have protested, now spreading to Italy, Spain and Albania; 3,000+ businesses damaged, 4,000 police injured, 2,500 arrested (70% adult men).
  • A 143-year-old city hall in Limoges, ~250 miles south of Paris, burned down; curfews came only now. Trump and Vance call it a 'third world immigration and welfare state' issue.
  • Chamath calls the rioters 'terrorists' who need a heavy hand, and mocks police being sent to protect a Christian Dior store; Macron framed it as suffering.
  • Jason: students weren't looting; the looting came from North African immigrants who haven't integrated; he cites one in nine people as immigrants, one in 19 from northern Africa.
  • Rioters targeted Chinese-owned (and reportedly Jewish) shops ('the Chinese have become too rich'); Chamath compares it to a bottom-up Kristallnacht (1938).
  • Friedberg's screen: if immigrants need social services, it's the wrong intake; remove those services and require shared values (free speech, women's equality, rule of law); importing 1.5% of the population a year with flat GDP is a warning sign.
  • An activist who pushed for open borders had her own shop destroyed; Friedberg calls it 'armchair do-gooder' behavior, alongside 'limousine liberals' and Rob Henderson's 'luxury beliefs'.

France's debt is the West's bellwether, and the US isn't far behind

Investors are dumping French debt; Chamath wants bond vigilantes to force a reset, and Friedberg says US fiscal numbers already match France's.

  • France's 10-year passed 5%, highest since 2002, while the US 10-year dipped just under 5%; the WSJ suggests money fleeing EU debt into Treasuries.
  • Polymarket: 91% odds Moody's (MCO) or S&P downgrades France by October 31; 18% odds of a state of emergency by year end.
  • Chamath: the €50B austerity plan must grow, the government will lose the next presidential election, and France sets how continental Europe finances its long-term debt.
  • Friedberg's RAND chart puts France's public spending above the peak of the Soviet Union (1989), communist Romania, Chavez's Venezuela and China; France also leads on tax take at 46% of GDP.
  • US parallel: $7.4T federal plus $4.6T state spending = $12T of a $33T GDP (~36%); both ~120% debt-to-GDP; deficit 6.2% in the US vs 5.4% in France; growth 2.3% vs 0.5%.
  • Top tax rates: France ~55-56%, Denmark ~60%; Friedberg says the US must cut 'from seven and a half a year to four' (trillions) through hard work, or the reset turns social.
  • Jason's pushback: most Americans want what the countries above the US on the chart deliver; Friedberg counters with youth unemployment (Brazil 15-16% and rising, US under 10%).

Friedberg's reset plan, Elon's SpaceX gift and the minimum-wage fight

Friedberg wants to unwind federal programs and turn Social Security into owned equity; Jason wants Trump to talk about minimum wage and housing.

  • Friedberg's four fixes: wind down federal student loans and delete existing debt (tuition right-sizes in ~10 years); shrink federal home-loan underwriting even if middle-class net worth drops 10-20%.
  • Ban defined-benefit plans, Social Security included, moving 10% of what you're owed into personal accounts each year for 10 years; push health care to employers, with HIPAA and housing rules in the way.
  • Chamath backs making student debt dischargeable in bankruptcy; Jason points to Australia's superannuation and Norway; Friedberg notes ERISA pushed private firms out of defined-benefit plans.
  • Chamath's challenge: France already has free college (300 euros a year, costing the state 12,000) and free health care and still riots; Jason answers with wait times and 60-kid classrooms.
  • Elon pledged a SpaceX share (~$150-170) to every American child, roughly a $10 billion gift; Jason wants Invest America accounts and calls Kara Swisher's 'just give cash' critique hypocritical.
  • Jason: raise the federal minimum wage $1 a year for 7 years to $15 by 2032 (affects ~15 million people); Friedberg: California's AB 1228 jump to $20 cost 18,000 fast-food jobs, and a zero tax rate for the bottom bracket is better.
  • Chamath: Trump is right to focus on data centers because the AI buildout is basically all of GDP growth and lifts middle-class wages; Jason says his approval is low because he doesn't talk about housing or wages.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Virtual patch — A rule in a firewall or perimeter product that blocks attempts to exploit a known bug before the server's code is fixed.
Open-weight model — A model whose weights you can download, change and run on your own hardware, which lets you inspect its chain of thought.
Bond vigilantes — Bond investors who sell a government's debt to push yields up and force fiscal discipline.
Defined benefit vs defined contribution — A guaranteed pension payout vs an individual account whose value depends on what's paid in and how it's invested.
Obliterated model — A model with its safety guardrails removed, sold for red-team testing.
Luxury beliefs — Rob Henderson's term for positions that bring status to the people holding them while the costs land on others.
