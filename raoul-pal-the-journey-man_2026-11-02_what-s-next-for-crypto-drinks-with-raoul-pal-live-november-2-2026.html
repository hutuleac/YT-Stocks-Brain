Framework THE EVERYTHING CODEmacro-ratesfinance

Demographics make debt, debt makes liquidity, liquidity drives prices

Pal's chain of causation: falling labour participation means slower growth, more debt, more money printing, and asset prices that follow liquidity.

  • Core chart: government debt is the inverse of the labour force participation rate; as growth slows, debt rises to "paper over the cracks."
  • Printing money to pay for the debt mutualizes the cost via losing about 8% a year of purchasing power; he puts total liquidity at about $25.5 trillion and still climbing.
  • Growth identity: GDP growth = population growth + productivity growth + debt growth. The bet (Kevin Warsh, Trump, Scott Bessent) is that AI and robots act as new "population" and lift productivity.
  • Evidence he cites: revenue per employee at major tech firms has "gone vertical"; he built his dashboards himself with Claude Code despite never having written code.
  • Broad liquidity roughly doubled (~12,000 to 24,000) while the Nasdaq went ~9 to 36; the gap is "the compounding of technology." Owning technology and crypto is the whole game.

High conviction AI RACE · CORRECTION, NOT BUSTai-infrasemis

AI capex can't stop: nobody can slow down, and demand is infinite

Pal argues the AI buildout is funded by the biggest cash-flow machines in history, so a correction does not mean a bust.

  • Race logic: if Google slowed capex, Microsoft is "three years ahead" within a year, because intelligence per joule doubles every ~4.5 months. Nobody can leave the game.
  • Anthropic at the top of the stack shows "the fastest growth of profits to this scale in all of corporate history."
  • Every layer (TSMC, Nvidia, data centres, hyperscalers, models) earns 30-65% margins, so "this is not the 2000s all over again."
  • Infinite demand: agents, not humans, are the main consumers of intelligence, and they started the year and are "going exponential."
  • Weak link: leveraged data-centre owners who can't get power permits will be bought by cash-rich rivals. Bottlenecks are energy permits and transformers.
  • Once those bottlenecks clear, newly built capacity filled with cutting-edge chips produces another step change in intelligence.
  • Elon Musk is probably ahead on power (Colossus 2, bought a mobile gas business, owns solar), so he can supply compute when others can't and "will probably make supernormal profits for a few years."

Bearish near term LIQUIDITY FLAT SINCE JULYcryptomacro-rates

Crypto and Nasdaq are stuck because liquidity is stuck

Total liquidity has gone nowhere since July, and Pal expects "pretty crappy markets" this year.

  • Narrow liquidity is flat because the dollar rose, the yield curve flattened and rates went up, so banks aren't levering fast enough.
  • Bitcoin follows US narrow liquidity; the forward projection "is not going to do great for a bit." Excess liquidity also says not great for a while.
  • Bitcoin is about 1 and a bit standard deviations oversold vs its log trend, so it is still cheap; he buys anything below those lines.
  • Nasdaq vs its log trend was almost 2 standard deviations overbought; he advised being cautious or not adding, expecting a return to fair value, then higher.
  • Liquidity vs Nasdaq hints at a possible squeeze post-election, then a drop again.
  • His actions: he bought only in February and at another low this year, changed nothing else; "all I do is accumulate."
  • GMI track record claim: 65% hit rate on every recommended trade since 2005, averaging well over 50% per position.

Another growth dip, then easing: dollar toward 93, rate cuts in 2027

Financial conditions point to a slowdown first, then looser conditions as the dollar and rates fall.

  • Financial conditions lead global liquidity by about a month and say another drop in its rate of change is coming; the ISM follows, so the business cycle slows.
  • Oil, the dollar and rates all rose; Nasdaq's 12-month rate of change tracks financial conditions with a 3-month lead.
  • He expects recession talk in the first 3-4 months of 2027, but "it's not going to happen at all because nobody can allow it." The capex boom cushions it.
  • Dollar: counter-trend rally, likely a final leg down to the bottom of its range (DXY ~93), then another leg lower in his view; the long-term view remains dollar-bullish.
  • Personal history: in 2011 he moved his business billing and savings into dollars and bought property in a dollar-linked place, a multi-decade call that paid off.
  • Two-year yield: failed at a prior level; watch for a break of the trend line and a gap close. Gold, the 2-year and the dollar are his three dials.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Financial conditions (FCI) — A measure of how easy credit is; Pal says it leads global liquidity by about a month and the ISM and Nasdaq growth by a few months.
