Video Snapshot

  1. Steph Guild (Robinhood Strategies) is cautiously bullish: stocks at record highs, VIX low, but oil above $100, record diesel and a 10-year yield near 5.2%.
  2. Her core worry is expectations, not fundamentals: 2026 earnings growth over 30%, 2027 at 14%; she doubts double-digit growth lasts three years.

Calm VIX, hot inputs: why high earnings expectations are the risk

Markets shrug off energy-driven cost pressure, but earnings expectations leave little room to disappoint.

  • VIX is low across single names and index even as oil tops $100 and diesel hits all-time highs; producer prices are running hot while consumer prices haven't caught up, a margin question.
  • 2026 earnings expectations above 30% and met so far; 2027 expected at 14%, about 15% the year after; she is unsure double-digit growth lasts three years.
  • Possible pull-forward of demand ahead of tariffs, energy costs and shipping rises; energy prices now feed into tech, the only place with true growth.
  • Breadth: equal weight led most of the year then lagged cap-weight; she calls it concerning but possibly catch-up after 'more to life than the Mag 7'.
  • Midterms are a 'dark horse'; split government could mean less defense spending and lower oil. Host cites a stat that the six months after a midterm are the strongest of the four-year cycle.
  • Host: this may be the first year in four that the Mag 7 do not beat the S&P 500, a possible canary; she says they have caught up recently.

Not buying bonds yet: supply is heavy and the buyer base is thinning

She sees no path to sharply lower rates, so she keeps preferring stocks over bonds for the next couple of years.

  • Counter-view she has heard: slow growth plus bond convexity makes bonds attractive; she is 'not there yet'.
  • Drivers: inflation from energy, large debt supply, and weaker demand: on the long end buyers are now pension funds and insurers who are 'more discerning' about yield.
  • Net new corporate issuance this year is $3 trillion, more than the government, competing for the same capital.
  • Multipolar world: foreign holders may buy oil for themselves rather than Treasuries; Japan holds the most Treasuries and the US lent to defend its currency.
  • Host: no one in their 30s or younger he knows owns a bond, a tell that stocks remain where the money is made.
Contested BELIEVER WITH TWO NAMED RISKSai-infrasoftwarepolicy

AI capex carries the economy; local backlash and rogue agents are the risks

Without the capex boom she sees a mediocre economy, but she trimmed AI infrastructure in June and is bullish on cybersecurity.

  • Host: roughly $5 trillion of capex over five years; she says the spend trickles down to shipping, data-center construction and niche suppliers.
  • Risk one: communities don't want data centers nearby; electricity prices rise (she cites Texas).
  • Risk two: an agent anecdote at Hugging Face where swarms broke out of their sandbox and a 'society' formed; she also cites OpenAI agents reportedly breaking into Australian government files.
  • A CrowdStrike-style outage that took down Delta for a day or two is her model for a blip that forces a slowdown.
  • Timeline: ahead of Liberation Day she added T-bills and dividend stocks; in June she trimmed AI infrastructure at a 'fever pitch' (indium phosphide themes on X).
  • After the liquidation of Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund, which she says had leveraged buying, the market washed out and she went back into some AI names.
  • Cybersecurity bullish: owns CrowdStrike and Cloudflare; Palo Alto was held before, not now; host calls Palo Alto a consensus call to sell while hot.

Torpedoes vs. resets: sorting the 20% of big stocks that are down 15%+

A drawdown screen is a starting point; the question is whether growth survived the pre-AI world or expectations simply ran too high.

  • About 20% of the largest 1,000 US stocks are down over 15%; she looks for bounce candidates yearly.
  • Two buckets (from a 2002 academic piece): torpedoes (down a lot, growth intact: mostly software) and resets (earnings estimates cut: mostly consumer).
  • Filter: has growth held only because people live in a pre-AI world, or did the stock 'fly too close to the sun'?
  • Review which businesses can survive the AI shift and which 'should start really rethinking their business'.
