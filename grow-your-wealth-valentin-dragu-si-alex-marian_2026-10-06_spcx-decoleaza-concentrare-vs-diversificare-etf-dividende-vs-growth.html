Core philosophy MEMBER EDUCATIONfinancemindset

Why 15-20 stocks can outgrow 100 over five to ten years

Diversification shrinks the damage from a loser, and it shrinks the payoff from a winner by exactly the same mechanism.

  • With 100 companies, one that 5x's barely moves the portfolio; with 15-20 names, a stock at 8-10% weight that doubles or triples changes the outcome.
  • He cites his own wins with Tesla, Palantir, SoFi, AMD and Robinhood, and names SPCX, RKLB, ASTS and Ondas as the 'new pearls' he expects to repeat it.
  • Concentration isn't a bet: ~15 companies spread over AI, semis, digital finance, space, energy, nuclear, robotics and autonomy, so the money doesn't hang on one industry.
  • The cost: it amplifies mistakes as much as good calls, so every holding must be one the investor understands and follows constantly.
  • When a mistake shows up, as CLSK did from 2024 to April 2025, cut it at the root and move the money into a position already proving its outlook.

Core philosophy FIRST 5-10 YEARSfinancemindset

Build capital first, then income: growth over dividends early

Someone aged 30-45 with a salary doesn't need the portfolio to pay bills, so total return matters more than yield.

  • The question isn't 'how much dividend do I get?' but 'how much does my money grow in total?'. A 4% yield plus 5% price gain is ~9%; a non-payer rising 15% wins.
  • Compounding example: $100k at 10% (the S&P 500's annualized average) becomes ~$259k in ten years, at 15% ~$405k, at 20% ~$619k. Not promises, and never linear.
  • Income math: $50k at 4% yield is ~$2,000 a year, ~$175 a month. Grow it to $500k-1M first, then shift toward stable payers, bonds, or option income.
  • GYW portfolios already earn monthly income from selling covered calls and cash-secured puts, not dividends; he argues that pays more annualized while keeping growth upside.
  • Lifecycle: build wealth, then protect it, then draw income. A 30% drop is $15k on $50k but $600k on $2M near retirement: same percentage, different consequence.
  • Young investors can stomach 30-50% drawdowns (see 2022) because salary keeps funding buys at lower prices.
  • Growth doesn't mean buying anything that rises: rising sales and profits, competitive products, a future industry, and a price future results can justify.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Covered call (CC) — Selling a call option on shares you own to collect premium, capping upside above the strike.
Cash-secured put (CSP) — Selling a put while holding cash to buy the shares if assigned, collecting premium meanwhile.
Vendor financing — A supplier funding its customer's purchases, so revenue comes with credit risk on the supplier's books.
Multiple compression — Valuation multiples (P/E etc.) shrinking, usually as rates rise, even if earnings hold.
Term premium — The extra yield investors demand to hold long bonds instead of rolling short ones.
HALEU — High-assay low-enriched uranium, fuel for advanced reactors; Centrus's structural edge.
Lockup expiry — The date insiders and early holders may first sell shares after a listing.
