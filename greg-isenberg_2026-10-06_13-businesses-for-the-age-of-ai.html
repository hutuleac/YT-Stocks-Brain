13 Businesses for the Age of AI

Channel: Greg Isenberg · Speaker: Greg Isenberg · 2026-10-06
6 threads · agent-delivered services, harnesses and vertical agents; distribution and proprietary data; offline, real-asset and fan-product businesses; robotics, compute and energy; health, marketplaces and care; security. · Watch on YouTube ↗
The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Greg's list of 13 businesses worth owning in a world of agents: AI-native service firms, offline businesses, distribution, proprietary data, domain-specific harnesses, robotics/physical AI, fan-following physical products, compute and energy, health/longevity/care, marketplaces and social networks, real assets, vertical agents, and security.
  2. He rejects the 'permanent underclass' framing: owning assets in an abundant agent world just puts you in a better position.
  3. AI-native service firms can let agents do ~90% of delivery (e.g. bookkeeping with human exception review), charging per completed job, with high margin and few clients.
  4. Distribution gets scarcer: Facebook clicks went from ~5 cents to $1-5, so owning organic audiences matters; his 'ACP funnel' is audience, then community, then product.
  5. Data and harnesses are two 'gold rushes': proprietary data sets sold to labs or niche buyers, and harnesses (tools, workflow rules, memory, checks) as 'the new GPT wrapper'.
  6. Physical-world businesses stay valuable: communal sauna clubs, window-cleaning robots, coffee gear, trades workshops, compute and energy for agents.
  7. He expects a major security breach at an agent startup and sees growing demand for access control and personal safety services.
Recommendation AI-NATIVE SERVICES, HARNESSES AND VERTICAL AGENTSsoftwarecareer

Let agents deliver the work: service firms, domain harnesses and vertical agents

Three of the 13 are variations on one idea: agents do most of the delivery inside a specific industry, and you charge for the outcome.

  • AI-native service firm: agents deliver ~90% of the work; example is a bookkeeping firm where agents prepare books, a human reviews exceptions, and you charge per completed month or job.
  • Why it's good: high margin and few clients needed; he is actively ideating on it, e.g. 'logos as a service' or 'ads as a service' for a creative agency (he did a prior episode).
  • Domain-specific harness: the model is the engine; the harness is the tools, workflow rules, memory and checks around it (body, steering wheel, paint).
  • His example: an operating system for a freight-brokerage agent; he calls harnesses 'the new GPT wrapper', arguing the top ~10% of wrappers evolved into harnesses (legal examples: Harvey, Spellbook).
  • Vertical agent: an agent owns a recurring job in one industry, e.g. purchasing for independent restaurants (check stock, compare offers, ask approval, place the order).
  • He says people have built vertical agents for ~12 months but he still sees a large opportunity.

Framework DISTRIBUTION AND PROPRIETARY DATAsoftwareconsumer

Own the audience and the data: reaching people costs more, data is scarce

When anyone can prompt a startup into existence, reach and unique information become the moat.

  • Facebook ads cost about 5 cents a click when the platform launched; now roughly $1-5 depending on target and country; he expects the price of reaching people to keep rising.
  • Owning organic audiences (multiple meme pages, YouTube channels, Instagram audiences) is 'so important', and advertisers can pay you to build the audience.
  • His ACP funnel: audience, then community, then product — build what the audience asks for.
  • Proprietary data sets: data-labeling startups selling to big labs reached hundreds of millions in ARR and billion-dollar valuations; he says 'data is the new oil'.
  • Solo-founder version: collect information people can't get from a personal agent or ChatGPT and build a business of a few million dollars a year; he thinks we're in the first inning of a gold rush for data.
Recommendation OFFLINE EXPERIENCES, FAN-FOLLOWING PRODUCTS, REAL ASSETSconsumerhealth

Offline experiences, niche physical products and real assets keep their value

People still pay for rooms, communities and well-designed objects, and AI makes niche hardware cheaper to attempt.

  • Offline: Othership's neighborhood sauna club (cold plunges, music, memberships) rides the wellness and community trends; the first version was in the founder's backyard.
  • Cost is the hard part in high-rent cities; his advice is to build the 'food-truck version' as the MVP.
  • Fan-following products: Fellow (coffee kettle, grinder) won design-conscious buyers; he is a fan and unaffiliated.
  • The Minimal Company makes phones for people who don't want to scroll and raised millions on Kickstarter; AI lets niche hardware be designed and prototyped that was too small before.
  • Real assets: small industrial workshops rented to local trades (electricians, cabinet makers, plumbers), warehouses and real estate — 'you need a place' in the new world.
Recommendation PHYSICAL AI AND SCARCE INPUTSroboticsai-infraenergy

Robotics-as-a-service and the scarce inputs agents need: compute and energy

Greg says physical AI is nearly here, and chips, electricity and cooling are the scarce inputs of the era.

  • Robotics/physical AI: a startup that raised about $6-7M is doing window cleaning as a service where building owners pay for clean windows and the company runs the equipment.
  • He quotes Brian Norgard: 'the best way to sell software is by selling hardware'.
  • Non-engineers can buy and rent out robots or sell them — you don't need to be an 'Elon Musk character'.
  • Compute and energy: every agent needs chips, electricity, cooling and somewhere to run; Crusoe raised $3.9B for vertically integrated AI infrastructure.
  • He also cites Michael Dell's son raising billions for an energy company, and says involvement in these businesses is 'a good place to be'.

Recommendation HEALTH, LONGEVITY, CARE AND MARKETPLACEShealthconsumersoftware

Older-adult care, longevity and marketplaces/social networks

Underserved older adults and hard-to-build marketplaces are opportunities because they are difficult.

  • Health, longevity and care: older adults are an under-served customer; opportunities include high-NPS products and care-service agencies such as caregiving.
  • Longevity: Bryan Johnson 'tapped into' the don't-die movement and Huberman health; he says room remains if you niche down.
  • Marketplaces and social networks: a real moat, but liquidity (supply and demand at once) is 'more art than science'; 2013-15 era examples were Uber, Thumbtack, Instagram and TikTok.
  • Marc Lou's TrustMRR, a marketplace for buying and selling startups, shows the return of marketplaces; personal agents will want to use marketplaces and networks.
Worth noting
Greg describes service businesses in care as 'hard businesses to do'.
Cautionary tale SECURITY IN THE AGENTIC ERAsoftwaregeopolitics

Security: things will get hacked, so access control and protection become businesses

Greg expects a major agent-startup breach and wants to own security assets.

  • Software security: password managers (1Password) and Cloudflare with AI security features; his example is agent access control for accounting firms (read invoices, draft entries).
  • Approach: sensitive workflows, working through firms' existing IT providers, with access controls to stop leaks.
  • Prediction: 'I wouldn't be surprised if one of the personal agent startups has a big security breach and things get put on the internet' — and that this happens more and more.
  • Personal security is also a category: he mentions an 'Uber for bodyguards' with bulletproof cars (name not remembered).

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🧑‍💼
Business: Pick one industry and build an agent-delivered service you charge per completed job for, with a human reviewing exceptions.
📣
Distribution: Start with an organic audience, then community, then product (the ACP funnel) before spending on ads.
🗄
Data: Collect niche information personal agents can't fetch and sell it as a small, high-value data business.
🏗
Offline: Prototype the food-truck version of an offline business before leasing a space.
🤖
Robotics: Consider renting or selling robots rather than building them if you are not an engineer.
🔒
Security: Add access controls on agent permissions in any workflow touching sensitive data.
