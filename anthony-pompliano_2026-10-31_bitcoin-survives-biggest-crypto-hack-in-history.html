Confirmed event BINANCE HACKED, $6B — BACKSTOP PROMISEDcryptofinance

A $6B Binance hack moved Bitcoin just 5%, and the crypto ethos softened with it

The largest theft on record barely dented Bitcoin, because stablecoin issuers froze funds and Binance promised to cover users.

  • Binance hacked for $6 billion, reportedly through an engineer's laptop; rumors point to North Korea. Bitcoin sold off ~5%.
  • Tether, Circle and World Liberty Financial froze multiple billions almost immediately, so Binance won't have to cover the full $6B it pledged to backstop.
  • Pompliano: 10 years ago this would have meant a 20-40% Bitcoin drawdown; the backstop doubles as marketing for Binance, but trades away some of the bearer-asset ethos (ETFs may gain).
  • Visser frames it as AI-agent hacking, not a crypto story: Revolut, an apparel company and healthcare firms have been hit too, a wave he dates to around June.
  • Normalize the move: Google fell 13%, Intel 25%, and Eli Lilly rose 10% in two days. Bitcoin is now less volatile than the AI names.
  • 1999 NASDAQ analogy: a new electronic system broke under load until it earned trust. Banks that once called crypto garbage (Jamie Dimon) are now in tokenization, so they will force a security plan.
  • Quantum is the bigger fear Visser hears: BlackRock is spending time on it after quantum firms said publicly they could break Bitcoin by 2028 (while raising capital).

Microsoft's 60,000 buyouts open a labor-versus-capital fight, and that's bullish Bitcoin

Visser sees the buyout as Microsoft signalling how much AI will replace, a shift that squeezes tax receipts and turns affordability into politics.

  • 60,000 people is 27% of Microsoft's 223,000 staff; the last offer went to 8,000. At ~$400,000 a person with healthcare, that's $24 billion a year if all took it.
  • Headcount would fall back to 2020 levels while revenue will have grown 70% by next year, so revenue per employee goes through the roof.
  • Tax arbitrage: the employees pay 50%+ in taxes, Microsoft pays far less in corporate tax. Visser's Substack compares this to France, with affordability as the common thread.
  • Those 60,000 people vote; QE will return in a new form as governments respond to affordability.
  • Pompliano's public company: compute was $550,000/month in March 2026, now down ~50%; he aims to leave frontier models for own models and hardware (97% per-token saving); ~$10M total savings expected.
  • Ten people shipped five products in five days. Grokbot ships 50-100 features a week, so productivity is exploding while costs fall.
  • Software split: Salesforce still down for the year; the four hyperscalers are flat as a group, with Microsoft up 9%.

Eli Lilly: a non-cyclical cash cow funding the most interesting AI bet in the market

Visser calls Lilly the biggest AI company of the next five years: obesity cash funds AI drug discovery, with no memory bottleneck.

  • Q3 revenue $24.4 billion, up 39% vs $22.6B expected; volume +53%, price -15%, gross margin 83%; non-GAAP EPS $7.48 vs $6.26.
  • Mounjaro $10B (+65%), Zepbound $5B (+50%); 2026 guidance raised to $91-92B from $85-87B. S&P revenue growth is ~12% by comparison.
  • Obesity runway: ~10% of diagnosed US patients reached and ~2% globally; the shift from injectable to oral pill and lower prices drive volume (a Jevons-paradox effect). Dementia, oncology and immunology are growing too.
  • AI bets: TuneLab, its own data center, a Silicon Valley site to work with Nvidia, CEO Dave Ricks on stage with Nvidia. Novo Nordisk used the same cash cow for dividends instead.
  • Unfinished biotech IP is a call option Lilly can buy through M&A, plus GLP-1 data on why dementia, inflammation and addiction improve. Moderna-style re-ratings show the upside.
  • Apple analogy: the iPhone cash cow let Apple move into services.
  • Nvidia contrast: 70% revenue growth but a P/E down to ~12-13, stuck in the mud on overbuild risk; Intel is down because memory costs choke PC sales.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Quarterly refunding — The Treasury's quarterly announcement of how much debt it will issue and at which maturities.
RPO — Remaining performance obligations: contracted future revenue not yet recognized.
CDS — Credit default swap; a wider spread means the market prices more default risk.
Real yield — Nominal yield minus expected inflation (breakevens); ~250bp on the 10-year here.
Base effects — Year-over-year inflation falling because last year's spike drops out of the comparison.
Triple-C (CCC) — The lowest junk-bond rating tier; borrowing costs there are ~16%.
