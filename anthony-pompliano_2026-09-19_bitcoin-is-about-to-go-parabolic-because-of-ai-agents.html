The liquidity mechanism is broken — and that's why rates don't sink crypto anymore

Jordi argues liquidity now turns into profit margins instead of jobs, so the old M2-drives-Bitcoin model and the recession playbook both stopped working.

  • Since June 30: NASDAQ and S&P unchanged despite higher oil, rising 10-year yields, the July Situational Awareness AI unwind, Bessent yen/buyback chatter and Clarity Act on/off headlines; Ethereum up over 50% quarter to date.
  • Jordi wants rates and crypto rising together: a true innovation isn't rate-sensitive — calling Bitcoin "just liquidity" is his biggest disagreement with the crypto community.
  • Pomp frames the bull view he's hearing: M2 and Bitcoin have diverged for ~18 months (the "alligator jaws"), and past closings brought violent catch-up moves; Jordi says the four-year cycle was just the liquidity cycle.
  • S&P 500 earnings growing 30% while hiring ex-healthcare is negative over 20 months; the capital-vs-labor share is the worst in history for labor.
  • The smartphone era showed productivity in profit margins, not GDP — Jordi calls GDP "a horrible index" for software and intangibles.
  • New liquidity comes from tokenization: $900 trillion of assets, two-thirds dormant, become programmable collateral — the system runs on velocity of money, not M2.
  • Stock-market reads: home builders and consumer staples earnings are down on oil and rates, but not Micron's or Google's; AIQ (AI ETF) up ~25% year to date.

Agent-on-agent commerce needs blockchains — and privacy

Jordi's thesis: everything bad about agent swarms is fantastic for crypto, because agents will transact with each other and need programmable, anonymous rails.

  • Delta anecdote (unconfirmed): a fare rose each time a buyer returned — Jordi reads it as agentic personalized pricing, now at the government-complaint stage; StubHub uses AI pricing too.
  • Agent-on-agent warfare normalizes pricing: the consumer agent can shop a billion channels anonymously, the company agent can't — wallet-controlling agents win.
  • That's why Zcash and Near lead in September: privacy for business-to-business agent deals, not "DeFi cash"; a Transformer-paper co-author is building agent-to-agent at Near.
  • Instinct AI in practice: Pomp had it move a breakfast from Thursday to Friday — calendar invite, cancel, rebook — in ~2 minutes with no credentials given.
  • Instinct valuation went $500M → $2.5B → $10B in under 3 months (founded to $10B in ~a year); Jordi cites Peter Thiel: the steeper the climb, the more likely it's undervalued.
  • Pomp: the ignored layer is shared memory across assistants (Grok, Instinct, Meta's Muse); Signull on X uses email only via the agent — "my email has just become a database."
  • Models ship faster than people can adopt (Claude updates, Chinese open source, Astra) — like Waze vs. Google Maps, but 1,700 new ones the next day; a chief-of-staff agent will pick the tool for you.

Ghost rails: Bitcoin for 30 years, the convergence for the asymmetry

Pomp says altcoins end at Pets.com; Jordi agrees Bitcoin is the only 30-year asset but argues empty crypto rails fill up once agents, not humans, become the users.

  • Ghost rails = China's ghost cities: built ahead of users; humans never needed fractional, instant settlement — agents do. "Swarm equals crypto guardrails."
  • Crypto is following the internet: Netscape 1994 → iPhone 2007 (13 years) → App Store ~2009 (15 years); Bitcoin's white paper is on the same timeline, agents are the smartphone.
  • Unlike railroads built on debt waiting for humans, the users arrived in October last year: agents consume the compute and tokens, next transactions and payments.
  • Pomp's steelman against himself: a company with <15 people and 100+ agents is a user base growing without humans — open question is stablecoins and Bitcoin only, or the long tail.
  • Big players converging: Tether investing hundreds of millions in AI (models + privacy + agents + stablecoins stack); Robinhood's Vlad; Stripe's Tempo chart ramping; Figure's Mike seeded his own decentralized DTCC.
  • Jordi's convergence bet is 2 years old, rooted in Singularity University 2013; he bought into blockchain in 2014-15; tokenization matters more than stablecoins, and a 5-year regulatory exemption makes the Clarity Act moot.
  • Macro endgame: bonds flat for 7-8 years, private credit holders face pain getting money back — "the fiat system getting old and dying"; crypto is the only way to own non-public entrepreneurs.

Ghost rails — Jordi's term for crypto infrastructure built ahead of demand, like China's ghost cities, waiting for agent users.
Alligator jaws — The chart gap between rising M2 money supply and a lagging Bitcoin price, which bulls expect to snap shut.
Navier-Stokes — Long-standing fluid-dynamics math problem; reported solved by OpenAI using 10,000 collaborating agents.
Recursive self-improvement — Jordi's loose use: models releasing faster than humans can absorb them.
SPY perp — A perpetual-futures contract tracking the S&P 500 ETF, tradable 24/7 on crypto venues like Coinbase.
Tokenization — Turning real assets (e.g., a house) into programmable tokens usable as short-term collateral.
