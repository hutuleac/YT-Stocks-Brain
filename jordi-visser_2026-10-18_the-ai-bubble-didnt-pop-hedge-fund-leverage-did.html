High conviction DELEVERAGING UNDER WAY — HE SEES NO RETURN TO PEAK LEVERAGEfinance

Hedge fund leverage is the bubble that popped

Record hedge fund leverage, now being forced down by losses on the crowded AI trade, explains the selling, not AI fundamentals.

  • Fed/OFR data: hedge fund leverage was flat to 2023, then more than doubled to all-time highs through Q1; long-short equity runs close to two to one, and prime-broker leverage is also at record highs.
  • Leverage growth of 15% in hedge funds plus fast growth at broker-dealers; he puts hedge fund positioning at $14 trillion, vs 40-60B of CoreWeave debt spread over six years.
  • WSJ's illustrative numbers: $11B of equity running $45B of positions, about 33B borrowed — fine only while it's making money.
  • A $20B flagship AI fund fell 44% in a month in August; Lone Pine (Steve Mandel), which he calls one of the best non-multi-strat funds of the century, erased a 44% gain — over 55% gross after fees, a GFC-sized drawdown he calls "LTCM-ish".
  • Situational Awareness fell an estimated 60-70% before being bailed out; Goldman Sachs crowded hedge fund longs fell 10% vs the S&P in July and 15% over four months, unlevered.
  • His own thematic portfolio fell 11% last week, worse than any week in July, which "felt a lot like capitulation".
  • Why it won't come back: terminal value past three years is unknowable, so Sharpe ratios get harder, short-lockup funds can't survive drawdowns, and retail with AI tools now matches hedge funds' speed to ideas.

Contagion is spreading from the AI pair trade into credit and converts

The equity hit is narrow, but credit and convertibles joining in is what makes this phase dangerous for multi-strat pods.

  • Week: Qs down 5.5%, S&P down 1.95%, IWM down ~3% (six straight down weeks for small caps), yet 350 S&P stocks rose; two 1% S&P moves after ~100 days without one; VIX jumped 25 points in a week but still closed below 20.
  • S&P up 12% and Nasdaq up 13% YTD; semis (SMH) still up ~50% YTD; the AI trade is ~25% of the market and the crowded long everyone shares.
  • Correlation break: the Mexican Bolsa went from near 52-week lows straight up while the Qs fell; fertilizer/crop-input and short-SaaS trades also unwound.
  • Credit: a long HYG / short IEF synthetic credit trade gave back all its summer gains in one week; he heard biotech was a major pod problem in September.
  • Converts (CoreWeave, MicroStrategy) were hit in a "waterfall", as giant AI capital raises — Broadcom and Google each over $100B this year, plus SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI — force holders to sell other assets.
  • Volatility: single-name vol exploded while index vol barely moved, so there is no S&P put that hedges a cross-sector AI book; the MOVE index stayed calm, keeping this an equity event.
  • Timeline: deleveraging could take another month; the pain trade runs into hyperscaler earnings, possibly the midterms, then a year-end snapback if he's right.

High conviction NOT A BUBBLE — SUPPLY SOLD OUT INTO 2028-2030ai-infrasemis

2030 doom forecasts versus contracts already signed through 2030

Bears forecast impairment five years out while suppliers report capacity sold out or contracted for the same period.

  • Gartner: over $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure could face impairment by 2030; Bain: AI needs 6 trillion and comes up 800B short by 2031 — he has never seen a market care about forecasts that far out.
  • Contracts: Micron's signed agreements cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030; ASML expects supply shortages through 2028; Lumentum sold out of AI parts to 2029; Andy Jassy says Amazon's AI compute is sold out beyond 2028.
  • Nvidia guided to ~70% revenue growth vs ~40% expected; its 2029 street estimate went from ~305B to about a trillion, yet the stock is flat for a year and P/E on next year's earnings is ~15 (closer to 10 on high estimates).
  • CoreWeave is at this price for the fifth time; clients are Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic, Mistral and Jane Street — 'not strippers in Florida' getting subprime loans.
  • GPU rental for old chips holds up: per Zuckerberg on Cheeky Pint, even an H100 is worth more than a year ago; DRAM prices made new highs.
  • Dot-com was built for real future demand but had no money coming in; fiber took 7 years to build, and here we're in year two calling a bubble.
  • Hyperscaler stocks (Amazon, Meta, Google, Microsoft) made new all-time highs as a group, which he says argues against capital being the breaking point yet.

  • Anthropic is on pace for 90-100B revenue this year vs 9B last year; OpenAI + Anthropic finish ~170B combined, and a 600-700B forecast for them implies sharp deceleration already priced — in line with what David Sacks, Gavin Baker and Chamath said about six weeks ago.

Consumer confidence at a 2013 low as agent workflows hit jobs

The jobs market is weaker than headline unemployment shows, and agent adoption makes it a profit-margin and political story.

  • Consumer confidence fell to its lowest level since 2013, below the pandemic.
  • Jobs plentiful vs hard to get implies unemployment closer to 6%, like 2013.
  • Warren Pies: financials and tech payrolls are shrinking; excluding health care, jobs growth has been negative for about 18 months.
  • S&P earnings growth is running at 29% even as job creation slows, which he frames as a margin story, not a recession, though it could drive politics and the Fed.
  • Normal distribution of outcomes is breaking: losers (retail malls) lose growth to AI, winners (Amazon) compound, and middle names like banks drift toward losers.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Multi-strat / pods — Hedge funds running many independent trading teams under one risk budget, relying on low correlation across strategies.
Gross vs net exposure — Gross is total longs plus shorts; net is longs minus shorts. Multi-strats run high gross and low net.
HYG vs IEF — Long high-yield bonds, short Treasuries: a synthetic trade that isolates credit spread exposure.
Terminal value — The value of a company's earnings beyond the explicit forecast window; he argues AI makes anything past three years unknowable.
Lessons from leverage — His LTCM checklist: a correct thesis still fails on timing, volatility, financing, crowding, correlation breaks, liquidity or position sizing.
Telebras spread trade — LTCM's bet that Brazilian Telebras preferred and ordinary shares would converge at privatization, a correct thesis wrecked by leverage and timing.
Vera Rubin — Nvidia's next GPU platform, the hub of the agentic compute names on his 15-name list.
