High conviction POSITIVE VIEW — no crisis signal

"Stop with the bond fears" — the crash narrative is a trap

Visser insists the bond-market panic circulating on X isn't backed by any of the actual stress indicators, and he expects the government to fight higher yields rather than let a crisis develop.

  • 30-year yields are at 5.23%, within range of the highest since 2004 — but he says a real crisis would show up in bond volatility first, and it isn't: bond vol "would be up here, they wouldn't be sitting down here."
  • Junk spreads are near their tightest level ever (though he flags CCC-rated spreads widening, which he expects as AI disruption pushes some companies out of business).
  • Inflation swaps (1yr-10yr) all sit at 2.48-2.52%, matching core CPI (2.48%) — stable, not spiking — and he cites the daily Trueflation reading falling as further evidence prices are actually cooling.
  • 20-plus-year Treasury total return this year is -3%, versus -32% in 2022 when it actually mattered; Japanese 30-year JGB yields are up 117 basis points since May 2024's breakout, yet the Nikkei is up 52% year-over-year over the same stretch.
  • Cites economist Robin Brooks: this selloff has been ongoing since 2022 and "won't spin out of control into a crisis because governments won't let it" — Japan, Germany, the US, the UK, and Switzerland are all fighting it; Bessent is coordinating with Japan specifically to strengthen the yen.
  • S&P PEG ratio (forward P/E versus earnings growth) is at its lowest level since 1998 — earnings growth is outrunning valuation, which he says contradicts any "stocks are a bubble" claim unless you're calling for an outright market collapse.

  • Nvidia (NVDA) is approaching new all-time highs even as the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) sits below its 50-day moving average and trending down — Visser calls this divergence bullish, not a bubble signal, since Nvidia is "the head of the circular finance" that every other chip stock follows.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Double debasement — Visser's term for crypto capturing a rising share of fiat-denominated wealth as AI disrupts businesses and governments simultaneously suppress interest rates.
Silent IPO — His term for long-time ideologue Bitcoin holders quietly reducing their positions as newer, less ideological buyers take over the marginal buying.
Circular financing — The loop where Nvidia sells compute hardware to providers whose revenue comes largely from OpenAI and Anthropic, who in turn depend on that same compute to run their models.
RWA (real-world assets) — Traditional financial assets — money-market funds, equities, sports-team ownership — represented as blockchain tokens; Visser cites this market growing 18x.
Clarity Act — US legislation that would give crypto formal regulatory status, which Visser says matters mainly because it lets pension funds and insurers enter the market without fear of a future rollback.
ATR% (average true range percent) — The average 14-day trading-range-as-percentage across a basket of stocks; Visser uses its decline across his 100-name AI portfolio as a signal that forced selling has stopped.
PEG ratio — A stock's forward price-to-earnings ratio divided by its expected earnings growth rate; a low PEG suggests valuation isn't outrunning growth.
