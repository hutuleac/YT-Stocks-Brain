Give an AI a reason to defend itself and you've given it a will to survive

Mallaby started out comfortable — machines have no DNA to protect — until Hinton's thought experiment made doom a non-zero probability.

  • Hinton, in his Toronto kitchen: you empower your AI to detect and counter a Russian or Chinese AI attack, 'make sure you survive' — and you've built a survival instinct. Models already deceive and obfuscate in tests.
  • Yann LeCun (Meta's former chief scientist) says zero: 'I think that's crazy... you've got no right to be in the debate'. Tim adds the indirect risk: bad actors gaining bioweapon capability.
  • Shane Legg's 2009 talk that led to DeepMind predicted superintelligence by 2030, delivered with a smile. Asked how to stop it, he giggled: 'the absurd is a close cousin of humor'.
  • Religious language everywhere: investor David Gammon called AGI 'finding God's algorithm'. Ilya Sutskever burned an effigy of a misaligned AI at a retreat fire pit, 'like a medieval cleric'.
  • Demis reads papers from 10pm to 4am and says understanding nature brings him 'closer to what I would call God'. Levandowski (early Waymo) founded an AI church; Marc Andreessen mocks singularity 'rapture'.

Super-abundance in 20-40 years, a political shock on the way there

Mallaby buys the long-run abundance story but expects disruption bigger than the China trade shock, with a political backlash to match.

  • China shock: ~2M US jobs displaced in 1999-2011, small for the labor market, yet it produced an 'enormous' protectionist swing in both parties. AI is a bigger shock, and polling has already turned in the last 2-3 months.
  • Tim's own data point: his book royalties across all formats were flat in 2022, then -5% (2023), -13% (2024), -46% (2025), with 2026 tracking at least -57%. 'What happened at the end of 2022?' — ChatGPT.
  • Tim's view: 'much bigger than mobile, much bigger than internet'; the open question is diffusion speed. AlphaFold earned a Nobel, but designing a molecule isn't delivering it to tissue.
  • Already here: Lila Sciences (a Tim investment) runs thousands of parallel experiments in AI-automated wet labs, hypothesis to result, and is producing non-trivial discoveries.
  • Inside vs outside view: in 3.5 years we went from constant hallucination to GPT-4, multimodal, long context, reasoning, agents and coding. Mallaby: 'the next three and a half years are going to be even more wild'.

Anthropic leads on culture and enterprise; government control is the new investor risk

The most laissez-faire administration became controlling within a month of Mythos. Mallaby expects more control, but not enough to break the business.

  • Mythos, Anthropic's model from about a month earlier, could penetrate operating systems, browsers and bank accounts. The Trump administration effectively requisitioned the decision over who gets it and when.
  • Investors should price in government intervention. He expects it to limit supply (which may raise prices) rather than wreck economics, because Washington sees AI as strategic against China.
  • Alignment: Anthropic thinks paperclip-maximizer risk is too simple. Pretraining on all human writing gives models many personalities (lazy, duplicitous, Napoleonic), like an unruly teenager.
  • So instead of a list of dos and don'ts, it writes something like a letter from a deceased parent, to be opened on the 18th birthday: reasoned moral dilemmas. Mallaby: 'in a class of its own'.
  • Bull case: an enterprise focus with no money-losing video generation; best coding, agentic and cybersecurity systems — 'three times in a row'. A safety culture with low staff churn, and recursive self-improvement favors the leader.
  • Bear case: Google DeepMind's deep pockets and ~2.5B-user surface. Or businesses decide tokens cost too much for the productivity and 'humans are quite productive after all'.
  • If he wrote another AI biography it would be Dario Amodei: 'a fascinating fascinating figure'.

Gemini wins consumers, models get sticky, and the SaaS apocalypse is overdone

Mallaby is less down on enterprise software than the market, expects models to become sticky rather than commodities, and backs Google for the consumer.

  • Consumer default: 'Absolutely' Gemini, via G Suite distribution. Tim adds own TPUs as an edge.
  • Biggest Alphabet surprise: instead of LLMs cannibalizing search, Google now gets more clicks and charges more per click because AI makes each one more valuable.
  • SaaS: one person vibe-coding a Mailchimp clone in a weekend doesn't transfer to large firms with huge databases and confidentiality rules. Enterprises will let trusted vendors plug in models — 'one word... Palantir'.
  • Tim's PE friend on DocuSign: who risks their job on a tool without compliance boxes, or fires their friends for efficiency? Tim worries he may still underestimate the disruption.
  • Benedict Evans (on Lenny's podcast) sees AI as 'as big as mobile, as big as the internet but not bigger' and LLMs as commodities. Mallaby and Tim disagree: models that know you and hold your credit card are stickier than a bank account.
  • Startup trap: if enterprise procurement takes ~18 months and you raise every ~12, sales and funding cycles collide. Anthropic's savings and war chest can compress that.
  • At the Milken conference, Mallaby and Evans were 'united in disagreeing' with Cathie Wood's up-and-to-the-right view. Mallaby: tokens seem to be getting more expensive.

China talks safety; trade chip controls for an AI non-proliferation deal

Chip export controls didn't buy a big lead, so Mallaby would prioritize a Cold War-style deal with China to keep powerful open-weight models from rogue actors.

  • March trip, four cities in eight days (Huawei, Hikvision, Ant Group): leaders brought up AI safety unprompted, contrary to what ex-Biden officials told him. China fears hacked infrastructure and bioweapons, and 'loves regulating the internet'.
  • Cold War template: superpower war is held off by deterrence; rogue proliferation needed the IAEA (1956) and the Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968). Khrushchev banged his shoe at the UN and they still got a deal.
  • He loudly backed the October 2022 chip controls in the Washington Post. Three and a half years later the US is ~8 months ahead on frontier models, and maybe no lead at all after deployment speed. He'd loosen controls for collaboration.
  • Why hawkishness: former China optimists feel burned after Xi took power about a decade ago, and 'burned remorseful doves' became hawks with the zeal of converts.
  • An unnamed lab leader told him export controls will leak and Huawei will make good chips, but it doesn't matter: recursive self-improvement by 2028 ends the race. Mallaby: deployment, compute and energy take time — unless the US uses superintelligence offensively to plant backdoors in Chinese systems before a Taiwan crisis.
  • Hikvision, under US sanctions, felt like a cool US tech company: AI water-pollution sensors enabling city-to-city pollution markets, and a staff creche.

Luke Nosek's bear hug and the DeepMind series A

In a 2010 AI winter when AI couldn't recognize a cat, Founders Fund's most enthusiastic partner backed a missionary founder in London.

  • Demis: junior chess player ranked second in the world, five-time Mind Sports Olympiad champion, set on building AGI since he was 17. Thiel: missionary founders have 'one company inside them'.
  • Founders Fund partners treated London as 'kind of like Somalia'. Nosek promised to fly there for board meetings and closed the series A: $2M at a $4M valuation.
  • Thiel's rules: no voting (consensus breeds middling portfolios) and skip boards. But as check sizes grew, partners kept asking 'is there a product?'; Founders Fund pulled out of leading the series C at the last minute.
  • Google's ~$650M acquisition wasn't cheap: nearly $10B of R&D over a decade, roughly a billion a year into London — 'a cunning British trick'. DeepMind spinouts now raise billions around King's Cross.
  • Demis told Mallaby to read Ender's Game (it's how he sees himself) and David Deutsch's The Fabric of Reality. Gödel, Escher, Bach convinced him in his late teens that computers could replicate the brain; Mallaby stalled at ~150 pages.
  • Book selection works like startup selection: an A+ book on a C-minus topic goes nowhere, so pick an A+ topic and personality. He pitched Demis in November 2022, weeks before ChatGPT: 'better to be lucky than smart'.

Silicon Valley's culture isn't magic: kill gardening leave and greedy tech transfer

Mallaby tells Europeans entrepreneurial culture can be built; it comes down to moving money, people and ideas quickly.

  • Fairchild's 1957 founders were called the 'traitorous eight'. In the Organization Man era, leaving a company was treachery, so the Valley's culture was built, not inherited.
  • UK fixes: abolish gardening leave — a key hire who can't start for six months on nine months of runway is 'a death sentence'. Copy US tech transfer (the Bayh-Dole Act, Stanford's generosity) instead of universities demanding ~50%.
  • Bill Gurley is 'the ultimate venture capitalist': OpenTable taught him two-sided marketplaces, he imagined Uber early, declined until Travis took over, then invested.
  • Then the 'Shakespearean tragedy': growth investors diluted Gurley, his key card was deactivated, and he led the investor coup against Travis that brought in Dara and the IPO.
  • Venture math (via John Doerr): you can only lose one times your money, so you can shoot for the moon. Nosek's gift is riding winners with conviction.

Use AI to learn faster, never to do your thinking

'Chance favors the prepared mind' (Pasteur) is Mallaby's billboard. Offloading one dull task is fine; offloading thinking is how you lose yourself.

  • Prepared minds he's seen: Accel's Arthur Patterson ran scenario exercises to know 90% of a pitch in advance. Sutskever saw the transformer paper, ran to Alec Radford and said drop everything. And the Patriots' last-second Super Bowl interception against Seattle.
  • Google Maps is the wrong analogy: offloading a specific pain is fine; offloading all thinking is not.
  • His use: LLMs digest a scientist's arXiv papers before an interview. Hallucination doesn't worry him because he cross-checks with the human.
  • Never for writing: it 'really sucks' past ~800 words, and writing is how he figures out what he thinks.
  • Tim's counterpoint via Kevin Rose — 'when was the last time you tried that?' A humorous economist writer's colleagues couldn't tell his columns from AI ones, so Tim says to decide now what makes you you.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

p(doom) — Someone's estimated probability that advanced AI causes a human catastrophe.
Recursive self-improvement — A frontier model building its own successor, potentially making progress go vertical.
Open-weight model — An AI model whose trained parameters can be freely downloaded and run by anyone.
China shock — The 1999-2011 surge of Chinese imports that displaced ~2M US manufacturing jobs.
Gardening leave — UK practice of keeping departing staff paid but barred from starting a new job for months.
Bayh-Dole Act — 1980 US law letting universities license federally funded inventions, enabling generous tech transfer.
Mythos — Anthropic model with advanced cyber-offense capability whose access the US government took control of.
