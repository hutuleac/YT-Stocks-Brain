Weak leu, 150 days of interim government, and debt-funded services

The host opens with a stress snapshot: a weaker leu, higher yields and the longest government vacuum on record.

  • Leu at 5.35 per euro interbank, 5.34 at the BNR; experts expect steeper depreciation and some talk of 5.5, which the host "wouldn't go that far" on.

  • Imports and food get pricier; a 50-liter diesel tank costs about 100 euros, with diesel at 11 lei (would be 12 without the 20% excise cut).
  • Host's blunt line on rule of the day: no one cares to talk about stability "in last place in Europe" or unity "in misery".

A society far from equilibrium, with no puppet master

Romania is an unpredictable system where nobody, including the elites, has a coherent plan.

  • Borrowed from physics: systems far from equilibrium "can go in any direction"; sociologists, anthropologists, economists cannot say what awaits in 10 years.
  • Hope gone: the 1990s had a "horizon of expectation"; now people feel abandoned, some radicalize (a young woman threatening to blow windows out on TikTok was taken to Obregia hospital).
  • Frustration defined by a psychiatrist: having something and then losing it, not a cut-short hope; people retreat to strong codes (family, nation).
  • Puppet-master thinking is anthropomorphism: we give evil a name and face (Fritz as Germany's man, Bolojan France's, Nicusor Dan Macron's); the powerful are many and do not coordinate.
  • His answer: drop the hunt for who is behind whom; rationalize institutions and communication and choose those who want change over those who preserve.
  • Existential parent worry: AI is said to remove 90% of jobs, and a child finishing 8th grade cannot pick a high school.
Pull quote
“Societatea romaneasca asta a ajuns. E o societate departe de starea de echilibru.”— Dumitru Bortun
Worth noting

Nicusor Dan "did not betray, he lied"; Bolojan and false urgency

Bortun says reform voters were misled by a president who was never open to authentic change toward Europe.

  • Promises unmet: reform, the election report, new heads of intelligence services; instead monastery visits and speeches to Orthodox youth.
  • Refuses early elections although the coalition cannot coordinate; now governs with PSD-AUR support, which AUR itself says it may suspend him from.
  • His "love letter" to Dilema: six years in the West and "it did not please" him because it favors individual over group identity; Bortun calls community a double edge (Dogville).
  • "Healthy nationalism" is a contradiction: nationalism is ethnocentrism, sick from the start; Nicusor Dan shares it but would not say so for fear of Brussels.
  • Bolojan "does not talk like a liberal prime minister" and never addressed entrepreneurs or the middle class; "there is always something more urgent" is a false argument, like stability and unity.
  • The 1990s EU acquis (27,000 acts, CVM for judiciary reform) gave elites a task list; its end left a parochial justice system.
  • Even in PNL, ~25 conservatives want the status quo; a liberal orientation means an open mind plus a state acting as traffic cop.

Universalism vs particularism: from school uniforms to Ceausism

Both poles have costs; the danger is particularism pulled to extremes or dressed up as universalism.

  • Cosmopolitan/universalist: judge people by honesty and reason, not kin or neighborhood; rule of law and tolerance, but excess brings televangelism and stereotyped beauty norms.
  • Particularist: celebrates the unique; at extremes it yields nationalism, tribalism, "super-patriotism", and hostility to human rights and equality before the law.
  • School uniforms are a universalist equalizer against status competition (jewelry, trainers); Bortun backs them now to restore order; top British schools ban watches, jewelry and phones, with spartan dorms.
  • Britain's democratic strain: round table, Robin Hood, House of Commons in green; Marx, Freud and Rushdie sheltered there.
  • Ceausescu was particularism with a universalist face: left-wing rhetoric, "creative development" of theory, paying off foreign debt, losing the US Most Favored Nation status, then hunger, darkness and cold.
  • Personal story: queueing from 4 a.m. in Brasov at -20 degrees for four liters of milk while a university assistant with two children.
  • Romania has had no societal project for 35 years; he proposes moving from periphery to semi-periphery by 2050.

Left behind: low-cost airlines, a faded diaspora status and TikTok sovereigntism

People feel uprooted and disrespected, and emotion-first reasoning plus short video feeds channels that into sovereigntism.

  • Host stranded at 1 a.m. by a low-cost airline (Wizz Air named; Ryanair similar): no assistance, 340 euros for a middle seat home; Gen Z "stare" (looking through you) at counters.
  • Bortun: low-cost carriers reinvented themselves for working-class passengers after EU opening; Air France-KLM, Lufthansa and Alitalia suffered; airports are International Style metal and glass.
  • Diaspora used to return with 1,000 euros and authority; Romania has caught up and now treats them like anyone else; some ask if emigrating was in vain.
  • A Roma woman at the airport praised community (coffee, talk) versus Romanians' individualism; both feel unmoored.
  • TikTok built for people with no reading habit; Basu's Reason to Be Happy (Romanian edition, Publica, 2024): reason is the least-used quality, emotion decides and reason rationalizes after.
  • Teodor Paleologu's What Do We Do with So Many Fools? cited: some are too ignorant to know it; ends in conspiracy and guru worship (Calin Georgescu named).
  • Three attitudes: passeists (golden age, from Ceausism to Dacians), progressives, and the stay-put beneficiaries (state employees, special pensions, local barons).

Authoritarian turn, Russkiy Mir and two-thirds resistance to change

The main threat is a right-wing authoritarian regime growing out of a stalled "mimicked democracy".

  • Past crises produced dictatorships: Carol II, Antonescu, the Soviet-imposed proletarian one; Matei Dogan's Mimicked Democracy dates the pattern from 1866.
  • Continental fight is Russkiy Mir (religion-backed particularism) vs Western universalism; Russian hybrid war, false news and low-education voters are the vector.
  • Secondary analysis of polls: about two thirds against modernization (45-50% sovereigntist plus conservers), ~33% pro-modernization, concentrated in big cities and slow to mobilize.
  • Early elections expected in spring or summer (2027), then a clearer picture; without EU support he doubts democracy withstands the wave.
  • He says he is on a blacklist ("page three or four"); compares to the Legionnaires' lists and McCarthyism.
  • Advice: the center is hard to hold and needs reading and effort; stop hanging out on social media and follow people who matter at the source.
Pull quote

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Far from equilibrium — A system state where behavior becomes unpredictable and can go in any direction.
Universalism — A culture applying the same rules and values to everyone regardless of group.
Particularism — A culture celebrating what is unique to a group or person, risking tribalism at extremes.
Mimicked democracy — Matei Dogan's label for a regime with democratic form but without functioning structures.
Russkiy Mir — The "Russian world" paradigm, a religion-backed particularism opposed to Western universalism.
CVM — The EU Cooperation and Verification Mechanism for judiciary reform in Romania and Bulgaria.
