Method OBSERVATIONAL INVESTING SINCE 2007financeconsumer

Observational investing: spot the shift, then check if it moves a stock

Camillo argues markets stay inefficient because investors chase the same 20 companies and the Fed, while consumer shifts show up on TikTok first.

  • Origin: as a kid, a half-empty Snapple door at 7-Eleven (Arizona Iced Tea competition) led to $300 of puts that tripled on Snapple's first bad quarter.
  • Four tests: does it move revenue, perception or cost for a public company; do institutional or retail investors already know; is something bigger happening at the company; when will others find out (release date, credit-card data, earnings call).
  • A viral TikTok bottled water failed test one: its owner had bigger brands, so it could not move the needle.
  • Roof repair: Google Trends searches ran about 3x the prior five years in a hail season, so he bet with options on Beacon Roofing ahead of the insurance report hedge funds pay for.
  • Past trades: Barbie, Lionsgate with The Hunger Games, every shoe trend in 20 years, 100% of his account in a Nintendo ADR after trying the Wii at E3.
  • He still spends 1-2 hours a day (down from four) in TikTok comments ('the gold is in the comments'), e.g. Chili's cheese pull; for AI he reads developer forums, Reddit and X.
  • LLMs now do the dot-connecting: ask ChatGPT, Claude or Grok to validate the trend, find exposed companies and check what is priced in; more than half his ideas come from his Dumb Money Live community.

Contested HIGH-RISK BUCKETfinancemindset

A separate leveraged bucket, funded by trade-offs, not savings

He says wealth gets built through leverage, but only with money you can afford to see drop 90%.

  • An option lets $100 control about 10x the stock: a 10-20% move can return 100-200%, a 5% drop can wipe it out; max loss is known, unlike margin.
  • Claim: a 3x leveraged S&P ETF returned about 15% a year over 100 years versus 10%; $1 at 18 grows to about $50 by 60 unlevered versus $350 levered, so $100k becomes $3.5M not $700k.
  • He puts the odds of that 7x outcome at 99% by his research, accepting drawdowns of 50-90%.
  • Fund it from trade-offs (casino money, $18 bar drinks), never college or retirement money; analogy: you use a stove safely rather than never lighting fire at home.
  • Warning: kids buying far out-of-the-money options act like his son's friends on six-game parlays, and lose almost every time.
  • Trading costs used to be 2-3% each way, about 5% round trip, by phone; now free on Robinhood-style apps.

The $10 million Amazon earnings trade

After losing on options that expired before earnings, he put about a third of what remained on one-day calls and won.

  • Account was down almost 50% over a few weeks; the final bet was about 20% of the larger account, expiring in about a day.
  • Research was a 'matrix': developer sentiment, AWS compute spot pricing and contracts; he expected a beat above the ~25% whisper number.
  • The key was CEO Andy Jassy handling capex as Microsoft had days earlier: demand is real and much equipment spend can be delayed.
  • He rated it 80/20, with the 20% being things outside his control (White House, Iran, a surprise investment); he says he should have made it a $30M+ day; his previous best was $5M.
  • Context: the selloff before it came from the liquidation of Leopold's fund, which he calls artificial and mostly over.
  • A 1.2 million-view livestream of his call with a friend drew 'degenerate' criticism; he says it sat in an account built for risk.
  • He still holds Amazon heavily with some leverage, though no longer about 70% of the portfolio.

  • Phases: discovery (ChatGPT, Nvidia, his first AI win), infrastructure (still underway, 20-30 companies in chips, data centers, energy, memory), then the efficiency wave.
  • In the efficiency wave, incumbents with distribution and supply relationships win, because their AI agents innovate as fast as startups; that could lift the whole market.
  • He holds Palantir-type names, memory and data-center stocks, plus Japanese chip-testing equipment makers; he thinks compute stays scarce despite Chinese open models.
  • Bloom Energy is a big holding: the fastest way to power data centers now told to bring their own energy; volatile and misunderstood.

  • He was an early investor in one of the big three robot companies eight years ago and says he backs nearly all of them.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Observational investing — Trading on real-world shifts in behavior noticed before they show up in reported numbers.
Whisper number — The unofficial earnings expectation circulating among traders before a report.
Call option — The right to buy a stock at a set price before expiry; leverage with a capped loss.
Strike price — The price at which an option lets you buy or sell the stock.
ADR — US-listed certificate representing shares of a foreign company.
