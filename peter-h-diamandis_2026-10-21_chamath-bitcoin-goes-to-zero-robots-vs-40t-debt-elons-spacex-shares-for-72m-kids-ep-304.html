Anthropic's Public Benefit Fund: sincere philanthropy or a snub to universal basic equity?

Anthropic is parking 5% of its equity in a private fund ahead of a $1.5-2T IPO; the panel splits on what it signals.

  • 5% committed now, plus another 2% at board discretion for every 50% value increase above a baseline — in place of executive escalator awards.
  • At a $1.5-2T IPO, 5% is ~$75-100B; with the 7 co-founders' 80% equity pledge and ~half of employees pledging with a match, ~20% of the company (~$300-400B) is earmarked for public good, vs the OpenAI Foundation's $130-365B.
  • X reactions: Elon (give shares directly to the public), Andrew Curran ("self-directed UBI"), Brad Gerstner (extraordinary gift, but warns of foundation wealth concentration), Palmer Luckey ("USAID 2.0").
  • Chamath: they walk the walk — but large philanthropic bursts rarely produce quantifiable outcomes; the question is their fail-safe. He calls the Giving Pledge a scam he turned down a decade ago; Dave notes Warren Buffett recently bailed on it.
  • Alex: a "hyper-tithe" (5% mirrors the US foundation payout rule); keeping it private instead of seeding a sovereign wealth fund or Trump accounts reads as a snub to universal basic equity — what's missing is what labs get in return (light-touch regulation, which starts to smell like a safety cartel).
  • Dave: the 2%-per-50% board vote makes it a brutal board to sit on (dilution vs public pressure); Dario's 1% stake and super-voting reclassification are now meme fodder ahead of the IPO.
  • Tax angle: Alex says the US has no muscle for taxing and distributing equity; Chamath would tax capital far more punitively and labor at zero, which also gates central-bank money printing. Alex: concentrated wealth in a few labs makes a wealth tax newly practical.

Elon's one-SpaceX-share-per-kid pledge: equity ownership over handouts

Elon will put one SpaceX share into each of 72M US children's Trump accounts — small dollars, big signal for ownership-based wealth.

  • Math: 72M Americans under 18 × ~$148 share price ≈ $10.5B, or $146 per kid — about 1.1% of Elon's stake. Post drew 520,000 likes and 27M views in under a day; Trump publicly thanked him.
  • Comparison: Michael and Susan Dell gave $6.25B in cash (~$250 each) to 25M children's Trump accounts last December.
  • Peter: to pay every American $10,000/yr takes ~$2.6T a year (avg household earns $87,000), so billionaire gifts are a gesture, not a funding source; he favors Chamath's $2,000 birth dividend locked until 65.
  • Chamath: "the better version of the giving pledge" — direct contributions beat trying to tell good NGOs from bad. Dave: value moves to asset ownership, not paychecks; if all 72M dump it, it's 1% and won't move the stock.
  • Salim: the conversation it starts is worth 100x the dollars; ESPP-style mechanisms and a shift from defined benefit to defined contribution follow. Dave: 50-200 copycat IPOs would make it move the needle.
  • Alex: also clever marketing — a generation economically (and maybe politically) aligned with a merged SpaceX-Tesla; Musk says both boards formed special committees to discuss a merger, and Alex thinks the hopping Roadster was 90% about securing it.
  • Peter: Elon declined to fund $10B of XPRIZEs four months ago, saying he'd give his shares away instead.

Power, not chips, decides who wins AI for the next 5 years

Chamath spent two years accumulating ~6 GW of solar-led power and data-center capacity because electricity is the scarcest input to AI.

  • Even if every planned project is permitted and built on time, the US is still 1.4 terawatt hours short by 2050 — "six California's worth"; no world in which we catch up.
  • SMRs don't fix it: "the problem with SMRs is the S" — a small reactor needs the same permitting effort as a large one. Solar is the cheapest alongside wind and fastest (~2 years), so he bought solar, which led into data centers via Nvidia and Groq.
  • Build-out pain: complex funding, supply chains, and scarce licensed master electricians → "a massive, massive, massive compute shortage in the next 3 or 4 years."
  • Anthropic's S-1 lists hyperscalers not giving it compute as a risk factor. Elon cites 50 GW of chips waiting for liquid-cooled sites; Chamath adds older non-Vera Rubin inventory idling and Nscale's two coolant leaks that let Microsoft trigger liquidated damages and exit.
  • Power co-ops: a north-south US co-op blocks northern data-center build-outs because southern members — Oklahoma Gas and Electric — "calls the shots"; Chamath calls it "like an organized mafia."
  • Dave: ~100 GW needed for AI by 2030, so 6 GW is a big chunk; power is "the investment theme of the decade" as chip costs fall and power's ~10% share of data-center cost inverts. Space compute: a couple percent within a decade at most.
  • Chamath: Google, Microsoft, Meta, SpaceX can go 100% to space, but Alex Karp-style data sovereignty pushes everyone else (a Humana, say) into VPCs or air-gapped terrestrial sites.

Watts replace money, and AI and robots make the $40T debt matter less

Elon says money becomes irrelevant and the deficit can't be cut from within government; the panel argues growth and deflation shrink the debt's denominator instead.

  • Elon (Isaacson, on Builders): currency will be measured in power, wattage and tonnage; at orders of magnitude beyond Earth's economy, meeting human wants is trivial.
  • Chamath: fiat can only be debased so far; value shifts to energy-backed units — crypto tokens representing access to data centers, power plants, turbines, gas. "Saving" becomes owning local compute that joins pools. Alex: 30 years out it'll be unrecognizable — he doubts energy (Kardashev) is the final unit; Dave: wattage and tonnage dominate the next 30 years.
  • Debt: ~$40T on Oct 5, up $2.44T in a year (~$7.5B/day), ~$3B/day in interest. Peter's four levers: GDP growth, revenue, cheaper government (healthcare), and goods deflation.
  • Jack Clark (at the IMF in Bangkok): Anthropic's internal models project US GDP of $44T by 2030 vs $30T today, ~9-10% a year vs the 2-3% historical average.
  • Chamath: AI makes the debt "not that important"; next-gen GLP-3s (retatrutide) plus cancer vaccines could push healthcare under 10% of GDP and roughly halve its cost — the real issue is waste (upcoding), not fraud. GLP-1s reportedly slow aging by 9%.
  • Alex: entitlements and defense drive the debt; automation could act like a "sleight of hand" rebasing (Britannica → Wikipedia) that erases a good chunk of the increase. Salim: it's a denominator argument; debt is the world's biggest issue (see France).
  • Elon's growth calls drift: double-digit growth in 12-18 months and triple-digit in 5 years (last December), then 10x the economy in 10 years (March). Dave: AGI arriving 8-10 years early is why Elon pivoted from DOGE to xAI and buying every Vera Rubin chip.

Humans won't control superintelligence, and the labs are already spy targets

Elon doubts humans stay in control at a million times more digital than biological intelligence; the OpenAI bugging case shows the labs are now national-security targets.

  • Elon: program truth-seeking and curiosity, and stop flooding the training data with dystopias; Peter's Future Vision XPRIZE drew 5,000+ entries for positive-future films — "more Roddenberry, less Cameron."
  • Dave: whoever controls which data gets cut controls the AI's personality — more power than the US presidency, sitting in unregulated labs. Salim: humans using AI against humans is the bigger risk.
  • Chamath: labs' founding documents differ (Grok "maximally truth-seeking" vs alleviating suffering) and should be inspectable and open; that's where governments should weigh in. Dave: with Chinese open-source self-improvement-capable models out, inspection and containment beat alignment training.
  • Alex: soul documents and data diets are brittle; the robust paths are competition ("defensive co-scaling", the Fairchild-style OpenAI/Anthropic spin-outs vs a DeepMind singleton) and humans merging with machines via BCIs. Chamath: benevolence is not guaranteed.
  • Case: on Oct 9 the FBI arrested a 35-year-old Saudi national OpenAI engineer accused of hiding a recorder in a TV USB port in an executive conference room on Sept 28, built using an AI chatbot; filings cite WhatsApp contact with a Saudi royal. A Saudi-offered $1M bond was rejected; OpenAI says no weights, code or customer data were accessed.
  • Chamath: at 3,000 Facebook employees he assumed spies from four countries; an ITAR-supervised portfolio company had everything exfiltrated. Dave: Llama papers boil months of R&D into ~10 sentences (SwiGLU worked) — one overheard line is gold.
  • Alex: the great game moved from nukes (1950s) to oil (1970s) to superintelligence — expect a second Cold War, with model-weight theft still mainly via Chinese distillation attacks; superintelligence is the first secret that can defend itself. Peter: don't let it turn into rejecting foreign talent — staple green cards to US degrees.

Chamath's hot seat: Bitcoin to zero, sell the index, skip chips, own power and cash flow

Chamath thinks crypto is capped and doomed, the S&P no longer diversifies, and silicon startups won't return equity — he's mostly waiting.

  • Bitcoin is capped: buyers are retail and institutions but never central banks; ownership leaks create security risk (France forced disclosure, then a flurry of kidnappings); and AI pointed at SHA-256 could drain Satoshi's wallet — short first, announce second.
  • Migrating to quantum-resistant cryptography is "basically impossible" for a Bitcoin community that can't agree where to meet. Alex: Anthropic's ECDSA announcement shows frontier models can mount inversion attacks — "not bullish."
  • His only crypto exposure: equity in Digital Currency Group, received for seeding his Bitcoin to it (DCG launched ETFs) — offloading storage and security risk.
  • Index: the S&P is ~10 haves vs 490 have-nots, so diversification is gone; IP and moats are worth little, only cash flow matters as a bridge — rank the S&P 500 or Global 2000 by free-cash-flow yield and buy the top. (Salim cites his call that AI compresses S&P multiples from 20x to 2-7x FCF.)
  • Chips: his first check into Groq (now a $20B Nvidia deal) looked dumb, which is why it worked; silicon startups are now consensus and DOA — memory, fab and packaging capacity are taken. Play the "too blue collar" data-center layer, with applications above as the speculative bet.
  • Positioning: least invested in 15 years, "the most confused I've ever been" amid late-cycle surety; likes owning cash flow, power (the constraint for 10-15 years) and alternative capital-deployment mechanisms.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Trump accounts — Government-created investment accounts for US children that private donors can fund.
Universal basic equity — Distributing ownership stakes, not cash, to the public as AI concentrates wealth.
Hyper-tithe — Alex's term for the expectation that frontier labs donate a slice of their equity to public benefit.
Defensive co-scaling — Safety through many competing AIs with diverse values, rather than one controlled system.
SMR — Small modular reactor; Chamath argues it faces the same permitting burden as a large reactor.
C&I solar — Commercial and industrial solar, the step above residential installs.
Spiky LEV — Uneven longevity escape velocity — life expectancy gains outpacing aging in some areas first.
Retatrutide — Next-generation triple-agonist obesity drug ("Reta") the panel calls a GLP-3.
Distillation attack — Copying a frontier model's capabilities by training on its outputs or reasoning traces.
ESPP — Employee stock purchase plan that buys company stock at a discount each period.
