AI capex, not the Fed, set this year's yields, and the debt wave is peaking

Visser blames concentrated AI borrowing for this year's rate rise, and sees that pressure easing in 2027.

  • Rising real yields this year came from nominal GDP plus front-loaded AI debt, as a few companies raced to fund a demand backlog.
  • Bank credit heads told him insurers bought any 10-year-plus paper in the first half, then hit concentration limits, since the biggest borrowers are also the biggest equity holdings; that, not distress, widened CDS.
  • Oracle raised ~$60B of debt and equity over 12 months; at its investor day it guided free cash flow positive in 2028, 'massively' positive in 2029, needing only ~$20B more.
  • Citing Gavin Baker: compute prices rose after the build decisions were made, so payback comes sooner and borrowing falls; Goldman sees IG issuance growth peaking in H1 next year.
  • SPVs and private credit pick up slack: SpaceX raised $40B through one (he thinks with Apollo), and private credit has dry powder a year after its scare.
  • Treasury raised buybacks and 10-year yields eased; with David Zervos now at Treasury, he expects the Nov 4 refunding to lean on supply, pushing long rates lower.
  • Net: rates stay higher than otherwise because capital demand is continuous, but less concentrated.

Microsoft's 60,000 buyouts: governments may have to back labor because capital is winning

When a growing company sheds staff at record highs, the pressure lands on government, and he expects profits to be redistributed toward labor.

  • 60,000 buyouts is ~27% of Microsoft; if all took it, headcount returns to 2020 levels while revenue is ~70% higher, so revenue per employee goes parabolic.
  • His math: ~$400,000 a head means ~$24B saved, a way to pay for capex without raising capital; even paying half a year's salary costs ~$12B.
  • Tech leads because it can absorb AI fastest (Block did the same); most Fortune 500 firms will find it harder.
  • This time it happens with stocks at highs and jobless claims at record lows, while the government needs individual tax receipts and runs a big deficit.
  • Not 'UBI': he expects higher corporate tax rates above a revenue-per-employee threshold and lower individual rates, which favors small businesses over large ones.
  • Productivity is already showing in profit margins: outside healthcare there's been no hiring for ~20 months while profits grow, which he says has never happened before.
  • GDP was built for industrial goods; the 1990s numbers were revised up, then revised again when R&D was counted as IP.

Relative negative MULTIPLE SHRINKING WHILE EARNINGS GROWsoftwareai-infra

Code-based public companies lose to AI natives, and the S&P multiple is the warning

He stays relatively negative on hyperscalers, not over free cash flow but because AI-native rivals threaten the businesses themselves.

  • Hyperscaler free cash flow is 'not a story': balance sheets are pristine and the spend is on future revenue; bearish talk comes mostly from people who don't use AI, in his view.
  • Anthropic's S-1 showed $16.4B revenue vs Google's ~$1.5B at its 2004 S-1, over a similar 5-6 years, with a fraction of Google's couple-hundred-thousand staff.
  • Headcount is now a risk: more people means more laptops and attack surface, while AI natives build on current tech from day one.
  • S&P earnings are up ~30% this year but the index only ~11%; he reads the shrinking multiple as the market warning.
  • He expects next year's hit to come from labor and politics, memory and data-center bottlenecks, or bigger hacks, not the Fed or Iran.
  • Phil Rosen owns Google and defends it (-$9B free cash flow, but ~$500B "this year"); Visser: 'you could have said the same thing about Ford 50 years ago.'

Eli Lilly: a non-cyclical compounder AI can't compete away

Healthcare is the one benefit of AI he thinks can't be competed away, and Lilly is the purest play.

  • Compounded 30%+ a year for 10 straight up years, yet almost nobody emails him about it.
  • Only ~10% of the ~100M obese Americans are on it; pills are coming after injectables, and revenue is up ~40% year over year.
  • Non-cyclical and regulated: buyers are aging, Medicare and Medicaid pay, and rivals face FDA approval, 'like a utility'.
  • Growth beyond obesity: immunology, oncology, dementia, with cash flow funding M&A.
  • Healthcare outperformed in both the dot-com bust and the GFC (agreeing with Adam Parker on Rosen's show).
  • Longevity as a theme: long travel and recreation, and 'you don't want to be long McDonald's anymore' as GLP-1 use spreads.
  • Rosen: Lilly is his largest single stock, bought after Visser's earlier call.

Back into Micron: no bubble, just a bottleneck, with tamer upside

Long-term memory contracts and Elon's compute plans convinced him to rebuild a position he had fully sold.

  • His call at $89 in early 2025 ran to ~$1,200; he sold fully in May-June at an average in the ~$700s and has bought back in the low $800s.
  • Position is back to roughly its January 2025 size, built in the two weeks before the interview, after five months of sideways price and retail/hedge-fund shakeout.
  • Micron and SK Hynix signed contracts running well into 2030; Corning and SpaceX reports show buyers locking supply five years out.
  • SpaceX talks of ~10 GW of compute possibly next year; Elon calls RAM 'the scarcest asset on Earth' for humanoids.
  • Risk-reward now: downside 10-20%, upside 60-80%; a double would surprise him, and a dip back to $700 wouldn't shock him.
  • Echoing Adam Parker: inning three of the AI trade but inning five for the stocks, so it's a harder trade than a year ago.
  • Rosen: Marvell had a bad week, Nvidia 'doesn't seem to move', and he bought more Micron too.

Crypto is his favorite theme, and 2027 is its year

Rate pressure on the government plus agentic commerce and tokenization make crypto his largest bet by far.

  • 95 of 100 people he meets with money don't believe in crypto or know how to play it, which he counts as a positive.
  • He was Bitcoin-only until summer, when Bessent effectively admitted a long-yield problem; he never expected QE-style support again.
  • Deficit: Bessent promised 3%, but spending can't be cut, so expect 5-6% with rising interest costs and an affordability-squeezed young electorate (he links France to Microsoft).
  • Agentic commerce takes off over the next year; he calls Stripe enormously important and under-followed.
  • Tokenization changes M2 and brings 24/7 trading; the AI trade was more volatile than Bitcoin this year.
  • Extended into Ethereum and privacy names; runs a 46-name crypto list (like his AI 100) and owns many of them.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

CDS — Credit default swap; wider spreads here reflect hedging of concentration, not default fear, in his view.
Quarterly refunding — Treasury's announcement of how much debt it issues and at which maturities.
SPV — Special-purpose vehicle that raises debt against specific assets, off the parent's balance sheet.
IG issuance — Investment-grade corporate bond supply.
Tokenization — Putting real-world assets on blockchains so they trade 24/7.
