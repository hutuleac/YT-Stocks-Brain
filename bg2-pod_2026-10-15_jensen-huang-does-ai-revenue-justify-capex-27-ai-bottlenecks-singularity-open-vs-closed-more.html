Reasoning, harnesses and falling cost multiply compute demand

Thinking takes far more inference than recall, and an AI that works gets more work. Jensen says those two effects compound into his billion-fold compute call.

  • Reasoning means planning, rolling out options, reflecting and pruning, which is 'strategic optionality' for AIs. Every one of those steps costs inference.
  • The 2025-26 unlock was the harness, the 'exoskeleton' around the model: working memory, offline 'compaction' into a knowledge graph (like sleep), and tool use.
  • Agents will use humans' existing tools (SPICE, Verilog, simulators, Photoshop, AutoCAD, Siemens tools) instead of rebuilding them. Coding was the easiest first on-ramp.
  • Next comes design and creative work. Nvidia uses OpenAI's Astra to turn a photo into an editable CAD model.
  • Navier-Stokes was solved with ~10,000 agents and ~$4M of compute (the hosts' estimate). Jensen notes it built on decades of human work: many fields sit on a 'precipice' that exhaustive AI search can push over.
  • Cost of intelligence: GPT-1 cost ~$10-20M to train and is ~$100 now, while models grew to 3T, 10T and 30T parameters and usage rose ~1,000,000x. He wants intelligence cost to fall '2x or 20x' a year, like Moore's law.
  • This was all done with OpenAI and Anthropic on ~2-3 GW each. The hosts expect them to 3-5x their compute next year.

Physical AI is already Nvidia's next $100B line, and he calls that 'understated'

Nvidia calls itself the world's largest physical-AI company: $5B, then $10B, now on a ~$20B annual pace.

  • $10B in the first six months of 2026, after $10B last year (its first $10B year) and $5B the year before.
  • Scope covers the whole electromagnetic spectrum: photon-counting CT, ultrasound, imaging radar, 5G/6G sensing networks, geospatial satellites and 3D world reconstruction.
  • The actuation layer covers self-driving, warehouse pick-and-place robots that learn instead of being programmed, home robots, and 24/7 hospital and surgical robotics.
  • Three computers per platform: one to train the brain, Omniverse as the digital twin where the robot learns, and the computer inside the robot or car. Nvidia builds all three stacks and sells any one of them.
  • The same logic applies to frontier labs: they need a digital twin of the internet so agents learn tools without touching the live web.
  • Every industry becomes a tech company: carmakers (as Elon did with Tesla), logistics (FedEx), retail (Walmart), and telcos (AT&T, Verizon) going 'back to technology'. The plan is to repeat the Alpamayo automotive playbook in each vertical.

Safety as engineering: zero-trust containment, out-of-band monitoring, a three-legged stool

Jensen rejects 'pace it' framings. Safety is part of the product, like airbags and ABS are part of the car, so accelerating AI means accelerating safety first.

  • Alignment is a forever problem: 'be successful in life' has infinite valid readings across cultures. If safety depends on perfect alignment, 'we're never going to get there'.
  • Treat a new AI like a toddler or a new hire: zero trust, minimum rights, a playpen. Widen the boundary only as testing earns confidence.
  • Monitoring must be out of band, because a system that knows it is watched behaves differently. It needs the power to escalate or shut down. Nvidia's OpenShell does this and has 100+ companies on board.
  • Nvidia also sells security now: OpenShell plus Sentry are part of its per-gigawatt content.
  • Gerstner's 'three-legged stool': CAISI/Commerce review of every frontier model (it already pulled Mythos using export-control authority), the White House Accord on Superintelligence (audits, board oversight, liability), and the SI Force led by Jay Clayton. Gates says it is not enough.
  • At the cabinet-room signing (with Dario Amodei and Greg Brockman) nobody needed 'a headlock'. Dario opened with 'whoever wins AI wins'.
  • Winning is redefined as every industry winning (Eli Lilly, Walmart, FedEx, Boeing, the US military, schools), not the tech sector. Unsafe AI is 'an existential issue for us' first.

Contested DEMAND > SUPPLY 'FOR A FEW YEARS'ai-infraenergysemis

2027's binding constraint is land, power and shell, and it pushes share toward Nvidia

Demand is 'definitely there and more'. The physical buildout is what slows things down. Jensen argues scarce watts go to the chip with the most tokens per watt.

  • SemiAnalysis (Dylan Patel) puts new AI compute at 43 GW in 2027, more than all AI compute in the world today, and another ~40 GW in 2028.
  • The street view: ~50 GW of chips announced versus ~35-40 GW of land/power/shell actually coming online.
  • Physical obstacles: permitting, power generation, local moratoriums, and skilled trades. Building these '$50 billion data centers' creates about a million jobs, and the skills 'are not fungible'.
  • Upstream constraints: substrates, where bigger packages with more chips worsen thermal-coefficient warpage and defects, and silicon photonics. 'Everything is supply constrained.'
  • Nvidia's hedge is global reach: every CSP, every NCP, Dell/HP/Lenovo, and new builds in Africa, Central Asia, Southeast Asia, Latin America and the Middle East. 'I don't love that the United States can't keep up.' A recent 2 GW deal in Australia is worth ~$80B to Nvidia, and Jensen hopes to announce more land/power/shell partnerships on his Europe trip.
  • Content per gigawatt rose from ~$18B to ~$20B+ and now $40B+, because Nvidia added networking (now the largest networking company), CPUs ('surely the fastest growing'), storage, knowledge infrastructure and security.
  • Share of world capex: single digits six years ago, ~40% at the CSPs today, ~80% outside them (on-prem and neoclouds). Both halves are 'doubling every year'.

Does lab revenue justify capex? 'We're putting way too much pressure on two labs'

The bear math needs lab revenue to grow from ~$200B to $400B to $800B. Jensen says that misreads who actually buys the compute.

  • The bear framing (Clark Tang): ~$200B of lab revenue this year has to double twice. A naive 40 GW × $40B/GW implies $1.6T of revenue the demand curve can't absorb. Claude Code drove a 10x jump in Anthropic revenue this year.
  • Jensen's thirds: ~1/3 is classical computing (data processing, simulation, XGBoost fraud detection at Visa/Mastercard, recommenders), growing 30-40% a year with no AI at all. 'Take that to the bank.'
  • ~25-30% is frontier labs, and 'five, six' of them, not two: Meta's Muse (zero last year), Grok, Gemini (newly on Nvidia) and Anthropic (new this year).
  • ~1/3 is 'AWS for the future': thousands of startups renting Nvidia compute, including Bezos's Prometheus, Noah, and physical, bio and materials AI. 80% use open models such as Nemotron to build domain superintelligence.
  • Moore's law is over: twice the transistors now costs twice as much. Pre-AI compute ran ~$0.5T a year, heading to ~$1.5T of compute within $3-4T of annual infrastructure by 2030 ('closer to four').
  • General-purpose compute cost ~$5B per gigawatt versus ~$50B for Vera Rubin. The test is whether it delivers 10x the value.
  • Market chatter that OpenAI's revenue was $50B rather than $70B hit infrastructure stocks. Jensen says the labs will be 'magnificently successful' without needing anywhere near $1T of revenue.

The sight-unseen moat: who else gets a $100B PO for an untested system?

You can't benchmark a $50B data center before you buy it, so buyers pay for track record and fungibility. Jensen says that makes the moat wider as the company gets bigger.

  • Vera Rubin Ultra is 'close to placing order time', bought sight unseen. Buyers trust it because Vera Rubin is 'awesome', and Vera Rubin was trusted because Grace Blackwell was.
  • A $100M purchase could be benchmarked. A $50-100B system needs 'a giant data center and a couple years to retrofit' just to evaluate. 'Just building it is a miracle.'
  • The analogy is TSMC: Nvidia gives it 100% of its business because it is the only foundry it can plan five years out with, and the only one big and agile enough to absorb Nvidia's swings.
  • Specialization is the enemy of fungibility and durability. CUDA runs every architecture shift (flash attention, NVLink 72 sharding, hybrid SSM/transformer and diffusion models), so capacity can be subleased or redeployed anywhere.
  • The chain: fungibility + durability → rentability → productivity → financeability → lowest TCO.
  • Altimeter's chart: consensus moved up 100-200% in a year (a year ago it saw flat or shrinking 2027; now +74%), yet the stock is up only ~20% at 13-14x earnings.
  • Guide was 70% revenue growth on demand 'well over 100%'. Jensen published it to manage allocation, because every CSP, NCP and OEM was getting less than it ordered. Orders arrive 'weekly and daily'.

Monetizing the installed base: software licensing, revenue share and a 'growth value' buyback

Jensen describes two new revenue lines tied to the installed base rather than hardware sales, while Nvidia returns most of its free cash flow.

  • Software licensing: CSPs take Nvidia's stack open-source, but enterprises without IT depth need Nvidia on the SLA to run AI factories. 'A couple thousand dollars per GPU per year' × ~20M GPUs a year, half going to enterprise, 'adds up'.
  • Revenue share: neoclouds that have land/power/shell lean on Nvidia for full stack, offtake and financing. Nvidia invests, books the sale up front and takes revenue share for '4, 5, 10 years'.
  • Buybacks: guided to 50% of free cash flow, did '60 some odd' percent last quarter. Gerstner's math is upwards of 10% of the company repurchased within two years.
  • Gerstner frames it as Apple's playbook (the stock went from ~$30 to $300+ after it started returning capital), answering Buffett's complaint that tech firms spend their cash windfall on worse bets.
  • Strategic investments: 'whoever Jensen invests in, you invest in'. Nvidia is 'in the flow of information'. These will shrink as a share of cash because the OpenAI/Anthropic opportunity 'isn't going to come around again'.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Harness / exoskeleton — The scaffolding around an LLM (memory, knowledge graph, tool use) that turns a model into a working agent.
Land, power, shell — Sited land, energized power and a built data-center building: the physical prerequisites before chips can be installed.
Out-of-band monitoring — Oversight running outside the AI system's own channel, so it can't detect being watched and can be escalated or shut down.
Three computers — Nvidia's physical-AI model: a training system, an Omniverse digital twin for simulation, and the onboard computer in the robot or car.
NCP — Nvidia Cloud Partner: neoclouds and regional clouds outside the hyperscaler CSPs.
Fungibility (compute) — Ability to redeploy the same infrastructure across workloads, models, customers and regions, which lowers financing risk and TCO.
Revenue share (Nvidia) — Nvidia invests in a neocloud, books the hardware sale, and takes a share of its revenue for years in return for offtake and software support.
