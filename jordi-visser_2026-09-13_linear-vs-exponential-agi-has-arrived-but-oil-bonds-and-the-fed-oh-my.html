Oil, bonds and the Fed stopped being the story in 2008 — AI capex is

The market shrugged off hot inflation data and rising rate-hike odds this week, and Visser says that's the whole point: you're either trading the old linear economy or the new AI-capex one.

  • This week: core CPI and PPI both came in higher than expected, Fed rate-hike odds jumped (Jackson Hole odds cited at up to 90%), oil rose, and the war news worsened — yet the S&P was up slightly over the two days. Old linear inputs didn't move the tape.
  • Visser dates the regime change to 2008: the iPhone's release the year before plus a sharp rise in government transfer payments/debt ended the old housing-and-autos economy that oil/bonds/rates actually drove.
  • Credit confirms it: HYG relative to IEF (his real-time credit-spread proxy) isn't falling even as bonds sell off — contrast with 2022, when credit sold off alongside equities as the Fed hiked aggressively.
  • Bessent is openly fighting the bond market (upsizing buybacks, coordinating with Japan's finance minister, daring Fed's Warsh) — a deliberate signal the administration doesn't want, or need, higher rates to matter.
  • Bitcoin Policy angle: Bessent's own framing — "there is no day after tomorrow if China wins" the AI race — is Visser's justification for not fighting either the AI trade or the crypto trade.

Jensen Huang: AGI has arrived with Astra — and agents reportedly solved Navier-Stokes

Between OpenAI's Astra release and a reported multi-agent solve of a 200-year-old physics problem, Visser treats this as the week AI crossed from "impressive" to "agentic."

  • Astra was trained on 100,000 GPUs; the next model is roughly 4x that size, with Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips behind it.
  • Research agents are now reportedly generating the equivalent of 3.1 workdays of output per human workday.
  • 10,000 AI agents working in parallel for 88 hours reportedly cracked the Navier-Stokes problem — a longstanding fluid-dynamics challenge with claimed knock-on implications for turbines, aircraft, cooling systems, batteries and reactor design.
  • OpenAI's Greg Brockman (in a 24-minute interview) describes Astra as merging chat, agents, desktop and Codex into one system — Visser's read: this is the point knowledge workers (he names accountants specifically) need to start paying attention.
  • Visser's framework: GDP has always been people + capital + productivity; AI adds digital labor that doesn't take breaks, so the constraint shifts to compute and energy, not headcount — and he argues GDP itself (a 1930s industrial-output metric) is the wrong tool to measure this productivity gain.
  • The corollary he draws for equities: as AI shortens corporate duration (how long a business can keep growing before disruption), terminal value and multiples compress — which is why he's fine with Nvidia and Micron's multiples compressing even while the businesses do well.

The GPU-depreciation bear case (Burry, Chanos) looks wrong — rental prices are rising

Visser stacks up evidence that H100 rental prices have not fallen the way depreciation-schedule bears assumed, and argues that flips the hyperscaler credit story.

  • H100 rental prices are almost unchanged from three years ago, when the normal aging curve would have implied a 40-50% decline by now.
  • Rental prices are up 22% this month alone, per Gavin Baker's thesis (which Visser flagged a month ago) that the market wrongly assumed GPU rental rates would steadily fall.
  • Visser's tomato analogy: chips bought at "$2" are now rented out at "$6" of compute value — hyperscalers get to reprice existing fleets as old contracts roll off, rather than eating a falling asset value.
  • Baker's point, per Visser: this could remove hundreds of billions of dollars of expected hyperscaler credit/financing needs if cash flow from repricing existing compute is this much higher than modeled.
  • This directly rebuts the Michael Burry/Jim Chanos accounting argument that H100 depreciation schedules were too generous because chip value would collapse within a few years.
  • Microsoft's reiterated capacity shortage this week is cited as corroborating evidence that demand — not oversupply — is still the binding constraint.

High conviction crypto

How Visser values Ethereum: network effects, trust, and an Amazon-in-1997 analogy

Ethereum just broke out to its highest level since January, and Visser lays out five to six reasons he thinks it deserves to keep outperforming Bitcoin in this cycle.

  • Network-effect + agentic case: Ethereum broke out to its highest level since January during "a huge week for Ethereum over Bitcoin"; Visser argues Ethereum should outperform Bitcoin in a real bull market because the ecosystem/network effect is the critical value driver, and the agentic AI use case (especially post-Astra) strengthens that.
  • Institutional rails are choosing it live: Robinhood Chain (built on Arbitrum, an Ethereum layer-2) already does $6M/day in fees after two months; European banks are separately planning to launch a euro stablecoin on Ethereum this same week — concrete money choosing Ethereum's rails, not just price speculation.
  • The original 2014-15 thesis is aging well: William Mougayar's decade-old TED talk on Ethereum/blockchain gets an 8/10 on direction and 5/10 on specifics from Visser — good for something made under years of regulatory uncertainty. Mougayar missed stablecoins and, crucially, missed AI agents — which Visser argues may be exactly what finally makes Mougayar's original disintermediation thesis work, since agents (unlike humans) actually need trustless rails.
  • No earnings model fits yet — that's normal, not disqualifying: per Mougayar's second talk ("Ethereum's Endgame"), crypto valuation is going through the same phase the early internet did — eyeballs, then revenue, then (25 years later) SaaS metrics. Ethereum's value drivers today are network effects, ecosystem value, productive assets, flow of money, and programmability, not a P/E.
  • The Amazon-in-1997 analogy: Amazon ran with no P/E (or none below 100) from 1997 until 2017-18, and is up roughly 27,000% from its 1997 lows; Ethereum is up about 3,000% from its own lows. Visser's read: the market will eventually give up trying to value Ethereum on old terms the same way it did with Amazon, and that capitulation point is still ahead, not behind.
  • Trust as the asset: Mougayar's forthcoming book ("Trust Shift") argues AI turns trust itself into an economic asset; Visser's extension is that any real-world asset that needs verifiable trust will eventually need to sit on Ethereum, and the current wave of AI-enabled hacking (see Other Notable News) is part of why he holds this even more strongly.

Visser built his own AI-agent trading system this week — and is sharing the workflow

Triggered by Astra's release, Visser built and back-tested an AI-agent trading desk and is walking subscribers through replicating it on Robinhood, Moomoo or Interactive Brokers.

  • He ran six AI agents (including ones he calls "Probe" and "Prism") against a real account, seeding it with $500 and reporting $5,100 by the next morning — presented as a demo, not a claim people should expect to replicate.
  • His filtering logic mirrors how multi-strat hedge funds vet ideas: thousands of daily ideas, 99% rejected at the first pass, then another 90% of the survivors rejected — the design goal is rejection, not idea volume.
  • His own technical model layers rate-of-change, ATR (average true range) and a structural-trend/reset cycle on a 100-stock AI-buildout universe (he notes 80%+ of those names are above a rising 200-day moving average).
  • He explicitly does not expect repeats of this year's biggest winners (e.g., no expectation Micron repeats its 5x/12-bagger move) and expects multiple compression to make old buy-and-hold approaches harder, with most trading days flat or down and just ~5% of days driving the year's return.
  • Practical path he lays out: build on GitHub, test small dollar amounts ($50-100) across multiple uncorrelated strategies to create a mini multi-strat, paper-trade first via Robinhood/Moomoo/Interactive Brokers agentic tools, then scale.
Worth noting
The $500-to-$5,100 result and the back-test performance are self-reported from Visser's own build this week, not independently audited.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

PEG ratio — P/E ratio divided by expected earnings growth; Visser cites it near multi-decade lows as evidence stocks aren't as expensive as headline P/E suggests.
HYG relative to IEF — Visser's real-time credit-market proxy (a high-yield bond ETF vs. an intermediate Treasury ETF) for whether credit is confirming or fighting the equity market's move.
ATR (average true range) — A volatility measure Visser uses to detect when a stock's trend has gotten "coiled" and ready for a fresh breakout.
Navier-Stokes — A long-unsolved set of equations describing fluid motion; Visser cites a reported multi-agent AI solve this week as a sign of AI's reach into hard physics problems with downstream effects on energy, transportation and manufacturing.
Corporate duration / terminal value — How long a company can be expected to keep growing before its earnings plateau; Visser argues AI disruption is shortening this for many businesses, which compresses valuation multiples even for strong operators.
Clarity Act — Pending US legislation meant to establish clear regulatory rules for crypto; Visser sees it as a catalyst for institutional (e.g., pension fund) participation if passed.
