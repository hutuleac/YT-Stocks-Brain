Muse Wonder puts Meta at the frontier — tuned for personal agents, not leaderboards

Meta's new model class is significantly bigger than Muse Spark and built around memory, persistence and domain expertise.

  • Two versions: Muse Wonder and the higher-end Muse Wonder Full (for Muse Code subscribers); Spark came from a pre-train at the end of 2025.
  • Active memory: the model itself, not a harness, manages long-term memory and compaction; measured on an internal 'Memory Bench'.
  • Active focus: persistence on long, ambiguous tasks and finding aligned workarounds when blocked.
  • Claimed state-of-the-art on finance and legal evals and on two long-horizon coding benchmarks; strong on Terminal Bench 4 and SWE-bench.
  • Wang says it sits at the frontier alongside Astra and Fable 'by the benchmarks', and is 'the best model in the world' for personal/professional agents.
  • Against Goodhart's-law benchmaxing worries, 'big model smell': Meta measures the gap between in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance, and says Wonder shows a large step up over Spark.
  • Too costly to serve free for now; the goal is a Spark-sized model as good as Wonder for hundreds of millions of users.

How a frontier model gets made: ladders, troughs of sadness and protractors

Wang and Zuckerberg describe a year-plus process where most bets are placed before any proof exists.

  • Pre-training climbs a ladder of models each ~4-5x bigger; data is the public internet, books, Meta's proprietary data and heavy image/audio/video for perception.
  • Domain data (shopping, personal legal, health, tax advice) goes in at pre-training, not just RL; with the internet not growing fast, data is a key scaling constraint.
  • Post-training: SFT seeds behaviors (maybe 10% of the time), RL drives them up; in RL 'less is more' on data. Spark 1.0 to 1.3 was RL-only yet felt 'night and day'.
  • Alignment is curating RL environments so the only path to success is aligned behavior; Meta aligns to the user's instructions and values — no 'moralizing lecture'.
  • Discretion matters for personal agents: booking a table without revealing an allergy or pregnancy.
  • Internal evals now outweigh public ones — chasing viral benchmarks means 'outsourcing all of your product thinking to that PhD student'.
  • Signals of success: perplexity evals in pre-training; Wonder beat Spark almost immediately in RL. Researchers literally hold protractors to loss curves.
  • Code names: Avocado (Spark), Watermelon (Wonder) — 'we're out of fruits'.

The $100-200B bet placed before the cards were dealt

Meta committed to clusters and serving capacity years ahead of knowing whether the model or product would work.

  • Rough math: ~$15B a year to operate a gigawatt; $50-60B capex up front per GW; 'tens of billions' to train a frontier model.
  • Prometheus (Ohio) scales past 1 GW; Hyperion (Louisiana) to 5 GW — both planned in 2023-24 under the Llama program, predating MSL.
  • Hawaii exec offsite, end of 2025: a '$100 billion or $200 billion' check with no model to show; CFO Susan Li repeatedly warned it would be 'very very bad' if it failed.
  • Zuckerberg's backstop: others would pay more than $15B per GW if AI works, and Meta has other internal uses for compute.
  • Accounting edge: owned data centers are depreciated over years, while rivals renting cloud pay annual rates; an H100 is worth more today than last year as compute stays constrained.
  • Wang: R&D overlaps several model generations at once (a point Dario Amodei has made), so spend looks front-loaded.

Agents as shoppers: Muse turns delegation into a business model

Meta plans to fund the buildout through subscriptions, a cut of commerce, business agents, an API and an enterprise unit.

  • Muse uses trained-in browser use so no business has to integrate; partners get direct APIs, and eventually 'every business is going to have agents'.
  • Far more businesses ask to integrate than try to block; Collison: thin-moat sites that profit from time-on-site or overcharging may rationally block — and get disrupted.
  • Zuckerberg opened an Instacart account for his Muse (weekend baking with his daughter) and now uses it 1-2x a week — agents may expand commerce's total market by holding accounts.
  • Collison: Stripe's agent-commerce volume, announced at Sessions in May, went vertical in September; Zuckerberg told Patrick Collison in 2023 he was 'behind the eight-ball'.
  • Revenue paths: subscriptions, a small cut of money Muse saves or makes users, a 'business in a box' for small businesses, the API, and a new Meta Enterprise platform.
  • The core ads business — 'hundreds of billions' and growing — funds the rest if models lift it a few percent.
  • Stripe agreed to acquire OpenRouter (not yet closed); Wang sees it helping developers grasp subtle model differences, a 'wine sommelier' view of models.

Personal superintelligence: multimodal, model-is-the-product, no model picker

Meta chose the consumer agent lane others left open, betting on multimodality while the industry obsessed over coding.

  • Use cases: Wang's Muse ('Oiler') buys obscure teas from Taiwan and Japan weekly; Zuckerberg's watches gym cameras and gives MMA feedback in 60-second breaks, writing its own video pipeline (first prototyped on OpenClaw, now an internal eval).
  • 'Everyone is actually going to be writing tons of code. They're just not going to know' — Zuckerberg.
  • Lineage: ChatGPT did the text assistant, Anthropic got coding agents to work; nobody built the personal consumer version. Meta AI remains the informational assistant.
  • 'The model is the product': complex product work is the privacy/security model and a dedicated VM per agent; personality and proactivity are trained in.
  • No model or effort selector — Wang mocks industry naming where 'the numbers go backwards'.
  • Voice (Siri-era promise still unmet): turn-taking is hard; input is exploding (students 'word vomit' 10-15 minutes), but listening is slow vs visual; Muse voice mode separates speakers, not fully rolled out.
  • Hardware: camera-and-audio glasses already useful; small-display glasses shipping; Muse Gadgets (e.g. a USB Home Link controlling Sonos and lights) with phone makers integrating.

Rebooting the lab: talent density, a flat org and a CEO who clears his calendar

After Llama 4, Zuckerberg rebuilt Meta's AI effort around a small elite team led by an outsider who had never trained a frontier model.

  • Llama 4 shipped April 4, 2025; ~2 weeks later Zuckerberg started calling for advice. After a 44-minute call, Wang sent a plan whose first point was talent density.
  • Research team under 200, 'closer to 100' plus infra; tens of people per stage vs hundreds elsewhere; compute concentrated per researcher, not 'peanut buttered'.
  • Collison's four unusual moves: calling the prior effort not good enough, paying 'ungodly amounts' (incl. 49% of Scale AI), betting on Wang, and clearing the CEO calendar for 1-2 months.
  • The full reboot was decided while recruiting; people came from Llama and FAIR too. One former lead had argued consumers didn't need a reasoning model.
  • Culture: ~200 direct reports to Wang, no non-technical managers, everyone uses agents and writes code; no deadlines. Wang records his 'insecure overachievers'' pessimistic forecasts and replays them.
  • Meta leadership got 'AI-pilled' — Instagram's Adam Mosseri runs an advanced personal vibe-code setup; Kunal Shah (founder of CRED) now runs WhatsApp to push commerce in India.
  • Not winner-take-all: execution varies across labs, distillation is an 'anti-hegemonic force', and many researchers chose labs that serve billions; Zuckerberg sees diversity of models as safer.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Pre-training — Training a base model on huge volumes of mixed data; the most compute-heavy, highest-stakes stage.
SFT — Supervised fine-tuning: showing the model example inputs and desired outputs to seed behaviors.
RL — Reinforcement learning on graded tasks to make desired behaviors reliable.
Big model smell — Meta's metric for real capability: small gap between in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance.
Distillation — Training a smaller model to mimic a bigger one, packing strong capability into cheaper models.
Talent density — Few people, each as strong as possible, with lots of compute per researcher.
Active memory — Meta's term for the model itself managing long-term memory and context compaction.
