Hut 8's Riverbend campus: the coordination game nobody prices

Seeing the site convinced Jackson the AI data-center race is won on coordination and financing, and that Hut 8 is doing both better than most.

  • Riverbend: 649 acres, ~30 minutes north of Baton Rouge, Louisiana; this is only phase one of the first campus, one of ~10 sites Hut plans in Louisiana.
  • Jacobs is the engineering contractor; a whole section is devoted to Vertiv cooling; Hut has had ~15 people living locally on site since February.
  • Transformers and substations bigger than he'd ever seen; United Rentals and Caterpillar (CAT) visibly 'making bank', which explains their 5-year stock runs.
  • Financed with $3.5B of non-recourse debt instead of equity, unlike rivals doing $6B dilutive equity offerings.
  • Total borrowing likely ~$10B; anchor tenant Anthropic via Fluidstack, with Google backstopping financially; his estimate is ~$15B of extra value once the site is lit.
  • Plan: recycle the $15-20B of created value into the next project, Beacon Point in East Texas, without diluting shareholders.
  • Local politics was a 'love fest' (Louisiana governor, Don Jr. present), the opposite of the 'everybody hates AI data centers' narrative; CEO Asher Genoot (32) spoke about community, not AI.

Opendoor and the SaaS sell-off: when the baby goes out with the bathwater

Jackson's edge is finding stocks the whole market leaned against, then surviving the wait.

  • Opendoor ran from ~70 cents (his entry) to almost $11 in a two-month run last year, near the top around September, and has struggled since.
  • 'Nothing's wrong with the stock'; he rates the CEO as hard to beat and blames the ripping 10-year yield for the sell-off.
  • SaaS was 'supposed to be dead' this year, yet CrowdStrike is up 125% YTD and Twilio up 100%; ZoomInfo is down a lot, so the consensus had 'a kernel of truth'.
  • Being misunderstood is the shared trait of Carvana and Opendoor before their multi-year runs.
  • Pompliano's counterpoint: retail investors have been right more than wrong, and Wall Street is now having to follow them.

A concentrated small-cap and crypto book, and the optimist's selling problem

Jackson runs far more concentrated than most would tolerate, and his real filter is timing: is the story about to inflect now?

  • Smaller-cap growth names (Hut 8, Opendoor, Nextdoor, Dave) plus crypto make up ~90% of what he plays in.
  • Dave makes small ~$200 payday-style loans using a Plaid integration.
  • Biggest mistakes: being right about a story but two years early; the test is whether revenue growth is about to jump from 10% to 30%, and why.
  • Selling starts with knowing yourself: optimists hold too long; he asks whether he misjudged management, was early, or a missed quarter is a blip vs. something ominous.
  • Daily routine: a morning video first thing (often from 5am), then markets and the 10-year, using AI to trace how rate moves hit Opendoor, Hut and the rest; self-described 'ADHD guy' who hops between problems.

Retail power needs a leader; AI research needs harder questions

AI makes retail research easier than ever, but neither a crowd nor a chatbot does the work without someone stepping up.

  • Public-company CEOs now court retail; his basics: be open, authentic, transparent, and hold video earnings calls instead of audio-only.
  • Online shareholder campaigns need leadership like the military or community groups; in his weekly AMAs most people still prefer typing to taking the mic.
  • Simple prompts ('show me the next Opendoor') waste tokens; better: ask why company A and company B attack the same space differently and who has the edge.
  • His model ranking: ChatGPT most 'artistic' (tweets, PowerPoints), Claude a solid workhorse without the pizzazz, Kimi best and most thorough for coding and website redesign.
  • His son Julian (17) pushed the daily videos outside Drake's house for Opendoor to reach people who don't watch CNBC or Bloomberg; it grew into hip-hop business videos (e.g. will Drake re-sign with Universal Music) with a non-investor following now asking about stocks.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Non-recourse debt — A loan secured only by the project's assets, so lenders can't claim the parent company's other assets if it fails.
Anchor tenant — The main committed customer whose lease makes a data-center campus financeable.
Regime model — A model that classifies the market state (bull, mixed, stress/bear) rather than predicting a price.
Prediction market — An exchange (Polymarket, Kalshi) where traders buy contracts on outcomes; prices read as crowd-implied odds.
