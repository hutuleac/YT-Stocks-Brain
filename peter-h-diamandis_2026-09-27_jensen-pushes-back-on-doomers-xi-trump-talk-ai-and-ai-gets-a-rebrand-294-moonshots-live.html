High conviction PANEL CONSENSUS: P(DOOM) LOW, DECEL IS THE RISKpolicyai-inframindset

Jensen calls Hinton's 10% doom figure unscientific, and the panel agrees the risk is human misuse

After two weeks of Dario, Sam, Elon and Demis warning of existential risk, the panel sides with Jensen and Zuck: the real danger is humans misusing AI, and deceleration is the worst outcome.

  • The backdrop: Dario's blog post, echoed by Sam Altman, Elon and Demis, then Sam and Dario telling the UN Security Council that AI is an existential threat, just before the Xi-Trump meetings.
  • Jensen: 'Nobody's building more compute today than the people asking to be slowed down.' On Hinton's 10% odds of societal destruction: 'All of his predictions have been wrong… not grounded on science.'
  • Zuck: labs don't need industry-wide coordination. Each lab should take the time it needs internally, and 'trust and alignment' are the next key capabilities.
  • Emad: OpenAI's 'outbreaks' came from bad security protocols and infrastructure. The near-term danger is humans misusing AI, and conflating every risk into 'AI kills us all' skips enforcing existing rules.
  • Alex: moral panic over nuclear power cost ~50 years of progress after WWII, and panic over superintelligence could cost another 50. His t-shirt reads 'p(doom) < 0'.
  • Salim: 4 billion years of evolution beat entropy. Wearing glasses or getting a vaccine already makes you transhuman, and the first 'robots take all jobs in 5 years' article appeared in 1964. Dave: Hinton 'calls it as he sees it', but doomerism is dysfunctional.
  • Peter's own line: AI is 'not a hand grenade, it's a jetpack'. He does see real danger in humans using AI with malicious intent.

Contested XI-TRUMP TALKS RESUME IN NOVEMBERgeopoliticspolicy

Xi-Trump leaves AI 'exactly where it is': liability stays with the labs, and Alex calls the rest a safety cartel

The summit produced a DOJ-as-guardrail stance and a rename to 'super intelligence', and the panel reads the labs' coordination push as a cartel attempt.

  • Trump posted: 'I want to leave it exactly where it is. That is China's position also. Our guardrail is the Department of Justice', which ties back to Bessent refusing to waive lab liability.
  • A White House contact at the UN delegation told Dave that the US agenda was to stop American panic and not lose to China. China's stated worry is the CCP's survival and AI piercing the Great Firewall. The two sides meet again in November.
  • Alex sees an attempted AI safety cartel, rooted in OpenAI's founding, when it was meant to be a counterbalance to a Google DeepMind 'singleton'. His fix is defensive co-scaling: banning cities because they breed crime would be absurd, and cities got police and fire departments instead.
  • Emad: the US and China won't trust each other, but a hotline makes sense for sub-ASI incidents (an OpenAI model reportedly hacked Australia's medical system). 'Utility AI' that runs courts, medicine and roads outside big-lab control should get liability waivers, like utilities do.
  • Salim: nation-states are 15th-century scarcity structures. Power is moving to city-states (LA with solar, satellite internet and vertical farming), and the real divide is urban vs. rural (Brexit, Trump). Alex takes the other side: audio recording created more accents rather than one, and superintelligence will multiply forms of government.
  • At the UN, Trump rejected any 'globalist scheme' and renamed AI 'super intelligence'. Alex likes the Bostrom term because it means a world with the cognitive equivalent of trillions of people. Salim: AGI is 'not artificial, not general, not really intelligence', and AI complements human intelligence rather than replicating it.
  • Emad: 99% of AI will be 'perspiration AI' for execution and 1% innovation AI, so 'the 99% should reach the 99%'. Peter wants full speed plus labs pointing '100,000 agents' at alignment.
Pull quote

Red flag PROPOSED — permanent ASI ban + pausepolicyai-infra

Sanders' superintelligence ban would bring a corporate death penalty and 20 years in prison for developers

The panel says the Sanders plan would freeze builders through fear alone, and that the data-center losses would be permanent.

  • The plan has three parts: a permanent ban on ASI (AI exceeding human ability in most domains), an immediate pause on advanced AI until new rules exist, and a cabinet-level Department of AI.
  • Penalties are modeled on illegal nuclear weapons: a corporate death penalty (shutdown) and 20 years in federal prison for individuals. Sanders wants a treaty that pauses advanced AI.
  • Dave: fear of prison freezes people before any rule is even written, a European 'when in doubt, do nothing' mindset. The real risks are terrorist bioweapons and China getting 'miles ahead', and those need technical fixes.
  • Dave: the one part that can't be reversed is data centers. Once they're built in other states, 'they're not coming back'.
  • Alex: a statutory cap on intelligence is 'an evil idea'. Since we'll merge with machines, it caps human intelligence too, 'burn the books and burn the minds'.
  • Emad: like the US attempt to ban encryption, banning ASI is banning math, which is free speech and unconstitutional. Salim: with local weights anyone can run, the Xi summit and Sanders are 'completely irrelevant'.
Pull quote
“Proposing a cap in whatever form on intelligence… is basically the equivalent of communism in the 21st century.”— Alexander Wissner-Gross
Contested MUSE #1 IN APP STORE — 2.8M DOWNLOADSconsumersoftware

Meta's Muse is the fastest consumer AI launch ever, and the market is already repricing search-led businesses

Muse proves a consumer agent doesn't need the smartest model to be the most useful, and investors are dumping companies they think it disintermediates.

  • Muse books flights, orders food, manages calendars and shops. It hit #1 in the App Store with 2.8M downloads, the fastest consumer AI launch, beating ChatGPT's. Meta's reach of 3.8B people is the distribution edge.
  • Dave sees a fork. Consumer AI will 'completely replace search' and other ways of using the internet, while the frontier race toward self-improving AI runs separately. Muse doesn't need the smartest model.
  • EverQuote, where Dave is chairman, fell 15% on back-to-back days on fears people will find auto insurance and mortgages through Muse. Dave: 'it's not going to actually play out that way.'
  • Alex: Meta needs Muse to work. Muse is effectively Manus, the Chinese computer-use agent whose acquisition China forced Meta to unwind. Instagram is cross-promoting Muse hard, because otherwise Meta 'risks oblivion' once its feeds become synthetic.
  • Emad expects Muse, Instinct and similar products to thrive as competent PAs and chiefs of staff.
Pull quote

Confirmed event 12 MAJOR RELEASES IN 22 DAYSai-infraroboticsenergy

Opus 5.5 at half price, GPT-6 Astra drives a Corolla, and the bottleneck moves to compute and energy

Releases arrive nearly every two days, general models are beating specialized ones in the physical world, and the constraint is now chips and power.

  • 12 major model releases in 22 days, 4 of them in the last 5 days. Alex expects one model a day by the end of the year. Peter skipped Grok 4.7 ('not that exciting, sorry Elon'). Blundin admits 'model fatigue'.
  • Opus 5.5 delivers Fable 5.1-level performance at half the price, and Peter moved his 'Skippy' agent to it. Emad: Opus 5 was 'completely unhinged', and 5.5 is 'the first truly competent model'.
  • Alex: Opus 5.5 generates procedural video, animation, images and games. Anthropic plays vector (everything is code) while OpenAI plays raster (pixel models), and the two collide in robotics. Anthropic shipping this a week after Dario called for a pause is 'the height of hypocrisy'.
  • GPT-6 Sol and Astra shipped the same day. Dave: hallucinations, once seen as permanent (a view 'rampant at MIT'), got scaled away. Emad: kids starting on GPT-5/6 will trust it because it's right 99.9% of the time, which is dangerous.
  • DrivingBench gave a general model a drive-by-wire Toyota Corolla, a 130m cone course and a single command. GPT-6 Astra finished 100% on its second try. Boris Power: a 'nail in the coffin of specialized models'.
  • Alex: 'the bitter lesson is bitter indeed'. Decades of specialized robotics research were 'total waste' in hindsight, and 'robotics is cooked'. Emad: no specialized model will outperform generalists in a couple of years.
  • Dave: completing it on the second try isn't good enough to drive for real, and compute efficiency matters. 'Compute is going to be forever starved', HBM RAM costs 5x more, and the US is short ~60 GW of energy over the next two years.

High conviction ANTHROPIC WET LAB + ENZYME DISCOVERYbiotechhealth

950 Claude agents found a CRISPR-like enzyme in 21 hours, and biology is becoming a search problem

The panel sees AI plus wet labs turning hard biology into verifiable targets, while Salim and Alex call out the doom-and-wet-lab hypocrisy.

  • Anthropic launched its own life-science group and wet labs. From one prompt ('search a massive DNA database for interesting reverse transcriptases'), 950 Claude agents ran for 21 hours and found a CRISPR-like pattern, now named the array-associated reverse transcriptase, likely useful for gene editing.
  • For scale: Jennifer Doudna shared a Nobel for CRISPR. Alex (co-author with Peter of Solve Everything): expect 'a thousand Nobel prizes in one year'. Dave: the Nobel Prize 'is completely irrelevant' as of now.
  • Alex again calls it hypocritical to say AI will kill us all and then run a wet lab synthesizing proteins of unknown function, but he backs every lab doing biology: without superintelligence there's no solving the top 5,000 diseases or reaching longevity escape velocity.
  • Salim feeds the doomers: we can now write to the genome like a Word document, each of us is ~50 trillion cells, and there are designer babies in China. The upside and downside both 'go through the roof'.
  • Sam Rodriques (Edison Scientific / FutureHouse) published Millennium Problems for Biology: hard to solve, checkable in a standard wet lab within days. The list includes origin of life, cryopreservation and limb regrowth. Emad notes age reversal is missing and wants a thousand agents on each, all open-sourced.
  • Peter: for the $101M Healthspan XPRIZE, Peter Thiel and Aubrey de Grey pitched longevity, and George Church suggested measuring age reversal instead, which takes days. Dave's daughter, who works at Moderna, calls the list 'perfect'.
  • Alex's roadmap: train digital twins of every cell type on large interventional data sets, then run AlphaGo-style tree search for a 'move 37' from diseased to healthy states. Every biology problem becomes a searchable mathematical target. Salim: 'Biology is cooked.'
Pull quote

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🛡
AI policy: Separate misuse risk (bioweapons, security failures) from rogue-AI risk when judging any safety proposal.
📉
Markets: Screen your holdings for businesses that depend on search or comparison-shopping traffic that consumer agents like Muse can route around.
💰
AI costs: Re-benchmark your agents on Opus 5.5 or GPT-6; frontier-level quality now costs roughly half as much.
⚡
Energy: Treat compute, HBM and power as the binding constraint, not model quality, when sizing AI infrastructure bets.
🧬
Biotech: Favor biology problems that can be checked in days, since those are where agent swarms will land first.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

p(doom) — The estimated probability that AI causes human extinction or civilizational collapse.
Defensive co-scaling — Alex's alternative to slowing down: grow safety institutions alongside capability, like cities adding police and fire departments.
CUA — Computer-use agent, an AI that operates apps and websites for the user (Manus, Muse).
Bitter lesson — Rich Sutton's observation that general methods plus scale beat hand-crafted specialized approaches.
DrivingBench — A benchmark that gives a general AI model control of a drive-by-wire Toyota Corolla on a cone course.
Reverse transcriptase — An enzyme that copies RNA into DNA, a building block for gene-editing tools.
Longevity escape velocity — The point where life-extension gains outpace aging, adding more than a year of life per year.
Move 37 — AlphaGo's creative, unexpected move against Lee Sedol, shorthand for search-discovered solutions.
