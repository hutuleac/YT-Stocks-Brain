Personal AI assistants cross from chatbot to proactive operator

Rose says a new class of always-on assistants that live in iMessage or a phone number is now handling real errands.

  • Instinct is a startup, reportedly valued at $10B four months from zero, that runs over iMessage (a phone number, no app) with Gmail and Google Drive access; it proactively flagged a missed DocuSign and an unfilled prescription timed to a flight.
  • It snagged a hard-to-book OpenTable slot via a stored login vault, changed flights, and friends' Instincts can swap calendars and set a dinner on their own.
  • Alternatives: Meta's Muse (app, number-one in the App Store for weeks; Meta published a security briefing), ChatGPT's Dots, and Grokbot (model weaker, more technical).
  • Voice: you can call your agent on a phone number; the call doesn't draw down AI credits.
  • Memory is the wedge: durable storage of your life makes users stop hopping to whichever model is best, so vendors will try to lock it in; export is partial.
  • Privacy trade-off: Rose and Ferriss both think in terms of 'blast radius' if the account is hacked and discuss separate offline storage for sensitive material.
  • Ferriss: he drafted a preface three months ago ('a letter from the future') arguing assistant overload is a 99% problem and that by publication AI assistants would handle 99% of it.

Agents become the front door: Amazon blocks, Shopify opens, software goes headless

Rose argues destination sites and apps lose their moat when an AI client decides and buys on the user's behalf.

  • Amazon reportedly blocked Meta's agent (sponsored placement and ad revenue get bypassed); roughly 24 hours later Shopify said agents are welcome.
  • Rose: the entry point is whoever the user talks to (on-device Apple Intelligence, Gemini on Android, ChatGPT, Anthropic); Amazon's sponsored-ad revenue 'goes away largely' as commerce funnels through them.
  • Analogy: in the 2000s tools moved online and Sears and JCPenney collapsed; the same transition now runs through AI front ends (e.g. 'Walmart in two hours or Amazon $2 cheaper tomorrow').
  • Blackmagic shipped an MCP server for DaVinci Resolve: Rose dropped in three camera shots and two audio tracks, told ChatGPT 'make the podcast', and watched it edit; Photoshop and CAD work the same way.
  • Rose on Apple: 'Don't count Apple out. Give them three years, right? And probably not even that. A year and a half.'

Ferriss' climbing endurance stack: beta-alanine first

Ferriss replaced creatine with beta-alanine for climbing endurance and credits a clear step-change after about 2.5 weeks.

  • Trial on 15 elite climbers (double-blind, 4 g/day for 4 weeks): traverse time 57% longer before falling versus 19% on placebo, +21 seconds beyond placebo; 10 moves completed versus 4 on placebo; helps 1-2 minute efforts, not short bouldering or campus board.
  • Ferriss takes ~6.4 g/day (eight sustained-release pills) and notes paresthesia (tingling) as the side effect; coaches saw a 'phase shift' at 2.5 weeks.
  • Collagen peptides (hide-sourced to limit heavy metals) plus vitamin C (50 to 1,000 mg) before isometrics for finger connective tissue.
  • Creatine: Ferriss cramps at 10 g/day and runs 5 g; Rose found the first delayed-release creatine (beaded) that doesn't upset his stomach, brand not vouched for.
  • A2 protein (Pioneer Pastures): 160 calories, 30 g protein, 2 g sugar, lactose-free, seven ingredients versus roughly 40 in Muscle Milk; both disclaim any affiliation.
  • Airfit respiratory trainer: 30% lung-capacity gain after 8 weeks cited; Ferriss uses it on an incline treadmill with a weighted pack; the app paywall at $79/month annoyed both.
  • Plum Flower curing pills (about 15 herbs, ~50 cents a packet) are Rose's go-to for upset stomach or bloating.
Worth noting
Ferriss stacks several changes at once (isometrics, collagen, coaching), so he says the effect can't be separated; the trial's funding source is unknown.

Nuts, fertility and 'you are what you eat ate'

Ferriss treats fertility as a proxy for general health and points to nuts, micronutrients and supply-chain contamination.

  • 2012 RCT in 117 healthy men aged 21-35: adding 75 g/day of walnuts (about 2.5 handfuls) significantly improved sperm vitality, motility and morphology.
  • A 2024 meta-analysis found at least 60 g/day of nuts improved motility, vitality and morphology but not sperm concentration; watch calories.
  • Ferriss could not confirm that walnuts raise testosterone; Brazil nuts supply selenium (needs 'product of Brazil'; soil-dependent), and friends reported big changes from three a day.
  • Psyllium husk can carry heavy metals; Ferriss has seen clean test results for Yerba Prima and Organic India; Rose adds that seaweed snack chips can be loaded with microplastics.
  • Meta-principle: 'you are what you eat ate' (feed and soil of salmon and greens); farmed salmon is dyed pink; beware undefined labels like 'all natural'; topsoil depletion lowers vegetable nutrient density.
Worth noting
Ferriss says he checks funding sources and that friends' Brazil-nut experiences are not randomized or placebo-controlled; the nut-butter brand he names is a personal favorite.

One-day brain stimulation, anesthesia dreams and dream engineering

Ferriss says neuromodulation and dream-based approaches offer a better risk-benefit profile than psychedelics for many people.

  • Ampa Health: one-day accelerated stimulation protocol (versus five days off work, around $30k), paired with a small dose of an antibiotic that induces plasticity; Ferriss self-reports OCD and anxiety dropping from about 8-9 to 0-1, with a mild headache for a day.
  • Ampa's provider device is ~$3,000/month and trunk-sized versus fridge-sized machines on a ~$4M lease; it uses targets more uniform person to person, so a half-centimeter miss wastes less.
  • Stanford 'anesthesia dream' protocol (five steps): tell the patient they may dream, taper propofol, monitor EEG, ensure at least 10 minutes of quiet emergence, then ask about recall; PTSD patients rehearsed a better ending and some showed zero complex-PTSD symptoms 10 months later.
  • Dream engineering: lucid dreamers have signaled researchers in REM with pre-agreed eye movements; Ferriss argues 'the trip matters', not just receptor binding.
  • Mouse study: electrical contact lenses matched fluoxetine for depression-like signs; Ferriss stresses mice are not humans.
  • DORA sleep drugs (dual orexin receptor antagonists): Ferriss uses them, citing amyloid-clearance hints in humans given his APOE 3/4 genotype; Matt Walker covered the class.
  • Anesthesia's mechanism is still unknown; Ferriss floats bioelectric or quantum-physics ideas and notes Michael Pollan's new book says it works on plants.
Worth noting
Ferriss discloses he is an investor in the dream-engineering startup (Dust) and was involved with Ampa Health; the DORA Alzheimer's benefit is unproven and both urge consulting a doctor.

Writing and attention in the AI era: reputation, simple premises and print

Ferriss won't let AI write his book, thinks reputation and lived experience are the moat, and wants a premise that passes the crowded-bar test.

  • Rose: Every's compound-engineering writer harness (installed into ChatGPT or Claude) can draft a book; Ferriss uses AI for version control and graphic comparisons only, 'because if I do that, I'm done'.
  • Ferriss: idea-based books are easy to front-run, lived-experience books more defensible; he'd 'rather write a book for 10,000 people than do short-form videos for 10 million'.
  • Simple premise: Fifty Shades began as fanfiction and won on a visceral premise; The 4-Hour Chef sold ~120,000 in week one on his audience but tried to be three books in one.
  • His next book (focus and 'saying no'): cut from 870 to ~350 pages, every remaining item rated 8-10 by test readers; only about one in ten strangers leans in, so he's testing title and positioning.
  • Plan: no advance, done in about 8 weeks, leaning toward Author's Equity, possibly one 10,000-copy print run; print is chosen to force single-tasking, and a traditional run takes about a year.
  • AI and leisure: Rose says it freed his inbox time; Ferriss says he hasn't seen anyone use freed time for knitting, 'something's always going to fill the gap'.
  • Both cite Sean Smucker's essay 'Please Use AI' and Vonnegut's envelope story: don't optimize away human interaction; 'we're dancing animals'.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

MCP server — A connector that lets an AI client operate a software product directly.
Headless — Software used through an AI or API with no human-facing interface.
Harness — A wrapper layer on top of an AI model that adds specialized behavior for a task.
Beta-alanine — An amino acid supplement that raises muscle carnosine to help 1-2 minute efforts; causes tingling (paresthesia).
DORA — Dual orexin receptor antagonist, a class of sleep drugs.
Lucid dreaming — Becoming aware you are dreaming, which can allow control of dream content.
PRP facial — A treatment applying your own platelet-rich plasma after micro-needling.
