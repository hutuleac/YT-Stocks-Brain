Yields keep climbing: 'nervous but not fearful'

A steepening yield slope the stock market ignores raises risk, but he reads it as caution rather than a collapse signal.

  • His ideal ceiling for the 10-year was about 5.27%; the 2s10s spread back near 50 bps is what Academy Securities calls shock territory (above it from Liberation Day to the original ceasefire without a shock).
  • High-yield spreads have widened but are not higher than 2022; the nominal 10-year level makes them riskier now.
  • Academy sees no Treasury bears: retail is buying TLT with the largest inflows since late 2024 even as TLT is below its ~78 line, last lost in Oct-Nov 2023.
  • Bearish forces for bonds: oil, deficit, long-term Treasury issuance, global sovereign issuance, corporate supply.
  • Alphabet's ~6.5% three-year preferred converting at roughly $440 versus a ~$340 stock, which he calls a bad deal because of the premium.
  • Sellers like Saudi Arabia, Japan and Europe may be raising cash to fund their own needs and AI buildouts, not turning bearish on 5.3%.

Hedge by allocation: a resistor on how much goes into stocks

You can be a big bull and still limit exposure by diversifying where your investable dollars go.

  • Think of an electrical circuit: the share of each $100 of investable capital sent into stocks (say 30%) is the resistor.
  • The rest goes to private equity, real estate, debt payoff and cash; each acts as a hedge to the stock market.
  • Trying to hedge a black-swan credit event is complicated and he'd get the timing wrong; he prefers watching the two canaries.
  • He bought the dip near 700 on QQQ calling it peak fear, after saying peak fear was three weeks earlier.
