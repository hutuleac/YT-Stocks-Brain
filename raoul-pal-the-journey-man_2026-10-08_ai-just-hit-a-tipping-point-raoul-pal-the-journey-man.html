Competence, taste and trust are what flipped; costs are collapsing at the same time

Mostaque argues the economically important threshold is a model that does the job with taste, and Opus 5.5 is the first to clear it.

  • Opus 5.5 gets high marks: Mostaque did three 12-hour days of motion graphics, apps and writing on 20% of his credits; both found Opus 5 unpleasant and Fable heavy on credits.
  • Mostaque's Claude spend fell from $13K (August) to $6K and he expects to stay within his subscription; he says annual run-rate usage dropped 10x in a week.
  • Opus 5.5 is said to be 4x more token-efficient than its predecessor; 10M tokens on DeepSeek V4 Flash cost about 50 cents vs $6,000 a few years ago.
  • Release cadence: a model every 4-5 days, heading to one a day by new year; Meta's Muse hit 2.8M users on day one and Instinct raised $1B at a $10B valuation, with about $1B run rate (half travel bookings).
  • OpenAI spends about $7,000 per employee per month on tokens; Harvey went to minus 50% margins as law-firm token use surged against its subscription pricing.
  • Mostaque says Anthropic revenue is $60-80B, and expects the enterprise effect to appear in numbers in 3-6 months ("turkey at Thanksgiving").
  • Pal's frictions: managing AI takes much of his day, with copy-paste fixes; both expect a one-or-two generation fix and a "chief of staff" that runs the day.

A 1.58-bit model fits in 6 GB: frontier models matter less than competent ones

Compression plus harnesses let small and open models reach the "good enough" bar, with large RAM-cycle implications.

  • Qwen 3.8 27B performs near Opus 4.5-4.6 level on short tasks; Prism ML cut it from 8-16 bit (30-60 GB) to 1.58 bit at about 5% loss — released as Bonsai 2 27B (ternary weights).
  • Result: about 6 GB, runs at 300 tokens per second on an RTX 5090 and fits ordinary 8 GB machines, which makes self-replicating swarms easier.
  • Mostaque: 95% of quality at one-tenth the size and RAM, weights going to 0, minus 1 and 1, with 2-3x inference gains from AI-optimized serving.
  • Frontier open models match closed ones on short tasks but halve on long tasks; Mostaque's firm's harness lifts DeepSeek V4.1 Flash above Astra on 24-hour tasks.
  • He expects swarms of smaller models to make training super-frontier models uneconomic.
  • Mostaque: "we don't need frontier models anymore"; most users can't exploit them, which makes competent models the economic baseline.

CPU and power shortages, then job losses and deflation; regulation as liability shield

Both see near-term infrastructure gaps, team-level replacement from 2027, and a deflation shock that stablecoins could offset.

  • CPUs are the next shortage: agents need cloud "houses" (VMs with CPUs and some RAM), and AMD and Intel can't supply enough; DRAM gap resolves in about 6 months.
  • Electricity: China is nearly all domestic chips with up to a trillion tokens a second; US energy is unblocking slowly and Europe is worse.
  • Labs asking for regulation are really asking for liability waivers (like utilities), per Mostaque; Sanders wants 20 years' jail for building ASI; Pal calls data-center opposition stupid.
  • Pal expects the first wholesale team replacements in jobs numbers next year; Mostaque's phasing: wages lag, then prices collapse as 2027-29 compute, energy and robots arrive.
  • Mostaque's book The Last Economy gave two more years until human cognition goes negative in value; cognition price drops about 100x, e.g. $1,000/hour legal work.
  • Pal and Mostaque say the Clarity Act failed; they argue stablecoins and RWAs would raise monetary velocity and export the dollar to offset deflation.
  • Trucking: a Tesla Optimus-driven cyber taxi hauling trucks could replace about a million US truckers, plus 3-4 million supporting jobs.
  • Mostaque's analogy: AI "dealerships" capture 80% of the value while labs get 20%, like car OEMs; household and clinic needs are on an S-curve, not exponential.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Competent intelligence — Models good enough to do real jobs with taste, even if not frontier-level.
1.58-bit quantization — Weights reduced to three values (-1, 0, 1), shrinking a model to a tenth of the size with about 5% loss.
RSI — Recursive self-improvement, where models help improve themselves.
Agent house — A cloud virtual machine with CPUs and some RAM where an agent runs around the clock.
Wright's law — Cost falls by a fixed share for each doubling of cumulative production.
