Leveraged hedge funds became forced sellers, and he reads the cascade as a bottoming signal

The rumored hedge-fund liquidations are now confirmed by WSJ and Bloomberg, and the author argues risk-limit selling mispriced AI leaders rather than revealing damaged businesses.

  • Morgan Stanley: October saw the largest active position cutting by stock pickers in a decade; over 40% are flat or negative year to date while indices are up. Of 46 major funds Bloomberg tracks, more than half finished October negative.
  • Millennium (over $95B under management): main fund -2.3% in October; after breaching its risk limit it pulled over $1.5B of leveraged capital from external teams, including over $1B from Troluce and $500M from Axebrook.
  • Lone Pine Capital ($19B) -8.8% in October alone, from a gain to -7.5% year to date; Marshall Wace's flagship Eureka strategy -5.4%; Balyasny -3.4% in October, -2.7% year to date.
  • Engineers Gate (~$4B) -5.5% in October and shut its Synthesis equities unit (14% of the firm), with unit head Mike Daylamani leaving; Samlyn Capital (~$7B) is -5% year to date and nearly fully exited non-financial long/short equity; Value Aligned, a concentrated AI fund, fell 33% in one month.
  • Goldman Sachs: hedge funds net sold about $20B of tech in two weeks and, in five trading days, wiped out all net long AI positions built since early 2025: in his words, a year and a half of accumulation erased in five sessions.
  • Mechanism: multi-strategy funds run 5-10x leverage with rigid limits: a 2.5% drawdown forces halving positions, a 5% drop forces immediate liquidation. Crowded AI-infrastructure trades plus earnings season and the elections triggered indiscriminate exits from GOOGL, ORCL, NVDA, AMZN and VRT.
  • He expects a real bottom only once institutional leverage is fully flushed and the last forced positions change hands; a more brutal liquidation exhausts passive selling faster. Wall Street trader Noel Smith summed it up as nothing but liquidation.

A hot jobs report: good for the US consumer, a reason for the Fed to stay restrictive

October payrolls beat consensus by 100,000, which he reads both ways: less recession risk, more pressure on rates and growth valuations.

  • October NFP: 165,000 jobs vs 65,000 consensus; unemployment 4.1% from 4.2%; wages +0.4% m/m and +3.1% y/y; August and September revised up a combined 84,000.
  • Good side: a solid labor market cuts fear of an immediate recession and supports US consumption, good news for long-term holders of US companies.
  • Other side: a resilient economy and rising wages reduce pressure on the Federal Reserve to ease.
  • Reuters: implied probability of a December rate hike rose to about 75-78% after the data; he stresses that is market pricing, not a Fed decision.
  • Yields rose: in the initial reaction Reuters had the 10-year near 4.878% and the 2-year at 4.528%, the gap narrowing.
  • He expects restrictive-policy risk to stay an essential variable for growth valuations in coming months, alongside earnings and concrete changes in the AI investment outlook.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Margin call — A lender's demand for more collateral when leveraged positions fall; unmet calls force the borrower to sell.
Multi-strategy fund — A hedge fund allocating leveraged capital to many internal or external teams, each cut automatically when it hits a drawdown limit.
Net long — Long exposure minus short exposure in a sector; Goldman says hedge funds took theirs in AI stocks to zero in five sessions.
VIX — Implied volatility of S&P 500 options, the market's 'fear gauge'; it rose 70% on Thursday and closed near 24.9 Friday.
NFP — Non-farm payrolls, the monthly US jobs report that moves rate expectations.
Short covering — Short sellers buying shares back to close positions, which can accelerate a rally.
