Take-Two: the GTA VI trade is about gameplay depth and online monetization

Camillo is long Take-Two because first gameplay suggests unit sales at or above the high end of the 30-50M range and a path to billions a year from online.

  • Sell-side calendar-year-1 unit estimates run 30-50M; based on the first 27 minutes of gameplay he expects the high end or beyond.
  • Monetization is the swing factor: $20 vs $60 per monthly active user — near $60 on 50-60M+ users, he sees Take-Two stock doubling.
  • He models $2-3B a year from GTA VI Online microtransactions, on top of 50-60M copies at ~$90-100 (most buyers take the $100 edition).
  • Precedent: GTA V still generates ~$600M a year in microtransactions, 13 years after launch; GTA V Online arrived ~17 days after the console release, so he doubts a rumored 2-year online delay.
  • Sensor Tower's ~4.8M pre-sales figure is its first console estimate (methodology unknown); he calls it unsurprising for a digital-only game, vs ~7M hard-copy pre-orders for GTA V in 2013. Take-Two called early pre-orders 'unprecedented'.
  • Sentiment read: he went through ~30,000 organic comments on the Netflix trailer and rates the gameplay reveal a 10/10; negatives about story don't hold because none was shown.
  • Risk he names himself: timing. He can't say when others will notice, and a miss on the holiday release window would hurt the stock badly.

Optimus is stalled and Apple has missed embodied AI

Camillo's old Tesla bull case was the Optimus robot, and his intel says it is far behind; Apple, he argues, is the one firm that could win the home robot and isn't trying.

  • Tesla: his only thesis was Optimus (once imagined as a ~$10 trillion division); two-plus years of intel say talent loss, friction with Elon, and unresolved hand strategy.
  • A promised public Optimus update for March-April this year never came; he says it isn't ready for scale.
  • He disputes an influencer claiming Optimus is a year and a half ahead of every other robot; his intel says the opposite.
  • Self-driving: Tesla may lead on robotaxi cost, but he sees Waymo and Chinese rivals (also exporting) closing in, so he won't pay up for it.
  • Apple: new CEO's $56M pay package is nonmaterial to him; he's neutral and owns none, seeing a cash cow with no clear 5-year growth driver.
  • Home robots are his Apple hope: trust, closed ecosystem and personal data are unique, yet his intel is that its robot program was 'an arm in a box' and the car team moved to robotics.
  • He sees embodied AI as potentially larger than AI, with big moats; commercial and home markets are each multi-trillion-dollar sectors.

Amazon: dips on non-structural news are entries, not exits

Camillo is out of his weekly calls but still levered, and plans to relever heavily within months because the stock keeps falling on noise.

  • Amazon beat earnings, popped, then slid back to pre-earnings levels; he calls it the 'perfect representation of the modern investor' — too big to promise 3-10x, so it's unsexy.
  • Decision rule: he looks at what's driving the drop. A story that doesn't change the long term gets bought; a meaningful one gets a reassessment and maybe a trim.
  • Latest drop #1: a North Carolina lawsuit alleging Amazon widened ad-auction bid spreads, with ~$20B potentially taken from advertisers; he expects years of litigation and a settlement.
  • Latest drop #2: a higher-than-expected 10-year yield; he thinks half-point moves are meaningless against growth accelerating from ~11% a year and a half ago to ~16% next year on the AI cycle.
  • He admits borrowing cost and the opportunity cost of risk assets both rise with rates, but says investors are reacting from 20 years of muscle memory.

The vibes market: GoPro, Markiplier and why being right early may not pay

Camillo argues today's retail flow trades on narrative, not benchmarks, which breaks the usual 'do the work, get paid' loop.

  • Hosts argued GTA hype is already priced to infinity; Camillo counters that 'baked in' is never quantified (30M? 40M? 50M units? what year-1 ARPU?).
  • In 1999-2000 traders had structural unit and beat/miss benchmarks; now many buyers and shorts of Take-Two don't know the sell-side unit forecasts.
  • GoPro: 'just vibes'. He bought ~$40K to hedge FOMO and sold for roughly a slight profit.
  • Markiplier disclosed an 8.5% GoPro stake when its market cap was ~$60M (roughly $5-8M invested), and it reportedly grew to ~$20M; Camillo reads it as a publicity play.
  • Build-A-Bear: a huge pandemic-era trade for him; he wanted to merge it with collectibles, Crocs-style collabs and limited releases, but didn't want the CEO job again.

New media is the market he'd buy if he could; short-form is the cost

Camillo's biggest unreachable bet is new-media content creators, while all agree short-form scrolling is damaging and trade fixes.

  • Thesis: a next phase of media sits between podcasting 1.0 ('two guys and a mic') and TV; top creators gain value as old-media firms buy in over the next 5 years.
  • He also names private embodied AI as something most people can't invest in.
  • Nobody watches TV anymore beyond sports and viral Netflix shows; one host can't get 10 minutes into a series before returning to TikTok.
  • He scrolls for market research (consumer behavior, spending, attention) and admits it's addictive and he overdoes it.
  • Fixes: keep a black-and-white phone filter outside intentional use, time-box research (e.g. 10pm-2am), meditate or journal around people without phones, vary routines so life doesn't 'compress'.
  • Host forecast: society will look back on shorts like cigarettes, with a split between people raised on shorts and those who weren't.

Glossary & Entities

Embodied AI — AI built into physical machines such as humanoid robots, which Camillo sees as potentially larger than software AI.
ARPU / ARPPU — Average revenue per user, used here for GTA VI's per-player revenue assumption ($20 vs $60 per monthly active user).
Sensor Tower — App and game tracking service; historically accurate on Steam, new to console estimates.
Vibes investing — Camillo's term for buying or shorting on narrative and hype rather than unit or revenue benchmarks.
