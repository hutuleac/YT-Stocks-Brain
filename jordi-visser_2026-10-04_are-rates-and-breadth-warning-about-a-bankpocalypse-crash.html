Why higher yields do not rhyme with 2000 or 2008

Today's index is dominated by non-cyclical tech names that do not need cheap debt, so old rate-shock comparisons do not apply.

  • Tech-like megacaps (Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia, Meta, Tesla) were barely 10% of the market at the financial crisis and are now 56%; 78 companies make up that 56%.
  • Nvidia plus Micron revenue went from $21B to $535B; Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft add another $2.4T of revenue. Nvidia alone is expected to add ~$300B next year (from ~$410B toward ~$700B), roughly the combined revenue of the old-economy group.
  • GM plus the four major homebuilders: $333B (2015) to $432B, a 2.4% CAGR; those names "deserve to go down" when rates rise and need rates lower.
  • The 14-year post-GFC era of negative-yielding bonds "should not count" on a chart; nominal GDP is ~6.5% and he would worry about bonds only if it pushes above 7%.
  • Financial conditions no longer tighten when rates rise (1971-1999 fit vs 2010-2026); 2022's rate shock did not cause a recession and SVB "wasn't a blip on anything".
  • Curve flattened as a bear flattener on hike expectations; that eased after John Williams signaled no October hike, then a weaker PCE and weaker payrolls.
  • Personal credential: ran the S&P options book at Morgan Stanley during the dot-com bubble.

Weak breadth as a bottoming signal, not a warning

Breadth was oversold where defensives made new lows and tech did not; he expects stabilization after earnings.

  • New lows are concentrated in staples and utilities; tech and financials show no spike in new lows.
  • A 14-day RSI of breadth below 25 (data since 2004) has been followed by strong six-month returns except in the GFC; Tom Lee's new-highs/new-lows view cited as a bottoming sign.
  • Breadth turning up: 20-day at 74, 50-day at 69, both highest since the peak; average distance from 52-week high is ~20% and he expects it to keep falling.
  • NDX over S&P and equal-weight tech both at/near all-time highs; staples over S&P making new lows (in the dot-com era staples held up because bonds yielded negative).
  • Equal-weight vs cap-weight S&P tracks the ISM; oil hit it first, then rates; "if rates had stayed low this trade would have continued".
  • The 10-name concentrated portfolio is up 106% for the year.
Worth noting
He says there is "no trend" in copper, gold or the dollar, and that macro funds fading crypto/AI for gold and commodities are losing; the S&P has consolidated since the June AI-trade break.

Micron and Rogers: AI demand keeps stepping up

Memory, power density and heat are the binding constraints, and every "AI bubble" call has been wrong so far.

  • Micron reported a revenue jump of about 11-fold and said it sees no line of sight to supply catching demand; he cites larger models, longer context, more concurrency, agents and edge/physical AI.
  • He started buying Micron "in the 800s" and was ready to build a sizable position near 500 that never came; position is not as big as Marvell, Nvidia or crypto.
  • A paper he wrote on May 15, 2025 ("The Inference Inflection") came when Micron was $98; it is now over $1,000, with a ~50% drawdown on the way.
  • Rogers (investor day, not earnings) stepped numbers up massively (~"$3 to 14"); he links it to heat and power density and the Rubin-era focus on efficiency and watts.
  • Chips "depreciate like a car" because a better one ships next day; the alpha is shorting the disrupted 440, not fearing it takes down the economy.
  • References: Howard Marks ("great investments begin in discomfort"), a16z State of the Markets, a Goldman Sachs token-consumption chart ("first inning").

  • 8 billion people using personal agents over five years; he is already consuming more tokens than any prior month on Muse and Grok.
  • He frames Mag 7 firms as moving from separate lanes to fighting over the same TAM as the attention economy becomes the agent economy.

Bank apocalypse: agents compress bank and card multiples

Like software, banks will not disappear but will suffer multiple compression as agents move money and strip out yield fees.

  • BKX closed below its 200-day average; his 46-token crypto index held while rates and Fed-hike odds rose, and gold broke down, so he rejects the pure debasement/liquidity story.
  • Torsten Slok's post that agents will move money to take away yield fees went viral this week.
  • Visa and Mastercard are "the BlackBerry": may benefit for two years but he sees "zero chance" they win long term; trust arguments are the same ones made against the iPhone.
  • Speed is a safety feature: Friday wires landing Monday; the GameStop episode pushed settlement from T+2 to T+1 (it took 3.5 years).
  • This week: Morgan Stanley digital-asset lab, Citi-Coinbase partnership, Franklin Templeton tokenized money-market funds as Bybit collateral ($1.83T), TD Cowen joining, Goldman going online, Robinhood AI trading bots.
  • Source cited: SF Fed on stablecoins; Visa survey (US adoption intent for international travelers 36% to 56% with bank-level protection); SEC 5-year transition for tokenized stocks.

Ethereum as the trust commodity for agent-era finance

Agents need machine-verifiable trust and instant settlement, which he argues points to Ethereum, Solana and Bitcoin rather than bank rails.

  • Ilia Polosukhin (Transformer co-author, NEAR founder; No Priors, May 2023) argued blockchains are an operating system for agents that can pay each other; he built an L1 because payments to people worldwide failed.
  • William Mougayar: Ethereum as four connected pieces, mispriced if valued only on apps on top; Visser is writing a foreword to his book on the trust shift.
  • Joseph Chalom (SharpLink CEO, ex-BlackRock): agents will touch a vast pool of financial-industry fees over a decade; ETH the settlement layer for almost all of finance and a "trust commodity".
  • Larry Fink framing quoted: Ethereum is the toll road to tokenization. Tom Lee and Chris Perkins (quantum could accelerate Wall Street's blockchain adoption) cited.
  • Equal-weight 46-token index split into 10 verticals; this week's two: AI agents in the machine economy and stablecoins/synthetic dollars; he says crypto studied now gives an advantage over the next year.
  • Tokenization takes growth from the "mag seven" to the "mag billion"; many firms like Ford and GM will never grow again.
  • Calls it the Peter Lynch approach: talk to founders early; a Peter Lynch Framework analyst prompt provided to subscribers.
