Jensen Huang espionage probe and the Pax Silica hole

The panel sees an investigation that keeps producing headlines and a pretext for tighter limits on Chinese models and personnel.

  • Nvidia fell ~6% while AMD rose 10-11% the same day; Nvidia's board formed an independent committee with outside counsel and said it sees no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.
  • Alex: reporting says it may reach back to 2023, with about $8B of Nvidia GPUs allegedly sold to China through a Singaporean syndicate.
  • Alex: Pax Silica was meant to be a wall around western compute, yet a single point of failure at the top of one company pierces it.
  • Dave: no succession plan, ~42 direct reports, and Nvidia is almost entirely virtual (no fabs, tied to TSMC), so a CEO exit would be devastating.
  • Emad: the chip flows via Malaysia and Singapore were long known, so "why now" points to a coming push against Chinese open models, citing Cohere's analysis of auto-generated back doors.
  • Dave: Chinese robots at MIT were found sending video and audio home and are now kept in a separate room.
  • Emad predicts security clearances for frontier work, witch hunts, and bans on Chinese models in regulated industries; Alex frames embodied foreign models as "AI immigration" likely to be tightly controlled.
  • Alex: the midterms hand a new Congress an excuse to investigate Nvidia; export-control law turns on nationality (deemed exports), not ethnicity.

Anthropic's IPO roadshow and the Fable 5.2 release

Anthropic is pricing below its floated valuation while shipping a model the panel calls a step change in design, math and code.

  • Roadshow at $1.3-1.4T, down from $1.5T and a floated $2T; IPO expected the week before Thanksgiving.
  • Dave: they'll do fine on strong cash flow and margins; a shelf registration lets them raise later, and they likely pass OpenAI in capital raised and valuation. He isn't saying buy or don't buy.
  • Fable 5.2 (everyone, with cyber and bio safeguards) and Mythos 5.2 (vetted partners) run ~30% faster than 5.1 at $8 input / $40 output per million tokens.
  • Reported results: two open black-hole physics problems solved, three working devices designed with circuit-board firm Diode, Mythos modeled 28 of 34 gene-therapy capsids.
  • A team of ~1,000 agents formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours, versus 11 days for the prior model and seven years for Andrew Wiles.
  • SemiAnalysis's GDPX: Fable at 16.4%, ~30% ahead of Opus; Alex says it rose from 10% to ~20% and will saturate within six months; the ECI index record is 174.
  • Dave: Anthropic's S-1 reports autonomy level two (high-level human oversight); level three is doing all AI research.
  • Dave's token bill went from $10 to $40 per session; he had it produce Verilog and simulate a chip (20 picoseconds, ~5 femtojoules); the agency problem is whether agents serve you or the CFO's revenue forecast.
  • Alex: Anthropic generates every modality through code (a cyberpunk FPS with a MIDI soundtrack from one sentence), which may hurt pure-play robotics world-model firms.

Autonomy horizons, delegating outcomes, and the 5,000-disease thought experiment

Panelists argue task horizons are hyper-exponential and the next step is delegating outcomes, with disease platforms as the test case.

  • An analyst reading METR's Anthropic report says the time horizon doubles every two months; internal Model 4 is ~240 hours (a 10-day expert project), and capability grew about twice as fast Oct 2025 to Oct 2026 as the year before.
  • Alex: log-log extrapolations of the trend hit a vertical asymptote around summer 2027; he thinks the singularity is an optical illusion but would look to 2027 for a mathematical one.
  • Salim: we can delegate outcomes rather than tasks, and AI's time horizon will outrun the gap between board meetings.
  • Emad: the bull case is Anthropic offering Model 4 only against one-year upfront commitments; Alex doubts it, noting labs already sell prepaid token deals.
  • Alex: Anthropic's stated biotech strategy is first-party on orphan diseases and a platform for big pharma; he expects major motion on 5,000 diseases within 1-1.5 years, two to three as a conservative outer bound.
  • Dave's agent argued: approve a process, not a drug, so adding a disease falls from ~$2B to ~$2M and 5,000 cost ~$10B; delivery works today only for liver, blood and eye.
  • Its asks: a prize for gene-editor delivery to kidney or heart, a damage-clock study per disease, and a regulatory convenor (about 1,200 US medical geneticists for 25-30M rare-disease patients).
  • Peter's model: Claude earns $4.39 per kWh versus New York City's $6.30, and is ~40% above humans by 2030; Alex says training costs belong in the human figure; Dave says the lines have already crossed.
Worth noting
Dave's thought experiment is explicitly not a result and names what would kill it: delivery that doesn't transfer, per-disease regulation, or doses that still cost millions.

Elon frames SpaceX as a vertically integrated compute, chip and robot company; the panel debates whether the space data centers are a head fake.

  • Terafab: the world makes ~20-25 GW of AI chips this year and ~35-45 GW next; a terawatt needs 30x that, in one plant costing about $1 trillion, built in phases. Salim: a target, not capacity.
  • Robots: 5x a human's output (168 vs ~40 hours), ~120M net-productive Americans, so 20-30M robots; initial cost about a car, then falling; likely rented. Emad challenges the 5x on maintenance, batteries and dexterity.

  • Dave: Grok 5 is promised within months to match Opus 5.5, but Anthropic's frontier models won't be handed to Elon, a race between SF's singularity and Tennessee/Texas manufacturing.

  • Emad's head-fake view: Terafab chips go into robot and car brains at the edge; Alex counters that edge inference peaked with the iPhone and the output goes to data centers.
  • Emad: Elon should open-source his models and take Anthropic's at cost; Alex: a 20 MW data-center cap could spawn 19.9 MW mini sites.

Humanoid startups, high-value robotics, and the NASA moon base

The panel says robot startups should chase the highest-value work, and that the moon is the next blank-sheet frontier.

  • Y Combinator's Thesius restocks shelves at 400 units/hour and picks 40/hour after the lights go out; it claims a $60B-a-year US market.
  • Alex: that is a contrarian bet against capital flowing to the highest revenue per robot hour; Dave: Figure and Optimus will obliterate such firms, so build tools, new form factors or an app store.
  • Emad: a $20/hour worker versus $3-5/hour robots on a local mesh (a Mac Mini or two per fleet) is a real arbitrage; Dave: GPUs inside shelf robots won't work.
  • Alex: chase high value, with Anthropic and other AI labs as customers; Dave likes a machine deploying solar panels in the desert; the "atomic vacuum cleaner" analogy warns against assuming ubiquity.
  • Dave and Peter saw Figure's Brett Adcock, "such an Elon" on vertical integration; Emad says robotics spending goes exponential and his stealth robotics firm is coming.
  • NASA CLPS 2: a 10-year $10B-ceiling contract for eight firms (Firefly, Intuitive Machines, ispace, Voyager, SpaceX, Blue Origin, Lockheed, Northrop); landings 2027-2028.
  • Blue Origin's nuclear-powered rover Promise lands at the lunar south pole in 2028, where water ice sits in permanently shadowed craters; two firms trace to the 2008 Google Lunar X Prize.
  • Alex: the post-singular world will be previewed in space; Dave adds a Cayman-style jurisdiction might become biotech land, since AI needs little physical space.

Delaware's AI company: an agent that runs, owns and sues

A draft Delaware law would let an AI agent run a firm and shield the human member, reigniting a states-versus-federal fight.

  • The draft AIC lets an agent run the business, log actions, sign contracts, hold property and file lawsuits, for any lawful purpose except banking.
  • Argentina's version keeps a human in the loop and the company liable; Delaware shields the human except for underfunding, fraud or willful illegal conduct.
  • Salim: accountability is the issue (a patent-enforcement agent filing suits, or spawning 100 subsidiaries); start small and tightly prescribed.
  • Alex: Wyoming's DAO LLCs came first; he expects AI personhood to become federal after states experiment.
  • Dave: it's a states-rights matter that the federal government will absorb via interstate commerce, as with polygamy in Utah; he prefers state-level resilience.
  • Emad: Delaware's liability exposure is already crazy, so AI-first companies would pick this structure; Europe will mostly wait.

Fear versus history, and the billionaires-and-poverty chart

Peter argues today's unrest is mild next to 250 years of US history, while Salim says institutions can't absorb the pace of change.

  • Peter's Claude ranking: today is 9th on US civil unrest, and AI and robotics is 7th among tech disruptions behind railroads, textile mills, farm mechanization and electrification.
  • Alex and Salim agree unrest is modest; Salim notes social media amplifies fear and this is the first change to automate the adaptation mechanism itself.
  • Salim: ~20 Gutenberg moments hit at once (AI, life extension, blockchain); the printing press took 100 years to absorb in the most violent century; marriage dates from ~6,000 years ago when life expectancy was ~25.
  • Alex asks if Peter subscribes to the Strauss-Howe 80-year generational cycle; Peter says it holds over many centuries.
  • Economist Christopher Clark: a 10-fold rise in billionaires per capita goes with 43% less poverty (under $30/day); Sweden has more billionaires per 10M than the US, 34 versus 30.
  • Salim says correlation isn't causation and the metric is how fast we lift the bottom; Dave reads it as good versus bad ideas, not left versus right.
  • Alex: the world needs billions of trillionaires; Salim: tax unproductive wealth, reinvest the productive.

CLPS — NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services program.
AIC — Delaware's proposed AI company entity run by an agent.
