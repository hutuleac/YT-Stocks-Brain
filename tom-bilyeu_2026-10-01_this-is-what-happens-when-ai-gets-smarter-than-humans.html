THIS is What Happens When AI Gets Smarter Than Humans

The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Emad Mostaque says human cognition goes negative-value in ~2 years (by late 2028): AI teammates will find humans the slowest people on the team. His evidence is a math takeoff: an OpenAI swarm of 10,000 agents for 88 hours (~100 human-years) produced a formal Navier-Stokes proof.
  2. Models went from error-prone agents (the OpenClaw era) to competent ones (Muse, Instinct); he says the newest generation barely drops anything, and AI now ties top human superforecasters.
  3. Cost is collapsing: a task on Opus 5.5 costs ~80% less than on Opus 5; he expects ~100x cheaper for equal performance within a year; a 27B open model now runs in 6 GB; etched-weights chips hit ~15,000 tokens/s.
  4. Labor: 12% of the cognitive workforce displaced by 2029-30 and 15% GDP growth (his reading of an Anthropic economics paper); he expects >10% white-collar job loss by 2029 arriving like a sand pile collapsing, not gradually.
  5. He sees a trifurcation: people who use AI daily, owners of AI infrastructure (GPUs, RAM, robots, Cybercabs), and everyone else. Tom pushes back on UBI-only fixes and on a permanent underclass.
  6. UBI math: the US tax base is ~$5T and poverty-level UBI is ~$5.1T, so tax-funded UBI 'doesn't work'. His alternative is citizen-owned AI vehicles modeled on TSMC's founding, with equity for every child.
  7. The second half of the episode (governance, persuasion, debt jubilee, longevity, community) is the same material as the Tom Bilyeu Clips cut of this conversation.
Confirmed event NAVIER-STOKES AND THE 'TAKEOFF POINT'ai-infrasoftware

Math is the tell: 10,000 agents formally prove a Millennium Prize problem

Emad calls the OpenAI Navier-Stokes result the delineating line: models have gone beyond their training data, in formally verifiable domains where compute can simply be thrown at the problem.

  • OpenAI ran 10,000 agents for 88 hours (~100 years of talented human time) and produced a formal, machine-checkable proof; Emad says two of the problem's four winning conditions were solved, and that either pair counts for the prize.
  • Clay Institute acceptance takes about 2 years (publication, then scrutiny), so the problem is still listed as active. Emad's own read of the paper took 3 days.
  • Anthropic reportedly formalized Fermat's Last Theorem (127-page human proof) as ~13 million lines of code. OpenAI says it has solved ~100 top math problems and is holding them back so as not to upset mathematicians.
  • Before that, OpenAI released 10 proofs of famous problems; Emad calls at least two definitively novel and elegant. Cost: about $2,000 of compute for the 10, versus ~$10-20 million for Navier-Stokes.
  • Trigger he names: from 29 August, a new OpenAI training approach took a model from ~10% to ~60% on the Frontier Math benchmark overnight, after which problems fell one after another.
  • Cost curve: Noam Brown told him the first reasoning model to win IMO gold cost ~$80,000 last July; today it costs ~$10.
  • Beyond math: AI has just tied the best human teams at the Metaculus superforecasting championship; image, video, music and web-design output gained 'taste'.
  • His own physics result went straight to GitHub to claim priority, because he expects the AI to figure it out.

High conviction FROM OPENCLAW TO COMPETENT AGENTSai-infraroboticssoftware

Competent AI: agents that don't drop the ball, digital twins and the end of 'AI slop'

Emad says the economic impact comes not from superintelligence but from competent intelligence that stops making mistakes and plugs into workflows.

  • Arc: the late-2025 'OpenClaw' agent craze (buy Mac minis, then it deleted your stuff) gave way to products like Muse and Instinct (both Meta, per the conversation) that book restaurants and track school activities without dropping the ball.
  • He claims the next models 'don't make mistakes': his team can hardly find drops in math or physics work, where earlier models were 'a grad student who gets a bit dopey'. The tell-tale 'not X but Y' AI writing is fading.
  • Digital twins: an agent trained on someone's calls and documents can replicate their voice, humor and live video; he expects one-button replacement of a workforce within 1-2 years, at ~$100 a month.
  • Call centers: a friend with 70,000 call-center workers expects to take them to zero over a few years because competitors will. Entry-level graduates are 'economically irrational' to hire for most digital jobs.
  • Robots: a friend's robot chef using Wuji dynamic hands outperforms human Cordon Bleu chefs in blind tests, per Emad; the underlying tech is a month old.
  • Media: a MiniMax H3 model does 2K video faster than real time (8 seconds to make a 15-second clip). Tom, who is building a video game with 100 people at peak and now 6-7, still hears the 'AIness' in commercial models.
  • Emad said '5.5 ends the entire video game industry'; later he softens: the industry grows but with far smaller teams. A Dark Souls-style game was reportedly built for roughly $500-1,000 with Opus 5.5 hooked to Unreal Engine.

High conviction COST OF INTELLIGENCEai-infrasemissoftware

The cost of intelligence is falling ~100x a year via models, harnesses and silicon

Emad's answer to Tom's cost objection: harnesses, newer models, quantization and etched-weights chips are all compounding, so the 2-year timeline survives.

  • Opus 5.5 does a task that cost X on Opus 5 for ~80% less and better; he expects a ~100x price drop for equivalent performance within one year.
  • His team released an open-source 'harness' (rules and code around a model) that made the cheapest DeepSeek model beat OpenAI's previous frontier model at ~50x lower cost (up to ~100x on website building). It uses Andrej Karpathy-style auto-research hill-climbing.
  • Nvidia's Vera Rubin: ~10x cheaper inference. A company etching model weights onto silicon (AMD reportedly bought it) runs a model at ~15,000 tokens/s versus ~50 for Opus.
  • Quantization: Alibaba's Qwen 27B (~60 GB) cut to ~25 GB, then ~6 GB by Prism ML (16-bit to ~1.5-bit, ~95% of performance), matching a January-2026 Opus-class model on a Raspberry Pi; a 1-bit version was announced for glasses.
  • Tom's objection: coders have spending caps after a $500,000 overnight bill story, so he only uses high-end models for known outcomes. Emad says cost is the milestone and it is dropping.
  • Once capability clears a floor, optimization (speed and price) matters more than the frontier; agents at 1,000x human speed make the human the bottleneck.

Contested THE TRIFURCATION: USERS, OWNERS, EVERYONE ELSEai-infrasemisroboticsmacro-rates

Jobs timeline and the ownership trade: GPUs, RAM, robots and Cybercabs

Emad expects a sand-pile collapse in white-collar work and says the winners are AI-daily users and owners of scarce compute, memory and autonomous machines.

  • Numbers: an Anthropic economics paper (per Emad) shows ~15% a year GDP growth by 2030 alongside a 12% drop in cognitive-labor workforce needed; he adds >10% white-collar job loss by 2029 and 'no more software developers in 2-3 years'.
  • Shape: 'turkey at Thanksgiving' (great jobs numbers, then a sudden drop), not 1% then 4%. Tom's team reduced from ~100 to 6-7 people while raising ambition; Emad rejects Jevons-paradox employment gains.
  • Owners: Blackwell chip prices tripled in 1-2 months; owners can recoup the purchase in ~3 months on 5-year lifetimes. About $1T has gone into data-center capex; RAM is ~40% of it and up ~700%; Emad says Micron is a trillion-dollar company.
  • Anthropic bought hundreds of thousands of chips from Elon Musk because Grok isn't that great yet, per Emad.
  • Robots: first 1X humanoid deliveries in the next few months; a realistic-humanoid maker sold 16,000 units in one day at up to $165,000; Chinese factories moving 'fully dark'; Cybercab ($30,000, Austin rollout) is 'start your own business'.
  • Musk's claim of a billion robots by 2030 is 'optimistic'; Emad says ~10 years is plausible, each robot doing ~5x a human's labor at ~$30,000 total (~$1.50-2 an hour).
  • Expected 5-year outcome: the biggest infrastructure boom ever (digital, roads, hospitals, flying vehicles in China and Dubai).

Speculative UBI MATH AND THE TSMC OWNERSHIP MODELpolicyfinancemacro-rates

Tax-funded UBI fails the math, so own the means of AI instead

Emad argues the US tax base can't fund UBI and proposes jurisdiction-wide, citizen-owned AI entities structured like TSMC's founding.

  • UBI math: the total US tax base is ~$5T (about $4T payroll and other taxes, $1T corporate); poverty-level UBI of ~$16,000 per American costs ~$5.1T.
  • Token tax and robot tax proposals fail because token and robot costs may fall to near zero. In 15 years he expects people to be 'paid for being human', with the intelligence economy taxed to pay them; he rejects universal basic capital in AI companies but favors ownership in robots and data centers; universal basic services is the Star Trek variant.
  • He says the Fed's mandate is challenged because firms may hire GPUs or robots rather than people when rates fall.
  • TSMC template: Taiwan's local institutions funded the whole $10 valuation, Philips took 25% for know-how, then it listed publicly. Per state, a US retail raise is capped at ~$75M; the UK has no limit; 10% of Americans own 93% of assets.
  • Design: ~10% of initial equity to every child under 18 plus 0.5% a year to each child born in the state (a Trump-account style default).
  • Tom's interim view: a window where AI-enhanced labor earns a premium; deconstruct your job into input-output tasks, test them on a frontier model, automate what it matches, and become an entrepreneur or owner. Public-sector jobs go last.

Persuasion, bias, status, debt jubilee and longevity: Emad's 'sand pile' warning

The second half repeats the Tom Bilyeu Clips cut: AI persuasion and ownership risks, Tom's insistence that wealth-seeking survives, and Emad's dated warnings.

  • Persuasion and bias: AI now out-persuades top debaters and a famous AI-safety author in an AI-box test; a cited study put Nigerian life ~10:1 (Pakistani ~7:1) over American in a trade-off, which he attributes to labelers; models lean one political direction except Grok.
  • Ownership risk: Meta's Instinct 'works for Mark Zuckerberg'; Meta's and Google's models sell ad space (a beer query returns Bud Light); 'cognitive colonialism' and cognitive atrophy; kids will trust always-listening AI.
  • Status: Tom (Kuwait trip, Minecraft) says stripping wealth acquisition via UBI 'will end in violence'; Emad proposes a citizen service corps and game-like digital asset worlds, an undisclosed project due in 'a couple of months'. Public sector is 43% of global GDP.
  • Debt jubilee: historically follows crisis or world war; Emad sees partial repudiation of student and medical debt as plausible; China-like AI adopters see ~30% growth.
  • Longevity: escape velocity in 5-10 years; a lung-fibrosis drug trial reportedly cut longevity markers by 4 years in 4 weeks; ladders pulled up for the poor. Tom answers that near-zero energy, intelligence and labor costs should prevent a permanent underclass.
  • Window: 'a sand pile that will start collapsing in two years'; recap advice is be the last man standing, find ownership and entrepreneurship, build stronger communities.

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🧠
AI strategy: Decompose your job into input-output tasks, run them through a frontier model, and automate what it matches.
📈
Markets: Test the ownership thesis: GPU, RAM and robot scarcity are priced in; check which scarce assets still pay back fast.
💵
Costs: Re-run budgeted AI workloads on the newest model and a harness; expect ~80% cheaper now and ~100x within a year.
🛠
Careers: Use frontier AI daily so you stay on the edge of each tipping point; the gap compounds.
🏛
Policy: Treat tax-funded UBI math ($5T base vs $5.1T cost) as a constraint when judging labor-displacement proposals.
🏘
Community: Build local community and family networks as a hedge in the next two years.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Harness — Rules and code around a model that make it behave and learn in set ways, e.g. note-taking and self-optimization.
Trifurcation — Emad's three-way split: daily AI users, owners of AI infrastructure, and everyone else.
Longevity escape velocity — Life expectancy rising by more than one year per calendar year.
Debt jubilee — A government zeroing out debts, historically in crisis or war.
Jevons paradox — Cheaper use of a resource can raise total consumption.
Intelligent capital stock — Emad's term for fixed productive assets, increasingly data centers and robots.
