What changed: AI-ban talk, data-center limits and a market repricing the cost of AI

The midterm result was largely priced in, so he points to AI-regulation fear landing on an over-levered market and calls the drop technical rather than fundamental.

  • Likely trigger: in the prior 24 hours Democratic lawmakers publicly called for restrictions on advanced AI development, including a total ban on superintelligence.
  • His view: a total ban will not happen, since it would hand China the global AI race and nobody in Washington will do that, whatever the party; regulation worries are still legitimate.
  • Mechanism: a sudden sentiment shift layered on excessive leverage can force big institutional liquidations, so price action is neither orderly nor rational right now.
  • Data centers: a Democratic Congress shifts expectations for limits on building, energy use and resources, the area behind much of 2026's market gains; Reuters says data-center fears were one of the main drivers, but possible limits do not mean existing projects are halted.
  • Cost of AI: investors now separate firms already making cash from AI from those spending billions before revenue, which is why AMD, Broadcom and Nvidia fell together despite very different business models.
  • Rates: yields fell on safe-haven demand (10Y ~4.836%, 2Y 4.475%, ~36bp spread), but risk aversion dominated; lower rates do not lift stocks when earnings estimates and confidence deteriorate.
  • Fed officials raised the chance of staying restrictive and even future hikes if inflation requires, with no new Fed decision Thursday; oil rose, partly reversing recent drops, which complicates the inflation outlook.

April 2025, not late 2018: his base case, and a five-step checklist for the days after

He leans on two past drawdowns and Buffett to argue for patience, and gives followers a concrete checklist instead of reassurance.

  • 2018: from the September high to the Dec 24 low the S&P 500 lost ~19.8% (US-China trade war, global slowdown fears, a restrictive Fed); it fully recovered by Apr 23, 2019, four months from extreme pessimism to highs.
  • April 2025: after Trump's tariffs the S&P fell ~18.9% from its February high to an Apr 8 low; on Apr 9 a 90-day pause sent it up ~9.5% in one session and the Nasdaq 12%+, and by early June it was 20%+ off the low. Yesterday reminds him of that day.
  • Worst case: a 2018 repeat (S&P -16% from Oct 1 to Dec 15, individual portfolios -50-60%, slow recovery over four to five months); his view is we are closer to April 2025, with a possible year-end rally, opinion not certainty.
  • Buffett: nobody can catch the bottom; when you buy a stock be ready for it to fall 50% or more. Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B) fell 50% three times with nothing wrong with the business; those who suffer bought on debt or react to every price move.
  • Checklist: (1) test the thesis, not the price: results, competitive edge, growth outlook, balance sheet, and whether your exposure fits your risk; (2) check cumulative exposure, not single weights; (3) buy selectively and in stages, conditioned on price stabilization; (4) keep a liquidity reserve suited to you; (5) no permanent decisions on temporary emotions.
  • Cumulative exposure: AMD, Broadcom, Oracle, Nebius and IREN can all be hit by an AI-spending repricing; SPCX, RKLB and ASTS move together on space-sector risk perception.
  • Don'ts: panic-selling at support, buying with all your cash before NFP, or changing a ten-year strategy after ten hours of volatility.

Glossary & Entities

Market breadth — How many stocks rise versus fall; 426 of ~499 S&P 500 names falling shows a market-wide move.
Correction — A drop of 10% or more from a recent high; the Nasdaq crossed it on Nov 5.
Buying on margin — Buying shares with money borrowed from the broker against the portfolio, which magnifies gains and losses.
Issue price — The price at which new shares were sold in an offering, Oracle's $116; a reference point, not guaranteed support.
ARR — Annualized run-rate revenue from capacity in service and contracted; not realized GAAP revenue.
GB200 NVL72 — Nvidia's rack-scale AI system of 72 GPUs, whose power and cooling Vertiv co-developed.
