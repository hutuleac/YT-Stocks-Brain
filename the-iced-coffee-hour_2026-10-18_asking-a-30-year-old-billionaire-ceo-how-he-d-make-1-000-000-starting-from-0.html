Shelves are real estate: how Bloom bought its way into every Target

Retail runs on paid placement and logistics; the margin lives in moving cans, not making them.

  • Bootstrapped to $180 million a year before the beverage, ~85% from a greens powder driven by influencers and Mari Llewellyn as the face. Bloom Pop does ~$130M a year.
  • July 2022: tested in 600 Targets; Target said it decides once a year. Greg offered $1 million to be in all 2,400 by January 1 — Target agreed. Bloom now spends up to tens of millions a year on shelf placement, e.g. at 7-Eleven.
  • A can retails ~$2.50 at Target; typical energy-drink gross margin is 50-60%, but logistics is half the expense. Bloom was most profitable as a 100% Shopify DTC business.
  • Kicked off Amazon for 45-60 days after an unsupervised agency bought fake reviews — 24 hours from mass layoffs. Lesson: never rely on one channel; now in 85,000 doors.
  • A 2026 mistake: ~$20M of root beer made, $10 million written off — 'there's a reason root beer isn't in the top five sodas'.
  • The barrier to entry is gone: 11 co-manufacturers, $100M+ lines he doesn't own, only 3PLs and Shopify. Bloom started with a $20,000 minimum order.
  • Designed backwards from the channel: logo placed above an influencer's hand, matte vs gloss tested under Target lights, the 'B' logo swapped for a word mark after shoppers couldn't find it on Amazon.

Celebrity posts have stopped selling — retention and micro-creators do

An unpaid Kylie Jenner endorsement moved nothing; Bloom's growth comes from repeat buyers and thousands of small creators.

  • Kylie Jenner blind-tested Shirley Temples in her story and crowned Bloom's — no tick-up in sales. Four years ago it would have sold out in 12 hours; he dropped plans to sign A-listers in 2027.
  • 575M cans sold to roughly a tenth as many people buying ~10 times: 'acquisition is a lot easier than retention'.
  • Prime's problem, per Greg: no repeat buying from a young, ritual-less consumer and marketing to kids. Congo Brands also owned Alani, sold to Celsius for ~$2B.
  • Influencer deals priced on a CPM from creators' recent averages; TikTok Shop affiliates are ~25% of influencer spend, run profit-neutral, with 30-40% of that revenue spilling into Amazon and Target.
  • AI plus Meta targeting: from 'are you a woman looking to grow your glutes?' in 2019 to 3,000 AI variants aimed at 'the wife of a plumber in Kansas'.
  • Brand over attribution: a ~$1M free Soho community event, $1M+ into Mari's Pursuit of Wellness podcast, ~$5M to open a new demographic like moms. One stay-at-home mom affiliate sold $50,000 of Clear Protein on TikTok Shop in a day. 'Half of marketing isn't working; they don't know which half.'
  • Bloom Energy (unrelated) sponsored LeBron's 76ers for ~$30M, flooding Greg with messages; Graham and Jack note they bought Bloom Energy at $57 on a Chris Camilo tip.

Energy drinks will outsell water — and Greg rates every rival

Consumers are 'obsessed' with energy and hydration; Greg says legacy brands win on distribution, not product.

  • In the blind test he spotted Celsius ('major medicinal aftertaste') and Red Bull by smell. His view: Celsius is a legacy brand that won only through Pepsi distribution.
  • C4: 'overengineered' for $2.50 a can; Bloom partners with Nutrabolt (C4's parent, Austin, founder Doss Cunningham) and shares its flavor lab.
  • Monster: huge respect — on Coca-Cola trucks, sold everywhere, the White Monster (grapefruit), the Call of Duty deal. The other flavor pole is 'melted-down Swedish fish' — Ghost has a literal Swedish Fish energy drink. Red Bull, private and unchanged for 40 years, is 'without a doubt' worth ~a quarter trillion — at least 50% more than Monster's ~$100B.
  • GLP-1s didn't change what people eat — they eat less of the same. Energy drinks are pacing to outsell unflavored water at Walmart within 5 years; Starbucks, Dutch Bros and Costa refreshers are energy drinks by drive-through.
  • Bloom was 'reverse engineered for the female consumer'; he expects more men by 2027-28 because wives stock the fridge — 'a woman will never try' a Monster.
  • Caffeine: ~100mg in Canadian Bloom vs ~180mg in the US; the line is marketing to kids, as Prime did. Hydration is hard — only Gatorade and Electrolit do it well.
  • Partnering with Dr. Pepper brought QA standards from a hundred-year-old company that has already made the mistakes.

Nine-figure days, a $20M house he 'hasn't spent', and a 'toxic abundance mindset'

Greg spends freely but structures it — cash-out refinancing, chartering instead of buying, and keeping luxuries off Bloom's books.

  • Several nine-figure days from selling Bloom stakes for cash — four wire transfers in 24 hours, into a new account. Early on, TD Bank kicked him out of a student account for daily cash withdrawals; Mari put $40K cash on the counter at Chase.
  • Aspen house over $20M paid in cash (~$25M), then a cash-out refinance at 2-3% — the money is back in his portfolio. Some cars (Ferrari, SLS) have appreciated.
  • Advisers talked him out of a G200 jet ($7-8M) — charter now rather than upgrade to a G4 in 2-3 years. Monthly budget ~$300-500K; he books jets as personal, not business, out of duty to shareholders.
  • Bought a $4.5M Santa Monica house he couldn't afford to force himself to grow; went all-in over a dozen times before 29 and moved five times in five years.
  • Failures: $15K of meme-page ads that delivered foreign followers and no Shopify traffic.
  • Wouldn't sell Bloom for $3B cash today; no longer owns over 50%; won't IPO — big-firm audits already 'know my blood work'. No other investments: all-in on Bloom for at least 5 more years.
  • Would rename the show 'The Iced Bloom Hour' for $1-5M a year and consider an ~$80K Bloom-wrapped Cybertruck.

TRT, BPC-157 and a $100K hyperbaric chamber — tuned for focus, not abs

Greg optimizes hormones and biomarkers to compete at '10 out of 10' with ADHD, and calls the stigma around enhancers pointless.

  • 150mg a week of bioidentical testosterone — 'a massive unlock' for his ADHD via stable hormones; froze sperm after a fertility scare.
  • Hyperbaric chamber ($100,000) three times a week, a 205°F sauna for 30 minutes five nights a week, red-light panels, several concierge doctors, quarterly bloodwork.
  • Findings: high inflammation from years of viral bulking (to 245 lb) and cutting, felt as brain fog; high hematocrit from altitude, fixed by donating blood quarterly.
  • Daily stack down from ~50 supplements to 4-5: Bloom greens, magnesium, oral BPC-157 for the gut, a micro-dose of Cialis (vasodilation; Greg Doucette's advice on the show). Likes creatine and digestive enzymes; skips NAD and glutathione.
  • Caffeine: 3-4 Blooms before 10am, 700mg to 1g a day, still asleep by 9:30 pm. Huberman reportedly takes 800mg+. Off ADHD meds since university.
  • Supplement rule: more isn't better — start high, taper down, watch how you feel; anything too good to be true is.

No finish line: entrepreneurship as 'GTA in real life' — and what it cost

Greg argues people are depressed after exits because they stopped working, not because they got rich — but his own grind cost him family traditions.

  • Chip on his shoulder: ADHD, special ed, a 2.4 GPA and academic probation three times in an academic Westchester town. 'Most entrepreneurs start with that chip.'
  • The 'lies of the last generation' (30 years at one company, a 401k at 70) hold people back; wanting more is good, not ungrateful.
  • Not Gary Vee's windowless grind: a 14-hour day is lunches, suppliers and tastings. Bloom's first HQ was his one-bathroom Santa Monica Airbnb; Bloom worked from co-working until $100M revenue.
  • 'We're going to beat Celsius' because it's run by 9-to-5 'suits'; founders who sell and stop working end up doing ayahuasca six times.
  • The cost: five missed Thanksgivings and Christmases (Black Friday, resolution season) — his family stopped holding them. Goal now: a larger family gathering.
  • Wealth and kids: a trust may be a disservice, so the Aspen, Colorado house's hard hikes and skiing simulate real challenge for his son.
  • Money buys delegation (he's never run laundry) but the best memories are camper-van trips and a $50 Yellowstone pass; a full-time chef cost them cooking together — the paradox of choice.

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🛒
Retail: Budget for slotting fees and logistics before celebrating a retail deal — shelves are rented, not won.
🔁
Marketing: Track repeat purchase rate first; one-time buyers from hype are a warning sign, not a win.
🎥
Creators: Run creator and TikTok Shop deals on a CPM ceiling and measure spillover into other channels.
🧱
Risk: Never let one channel or one unsupervised agency hold most of your revenue.
📈
Consumer: Watch energy drinks vs water at Walmart and Celsius' growth against new entrants as category signals.
🧪
Health: Get baseline bloodwork before adding supplements, and cut rather than stack.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Slotting fee — What a brand pays a retailer for shelf or display placement.
CPM — Cost per thousand views, the basis Bloom uses to price creator deals.
3PL — Third-party logistics provider that warehouses and ships for a brand.
Cash-out refinance — Borrowing against an owned asset to pull the cash back out for investment.
TRT — Testosterone replacement therapy.
BPC-157 — A peptide Greg uses for gut lining and recovery.
Hematocrit — Share of blood volume made up of red blood cells; high levels thicken blood.
