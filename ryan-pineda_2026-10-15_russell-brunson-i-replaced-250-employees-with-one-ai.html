One AI box runs the company: 250 support staff and 100+ developers replaced

Todd Dickerson built an AI "box" on OpenClaw that Brunson says now runs most of ClickFunnels, and staff were told to manage AI agents or leave.

  • Headcount: ~500 at peak, under 100 now and "still optimizing". Without the box they would need 350-400.
  • In January the ClickFunnels team was flown to Canton, Georgia and told to 10x productivity with AI tools or leave; the coaching side got the same message a month ago. "You should not be writing copy."
  • Myron Golden's four levels of value: muscle (caps ~$60-70k), management (~$100-150k), communication (no ceiling), imagination. Using ChatGPT or Claude to do 3x more task work drops you back to muscle; the move is managing 10 agents, then skilled agents, then imagining.
  • The FHL AI offer: $150,000 to set up the box and sub-agents for your business, vs. the usual $25,000 FHL coaching. He needed 20 sales and sold ~31 with 5-6 pending (~$4.5M).
  • Ryan's pushback, echoing an Alex Hormozi video: doing more tasks isn't making more money. Brunson says it's "definitely making more" but hard to quantify. The revenue-tied uses are VSLs, ad creative and Overskill; the rest is speed (contracts back in an hour, not a week).
  • His assistant replaced an eight-person book-publishing division with one Overskill app she runs in about an hour a week.

Big SaaS dies, custom software wins, and maybe software goes to zero

Brunson thinks one-size SaaS is finished because anyone can build exactly the tool they need. Ryan goes further and says software itself is heading to zero.

  • A team member, Sean, replaced a sales CRM they used 10% of with a custom build; new features take ~30 minutes. Brunson has built 37 software companies in three months with no coders and uses them as bonuses that lift conversions.
  • He took screenshots of a $150,000 custom app, uploaded them to Overskill, and had it rebuilt in 30 minutes for ~$500 of credits. Ryan's M19 app took ~3 months with one tech guy, and Brunson says it could now be done in 24 hours.
  • ClickFunnels is "still a $50 million business", but nobody builds 40-page funnels in an editor anymore. The new ClickFunnels AI is a funnel builder only: no CRM, real-time split testing by traffic source, $25 a month plus credits.
  • Ryan: "all software results in zero". He told a friend his AI product was worth $0 as a core product, only as a lead magnet or bonus. He also notes AI credits are subsidized today.
  • Brunson's counter is to monetize transactions: ClickFunnels has processed $14.1B through Stripe for a cut, and Overskill is partnering with a payments platform. Pre-Salesforce, CRM installs cost ~$500,000; SaaS made it $100 a month, and he sees AI as the next shift of that size.
  • Eben Pagan's Opportunity: fewer billion-dollar opportunities but "a billion $100,000 opportunities". Brunson says he could "pump out a $100,000 a year thing every 15 minutes", and that a portfolio of ten of them is where most people's opportunity is.

Recommendation 14 AI VSLS IN FOUR MONTHSsoftwarecareer

AI beats him at copy, and it's starting to close sales

Out of the box AI copy "always sucks". Fed the right structure, voice and stories, Brunson says it now beats him.

  • Method: (1) funnel-hack the best: AI transcribed and analyzed 37 Agora VSLs, and all but two used the same script structure; (2) teach it his voice; (3) dump in the stories. The hour-long secretsofpropaganda.com VSL came out of this.
  • 14 VSLs in four months, all AI-written; sometimes he reads the script on camera with AI animation overlaid.
  • ElevenLabs learned his audiobook voice and now "reads" 150 personal-development books on his membership site. He says an AI version of his stage pitch is close now. Precedent from ~15 years ago: a webinar seller with a thick New York accent re-recorded his script with a UK voice actor and it "crushed".
  • AI phone rep: called challenge non-buyers, disclosed it was AI, offered the AI-caller setup free with purchase, and sent an order link that expired when the call ended. "They closed a ton of more sales." Ryan says the best AI he has seen is Hormozi's, texting him over iMessage.
  • He expects appointment setting and much closing to go to AI, with human closers still needed.
  • "Courses are dead" (not today, but getting harder; Ryan jokes "dead" like Joe Biden: still here, but we know): the future is information plus software. He turned each of Vincent James's books into a software product in 30 minutes and is rewriting his own books so each exercise is a prompt; an FHL speaker teaches a "prompt path" coaching model.

Contested LIVE SEMINARS LOSING TO VIRTUALconsumercareer

Virtual challenges on YouTube beat live seminars

Both hosts are done with big live seminars: they cost millions, one bad pitch loses six months, and a virtual challenge makes far more.

  • FHL10 cost ~$7M to produce and launch; run as a challenge, Brunson estimates ~20x the return. This FHL broke even on tickets at the Fontainebleau, where the built-in stage saved the usual ~$3M setup. Ryan says last year's WealthCon was his last.
  • Side Hustle Summit with Iman's team: just under 2M registered, 203,000 active at peak on day one. International traffic converted worse but cost so little that ROAS was higher than anywhere else. ClickFunnels has mostly stayed in the UK and Australia, since its $97 a month is a year's income in some countries.
  • They ran it 100% on YouTube, not Zoom: 35,000 new subscribers after day one, and Brunson guesses ~500,000 over the campaign. Brunson added 90,000 Instagram followers that week. "I don't think I'll ever use Zoom again."
  • 4,500 unique ad creatives: five scripts x 15 hooks x 3-4 CTAs, re-shot across sets and in a car. AI's role is writing the hooks and scripts.
  • Iman's team launches every 90 days, buys traffic for ~3 weeks and floods content the rest of the time, so ad costs drop each cycle. Brunson's longest-running offer was ClickFunnels (~7 years); Jeff Walker has run Product Launch Formula one week a year for 20+ years.
  • Live risks: a guru switched his talk to an anti-pitch right before Ryan's pitch and texted attendees about "five more spots"; a remote speaker pitched a cheaper version of Brunson's offer by QR code. At FHL a friend booked $600,000 of sales in the back room on a $1,000 ticket.
  • Brunson's flops: a $2,000 FHL offer before Tony Robbins' keynote that didn't break even; a $497 offer in Atlanta to ~3,000 people in 9,000 chairs that sold ~30; a Salt Lake slot where his demo video failed to load; and an early ClickFunnels webinar for Australia at ~4 a.m. where three people showed up.

The $500M no, and the $40M Voomly mistake

Brunson turned down two offers valuing ClickFunnels at about $500M, and now calls buying a business and not selling his own his two biggest financial mistakes.

  • A broker said a buyer valued ClickFunnels at over $1B on merchant volume. Two final offers came in at ~$500M for a majority stake, roughly 10x EBITDA of $45-50M.
  • Volume ran through Stripe, so buyers discounted it; owning the merchant of record would have made it a $1B+ company, and he now has a payments business for that.
  • With no outside capital they had skipped the usual professionalization rounds. Buyers flagged the lack of professional management, which Kajabi had and "ended up getting sold for $2 billion" while ClickFunnels was "five, six times bigger", he says.
  • He says building out a CRM to compete was a mistake; he should have stayed the best funnel builder, which is what ClickFunnels AI now is. Asked what it's worth today: "A lot less."
  • Voomly: bought for $40M when it was doing $40M a year and $10M net; Facebook algorithm changes hit as it closed and it "never made money". By contrast Dan Kennedy's business cost "a couple million" and paid back during the first launch.
  • Next: he will "100%" sell. The plan is to run Overskill hard for two years, then read, write and run events from the event center and museum he's building.

A celebrity virtual event as the front end for M19

Brunson's pitch for Ryan's golf mastermind: a short virtual event with one celebrity golfer and member testimonials, followed by an offer.

  • M19 today: 500+ members, all over seven figures, average eight figures in revenue. They pay an initiation fee, annual dues and per event (~$5,000 for three days with golf, hotel and food). Events of 30-40 sell out with no sales team.

Personal story ZERO STOCKS — BOOKS INSTEADsoftwarefinance

Underserved niches, and a net worth held in rare books

Brunson sees the opportunity in tiny passion niches nobody would have paid to build software for, starting with his own book collection.

  • He owns ~25,000 books bought over four years and kept buying duplicates, so he built a cataloging app he says is the best on the market. He plans to offer it to the 30 Facebook groups of collectors he's in: ~1,000 users at $10-20 a month = ~$120,000 a year with no ads.
  • Same play in wrestling: there is only one wrestling software company, so he bought wrestler.com for $30,000 (wrestling.com was ~$500,000). He holds ~3,500 domains and has never flipped one.
  • Zero stocks: most of his net worth is books, ~$13-14M invested. Mormon books trade in a defined, liquid dealer market; Napoleon Hill and personal-development items are illiquid, and he's "the whale" bidding against himself.
  • Two $1.5M purchases: the only complete known copy of Napoleon Hill's self-printed, signed precursor to Law of Success (1928), and one of two privately held copies of the Book of Commandments.
  • A first-edition Book of Mormon cost him $30,000 ten years ago and would sell for ~$250,000 today (5,000 printed, ~350 known). He estimates a Gutenberg Bible at $300-400M.
  • He also collects authors' objects: Napoleon Hill's typewriter and glasses, Earl Nightingale's typewriter and microphone.

What to do with this

Best Actionable Takeaways

🤖
AI ops: Move each role from doing tasks with AI to managing a set of agents. Measure output per person, not hours saved.
🧰
Software: Before renewing a SaaS seat you use 10% of, price a custom rebuild; screenshots plus an AI builder may cover it in an afternoon.
✍️
Marketing: Give AI the best-in-class structure, your voice and your stories before asking it to write copy.
📺
Events: Run virtual launches on YouTube, not Zoom, so every registrant becomes a subscriber for the next launch.
💵
Pricing: Charge for transactions or outcomes, not seats. Assume software itself trends to a free bonus.
🏷️
Exits: If you might sell, professionalize management and own your payments early; both drove the discount on ClickFunnels.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

FHL — Funnel Hacking Live, ClickFunnels' annual live event.
VSL — Video sales letter: a scripted long-form video that sells an offer.
ROAS — Return on ad spend: revenue generated per dollar of advertising.
Funnel hacking — Reverse-engineering the best-performing competitors' funnels and scripts before building your own.
Merchant of record — The legal seller that processes the payment; owning it puts the transaction volume on your books.
Four levels of value — Myron Golden's ladder of earning: muscle, management, communication, imagination.
