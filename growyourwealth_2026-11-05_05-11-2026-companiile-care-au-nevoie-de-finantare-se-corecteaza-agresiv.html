Broad rally, narrow AI: the first post-election session splits growth in two

The author calls Nov 4 a rotation, with a broad risk-on tape sitting on top of a semiconductor sell-off, and says continuity, not the index color, is the test.

  • Nov 4 closes: S&P 500 7,688.62 (+1.58%), Nasdaq Composite 25,736.51 (+1.44%), Dow 53,356.12 (+3.03%, over 1,500 points), Russell 2000 2,856.95 (+1.58%).
  • 9 of 11 S&P sectors rose: consumer discretionary first at +4.3%, energy last at -4.8% as oil collapsed.
  • Philadelphia Semiconductor fell 3.1% to a six-month low, opposite to the broad market; the Nasdaq swung nearly 5% within 12 hours.
  • If the S&P keeps rising while semis and AI infrastructure stay under pressure, that is sector rotation, not a recovery of the whole AI theme.
  • Scenarios: positive (S&P holds, yields stable or lower, AMD stabilizes, SPCX back above $140, less pressure on NBIS and IREN); base case positive market with sector rotation toward profitable, cash-rich companies; negative (yields up, budget gridlock or tighter AI-infrastructure limits) points to 7,570-7,600 on the S&P, the pre-rally zone, as a reference, not a guaranteed support.
  • Approach: sell nothing on one session, hold a cash reserve, avoid aggressive buying right after extreme moves, and watch each theme's weight in the portfolio.

Data-center moratorium fear makes connected capacity scarce and rewards the hyperscalers

The market began pricing fewer new data centers, which lifts owners of existing capacity and hits the vendors who depend on new builds; the author says demand is intact, building capacity is what got scarce.

  • Trigger: Democrats take Congress and, in Texas, Hinojosa beats Abbott on a platform that includes stopping new data-center construction; he stresses they can change their mind.
  • Wells Fargo: Texas is 27% of planned US capacity in gigawatts; Goldman estimates possible moratoria could affect about 45% of planned expansion.
  • Winners: AMZN +7.2%, MSFT +6.6%, GOOGL +4.1% (~$750B of market cap in one session). He thinks hyperscalers may cut capex over the next two quarters, which lifts free cash flow, and expects META to rebound hard alongside Google and Amazon, with Microsoft already up a lot.
  • Losers: CRDO -9.9%, CRWV -9.5%, COHR -7.4%, MU -5.6%; NVDA held to -1.3%; AMD erased its +9% post-earnings gain.
  • Hyperscaler spending is over $740B this year versus about $400B last year, and the market is starting to price that it will not rise further.
  • Demand check: Google guarantees roughly $19B of leases between TeraWulf and Anthropic for 401 MW, delivered from late 2027 to Q2 2028, signed and financed.
  • Some AI infrastructure trades under 10x estimated 2027 earnings; his read is the market is selling 2028 projects while 2027 projects are already launched and funded, so the dividing line is new-project dependence versus multi-year committed customers.

Oracle's $20B raise: dilution and debt cost now, cash flow is the real test

The author's second focus after SpaceX, buying in tranches, but only cash-flow conversion of contracts, not backlog growth, will tell him if the build pays off.

  • Wed close $123.73 (-1.95%), range $121.78-129.85, volume about 112 million shares; market cap about $386B and trailing P/E about 19-20.
  • Financing of about $20B: roughly $10B equity (86.2 million shares at $116) and roughly $10B bonds; effects are dilution and interest obligations that operations must cover.

  • P/E misses lease commitments and future investment, so a proper view uses enterprise value, net debt, lease obligations and free cash flow.
  • Levels: first support $121-122, second $118.43, issue price $116 (a financial marker, not a guaranteed technical support), resistance $128-130.
  • Decisive metric for him: conversion of the contract portfolio into revenue, operating profit and free cash flow.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Hyperscaler — A large-scale cloud provider such as Amazon, Microsoft or Google that operates the data centers AI vendors sell into.
Philadelphia Semiconductor index (SOX) — The benchmark of US-listed chip companies, used here as the gauge of AI-hardware stress inside a rising market.
Contracted vs recognized vs collected revenue — Signed backlog, revenue booked under accounting rules, and cash actually received; his point is that only the last funds capex.
OCI — Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, the unit Oracle is turning into a major AI-infrastructure provider.
Non-cash charge from customer share rights — An accounting expense from granting customers rights to buy shares, with no immediate cash outflow but possible dilution if exercised.
